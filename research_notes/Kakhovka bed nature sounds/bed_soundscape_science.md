Researched 4 November 2026. ## 1. What the bed sounds like: published descriptions (frogs, birds, insects, the forest)

### Takeaway
Every published description of the bed's sound is about birdsong, above all nightingales and reed warblers in the young willow forest in May, said by UNCG's Oleksii Vasyliuk and Anna Kuzemko after their May 2026 expedition (Kherson oblast bank, near Novovorontsovka, about 48 km downstream of the crossing). No reporter or scientist has published a description of frog choruses, insect sound or wind in the willows on the bed. TSN's crew recorded the nightingales "mixed with the humming of front-line drones".

### Inferences
- What can be said on screen with a source: in May the young willow forest on the bed is loud with nightingales (two species) and reed warblers, by day and into the night, and on the Kherson bank in May 2026 it was heard with distant drones. Anything about frogs, insects or wind in the trees would be convention, not report.
- The TSN and Texty accounts are almost certainly the same May 2026 UNCG expedition (dates and place agree with Vasyliuk's iNaturalist recordings of 13–14 May 2026 at Novovorontsovka, section 2).

## 2. Surveys of the bed's fauna, and whether recordings or acoustic monitoring exist

### Takeaway
There is no published passive acoustic monitoring (AudioMoth, BirdNET) or bioacoustic study of the bed. But real, dated, georeferenced recordings from the bed do exist and are public: Oleksii Vasyliuk uploaded 144 sound observations to iNaturalist in May and July 2026 (CC BY), 96 of them inside OpenStreetMap's outline of the drained bed near Novovorontsovka and Mar'ianske, including nine thrush nightingale recordings. Another recordist (Kateryna Kormina, "uabird") has recorded birds monthly at Marhanets, about 12–17 km from the crossing, since 2022 (CC BY-NC), but in town and north of it, not on the bed. Formal surveys are vegetation papers. Fauna work is expedition-based, restricted to a strip about 200 m deep, and unpublished as papers.

### Inferences
- Vasyliuk's CC BY recordings are the strongest evidence-side source for the film's nature sound: made on the drained bed, dated, georeferenced, by a named scientist, licensed for reuse with credit. They are about 48 km downstream (west) of the crossing, on the same bed and in the same habitat, so a caption would have to say "recorded on the former reservoir bed near Novovorontsovka, 13–14 May 2026, Oleksii Vasyliuk (CC BY)", not "under the lines". These are short identification clips, not long ambiences; their sound quality and length are unchecked.
- The Kormina recordings (CC BY-NC) are the nearest to the crossing in place and the longest in time (2022–2026, month by month) but are from Marhanets town and its north, off the bed, and NC licensing needs her permission for a film that may be distributed.
- No frog sound has been recorded on the bed in public. Marsh frogs and green toads are present in the reach (iNaturalist), so a marsh frog chorus is plausible but would have to be a recording from elsewhere, labelled so.

## 3. Birds and the power lines: the cormorant colony on the crossing towers

### Takeaway
The 2012 survey is verified in the paper itself: Sidorenko and Siokhin (2016) counted 1,240 great cormorant nests on six towers of the two Enerhodar crossings (330 kV: 320, 280, 200, 100; 750 kV: 190, 150), with Caspian gulls also nesting; towers near the right bank weren't reached. No count after 2012 and nothing about the colony after the drainage has been published; no collision or electrocution data for these lines were found.

### Cited Findings
- [peer-reviewed] Sidorenko A.I., Siokhin V.D. (2016). "Гнездование большого баклана (Phalacrocorax carbo) на техногенных сооружениях в Украине" / "Nesting of Great Cormorants (Phalacrocorax carbo) on man-made structures in Ukraine". *Visnyk of Dnipropetrovsk University.   - Survey year: "Исследования гнездовых поселений баклана на опорах ЛЭП проведены в 2012 г."

### Inferences
- The colony depended on fish in open water and on isolation from land predators; with the towers' caissons standing on drained ground or by the channel since June 2023, both are reduced. Whether birds still nest on the crossing towers is unknown. It must not be shown or said as current.
- The 2012 nests were mostly at 20–25 m or lower, below the conductors of either line; this is a drawing note for the 3D towers if the colony appears in a pre-drainage moment, dated 2012.
- The towers at the crossing are a military front-line site; a drone or satellite check for guano on the crossarms would also touch the project's safety rules (towers, damage), so ask before trying.

## 4. Timing by season and hour; war noise and the bed's fauna

### Takeaway
The only dated, sourced sound of the bed is May (nightingales, warblers, by day and at night). The iNaturalist recordings give real dates for when species are heard in the reach (thrush nightingale from late April to mid June; scops owl at night April–July; waders in July–August; redwings, waxwings and dunnocks in winter). Frog-call timing has no Ukrainian source here; a Bulgarian study recorded marsh frog mating calls April–June. War noise affecting the bed's fauna has been reported but not measured.
