# e-flux: "Nuclear Cyberwar: From Energy Colonialism to Energy Terrorism"

Svitlana Matviyenko, *e-flux Journal* #126, April 2022.
https://www.e-flux.com/journal/126/460842/nuclear-cyberwar-from-energy-colonialism-to-energy-terrorism/

Read in full on 5 November 2026: the page's HTML saved to a scratch folder and stripped to text, so every quote below is
the page's own wording. The essay has an untitled opening and three sections ("Nuclear Tensions in the Cyberdomain",
"The Imperial Roots of Nuclear Occupation", "Nuclear Colonialism"), with 41 notes.

**The author.** Svitlana Matviyenko is Associate Professor of Critical Media Analysis in the School of Communication at
Simon Fraser University (Vancouver) and associate director of its Digital Democracies Institute. There she leads the
research streams "Cyberwar Topologies" and "Media, Infrastructure, Environment". With Nick Dyer-Witheford she wrote
*Cyberwar and Revolution: Digital Subterfuge in Global Capitalism* (University of Minnesota Press, 2019; the essay's
own note gives 2021). Her work since 2022 is on nuclear terror, the weaponising of pollution and technogenic
catastrophe in Russia's war on Ukraine (profiles at [VCU ISEE](https://isee.vcu.edu/research/data-ecologies-network/svitlana-matviyenko.html),
[The World](https://theworld.org/contributors/svitlana-matviyenko), [Berliner Gazette](https://berlinergazette.de/author/svitlana-matviyenko/)).
She writes as a media theorist, not as a nuclear engineer or an investigator.

**When it was written.** It was written in the war's sixth week: it reports the Russian withdrawal from Chernobyl on
31 March 2022 as recent. So it comes before everything the film's record shows from that summer on: the lines cut one
by one, island mode, the first complete loss of off-site power, cold shutdown (the last unit on 13 April 2024), the
dam (6 June 2023) and the truces. When it says "six active … reactors", that was true at the time. It is not now.

**The title's words are not the body's.** "Energy colonialism" and "energy terrorism" appear only in the title. The body
says "nuclear terrorism", "nuclear colonialism" and "waste colonialism". It never mentions the power lines, off-site
power, the grid, the cooling water or the reservoir.

## 1. The argument

Matviyenko reads Russia's seizures of Chernobyl (24 February 2022) and Zaporizhzhia (3–4 March 2022) as acts of
*nuclear terrorism*. Taking a plant whose safety cannot be defended by fighting turns a civilian energy plant into a
weapon and a hostage, aimed at "local and remote publics" alike. She places this inside her and Dyer-Witheford's theory
of cyberwar, which refuses to separate the digital from the "kinetic". Read that way, the shelling of Zaporizhzhia is
Stuxnet reversed. Stuxnet was code that slowed Iran's centrifuges to keep a bomb from being made. At Zaporizhzhia,
projectiles entered a plant's systems to "accelerate the transformation of the industrial object into a potential
nuclear bomb". She then traces the occupation to empire. Ukraine's plants were built in the Soviet 1970s under the myth
of the "peaceful atom", so they are "remainders of the Soviet atomic past", and that legacy "attracts the Russian
imperial army". She uses "internal colonisation" (Hrytsak, Snyder, Etkind) and Virilio's "endocolonisation" for this,
and in Polissia she finds an earlier "nuclear colonialism": military rule, dispossession, and contamination that began
before 1986. She ends by turning Virilio's "original accident" around. The nuclear power station was not fully
"invented" by its accident at Chernobyl in 1986. It was invented in 2022, "in the non-accidental act of nuclear
terrorism with an imperialist genealogy".

### Key ideas, with quotes

- **Nuclear terrorism, as the negation of combat.** The staff at the gate, by megaphone: "This is nuclear industrial
  infrastructure! There is danger of a nuclear accident! Stop shooting and leave the premises! This is an act of nuclear
  terrorism!" She takes the definition from Charles Townshend: "The essence of terrorism … is surely the negation of
  combat. Its targets are attacked in a way that inhibits (or better prohibits) self-defence."
- **The plant as weapon and hostage.** "The occupation of Chernobyl weaponized the plant's entire remaining nuclear
  energy production infrastructure by turning it into a nuclear weapon." She calls it an example of "the 'hostage-holding'
  function of military control in contemporary mediatized societies" (after Benjamin Noys), which "equally targets local
  and remote publics".
- **Controlled exposure.** "As with any act of terrorism, Russian forces' terrorist takeover of these nuclear power
  plants is highly media oriented, but only strives for exposure it can control." Her example is fourteen Russian state
  media journalists at the plant on 7 March 2022, while the IAEA "did not have access".
- **Cyberwar as one continuum.** "We do not distinguish between cyberwar (in the form of cyberattacks, disinformation,
  psyops) and war on the ground." And: "an act of cyberwarfare always assembles at the intersection of different
  domains; it involves different materialities; and it is necessarily linked to war."
- **Stuxnet reversed.** "These cases are structurally similar; the latter is simply a reversed version of the former."
- **Networks beyond the site.** The projectiles "penetrated the nuclear plants' systems to destabilize their complex
  assemblages of networks that expand far beyond Ukraine". She means international networks of information and safety
  regulation, not the grid.
- **Soviet nuclear history.** "With their Soviet/Russian-built … reactors, these plants simultaneously materialize the
  Ukrainian atomic present and constitute the remainders of the Soviet atomic past. Unsurprisingly, this infrastructural
  legacy attracts the Russian imperial army." And the Pripyat rooftop slogan: "Let the atom be a worker, not a soldier"
  (Хай буде атом робітником, а не солдатом).
- **Empire, and the occupation as an exploit.** "The occupation of the Zaporizhzhia NPP is not accidental. It is an
  imperialist zero-day exploit of cyberwar, when a barbaric army from a different age enters your land as if it still
  belongs to the politico-economic Union that collapsed over thirty years ago."
- **Nuclear and waste colonialism (Polissia).** Construction of the Chernobyl plant and the Duga radar "introduced
  military rule to the Polissia region". The *tuteshni* ("those who live here") became "dual hostages of Soviet nuclear
  colonialism". She quotes Max Liboiron: "Pollution … is best understood as the violence of colonial land relations
  rather than environmental damage".
- **The accident turned around.** Virilio: "just as the Chernobyl meltdown is the invention of the nuclear power
  station". Matviyenko: "The full realization of the nuclear power station as a key technology of modernity was not in
  its accidental meltdown, but in the non-accidental act of nuclear terrorism with an imperialist genealogy".

**Opinion and theory, not evidence:** "nuclear terrorism" as a description of the seizure; that the plant was
"weaponized" or turned "into a nuclear weapon"; that cyberwar and kinetic war are one thing; Stuxnet reversed; internal
or endo-colonisation; nuclear and waste colonialism; "zero-day exploit"; "a barbaric army from a different age"; the
reversal of the original accident. These are a theorist's frames, worth knowing and citing, but none of them can be
drawn as a fact.

**Cyber is a frame here, not an event.** The essay reports no cyberattack on the ZNPP or its lines, and the project
has found none. The documented attacks on Ukraine's grid (Industroyer in 2016, Industroyer2 against high-voltage
substations in April 2022, by ESET and CERT-UA) are not about this plant and are not in the essay.

## 3. Ideas for the film

### Framing and chapter titles

- **Leave the essay's words out of the titles.** HOSTAGE, ENERGY TERROR, NUCLEAR COLONIALISM or ZERO-DAY as chapter
  cards would put a theorist's verdict in the film's own voice. That breaks "claims are attributed" and the wording
  rules (rule 4: "occupied" and "cut"). The current titles (TEN LINES, POWER IN, THE WATER, THE COUNTDOWN, AT NIGHT, THE
  MEADOW) say what happens, which is the forensic film's way (`research_notes/Forensic film structures/`). Keep them.
- **"A worker, not a soldier"** is the one phrase that could carry a chapter. Built to work, the plant now does no work:
  it makes no power and draws power in. As the film's own title it is the film's judgement, and it brings in Chernobyl
  and Pripyat, which are off the site. At most use it as an attributed epigraph (see 4). The joke version the essay also
  quotes, the "Хуй буде…" graffiti, is never usable.

### A concept to organise the narrative

- **The reversal.** Her Stuxnet structure ("a reversed version of the former") rhymes with the film's own turn in
  chapter II: power out, then power in. The film doesn't need her cyber frame to use it. The useful part is the method:
  name a structure, then show it reversed. The film already does this with evidence (`power-flows`, `brief-3`). No
  change, but it is a reference for the inversion graphic (`power-flows`, missing).
- **Turning the accident around a second time (the strongest idea for the film).** Virilio says Chernobyl's meltdown
  "invented" the nuclear station. Matviyenko says the seizure did, in 2022. What the film shows four years on is a
  third answer, and the film doesn't have to say it, because the record shows it: no meltdown and no bomb, but a plant
  kept safe by one wire across a front line, mended in truces of hours. Twenty-six complete losses, seven truces, one
  line of ten. The essay's "networks that expand far beyond Ukraine" become literal on screen in the zoom-out's 214 km of
  line. This sits under chapters II and IV as the film's argument. On screen it stays implicit: one attributed quote at
  most (see 4), never the film's narration.
- **Controlled exposure, and the film's sources.** "Only strives for exposure it can control" names what the film is
  made from: the IAEA's limited access, the occupier's footage, "technicians" with no names. The dotted lines (March to
  August 2022: "the reports don't say which") and `the-gap` already show it. The essay gives them a reason. This belongs
  in chapter IV beside `the-gap`, or in the method card (`brief-8`).

### Lines for cards (quotable, attributed)

1. "highly media oriented, but only strives for exposure it can control" (Matviyenko, e-flux, 2022). Next to
   `the-gap` or in a method statement. It is a characterisation of the occupier, so it needs her name on screen.
2. "These plants simultaneously materialize the Ukrainian atomic present and constitute the remainders of the Soviet
   atomic past" (Matviyenko). Behind a fact the film can state itself: the crossings were built in 1977 and 1984. Better
   as a fact than as the quote.
3. "Let the atom be a worker, not a soldier" (the 1970s Soviet slogan on a Pripyat roof, as quoted by Matviyenko). A
   possible epigraph for the Coda or for `water-last`.
4. The staff's megaphone words at the gate, 3–4 March 2022, for `first-cut`. Only as relayed words ("as Energoatom's
   head recalled it"), unless the livestream's audio is found and traced.

Not for cards: "a barbaric army from a different age", "zero-day exploit", "potential nuclear bomb", Virilio's
"Chernobyl meltdown", and Liboiron's "pollution is colonialism" applied to the bed's silt (the film has no evidence for
a colonial reading of the heavy metals; `water-voices` already handles the silt with attribution).

### Visual ideas

- **The lines south, as the material form of "energy colonialism".** The essay's only concrete hint at energy
  colonialism is Kotin's 2022 guess that Russia might join the plant to Crimea's grid. Since then it has been built:
  about 80 km Berdiansk–Mariupol and 86 km Melitopol–Henichesk, seen in satellite imagery (NYT; Greenpeace). The parked,
  missing `new-lines-south` shot is the film's evidence-based answer to the essay's title. Draw them in the zoom-out's
  style, in the proposal colour or dashed, as reported and not OSM-traced unless mapped, each with its source. This is
  the most useful thing the essay prompts. It sits in II after `share`, or in the Coda next to `point-12` (see 4).
- **The dates as a line drawn under the record.** The Soviet build dates (Enerhodar 1970, the 330 kV crossing 1977, the
  750 kV crossing 1984, units 1985–96) could open the zoom-out's chord as faint dated ticks before 24 February 2022, in
  answer to the critique below. Cheap, sourced, and it needs no theory. Optional, in I (`zo-chord`).
- **Rejected.** A map of Ukraine's five Soviet-built plants: off the site and off the story. Pripyat footage: off the
  site, with rights to clear. A Stuxnet or "cyber" graphic: it would make viewers think a hack happened. Any glitch or
  "data-corruption" look on the lines: the same problem, and it is a cliché of the genre.

### Critiques the film should anticipate

- **"Whose grid? Built by whom, for whom?"** A reader of Matviyenko will say a film of neutral lines, lit and cut,
  depoliticises an imperial seizure. The film's answer is evidence, not adjectives. One fact line: the plant and its
  crossings were built under the Soviet Union (1977–1996). The occupier is named where sources name it ("Russian forces
  seized the plant on 4 March 2022", already a caption in the record). And `new-lines-south` shows the plant being
  rewired toward the occupied grid. Rule 4 stands: "cut" unless a source says who.
- **"Why not call it nuclear terrorism?"** Ukraine's government and Energoatom do. As a characterisation, attributed,
  it is legitimate. In the film's own voice it is not. If it is asked, the answer is the method card: the film marks
  what is observed, drawn and inferred, and a judgement is none of these.
- **Alarmism by quotation.** "Dirty bomb" and "10 percent of Chernobyl per cask" are a party's worst-case figures from
  March 2022, when the reactors were hot. The wording rules (no meltdown; cold shutdown since 13 April 2024; damaged fuel
  "cannot explode like a bomb") exist to keep these out. Quoting them, even with attribution, brings back the fear the
  film refuses.
- **Theory as evidence.** Matviyenko writes as a theorist, five weeks into the war, from news reports and interviews.
  Two of the facts she states are wrong (1972, "27 March"). The film shouldn't cite her for facts, only for ideas.
- **A cyber framing would be overreach.** Nothing in the record is a cyberattack. "Cyberwar" anywhere on screen would
  claim one.

## 4. What it suggests changing in the cards and chapters

None of these is needed. Each is a proposal for the user (NEXT.md N1), and each card's sources would go in the shot's
`tells`.

1. **Bring `new-lines-south` back from the pool into the cut.** Put it in II after `share` (power in, and the occupier
   building lines to take power out the other way), or in the Coda just before `point-12` (the plant's future as two
   grids pulling at it). Proposed card, or a caption over the drawn lines:

       Since 2025, new lines have been built through occupied land
       toward the plant: about 80 km from Berdiansk to Mariupol,
       86 km from Melitopol to Henichesk.

       They are not connected to it. (NYT, May 2025; Greenpeace Ukraine, 2026)

   Check before using it: whether either line is complete or energised as of the record's end (11 September 2026), and
   the voltage (the 150 kV given for Melitopol–Henichesk). Keep "not connected to it" only if a source says so; otherwise
   cut that line.

2. **One fact line on the Soviet build, in I or on the tower in III.** On `tower-monument` or `tower-elevation`, under
   the tower:

       The crossing towers were built in 1977 and 1984,
       when this was the Soviet Union.

   Wikipedia is the only source now. Find a primary or published history before release.

3. **`first-cut` (missing): the staff's words as an attributed card, if the footage stays missing.**

       3–4 March 2022, at the plant's gate:
       “This is nuclear industrial infrastructure!
       Stop shooting and leave the premises!”

       The staff's warning, as Energoatom's head recalled it (LatyninaTV, 22 March 2022)

   The essay's last sentence of the warning ("This is an act of nuclear terrorism!") is left out on purpose. It is the
   characterisation, and the card should carry the request, not the verdict. Stronger if the livestream's audio carries
   the warning, so check the CCTV upload (YouTube fYUT36YGOh8) and NPR/CIR before using it. No names; no fighting sound.

4. **`the-gap` (chapter IV), a second line that names the gap's cause without blame.**

       Repaired by: “technicians”
       (IAEA, 8 November 2025)

       What is known of the plant is what is let out:
       the IAEA has been on site since September 2022, where it is allowed to look.

   The second line is the film's own reading of Matviyenko's "exposure it can control", put as fact. It needs the
   IAEA's own wording on its access limits. If it reads as a charge, keep only the first line.

5. **An optional epigraph for the Coda (`ch-meadow`) or before `water-last`.**

       “Let the atom be a worker, not a soldier.”
       A Soviet slogan of the 1970s, on a roof in Pripyat

   It sets the forest-or-reactor question against the plant's purpose. The risk is irony and Chernobyl's shadow at the
   end of a film that says "not meltdown". The film works without it. I would try it once in the animatic and probably
   park it.

6. **The chapter titles stay as they are.** Nothing in the essay should rename them (see 3, Framing).

**What not to take from the essay:** its facts (two are wrong, and it predates the record); its present tense; the
cyber frame; "terrorism", "hostage", "weapon", "bomb" and "barbaric" in the film's voice; Chernobyl and Polissia, which
are not this film's site.
