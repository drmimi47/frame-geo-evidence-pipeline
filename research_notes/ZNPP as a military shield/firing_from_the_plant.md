# The ZNPP as a "nuclear shield": Russian firing from the plant and Enerhodar, March 2022 to November 2026

Scope note: locations are kept general (the plant's site, Enerhodar, the north bank), per the film's safety rule. Where sources
name a specific building, unit or distance, these notes give only "within the plant's site". Labels used below:
**Observed/verified** = seen by the IAEA or geolocated by an independent outlet; **Party claim** = Ukrainian or Russian
government, military or operator statement not independently confirmed. Research done 9 November 2026, about 20 searches
and fetches; several primary pages (NYT original, Truth Hounds report, ISW originals) could not be fetched and are cited
via syndications or summaries, marked as such.

## Ukrainian claims (Energoatom, GUR/HUR, MoD, Nikopol/Dnipropetrovsk officials, Zelensky)

### Takeaway
Ukrainian officials have said consistently since mid-July 2022 that Russian forces fire on Nikopol and Marhanets from the
plant's site and Enerhodar, and keep vehicles, launchers, ammunition and (from 2023–24) drone launch points there. The
specifics (Grad launchers next to a reactor, about 50 heavy weapons in turbine halls, drone command posts) are party claims.
Most come with little or no published evidence; the 2024 and 2026 claims came with drone footage that has not been
independently geolocated.

### Inferences
- The Ukrainian account follows the war's phases. In 2022 it was artillery and Grad MLRS fired from the site, coinciding with HIMARS making other Russian positions vulnerable. From 2023–24 it was drones (FPV, then Geran/Gerbera-type) launched or controlled from the site, which fits the switch to drones in the Nikopol attack statistics (below). In 2026 it was a "military base", with ammunition depots and command points.
- The best-evidenced Ukrainian claims are the ones about presence: vehicles and equipment on the site, which the IAEA confirmed for 2022. The weakest are the specific numbers and weapon placements (50 heavy weapons; Grads by the spent-fuel store), which have no published imagery, and the 2023 claim was contradicted by what the IAEA saw.

### Gaps
- None of the Ukrainian claims I found came with independently geolocated imagery of a launch *from inside the site's fence* aimed at Nikopol. The 2024 and 2026 footage has not been geolocated by an independent group, as far as I could find.
- Kotin's "military base" statement and an Energoatom claim about weapons and explosives "in the turbine department of unit four" (2023) surfaced only in search summaries. One summary attributed the second to the IAEA, which looks like a misattribution (the IAEA did not report explosives there). I did not verify either.

## Western and independent reporting, OSINT, imagery: what is observed, and how near

### Takeaway
Independently observed: Russian military trucks and armoured vehicles *inside* turbine halls on the plant's site (CNN-geolocated video, August 2022; confirmed by the IAEA mission, 6 September 2022, with up to about 40 vehicles on site), military equipment under the units' overpass (Maxar, 29 August 2022), fortified positions at the entrance, and mines at and outside the perimeter (IAEA, 2023). One MLRS launch near the plant, on the night of 2–3 September 2022, was verified by the Centre for Information Resilience (CIR). It was filmed from the north bank and placed in the Enerhodar industrial area by the plant, within a few km of it. I found no independent geolocation of routine artillery or drone fire on Nikopol from inside the fence.

## The "nuclear shield" logic, and Ukraine's restraint and its reasons

### Takeaway
ISW (6 August 2022), the US Secretary of State and the UK MoD described the plant as a "nuclear shield". On their account, Russia exploits Ukraine's inability to answer fire at or near reactors, and Western fear of a nuclear accident. Ukraine's restraint is real but partial: no counter-battery barrages near the plant (the NYT), a stated no-fire distance of about 1.5 km (HUR, 2024), and only precision strikes on Russian personnel and launchers at or near the site, which Ukraine itself claimed (July 2022, September 2022).

### Cited Findings
- ISW, 6 Aug 2022: Russia is "using the plant as a nuclear shield to prevent Ukrainian strikes on Russian forces and equipment", and "using the NPP to play on Western fears of a nuclear disaster" to "degrade Western will to provide military support to a Ukrainian counteroffensive" — [Ukrinform summarising ISW](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3545204-isw-explains-why-russian-forces-use-power-plants-as-nuclear-shield.html)
- ISW (August 2022, per summaries): Russian forces had repeatedly shelled Nikopol "likely from positions in or around the NPP" since July. Returning fire would risk condemnation and a nuclear incident, leaving Russian forces a "safe zone". ISW said it could not independently determine who shelled the plant on 6 August — [Kyiv Post (ISW reprint)](https://kyivpost.com/post/1860); [Liga.net](https://news.liga.net/ua/politics/news/rossiya-pugaet-zapad-vozmojnostyu-yadernoy-avarii-chtoby-oslabit-podderjku-ukrainy-isw) (both summaries, not the ISW original)
- US Secretary of State Antony Blinken accused Russia of using the plant as a "nuclear shield", "the height of irresponsibility" (August 2022); a UK MoD update said Russian troops used the plant to reduce the risk of their equipment being attacked — reported in [Kyiv Post](https://kyivpost.com/post/1860) and [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/occupied-ukrainian-plant-becomes-epicenter-of-russias-nuclear-blackmail) (via search summary)
- NYT, 1 Aug 2022: Ukraine "cannot unleash volleys of shells in return" with US-provided rocket systems, as that "would risk striking" reactors or stored radioactive waste, so its alternative is precision strikes. Battalion commander Col. Serhiy Shatalov, asked how Ukraine can respond: "This is a nuclear site." Orlov: only a direct hit by a powerful weapon would breach containment — [Irish Times / NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2022/08/01/ukraine-faces-new-obstacle-as-russia-uses-nuclear-power-plant-for-cover/)
- HUR (Cherniak), May 2024: Ukraine cannot fire back within about 1.5 km of the plant — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/military-intelligence-russia-flies-attack-drones-over-occupied-zaporizhzhia-plant/)
- Voloshyn, Jan 2026: Russia hides equipment there because Ukraine would not fire on a nuclear facility, which he called a violation of IHL — [RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/russian-troops-launch-drones-from-zaporizhzhia-1768396318.html)
- Ukraine's claimed precision strikes at or near the site: 19–22 July 2022, drone strike on a Grad, an air-defence system and a tent camp (party claim) — [Irish Times / NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2022/08/01/ukraine-faces-new-obstacle-as-russia-uses-nuclear-power-plant-for-cover/); 2 Sep 2022, General Staff "precision strikes" round Enerhodar — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant_crisis); Zelensky's "special target" warning, 13 Aug 2022 — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/8/13/russians-damaging-or-occupying-nuclear-plant-are-targets-kyiv); Oct 2024, the plant's security chief reportedly killed by a car bomb in Enerhodar, attributed to HUR — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant_crisis)
- Russia's counter-narrative: Ukraine shells the plant and Enerhodar (UNSC, 11 Aug 2022, and after); "never" any heavy weapons at the plant (May 2023); drone strikes on the plant blamed on Ukraine (April 2024 onward, including a strike on a reactor building reported 30 May 2026, where the IAEA confirmed drone remains but did not attribute them) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant_crisis)

### Inferences
- The shield works in two ways, and both rest on sources. One is tactical: launchers near the reactors are safe from counter-battery fire, as the NYT, ISW and HUR all say. The other is political: any explosion at the site prompts mutual accusations, and the IAEA as a rule does not attribute them, so blame stays contested.
- Ukraine's restraint has limits by its own account. It claimed drone strikes on Russian assets inside or right beside the site in 2022 (the July Grad strike), so "Ukraine never fires near the plant" is not supportable. What is supportable is no area fire or counter-battery barrages near the plant, and a self-reported no-fire distance of about 1.5 km (2024).

### Gaps
- No RUSI or arms-control-scholar analysis was reached in this pass. The nuclear-shield framing here rests on ISW, US and UK government statements and the NYT.
- I found no official Ukrainian rules-of-engagement document. The 1.5 km figure is a paraphrase of one HUR spokesperson's remark to Babel.

## Scale of shelling of Nikopol and Marhanets, and how much is attributed to fire from the plant or Enerhodar

### Takeaway
Nikopol has been hit almost daily since July 2022: about 61–67 civilians killed and about 370–500 injured by 2024 (local officials, figures that conflict). From mid-2023 drones replaced most artillery: Nikopol City Council counted 0 drone attacks in June 2023, 290 in June 2024 and 362 in June 2025, and Truth Hounds counted an average of 459 drone attacks a month on Nikopol district from September 2024 to March 2025. No source gives the share fired from the plant or Enerhodar as against elsewhere on the south bank (Kamianka-Dniprovska, Vodiane and others). Attribution to the plant is anecdotal and claimed.

### Cited Findings
- Head of the district military administration, Jan 2024: 67 civilians killed and 372 injured in Nikopol since summer 2022 — [Hromadske Radio](https://hromadske.radio/en/news/2024/01/14/67-civilians-have-been-killed-in-nikopol-since-the-beginning-of-the-shelling-head-of-district-military-administration)
- Hromadske correspondent, Sep 2024: 61 killed and nearly 500 injured since summer 2022 (lower death count than January's, unexplained) — [Hromadske Radio](https://hromadske.radio/en/news/2024/09/30/since-the-summer-of-2022-61-people-have-died-in-nikopol-from-russian-shelling-a-hromadske-correspondent)
- Aug 2022: the Dnipropetrovsk governor reported 60 rockets fired at Nikopol in one attack — [search summary of 2022 coverage; e.g. CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6540764) (not fetched)
- Truth Hounds, "Killzone: How Russian Drones Are Devastating the River Dnipro's Right Bank" (Nov 2025; 39 interviews with survivors and witnesses, 3 expert interviews, open sources): Nikopol drone attacks 0 (June 2023) → 290 (June 2024) → 362 (June 2025), per Nikopol City Council; an average of 459 attacks a month in Nikopol raion, Sep 2024 to Mar 2025; Russian drone units post strike videos on Telegram — [Truth Hounds PDF](https://dev.truth-hounds.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/killzone-how-russian-drones-are-devastating-the-river-dnipros-right-bank.pdf) (figures via search summary; the page returned 401); [bpb documentation](https://www.bpb.de/themen/europa/ukraine-analysen/nr-321/573076/dokumentation-angriffe-russischer-drohnen-auf-die-ukrainische-zivilbevoelkerung-entlang-des-dnipro/)
- Artillery continued alongside the drones in 2025, e.g. heavy artillery on Nikopol killing two and injuring five — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3962331-enemy-shells-nikopol-with-heavy-artillery-two-killed-one-injured.html); Nikopol "one of the hardest hit areas" with near-daily bombardment — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/82092)
- Attribution to the plant: ISW said "likely from positions in or around the NPP" (2022, summary) — [Kyiv Post](https://kyivpost.com/post/1860); Energoatom said launchers on the site *could* reach Nikopol and Marhanets (Dec 2022) — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/energoatom-russia-places-grad-rocket-launchers-next-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-reactor/); HUR's 2024 footage was said to show drones flying from the site toward Nikopol and Marhanets — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/military-intelligence-russia-flies-attack-drones-over-occupied-zaporizhzhia-plant/)

### Inferences
- The scale is well documented by Ukrainian local authorities and a Ukrainian NGO (Truth Hounds). The origin of individual strikes is not. The whole occupied south bank across from Nikopol and Marhanets is within artillery and FPV range, so the plant and Enerhodar are among several plausible firing areas, and nothing found quantifies their share.
- For the film, the defensible line is: "Ukrainian officials say some of the fire on Nikopol comes from the plant's site and Enerhodar; the IAEA saw military vehicles inside the plant; one rocket launch beside it was verified by open-source investigators in September 2022." It is not defensible to say that the shelling of Nikopol comes from the plant.

### Gaps
- No cumulative 2025–2026 Nikopol or Marhanets casualty total was found, and no OHCHR district-level figure. The Dnipropetrovsk OVA Telegram and OHCHR monthly updates would be the places to check.
- No source breaks the attacks down by launch area.

## What remains unverified or disputed

### Takeaway
Disputed or unverified: every specific weapon placement inside the site after September 2022 (Grads by the spent-fuel store, 50 heavy weapons, drone command posts, ammunition depots in basements), the claim that launches *from inside the fence* hit Nikopol (as opposed to launches from Enerhodar or elsewhere), and the attribution of attacks on the plant itself. Russia denies placing heavy weapons there. The IAEA confirmed the 2022 vehicles but saw no heavy equipment in 2023, and has never attributed fire from or at the site.

### Cited Findings
- Russia's MoD (Aug 2022): imagery shows heavy weapons are not placed at the station — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/08/19/europe/ukraine-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-russian-vehicles-intl-hnk); Russia at the UNSC (May 2023): never any heavy weapons — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant_crisis)
- CNN could not say whether the turbine-hall trucks were stored there or sheltering after the July strike, nor when the video was filmed — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/08/19/europe/ukraine-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-russian-vehicles-intl-hnk)
