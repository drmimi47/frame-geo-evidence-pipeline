# Published scenarios for the future of the Zaporizhzhia NPP (for the film's speculative ending)

Compiled 4 November 2026. Every finding below is from a published source with its date. "HUR" (Ukrainian Defence
Intelligence) and Greenpeace Ukraine (an advocacy group) figures are marked as such and are not independently
confirmed unless stated. Anything about the future is the source's projection or opinion, not fact. Framing note
for the film: the six units have been in cold shutdown since April 2024 (Euromaidan Press, below). With the reactors
cold, a total loss of power starts a countdown of days to weeks: diesel stocks, then the time before the spent-fuel
pools boil off. It is not an instant meltdown. The IAEA's own wording is "station blackout" risk.

## 1. If the war goes on for years: a plant in long cold shutdown under occupation

### Takeaway
By autumn 2026 the plant is holding on by a thread. It has one external line (Ferosplavna-1, 330 kV, restored 7 Sep
2026). Diesel has been short, with no deliveries since March 2026 (IAEA). Staff are far fewer than before the war. The
cooling pond keeps falling: about 12.27 m on 12 Oct 2026 (HUR), against the roughly 12 m pump cut-off the IAEA's 2025
reporting implies. No published source lays out a concrete decommissioning path. The only "long cold shutdown"
options in the literature are indefinite cold shutdown (the Krško precedent) or defuelling until the war ends.

### Cited Findings
**Power lines (the film's record):**
- Dniprovska 750 kV was disconnected on 24 Mar 2026. It was repaired under the sixth IAEA-brokered ceasefire by 26 Jun 2026, but could not return to service because a substation more than 100 km to the north-west was damaged in May. — [WNA, Ukraine: Russia-Ukraine War and Nuclear Energy](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- Ferosplavna-1 330 kV was cut 7 May 2025 and reconnected 8 Nov 2025. It was then cut repeatedly in 2026 and disconnected on 20 Aug 2026. It was restored on the evening of 7 Sep 2026 after the seventh IAEA-brokered local ceasefire. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- Complete losses of off-site power, from the WNA chronology:
  - 9th, 4 Jul 2025 (about 4 h); 10th, 23 Sep–23 Oct 2025, "almost exactly a month".
  - 2026: 18th (early Jun, about 15 h); 19th (10–13 Jun); 20th–25th (Jun–Aug).
  - 26th, 20 Aug–7 Sep 2026: 18 days on emergency diesels. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- HUR said the Kremlin rejected Ukraine's proposal to supply the plant through the 750 kV Dniprovska line (unverified, HUR). — [Euromaidan Press, 21 Oct 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)
- On 15 Oct 2026 Rosatom said an IAEA-mediated truce was in place for repairs to a thermal power station supplying the plant. Likhachev: "Our specialists are now going out and beginning repairs." Seen only in a search summary of the Reuters/AOL item, not read in full. — [AOL/Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/russias-rosatom-says-truce-place-111418000.html)

**Diesel (the countdown):**
- 4 Jul 2025: about 20 days of fuel on site for the emergency generators. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- 28 Aug 2026: about ten days of diesel, the regulatory minimum. Grossi warned of a possible station blackout. Early Sep 2026: running generators cut from four to two, cooling pumps run intermittently to save fuel. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- IAEA Update 364 (late Aug 2026): without off-site power or prompt diesel deliveries, a station blackout was possible within about ten days. — [IAEA Update 364](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-364-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- IAEA Update 369 (2 Oct 2026): the plant was considering cutting the number of emergency diesel generators kept on automatic standby. Reserves are low after nearly three weeks on generators in Aug–Sep. — [IAEA Update 369](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-369-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine) (search summary; page not retrievable, HTTP 402)
- IAEA, 16 Oct 2026, as reported by Euromaidan Press:
  - no diesel deliveries since March;
  - the situation "may become acute in winter";
  - since late September only 4 of the 20 emergency generators would start automatically in a blackout. — [Euromaidan Press, 21 Oct 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)
- HUR (unverified):
  - a prolonged blackout burns about 150 t of diesel a day;
  - Russian managers are seeking covert fuel, including low-quality fuel, without telling the IAEA. — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)

**Spent fuel:**
- 7 Aug 2026: spent-fuel pool water levels were raised to extend the time before boil-off in a prolonged blackout. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- 22 Oct 2025: Rostekhnadzor issued a 25-year licence for the plant's dry spent-fuel storage facility. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)

- The 12 m threshold: in late Aug 2025 the IAEA sought access to a new dam isolating a service-water channel. The dam was meant to hold the pond at about 14 m, "two metres above the pump cut-off" (so about 12 m). Access was reportedly denied for security reasons. Reactors and spent-fuel pools are cooled from on-site groundwater wells. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy); [WNN, IAEA team seeks access to new dam](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-team-seeks-access-to-new-dam-at-zaporizhzhia-plant)
- An earlier IAEA update said the pond had dropped just over 3.2 m since June 2023, with the service channel held at about 14.1 m. — [IAEA Update 312](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-312-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- Do not conflate this with the IAEA's 2023 "12.7 m" figure. That was the *Kakhovka reservoir* level below which pumping into the plant's supply could not continue. — [IAEA Update 162](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-162-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- Wells: 11 wells were planned in 2023 to supply about 250 m³/h for reactor and spent-fuel cooling ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)). The IAEA said nine wells could supply almost all cooling needs in shutdown, about 200 m³/h ([WNN, Groundwater extraction begins](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Groundwater-extraction-begins-at-Zaporizhzhia)).
- Victoria Voytsitska (We Build Ukraine) says Russia's well drilling threatens the plant's foundations. — [Kyiv Independent, 26 Nov 2025](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)
- Aybars Gürpinar (former IAEA safety official) raised the risk that groundwater-driven subsidence could crack the base slab or affect control rods. Rosatom: "No ground level changes or signs of subsidence have been observed." — [IEEE Spectrum, P. Fairley, 4 May 2025](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)

**Ageing and maintenance:**
- Ukrainian unit licences began to expire in 2025.
- Georgiy Balakan (former adviser to Energoatom's president) warned of damage from long low-temperature, low-flow cooling and boric-acid crystallisation in pipes and steam generators. He said the Russians acted as if they "could operate the water-chemical regime for an unlimited time".
- A US engineer warned of possible steam-generator damage. Only Russian firms make these parts. — [IEEE Spectrum, 4 May 2025](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)
- Voytsitska: keeping cold shutdown is the safest option, safer than hot shutdown. — [Kyiv Independent, 26 Nov 2025](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)

**Long-shutdown and decommissioning options (the only published ones found):**
- Mark Hibbs proposed to "shut down all reactors, depressurize circuits, and remove fuel until the war is over".
- Brookings adds: "Zaporizhzhia could also be placed into cold shutdown mode indefinitely, as was done for Krsko" (Slovenia, 1991). It concludes that "neither solution speaks to the motivations that Russia and Ukraine have for keeping the plant operational." — [Brookings, A. J. Nelson & C. Norteman, 23 Mar 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-to-do-about-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant/)
- Volodymyr Kudrytskyi (former head of Ukrenergo) on the plant's place in Ukraine's future grid: "We are able to use this Soviet legacy to survive, but this is not the way forward." — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)
- The San Onofre plant (California, 2013) is cited as a precedent: steam-generator failure ended a plant. Its repair would have cost nearly $2 billion. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)

### Inferences
- The physical arithmetic of the "war goes on" future, from the sources above:
  - one line;
  - the pond falling about 0.6 m between July and October 2026 (HUR), to within about 0.3 m of the roughly 12 m cut-off the IAEA implied in 2025;
  - wells of a few hundred m³/h keeping cold cores and pools cooled;
  - about ten days of diesel at the regulatory minimum.

  The film's speculative ending could show this as a countdown, not a catastrophe. Mark it as speculation.
- If the pond drops below the pump cut-off, the wells (about 200–250 m³/h) and sprinkler ponds become the only heat sink. That is enough for shutdown cooling by the IAEA's account, so a falling pond is not by itself a disaster in cold shutdown.
- Ageing and the loss of Ukrainian licensing push the plant, whoever ends up holding it, toward a choice between costly refurbishment (steam generators, chemistry) and decommissioning. This is our inference. No source models it as a scenario.

### Gaps
- No IAEA-published cooling-pond level for 2026 was found. The 2026 series is HUR's.
- No Bellona, NEA, Energoatom or IAEA published plan or study for decommissioning ZNPP was found.
- The number of dry casks on site and the capacity of the dry storage were not found.
- Exact design-life end dates per unit were not found (units 1–6 entered service 1984–1995, from general knowledge, not checked here).
- The IAEA Update 369 page itself could not be fetched (HTTP 402). Details are from search summaries.

## 2. If Russia restarts it

### Takeaway
Russia has the paperwork moving. Rostekhnadzor has licensed units 1 and 2, unit 6 is applied for, and units 3–5 are
to follow so that all units comply with Russian rules by 1 Jan 2028. It is also building a grid: about 80 km
Mariupol–Berdiansk in 2025, 201 km Melitopol–Mariupol by Sep 2025, and 86 km at 150 kV Melitopol–Henichesk in 2026.
Rosatom says no restart while fighting continues. The binding constraint is water: wells give a few hundred m³/h,
while operation needs orders of magnitude more. The IAEA has said there is no imminent restart.

### Cited Findings
**Licences:**
- Dec 2025: 10-year operating licence for unit 1. 18 Feb 2026: 10-year licence for unit 2. Unit 6 applied for. Units 3, 4 and 5 to be applied for by end-2026. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy); [NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/russia-s-nuclear-regulator-issues-10-year-operating-licence-for-zaporizhzhia-2-2-5-2026); [WNN](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/russian-regulator-issues-operating-licence-for-second-zaporizhzhia-unit)
- The Russian-appointed director expects licences for units 3–5 in 2027. All units must meet Russian requirements by 1 Jan 2028. — [www1.ru, 27 Feb 2026](https://www1.ru/en/news/2026/02/27/vse-energobloki-zaporozskoi-aes-proidut-licenzirovanie-po-rossiiskim-trebovaniiam-do-2028-goda.html)

**Restart plans and conditions:**
- 4 Apr 2025, Russian-appointed director Yuriy Chernichuk:
  - units 2 and 6 are the most realistic first restarts, since their cores hold Russian fuel;
  - there is "no railway line to the plant" for fuel;
  - the Dnipro is studied as a water source, "not one day or one month";
  - the lines must be restored and customers decided;
  - Rostov NPP's output could be redirected to the North Caucasus.

  Energoatom called a restart "technically impossible", citing limited grid links, fuel and equipment past their design life, unlicensed personnel and "no ultimate heat sink". — [WNN, 4 Apr 2025](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/russia-outlines-zaporizhzhia-restart-timetable-ukraine-rejects-plan)
- Fuel: four units use Westinghouse fuel, which Rosatom has no licence or experience with ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)). Converting them back "could take years". Voytsitska: designing the cores for American fuel took about 20 years ([Kyiv Independent, 26 Nov 2025](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)).
- 18 Feb 2026: Rosatom said units will not restart while the conflict continues. Grossi said generation can resume only once hostilities end. — [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)
- 13 Mar 2026, Likhachev (Rosatom), at the Russia–IAEA consultations in Moscow:
  - "We are ready to restart work, the necessary equipment is ready";
  - "As soon as the opportunity arises, we will begin starting up and operating the station under IAEA supervision";
  - "Under certain conditions there could be discussion of supplying electricity, including to Ukraine";
  - with 6 GW the plant could power data centres, with "new partners... the United States, among others". — [Reuters via Yahoo/US News, 13 Mar 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/rosatom-boss-warns-increased-military-110059643.html)
- Dec 2025: Likhachev said the move to restart will begin "based on the military-political situation". — [Izvestia, 23 Dec 2025](https://en.iz.ru/en/2013978/2025-12-23/likhachev-announced-rosatoms-readiness-international-cooperation-nuclear-power-plant)
- Grossi on Greenpeace's restart warnings: "We are not in a situation of imminent restart of the plant." — [Beyond Nuclear, citing IAEA](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)

**Transmission lines in the occupied south (satellite analyses by Greenpeace Ukraine):**
- Since early Feb 2025, more than 80 km of high-voltage line has been built from Mariupol to Berdiansk along the Azov coast. It ties into a large substation near Mariupol that is linked to the plant. The New York Times verified the imagery. Experts said several more lines would be needed to tie the plant to Russia's grid. — [Kyiv Independent, citing NYT](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-builds-power-lines-to-connect-occupied-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-to-its-grid-nyt-reports/)
- Melitopol–Henichesk, from imagery of Jan–Jul 2026, published 21 Jul 2026:
  - about 86 km of new 150 kV line along the E105/M18 highway;
  - started mid-Jan to mid-Feb 2026, nearly complete by late May;
  - no progress in imagery of 1 Jul, which Greenpeace links to Ukrainian drone strikes on the Henichesk substation in early July.

  The same report says 201 km of line between Melitopol and Mariupol had been installed by late Sep 2025. — [UNITED24, 21 Jul 2026](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukrainian-drone-strikes-halt-russias-bid-to-connect-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-to-its-grid-20958)
- Greenpeace's reading of the plan: disconnect the plant from Ukraine's grid, restart it under Rosatom and feed the occupied territories, Crimea and southern Russia. It would power military logistics, mining and agriculture in the south. Rosatom reportedly sees the plant as cheaper than new reactors in Rostov or Krasnodar. These are Greenpeace's claims. — [UNITED24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukrainian-drone-strikes-halt-russias-bid-to-connect-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-to-its-grid-20958)
- Greenpeace (2025, imagery of 12 Aug 2025) says the long loss of power from 23 Sep 2025 was deliberate Russian action, part of a restart plan. It notes a newly completed water-management system at the pond. A later Greenpeace–McKenzie Intelligence analysis found no evidence that Ukrainian strikes damaged the plant's lines. The IAEA said it did not agree with Greenpeace's conclusions. — [Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/4411/record-breaking-external-power-cut-at-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant-is-it-a-part-of-russias-plan-to-restart-it/); [Greenpeace Ukraine, 750 kV line](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/4460/the-750-kv-power-line-at-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant-shows-no-signs-of-major-damage-new-satellite-investigation-by-greenpeace/); [Beyond Nuclear](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)
- A satellite report of a new line toward Crimea from the plant. Only the headline was seen, not verified. — [Intent, 2026](https://intent.press/en/news/society/2026/satellites-have-detected-the-construction-of-a-power-line-for-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-and-crimea/)

**Cooling water for operation:**
- Rosatom's 2024 submission to the IAEA described a pumping station of up to 18,000 m³/h into the cooling pond. Greenpeace estimates one reactor needs about 6,000 m³ *per minute* (about 360,000 m³/h) of circulating water, which would need a new pumping system. — [Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/3301/greenpeace-urges-iaea-to-prevent-russian-restart-of-zaporizhzhia-reactors/)
- A floating modular pumping station was reported under construction for the restart. — [Yahoo, Russia begins building pumping station](https://www.yahoo.com/news/russia-begins-building-pumping-station-155450234.html)
- The wells cool the fuel in shutdown, "but not enough to do so if they are restarted". — [Beyond Nuclear](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)
- Greenpeace (Sep 2025) said pond works and new lines could give enough water to restart one reactor at reduced power. The IAEA disagreed. — [Beyond Nuclear](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)

**Russia's position on ownership:**
- Russia's letter to the UN Security Council (S/2025/189, Mar 2025) calls the plant a Russian facility under Russian jurisdiction. It rejects Ukrainian control and any joint arrangement with Ukraine or the US. — [UN digital library](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4079939/files/S_2025_189-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- The brief's figure of "up to ~80,000 m³/h" for operation was not found in any source. Published figures range from 18,000 m³/h (Rosatom's pond make-up station) to about 360,000 m³/h per reactor (Greenpeace, circulating water). They likely measure different things: make-up water to the pond versus flow through the condensers. Either way, both are two to three orders of magnitude above the wells' 200–250 m³/h. That is the physical gap a restart would have to close: a Dnipro pumping station, or the reservoir back.
- A restart scenario on screen would show:
  - a new grid lit eastward and south (Melitopol, Berdiansk, Mariupol, Henichesk toward Crimea) in place of the 750 kV lines north;
  - units 2 and 6 first;
  - water pumped up from the Dnipro channel.

  Speculation, built from the sources above.

### Gaps
- No published hydraulic figure (m³/h) for the cooling water one unit needs at full power, from the IAEA, Rosatom or Energoatom, was found. The 80,000 m³/h figure is unsourced.
- No independent confirmation of the line toward Crimea was found.
- The current status of the floating pumping station (late 2026) was not found.
- Unit 6's licence date (if issued by Nov 2026) was not found.

## 3. If it returns to Ukraine or comes under another arrangement

### Takeaway
The plant is one of the two unresolved points of the US–Ukraine 20-point framework (Dec 2025). Point 12 bundles
control of the plant with restoring the Kakhovka HPP. The US proposed three-way operation led by Washington; Kyiv
proposed a US–Ukraine joint venture; Russia rejects any handover or joint control. Ukrainian estimates are "up to two
years" for all six units after full Ukrainian control. The dam would take 6–8 years after deoccupation, at $1–1.2
billion (Ukrhydroenergo) or about €2 billion (Zelensky).

### Cited Findings
**US proposals and the peace drafts:**
- Mar 2025: after a Trump–Zelensky call, the White House said "American ownership" would be the "best protection" for Ukraine's plants. Zelensky said only ZNPP was discussed, that he could not legally negotiate its ownership, and that the plants "belong to the people of Ukraine". — [Meduza, 21 Mar 2025](https://meduza.io/en/news/2025/03/21/zelensky-says-he-and-trump-discussed-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-but-not-u-s-ownership); [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/amp/post/49297)
- The US 28-point plan (mid-Nov 2025) proposed equal power sharing under IAEA supervision, with the front frozen so the plant stays on the Russian-controlled side. — [Kyiv Independent, 26 Nov 2025](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)
- The revised 20-point plan (23 Dec 2025):
  - Point 12 covers control of ZNPP and restoring the Kakhovka HPP.
  - The US proposed joint operation with each of the three countries holding 33%, a three-party system led by Washington.
  - Kyiv proposed a US–Ukraine joint venture, with Ukraine taking half the output and the US allocating the other half.
  - Ukraine requires the plant, Enerhodar and the Kakhovka HPP to be demilitarised.
  - Points 12 and 14 (Donbas) were unresolved. — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/zelensky-reveals-20-point-peace-plan-draft-backed-by-ukraine-us/); [Yale PDF of the plan](https://politicalscience.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-12-23-revised-peace-plan-announced-by-zelenskyy.pdf); [France 24, 29 Dec 2025](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20251229-what-next-for-ukraine-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-russia-peace-plan)
- 24 Dec 2025: Putin said the US and Russia were negotiating joint control without Ukraine, and that Washington wanted the plant's power for cryptocurrency mining (via Kommersant). — [Kyiv Post, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/66961)
- Feb 2026: Russia rejected a US proposal giving Washington control of the plant (Reuters). Russia's Foreign Ministry: the plant is Russian property, cannot be handed to anyone, and joint operation would make safety impossible. — [Euromaidan Press, 7 Feb 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/02/07/reuters-russia-refuses-us-control-of-zaporizhzhya-npp-in-peace-talks/); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-rejects-calls-to-return-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)

**Experts on shared or split operation:**
- Jan Vande Putte (Greenpeace): "Maybe on paper you can develop a plan to split it, but practically it's not realistic... It's never been done before."
- Najmedin Meshkati (USC): the IAEA should "facilitate Ukraine's return to the plant, not to legitimize Russian occupation."

## 4. The dam and the plant together; the Great Meadow and the new forest

### Takeaway
Long-term operation needs a heat sink that the drained reservoir no longer gives. Ukrhydroenergo's first phase is
explicitly about raising the water for irrigation and ZNPP cooling. Ukrainian scientists are split three ways:
- rebuild, possibly smaller (Kherson agrarian researchers);
- do not rebuild, protect the Great Meadow, with a UNESCO bid backed by over 100 organisations and a call of "ecocide";
- small side reservoirs or a cascade, leaving the main channel open (Moysienko; Benatov et al. 2024).

The willow–poplar "pseudo-forest" on the bed is itself an argument against refilling.

### Cited Findings
- The reservoir was about 2,155 km² at normal level, 18.2 km³, 240 km long and up to 23 km wide, with a 15 m head. It supplied more than 900 m³/s to the main canals. — [Hydropower & Dams, 24 Jun 2023](https://www.hydropower-dams.com/news/future-possibilities-following-the-destruction-of-kakhovka-dam-and-hydro-plant/)
- A Ukrainian engineer's three options (Hydropower & Dams, 24 Jun 2023):
  1. Do not restore: the most environmentally sound, but needs new pumping stations, including for "supplying the cooling reservoir at the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant", and ends navigation.
  2. A cascade of smaller reservoirs.
  3. Restore: the dam sits on soft loess, so its foundation may be compromised; it would take several years and must pass at least 7,000 m³/s of flood meanwhile. — [Hydropower & Dams](https://www.hydropower-dams.com/news/future-possibilities-following-the-destruction-of-kakhovka-dam-and-hydro-plant/)
- Energoatom (2023): the cooling pond is separate from the reservoir's level, with the wells as a back-up. — [Energoatom, 6 Jun 2023](https://www.energoatom.com.ua/app-eng/eng-0606232.html)
- Pichura, Potravka, Stoiko & Dudych, "Scenarios for the Functioning of the Kakhovka Reservoir Territory", *Journal of Landscape Ecology* 18(3), 2025. Three scenarios:
  1. rebuild and refill;
  2. a natural plant ecosystem restoring the Great Meadow;
  3. a partly watered, quasi-natural system.

  The forecast "depends on assessing Ukrainians' ability to adapt" after the "ecocide". — [J. Landscape Ecology, 2025](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/jlecol-2025-0023)
- Benatov, Stefanyshyn, Korbutiak & Sikorsky (2024), "Kakhovka Reservoir – prospects of restoration": several smaller reservoirs and HPPs inside the former basin, with channels, culverts and fish passes, on integrated water-resources management and nature-based principles. — [AGRIS/FAO](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/67a091a020478411b0259d25)
- Kherson State Agrarian and Economic University researchers: refusing to rebuild could cause long-term problems in the south. "No alternative but restoration", possibly a smaller reservoir with its upper section turned into meadow-forest. — [Liga.net](https://biz.liga.net/en/all/news/kakhovka-reservoir-no-alternative-but-restoration-scientists-warn); [MOST](https://most.ks.ua/en/news/url/mizh-ges-ta-velikim-lugom/)
