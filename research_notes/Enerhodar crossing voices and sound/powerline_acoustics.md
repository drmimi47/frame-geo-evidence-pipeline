# The sound of high-voltage lines (750 kV / 330 kV): physics, measured levels, and precedents for the film's synthesized "Hum"

Scope note: the film's two lines are on a **50 Hz** grid (Ukraine). Most measured literature is North American (60 Hz), where the corona and transformer hum sits at **120/240 Hz**. On a 50 Hz grid the same mechanisms give **100/200 Hz**, as Australian (50 Hz) measurements confirm (TransGrid 2025, below). "Measured" = field measurement; "modelled" = prediction by the EPRI/BPA empirical formulas; "design criterion" = a utility target, not a measurement. "Snippet-only" = seen only in a search-result summary, not in the full source text.

---

## 1. Physics and measured levels of corona audible noise (330 kV vs 750 kV, fair vs wet), and Ukrainian norms

### Takeaway
Line noise is corona: a broadband crackle/hiss (roughly 0.5–10 kHz) plus a tonal hum at twice the grid frequency (100 Hz and 200 Hz on a 50 Hz grid). It is mainly a wet-weather sound. The best full-scale measurement at a comparable voltage, Hydro-Québec's 735 kV Saint-Césaire–Hertel line, rose to about **53 dBA in rain and 48 dBA in snow at the right-of-way edge (40 m from the centreline)**. In dry weather it had **no measurable effect** on ambient levels, only a faint crackle. Lines at 330 kV are much quieter. Utilities treat corona noise as a design issue only at about 345 kV and above.

### Cited Findings
**Mechanism and spectrum**
- Corona: when the field at the conductor surface exceeds the breakdown strength of air, the air ionises. The audible part is "typically described as a crackling or hissing sound, and it originates from the rapid compression of air caused by corona current pulses". It has "tonal elements … at harmonic frequencies of the power line's operating frequency, such as 100 Hz and 200 Hz" (50 Hz grid). The main factors are the Surface Voltage Gradient, conductor surface condition, contamination, altitude and weather. Aged or polluted conductors are noisier. — [TransGrid/WSP, *Audible coronal noise study*, Nov 2025](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/llsbcso3/audible-coronal-noise-study-investigating-audible-noise-from-transmission-lines-in-australian-climates.pdf)
- Measured spectrum, 735 kV line in rain or snow: the crackle is broadband (**800–8,000 Hz**) and the hum is tonal at **120 Hz and 240 Hz** (60 Hz grid). Both are "clairement audibles et identifiables". After the line was energised, LAF95 in the 2,500 Hz third-octave band rose by **20 dBA in rain and 30 dBA in snow**. — [Duchassin, Chamberland & Gosselin, "Suivi du bruit audible généré par une nouvelle ligne à haute tension à 735 kV", *Canadian Acoustics* 35(3), 2007, pp. 182–183](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/1960/1707/2097)
- Hydro-Québec's own synthesis: the typical AC-line spectrum in rain is a **120 Hz component, a weaker 240 Hz component, and broadband noise from about 500 Hz to 10 kHz**. — [Hydro-Québec, *Synthèse des connaissances environnementales pour les lignes et les postes 1973–2013*, ch. 12 "Ambiance sonore", p. 18](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- The hum (pure tones) comes from "the oscillatory movement of ionic space charges created in the vicinity of the conductor" (**snippet-only**, from an SRP/Arizona CEC exhibit). — [SRP Exhibit I, Noise Emissions and Communication Interference](https://azure-na-assets.contentstack.com/v3/assets/bltefc1fc708dcc94e7/bltfbf5b58bfce566ef/Exhibit_I_Noise_Emissions_and_Communication_Interference_SPL_Final.pdf)
- A utility's plain-language description: "crackling, frying, sputtering, and low frequency tones which are best described as humming sounds". In dry weather the conductors are below corona inception, and noise is "typically only slightly audible at the edge of the … right-of-way". — [SRP High-Tech Interconnect Project, CEC Exhibit I](https://www.srpnet.com/assets/srpnet/pdf/grid-water-management/grid-management/improvement-projects/hip/cec/SRP-HIP-CEC-Final_ExhibitI.pdf)

**Measured levels: 735 kV (closest analogue to the 750 kV Dniprovska line)**
- Saint-Césaire–Hertel 735 kV. Measured in 2001 (before energisation) and 2004–2006 (after), at four sites, at the ROW edge **40 m from the line's centre, mid-span**, microphone 1.5 m above ground. With the line on, mean LAF95 rose **+11 dBA during rain and +14 dBA during snow**. In dry weather, and after rain or snow, levels were the same as before the line was built: "a slight crackle of the line is perceived" but it does not change the overall levels. After rain, the hum and crackle persist at low levels. After snow, only the crackle persists. Transitions between weather states changed levels by 6–16 dBA over 5–60 minutes. Noise rises faster when rain starts than it falls when rain stops. — [Duchassin et al. 2007](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/1960/1707/2097)
- The same monitoring as summarised by Hydro-Québec: after precipitation starts, noise rises over about **15 min** to about **53 dBA (rain) / 48 dBA (snow) at the ROW edge**. After it stops, noise falls over about **40 min** to below 40 dBA. Even though the hum is at single frequencies, no spectrum met Québec's (MDDEP 98-01) definition of a tonal noise. — [Hydro-Québec synthesis, p. 18](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- Hydro-Québec's IREQ test-line studies (1970s): fair-weather noise is **15–20 dBA below** the maximum in heavy rain. The after-rain period stays noisy for "a few minutes to a few hours" depending on humidity. The semi-empirical prediction formula is within 3 dBA of measurements. AC-line noise **decreases over 6 months to 3 years** as conductors age and hold less water. — [Hydro-Québec synthesis, pp. 17–18](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- Hydro-Québec's table of example levels: "Crépitement d'une ligne à 735 kV, en bordure d'emprise, sous la pluie", **50 dBA** (a typical value, not a specific measurement). Its design criterion is **55 dBA at the ROW edge with conductors wet after rain**, which an **80 m** 735 kV ROW generally meets. — [Hydro-Québec synthesis, pp. 12, 17](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- Hydro-Québec in the BAPE hearings (utility estimates, **snippet-only**): a 735 kV line is "generally between 30 dBA and 40 dBA in fair weather". The wet/dry difference is about 15 dBA and "not rare" to reach 25 dBA. At 100 m beyond the ROW: about 40 dBA in bad weather, 25 dBA or less in fair. — [BAPE, Chamouchouane–Bout-de-l'Île, DQ17.1](https://archives.bape.gouv.qc.ca/sections/mandats/ligne_chamouchouane_bout-de-lile/documents/DQ17.1.pdf)

**Measured levels: 330 kV and 500 kV on a 50 Hz grid (Australia)**
- TransGrid/WSP measured continuously in April–May 2025 next to a **330 kV** line (Lismore) and a **500 kV** line (Martinsville), NSW. At 330 kV, corona noise ranged from about **25 to 60 dBA** across rain rates, with "no clear or consistent trend". The EPRI and BPA models **overpredicted** for 330 kV, so international models may overpredict below 500 kV. At 500 kV, noise ranged from about **35 to 60 dBA**, levelling off at **50–60 dBA above about 5 mm/h** as the conductors became "uniformly wetted". Fog and mist (calm, dew-point depression 0–0.8 °C, mostly 6–7 am) gave "a modest but consistent increase" at 330 kV, meaning "surface wetting by microdroplets is sufficient to sustain partial discharges even in the absence of rainfall". — [TransGrid/WSP 2025, §5.2–5.3](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/llsbcso3/audible-coronal-noise-study-investigating-audible-noise-from-transmission-lines-in-australian-climates.pdf)

**Design criteria and modelled levels (not measurements)**
- BPA: corona noise is "basically a foul-weather (wet conductor) phenomenon", made worse by rain, fog, snow and icing, and "of concern primarily for transmission lines operating at voltages of 345 kV and above". The design criterion is **L50 = 50 dB(A) at the ROW edge** in typical foul weather. — [BPA TBL Policy T2006-1, *Audible Noise Policy*, Oct 2005 (released via FOIA 2023)](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/foia/foia-2024/202400429F.pdf)
- BPA method (as summarised in a CPUC EIR from EPRI's "Red Book", 2005): the median fair-weather level is **about 25 dBA below** the median level in measurable rain for "normal lines". BPA adds 3.5 dBA for heavy rain. — [CPUC, El Casco System Project Recirculated Draft EIR, §D.9, July 2008, p. D.9-5](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/aspen/elcasco/recircdeir/D%209%20Noise%20Rev3.pdf)
- Modelled 500 kV line, 250 ft ROW: foul-weather L50 **52.1 dBA** vs fair **27.1 dBA** at the edge (**snippet-only**). — [Utah PSC filing (Mona–Oquirrh)](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/10docs/1003539/66591ExNNNApp.pdf)
- 765 kV (Korea, KEPCO test line, 6-conductor bundle): the foul-weather L50 is reported "below 50 dB(A)". The bundle met the noise criterion in stable rain (**snippet-only**). — [KIEE 1996](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO199611919599474.pub?lang=en); [OSTI 433830](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/433830)

**Ukrainian norms and the 750 kV EIA**
- Protection zones (охоронні зони) of overhead lines in Ukraine: **330 kV – 30 m; 750 kV – 40 m** (from the outermost wire, per the Rules for the Protection of Electrical Networks, KMU Resolution No. 209 of 4 March 1997). — [Держенергонагляд via kmu.gov.ua](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/news/derzhenergonaglyad-nagaduye-pro-dotrimannya-pravil-ohoroni-elektrichnih-merezh); [КМУ №209-97-п text](https://kodeksy.com.ua/norm_akt/source-КМУ/type-Постанова/209-97-п-04.03.1997.htm)
- The Rules also set sanitary-protection zones (СЗЗ) "for protecting the population from the electric field" for lines of 330 kV and above (snippet of the Rules). — [КМУ №209-97-п](https://kodeksy.com.ua/print/norm_akt/source-КМУ/type-Постанова/209-97-п-04.03.1997.htm)
- The EBRD-financed ZNPP–Kakhovska 750 kV EIA (non-technical summary, Mercados EMI for Ukrenergo, July 2009): Ukrainian norms set СЗЗ where the electric field exceeds **1 kV/m**, and the minimum distance from 750 kV lines to houses in settlements is **250 m**. The EU comparison given is 5 kV/m. Its figure caption also gives "40 m minimum distance to housing" for the 750 kV protection zone (internally inconsistent; possibly zone vs settlement rule). On noise, the EIA says operation will generate **low-frequency noise from "the corona discharge effect"**, that its intensity depends on line design, topography "and especially natural conditions, with humidity always increasing the effect", and that impact is expected to be minor because of the EMF buffer. **No dBA figure is given.** — [EBRD, ПЛ 750 кВ Запорізька АЕС – Каховська, ОВНСС нетехнічне резюме, 2009, pp. 9–11, 20](https://www.ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/english/pages/project/eia/40147u.pdf)
- Soviet guidance РД 34.20.172 (corona losses and radio interference): precipitation strongly affects corona. Weather is grouped, including rain above a critical intensity. Local corona depends mainly on surface field, diameter, surface state and meteorology (**snippet-only**). — [РД 34.20.172 (stroyinf)](https://files.stroyinf.ru/Data2/1/4294817/4294817291.htm)
- A KPI conference paper says corona interference from Ukrainian overhead lines arises "only at 500–750 kV" (**snippet-only**). — [confopcbproc.iee.kpi.ua](https://confopcbproc.iee.kpi.ua/article/view/142501/140010)

### Inferences
- On the 50 Hz Ukrainian grid, the Dniprovska line's hum would be at **100 Hz (strong) and 200 Hz (weaker)**, with crackle at about 0.5–10 kHz, most energetic around 2–3 kHz. This matches the film's "100 Hz hum and harmonics with corona crackle" in kind. A faithful mix would make the crackle, not the hum, the main wet-weather sound, and keep both faint or absent in dry weather.
- **Dry weather:** at 40 m the 750 kV line is close to the threshold of hearing against a quiet rural background (Hydro-Québec found no measurable increase, only a faint crackle). **Rain, snow, fog, dew:** around 48–55 dBA at 40 m from a 735/750 kV line, comparable to a quiet conversation, rising over about 15 min and falling over about 40 min. The **330 kV Ferosplavna-1** would be much quieter (TransGrid; BPA's ≥345 kV threshold). A mix with the 330 kV voice clearly lower and drier than the 750 kV voice is supported.
- The drained bed with its young willow forest is humid, with morning mist and dew over the old river channels. Fog/mist corona (TransGrid) is plausibly the most frequent "wet" state there. This is site-specific and not measured.
- The 750 kV crossing conductors (five sub-conductors per phase, per the project's tower model in `docs/crossing-3d-plan.txt`) and the 330 kV (two sub-conductors) differ in surface gradient. That is what governs corona, not the bundle count alone (see §2).

### Gaps
- No **measured dBA for any Soviet/Ukrainian 750 kV line** found. The ВНИИЭ/ВНИИЭ-era 750 kV corona studies and the 2009 EIA's full text (beyond the non-technical summary) were not reached. The Chinese 750 kV high-altitude study (Authorea preprint 10.22541/au.167528123.34243681) returned 403.
- ДСП 173-96 (sanitary rules for planning) and ДСН 3.3.6.037-99 (residential noise limits) were not read in full. Whether Ukraine has any noise limit specific to overhead lines was not confirmed.
- The TransGrid measurement distances from the conductors were not extracted.
- The hum mechanism (space-charge oscillation) rests on a snippet. The EPRI "Red Book" itself was not available.

## 2. Load, voltage, weather, bundle: does a nearly unloaded 750 kV line sound different? Is a de-energised line silent?

### Takeaway
**Corona noise follows voltage, not load.** A line energised at 750 kV but carrying only about 11–50 MW still crackles and hums in rain and fog, at essentially the same level as when it carried gigawatts. One second-order effect points the other way: load current warms the conductors and helps keep droplets off. A lightly loaded line may therefore stay wet, and noisy, a little longer after rain or in fog. A **de-energised** line has no corona and no electrical hum. What remains is wind: Aeolian vortex shedding (vibration about 3–150 Hz, which is what Stockbridge dampers absorb), galloping in ice, and sounds of the hardware and of birds.

### Cited Findings
- Corona "can occur on any AC electrical line, **regardless of the amount of current (load) flowing on the line**". "Even a line that is energized but not carrying load may emit corona noise." The EIR was corrected after the utility clarified that an existing 115 kV line was "energized at all times, thus creating corona discharge noise". — [CPUC El Casco RDEIR §D.9, July 2008, pp. D.9-1, D.9-7 (citing SCE 2008b)](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/aspen/elcasco/recircdeir/D%209%20Noise%20Rev3.pdf)
- From the same source, summarising EPRI's Red Book (2005): the conductor surface gradient is "the most important parameter", combining nominal voltage, conductor diameter, number of sub-conductors, height and phase spacing. "For a constant voltage, the various corona phenomena decrease as the conductor diameter increases". For a fixed field and diameter, noise "may slightly increase as the number of conductors in a bundle increases". Hydrophilic (aged) conductors are quieter than hydrophobic (new) ones. On weather and load: "**conductor heating due to current flow will discourage formation of water droplets during fog or high humidity**". Noise is highest in heavy rain or wet snow, but "the most critical period is in the quiet immediately following a significant rainfall … until the heat of the conductor evaporates the water droplets". At higher altitude corona starts at lower gradients (a minor effect). — [CPUC El Casco RDEIR, p. D.9-5](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/aspen/elcasco/recircdeir/D%209%20Noise%20Rev3.pdf)
- Corona is "a function of the voltage gradient at the conductor surface … a function of voltage, phase spacing, conductor diameter, conductor bundle, height … and meteorological conditions". Nicks, contamination, insects and water droplets increase it. — [SRP HIP Exhibit I](https://www.srpnet.com/assets/srpnet/pdf/grid-water-management/grid-management/improvement-projects/hip/cec/SRP-HIP-CEC-Final_ExhibitI.pdf)
- The voltage gradient is "the most important parameter influencing the corona effects" (Piana et al., ICSV24, 2017, **snippet-only**). — [IOA](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/EA%20PIANA%20A%20DONINI%20R%20SPEZIE%20R%20TURRI%20R%20CORTINA%20PREDICTION%20OF%20THE%20AUDIBLE%20NOISE%20GENERATED%20BY%20CORONA%20DISCHARGE%20ON%20A%20POWER%20TRANSMISSION%20LINE%20A%20MODEL%20VALIDATION.pdf)
- In a counterpoint at lab scale, a 2024 IEEE Access experiment found corona current *rises* with conductor temperature, with a 25–32% drop in inception voltage. That was heating by test transformer, on dry conductors (**snippet-only**). — [IEEE Access, Jan 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/28ccc8b473e94de3bc46e2874c91c2de)
- TransGrid lists "variable line load" among unmodelled causes of scatter at low rain rates. It is a hypothesis, not a measured effect. — [TransGrid/WSP 2025, §5.2](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/llsbcso3/audible-coronal-noise-study-investigating-audible-noise-from-transmission-lines-in-australian-climates.pdf)
- A Hydro-Québec patent addresses "pulsatory discharges of the corona effect … when the conductors are wet", showing that the wet-conductor regime is the problem utilities engineer against. — [US 4,458,107](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4458107)
- **Wind, with or without voltage:** Aeolian vibration of conductors lies in about **3–150 Hz** (some sources 3–200 Hz), driven by steady winds of about **1–7 m/s**. The Strouhal number for overhead conductors is about **0.185** (f = St·V/D). Stockbridge dampers are tuned so their resonances fall in the conductor's dangerous frequency range and dissipate more power than the wind puts in. — [Politecnico di Milano, FDMP paper](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1169364/1/FDMP.pdf); [Mechanical Sciences 12, 2021](https://ms.copernicus.org/articles/12/863/2021/)
- Aeolian vibration is usually reported at about 5–100 Hz and is mainly vertical. Helical outer strands change the lift and drag with wind yaw. — [GlobalSpec, *High Voltage Engineering and Testing* §5.4 Dampers](https://www.globalspec.com/reference/36198/203279/5-4-dampers-and-spacer-dampers)
- Acoustic Aeolian noise from single and twin bundled conductors has been modelled numerically (directivity becomes figure-of-eight near the dominant frequency; **snippet-only**). — [J. Vibration & Shock 39(16), 2020](https://jvs.sjtu.edu.cn/EN/Y2020/V39/I16/31)
- Lines running below their surge-impedance loading **generate** reactive power (their capacitance outweighs their inductance), which shunt reactors absorb to prevent overvoltage. — [CURENT/UTK](https://curent.utk.edu/index.php/download_file/view/3857/1651); [GE Vernova air-core reactors whitepaper](https://gevernova.com/grid-solutions/products/applications/Air-Core-Reactors-Whitepaper-EN-31994F.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Energised but nearly unloaded (the state since September 2022): it still sounds.** The electric field and the surface gradient are set by the 750 kV / 330 kV voltage, so the crackle and the 100 Hz corona hum would be essentially **the same in kind and level** as before the war. If anything, they would be slightly more persistent after rain and in fog, because 11–50 MW warms the conductors far less than gigawatt export did. For the film: a cut-off line should go silent, but a connected line carrying almost nothing should **not** be quieter than a fully loaded one. Its voice should be modulated by weather (rain, mist), not by MW.
- Order of magnitude: active power alone gives I = P/(√3·V). That is about 8–40 A at 750 kV for 11–50 MW, against about 770 A for 1,000 MW. At 330 kV it is about 19–87 A. But long 750 kV lines also carry capacitive charging current. A rough estimate is ~200 A per 100 km of line, partly compensated by shunt reactors. So the conductor current, and the magnetic field, does not fall in proportion to MW (see §5). This is my own calculation from textbook relations; the Dniprovska line's length, reactors and operating reactive flow were not sourced.
- **"Silent when cut" needs care.** A line tripped only at the plant end can stay energised from the far substation (open-ended) and would still corona. Only a line de-energised from both ends (as for repair, when it is also earthed) is electrically silent. The record's "cut" states don't always say which. The film's statement could say that the Hum's silence means "not connected", not "physically silent".
- **De-energised line:** no corona and no 100 Hz electrical hum. Wind can still make the spans "sing" at low frequencies, from vortex shedding at f ≈ 0.185·V/D. For an assumed 25 mm sub-conductor at 1–7 m/s that is about 7–52 Hz (my arithmetic; the sub-conductor diameter of these lines is not sourced). This structure-borne vibration is mostly below or at the bottom of hearing in air, but it is audible by contact microphone (Alan Lamb, §6). The 126 m crossing towers' long spans over the open bed would see steady winds.
- The Stockbridge dampers already modelled on the crossing (`docs/crossing-3d-plan.txt`, "2 m from every clamp") are there precisely to absorb this Aeolian vibration. They are a visible sign of the lines' wind sound.

### Gaps
- No primary EPRI/IEEE text was found quantifying how much load current (via conductor temperature) shortens the wet, after-rain period. The CPUC/EPRI statement is qualitative.
- No acoustic measurement found of audible Aeolian tones from EHV conductors (sound pressure at ground level).
- The Dniprovska line's conductor type and diameter, length, shunt-reactor compensation and actual current since 2022 are unknown. IAEA and Energoatom report MW, not amperes.

## 3. Substations and transformers: hum, harmonics, switching

### Takeaway
Transformer hum is **magnetostriction**: tonal at **2× grid frequency (100 Hz in Ukraine) and its harmonics**, present as soon as the transformer is energised, and dependent on **voltage, not load**. Winding noise does depend on load (∝ current⁴, +12 dB per doubling), also at 2f. Breakers and disconnectors give rare, momentary sounds.

### Cited Findings
- Core noise "comes from magnetostriction. It is generally a noise emitted at 120 Hz and its harmonics. It is present as soon as the transformer is energised. It varies with voltage but not with load." Winding noise comes from electromagnetic forces, "varies with load and is proportional to current to the power 4 (**12 dB each time the current doubles**)", and is "essentially a 120 Hz component". Cooling fans can matter. — [Hydro-Québec synthesis, pp. 21, 24](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- BPA: transformer and reactor noise comes from magnetic forces in the core and is "more pure in tone than is transmission line AN … generally characterized by a 120Hz hum". It varies with system voltage. A DC or geomagnetically induced current adds harmonics. Substation design limit: **50 dBA at the property line**, transformers evaluated at 100% rated voltage with all cooling on. — [BPA Policy T2006-1](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/foia/foia-2024/202400429F.pdf)
- The sound is "tonal in nature, consisting of even harmonics of the power frequency". Core noise dominates **100–600 Hz** and cooling equipment dominates the extremes. DC magnetisation adds odd harmonics (**snippet-only**). — [ABB transformer sound paper](https://library.e.abb.com/public/6d7d5b901f171797c125746a002c16d6/47-51%202M841_ENG72dpi.pdf); [EEP, Sources of sound in transformers](https://electrical-engineering-portal.com/sources-of-sound-in-transformers)
- Transformer hum is in "the frequency range of 75 Hz to 1200 Hz". "Disconnect switches and circuit breaker operations create momentary, but very infrequent, noise." — [SRP HIP Exhibit I](https://www.srpnet.com/assets/srpnet/pdf/grid-water-management/grid-management/improvement-projects/hip/cec/SRP-HIP-CEC-Final_ExhibitI.pdf)
- Corona noise from busbars in 735 kV substations can exceed limits in rain. — [Hydro-Québec synthesis, p. 17](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf)
- The Kakhovska 750 kV substation built for the ZNPP–Kakhovska line has **two 1,000 MVA autotransformers**. — [EBRD 2009 NTS](https://www.ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/english/pages/project/eia/40147u.pdf)

### Inferences
- At the ZNPP's 750 kV switchyard and at Dniprovska and Ferosplavna substations, with the plant importing only tens of MW, **core hum (voltage-driven) would continue** while **winding noise would be far lower**. On the ∝I⁴ rule, current at 1/20 of full load means about −52 dB of winding noise (my arithmetic). The "100 Hz and harmonics" in the Hum is therefore physically a substation and transformer sound as much as a line sound. Both are justified on a 50 Hz grid, provided the film says the sound is synthesized.
- Breaker operations (a line tripping or reconnecting) are real, brief, impulsive sounds at substations, not along the line. If the film marks cuts with a sound event, that is closer to a breaker than to the line itself, and should be said to be drawn and synthesized.

### Gaps
- No published sound-level data found for ZNPP's switchyard or for Dniprovska or Ferosplavna substations, and no Ukrainian transformer noise norms located.

## 4. Effects on wildlife and birds

### Takeaway
No study was found that measures birds' response to corona *noise*. The strongest literature concerns **corona light**: UV flashes that UV-sensitive birds and mammals may see, offered as a hypothesis for avoidance of lines. Cormorants are documented nesting on power-line structures in the Azov–Black Sea region. Pelicans have returned to the former reservoir near Nikopol.

### Cited Findings
- Tyler et al. (2014, *Conservation Biology*): birds, rodents and reindeer have UV-permissive ocular media. Corona flashes are temporally random, which likely impedes habituation. In darkness these animals may see lines "as lines of flickering light stretching across the terrain". The authors note the explanation does not cover avoidance by daylight or of lines not transmitting. — [PMC4232876](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4232876/); [UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1443241/1/cobi12262.pdf)
- Reindeer ocular media transmit about 15× more corona light than human eyes, and detection distances are on the order of hundreds of metres (Nellemann, Tyler et al., *Wildlife Society Bulletin* 2016, as summarised). Critic Michael Land called it "a plausible suggestion, rather than a convincing theory". — [UiT Munin](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/24947); [BioEd Online / Nature News](https://www.bioedonline.org/news/nature-news-archive/why-reindeer-steer-clear-power-lines/)
- Cormorants in the Azov–Black Sea region nest on islands and on man-made structures including "опори ліній електропередачі" (power-line supports) (**snippet-only**, 2025). — [Visnyk of Kharkiv University, Biology](https://periodicals.karazin.ua/biology/article/download/26911/23894)

### Inferences
- The crossing's real soundscape now probably includes birds of the new willow forest and wetland (and cormorants, if they use the 126 m towers). These would often be louder than the line in dry weather. That is a reason, if field audio were ever used, to separate the line's voice from the place's.

### Gaps
- No observations found of birds nesting on the specific Kakhovka crossing towers, and no studies of the acoustic (not optical) effect of corona noise on birds.

## 5. Electromagnetic listening: what an induction coil would "hear" under a lightly loaded line

### Takeaway
A coil (Kubisch's headphones, a telephone pickup) responds to the **magnetic** field, which arises from **current**. The **electric** field arises from **voltage**. So under a line carrying little power, the magnetic "voice" is weaker than in the export years, while corona, which is voltage-driven, continues. The magnetic field will not fall in proportion to MW, though, because of charging current (see §2). Mains hum in recordings (ENF) is also an established forensic tool for dating audio.

### Cited Findings
- WHO Fact Sheet N°322 (June 2007): "Electric fields arise from electric charges, are measured in volts per metre … Magnetic fields arise from the motion of electric charges (i.e. a current), are expressed in tesla". Under power lines magnetic fields "can be about 20 µT and electric fields can be several thousand volts per metre". — [WHO FS 322 (BPA copy)](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/lands/EMF_WHO_EMF-Fact-Sheet-2007a1.pdf)
- When a line is energised but no current flows, the magnetic field falls away while the electric field stays roughly the same (secondary source, attributed to WHO, **snippet-only**). — [Northern Ireland Assembly Research Paper 96/08](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/globalassets/documents/raise/publications/2008/environment/9608.pdf)
- An induction coil produces a voltage proportional to the fluctuating field through it. Hobby guides advise that "current [must be] flowing in the wires to be traced" with a coil. — [Zach Poff, Inductive Pickup Experiments](https://zachpoff.com/resources/inductive-pickup-experiments/); [techlib, Induction receiver](https://techlib.com/electronics/induction.html)
- Christina Kubisch's *Electrical Walks* (from 2003; first walk Cologne 2004; ongoing, presented in more than 75 cities) use "special, sensitive wireless headphones" with "built-in coils which respond to the electromagnetic waves in our environment". Her listed sources are lighting systems, radar, anti-theft gates, wireless networks, tram cables, ATMs and neon. Power lines are not named on her page. — [christinakubisch.de, Electrical Walks](https://christinakubisch.de/?p=123); [Ludwig Forum Aachen 2026](https://ludwigforum.de/en/?p=52948); [Ars Electronica 2010](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/festival/2010/repair/en/program/electricalwalks)
- Electric Network Frequency (ENF) analysis: the grid's small frequency wander, captured as hum in recordings, can be matched against a reference to date recordings and detect edits (Grigoras 2005; SWGDE guidance). — [Grigoras, *IJSLL* 12(1), 2005](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/IJSLL/article/view/9977); [SWGDE ENF Discussion Paper](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/09-a-001-swgde-electric-network-frequency-discussion-paper/)

### Inferences
- A pair of sounds the film could state as fact (with sources): **"The line still crackles in the rain because it is still at 750,000 volts. A coil held under it would hear less than before, because almost no power flows."** Qualify the second half: the conductor current includes reactive charging current, not only the 11–50 MW, so "less" not "almost nothing".
- The magnetic field (and so a coil's 50 Hz signal) is at the grid frequency, **50 Hz**, with odd harmonics. Corona and transformer acoustic hum is at **100 Hz** (2f). These are different sounds from different physics, and a "voice per line" could keep them apart.
- ENF is a direct precedent for "the grid's hum as a witness". The Ukrainian grid has been synchronised with ENTSO-E (continental Europe) since 16 March 2022 (not sourced in this note; verify). So ENF in recordings made in Ukraine since then would follow the continental European grid.

### Gaps
- No measured magnetic field or recording under a lightly loaded EHV line was found. No source states the charging current of the Dniprovska line.

## 6. Precedents in sound art, field recording and forensic listening

### Takeaway
There is a strong lineage: **wire as instrument** (Lamb, Lucier), **making the electromagnetic audible** (Kubisch), **listening to dangerous or contaminated places** (Cusack, Kirkegaard at Chernobyl), and **forensic listening** (Abu Hamdan with Forensic Architecture; Schuppli's *Material Witness*). Abu Hamdan's insistence on saying what is measured, reconstructed or re-enacted is the closest model for the film's "synthesized, and said so" Hum.

### Cited Findings
**Wires and structures**
- **Alan Lamb** (1944–2025), *Primal Image* and *Beauty*: contact-microphone recordings of kilometre-long spans of telegraph wire on 12 acres at Baldivis, south of Perth, WA ("Faraway Wind Organ"). Recorded and edited 1981–88, issued on Room40 as *Archival Recordings: Primal Image / Beauty* (Primal Image 29:22, Beauty 16:46). Sources differ on dating (1981 vs 1982/84). Relevance: wind-excited spans, heard through the structure, are the "de-energised" voice of a line. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Lamb_(musician)); [Forced Exposure, RM 4240](https://forcedexposure.com/Catalog/RM.4240CD.html); [SLWA interview 2015](https://purl.slwa.wa.gov.au/slwa_b4260655_4.pdf)
- **Alvin Lucier**, *Music on a Long Thin Wire* (1977; Lovely Music LP 1980): a piano wire stretched across a room, its ends connected to a power amplifier driven by a sine oscillator, a magnet straddling one end. The wire is set vibrating by AC current in a magnetic field. Lucier noted air currents, temperature and people nearby change it. Relevance: current plus field equals a singing wire, the physics behind line and transformer hum. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_On_A_Long_Thin_Wire); [DRAM notes](https://www.dramonline.org/albums/alvin-lucier-music-on-a-long-thin-wire/notes)
- **Jez riley French**: has built contact mics since the late 1970s/early 1980s, plus hydrophones and geophones, and listens to "a place, a structure, or a situation" for hours (*salts | adagios*, 2018). A contact-mic recording of the guy-wires of a large radio mast in Iceland is reported only in student lecture blogs (**weak source; no pylon recording confirmed**). — [Bandcamp Daily, 2019](https://daily.bandcamp.com/jez-riley-french-feature); [Lincoln student blog](https://ansonaudioprojects.blogs.lincoln.ac.uk/?p=53)
- **Daniel Menche**, "Electrical" (2011): a raw field recording of power lines in Mt Hood National Forest (**snippet-only**). — [Bandcamp](https://danielmenche.bandcamp.com/track/electrical)

**The electromagnetic made audible**
- **Christina Kubisch**, *Electrical Walks* (2003–), as in §5. — [christinakubisch.de](https://christinakubisch.de/?p=123)

**Listening to dangerous and contaminated places**
- **Peter Cusack**, *Sounds from Dangerous Places* (ReR Megacorp, 2012; book + 2 CDs: Chernobyl; Caspian oil fields and UK sites). He calls it "sonic journalism", asking "what can we learn of dangerous places by listening to their sounds?" The Chernobyl CD includes "sounds of work, electricity and radiometers", Pripyat, the Samosely, wildlife, and evacuees' songs. — [Grünrekorder](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?p=9070); [ZKM 2013](https://www.zkm.de/en/event/2013/01/peter-cusack-sounds-from-dangerous-places); [art-into-life listing](https://art-into-life.com/product/2347)
- **Jacob Kirkegaard**, *4 Rooms* (Touch TONE 26, 2006): a church in Krasno and an auditorium, swimming pool and gymnasium in Pripyat (Chernobyl zone). Each room was recorded for about 10 min, played back into itself and re-recorded up to ten times until its resonance dominated, after Lucier's *I Am Sitting in a Room*. He recorded "the room's own voice". — [Touch](https://touch33.net/?p=339); [Tiny Mix Tapes](https://www.tinymixtapes.com/music-review/jacob-kirkegaard-4-rooms)
- **Chris Watson**, *Weather Report* (Touch TO:47, 2003): three long pieces from Kenya, Scotland and Iceland, compressing time and place, with little processing. — [Touch](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to47-chris-watson-weather-report.html); [chriswatson.net](https://chriswatson.net/page/12/)
- **Hildegard Westerkamp**, *Kits Beach Soundwalk* (1989): a soundwalk composition in which the narrator filters out the city to hear barnacles. It is a model for a voice guiding attention between a small sound and a masking din. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_Westerkamp); [ISEA archive](https://isea-archives.siggraph.org/?p=38855)
- **Félix Blume** (b. 1984), *Rumors from the Sea* (Thailand Biennale, Krabi, 2018): about a hundred bamboo flutes made from seawall bamboo, played by the waves. A site that sounds itself through its infrastructure. — [CENSE](https://cense.earth/felix-blume); [SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/felixblume/rumorsfromthesea)

**Forensic listening**
- **Forensic Architecture / Lawrence Abu Hamdan**, *The Killing of Nadeem Nawara and Mohammad Abu Daher* (Beitunia, 15 May 2014): audio analysis of the gunshots in the recordings showed live rounds fired with a rubber-bullet extension, against the army's claim. A border policeman was charged with manslaughter (Nov 2014). Abu Hamdan's video *Rubber Coated Steel* (2016) builds on it. — [Forensic Architecture](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/the-killing-of-nadeem-nawara-and-mohammed-abu-daher); [ICA](https://www.ica.art/exhibitions/forensic-architecture-counter-investigations/recent-investigations/the-killing-of-nadeem-nawara-and-mohammad-abu-daher)
- **Abu Hamdan**, *Saydnaya (the missing 19db)* (2017, some sources 2016; Sharjah Biennial 13; about 12 min): ear-witness testimony of former detainees of Saydnaya, which was inaccessible, commissioned by Amnesty International with Forensic Architecture (2016). A 19 dB drop in how loud detainees dared whisper after 2011 is read as evidence of the prison's change. Form: testimony, voice-over, re-enacted whispers, silence, test tones. — [Sharjah Art Foundation](https://sharjahart.org/sharjah-art-foundation/projects/saydnaya-the-missing-19db); [Urbanomic](https://www.urbanomic.com/chapter/the-missing-19db/)
- **Abu Hamdan**, *Earwitness Inventory* / *Earwitness Theatre* (Chisenhale Gallery, London, 2018): about 95 objects made to reproduce sounds described in legal testimony (e.g. a building collapsing "like popcorn"), because stock sound effects were too imprecise. — [Domus 2018](https://domusweb.it/en/art/2018/09/21/earwitness-theatre-non-neutral-sound-evidences.html); [CAM St Louis guide](https://camstl.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Lawrence-Abu-Hamdan-Gallery-Guide_Final.pdf)
- **Susan Schuppli**, *Material Witness: Media, Forensics, Evidence* (MIT Press/Leonardo, Feb 2020, 392 pp.): matter as witness, structured as a trial, with cases including Kosovo war-crimes video and Fukushima contamination. — [Goldsmiths Research Online](https://research.gold.ac.uk/28197); [Penguin AU](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/material-witness-9780262043571)
- Gunshot acoustics and multilateration (TDOA from unsynchronised recordings; echoes, reverberation and noise as confounders): Maher & Hoerr, NIJ 2018. This is general audio-forensic method, not FA's. — [NIJ](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/audio-forensic-gunshot-analysis-and-multilateration)

### Inferences
- The Earwitness approach (sounds made to stand in for described sounds, labelled as such) is a direct precedent for the film's synthesized Hum. The difference is that here the "testimony" is the dated record of connections, not memory. Citing Abu Hamdan alongside the statement "synthesized, never a recording" would place the Hum in the forensic-aesthetic tradition.
- Lamb (wind on unpowered wire), Lucier (current in a field) and the corona literature give three distinct physical voices. A line could plausibly be given these in the film: wind and structure (always), corona (when energised, by weather), magnetic hum (with current).

### Gaps
- **Disinformation (Joe Banks)**, known for recordings of electromagnetic emissions including power infrastructure, could not be verified by search; check before citing. No verified recording by Chris Watson or Jez riley French of EHV lines or pylons specifically.
