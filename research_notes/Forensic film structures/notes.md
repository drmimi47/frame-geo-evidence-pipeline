# Forensic film structures: notes for restructuring *Powerlines*

Research for the storyboard of *Powerlines* (4 November 2026). Each takeaway names the film and links a source.

**How to read these notes**
- A quote in "double quotes" is wording seen on the page linked beside it. Quotes marked *(auto-captions)* come from
  YouTube's automatic captions of the film's own narration, downloaded 4 November 2026. They are verbatim apart from
  obvious mis-hearings, which are corrected in [square brackets].
- **[unverified]** means one secondary source only, or memory, or nothing confirming it.
- **Observed** means looked at frame by frame. Begley's film was downloaded to a scratch folder, and contact sheets
  were made of every second. Nothing went into the library.
- Running times are from the makers' own pages where possible.

**Takeaway.** FA's single-incident films run 6 to 11 minutes. The longer pieces (17 to 33 minutes) are either built
for gallery or court, or are anthologies. Ten minutes is FA's own length for one case told whole.

### 1.2 The Murder of Halit Yozgat / *77sqm_9:26min* (2017, 28:55)

The clearest model of FA's evidentiary structure.
- **Opening on the exact time and place.** The narration *(auto-captions,
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zWZHDLVTz38)* opens: "5 p.m. on the 6th of April 2006 [Halit Yozgat] was found dead
  behind the counter of his family-run internet cafe". Context follows (the NSU series, the agent present, the leaked
  files). Only then comes the commission.
- **The questions stated as a list.** "what time did the murder happen where was [Temme] at that time could he not have
  witnessed the incident". The film is then the answer to them, in order.
- **The frame named as a microcosm.** FA's page: "The seventy-seven square metres of the internet café, and the
  nine-and-a-half minutes during which the incident unfolded, can be seen as a microcosm of the NSU Complex." The
  title is the space and the duration.
- **Method as a matrix.** The narration says every witness was tied to a time-coded device, so the login data gave a
  timeline. It calls the result "the space-time matrix within which different possible events could take place."
- **Graded conclusions, spoken plainly.** Of three scenarios: scenario one "is not possible", "the evidence does not
  exclude scenario two", and scenario three "is also possible".
- **The ending turns from proof to meaning, then to a person.** The last minutes give the father's words (he asked the
  court to visit the shop). The final line: the state's stance amounts "to the continuation of violence by other means".

### 1.3 Russian Strike on the Kyiv TV Tower (2022, 9:16; FA with the Center for Spatial Technologies)

The closest FA precedent for *Powerlines*: a strike on a lattice tower of civilian infrastructure, then a turn down
into the landscape's buried history.
- **Hook: the event and its echo.** *(auto-captions, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c87a3KkCqbg)*: "the March 1st
  Russian strike on the TV tower in the heart of [Kyiv] has raised a number of tragic historical echoes".
- **Method shown on the first strike.** The narration covers sizing the missile against the tower and syncing the videos
  by the flash and the smoke plume. It fixes the time ("5:08 pm") and checks it against the first reports.
- **The turn: from infrastructure down into the ground and back in time.** "the missiles aimed at the tower are like
  needle probes piercing through layers of historical strata". Then come a 1924 topographic map, 19th-century cemetery
  plots, 1943 aerial photographs, and the ravines filled with mud in 1951 and "flattening the topography of the site".
- **Close: what the strike means, said once.** "these strikes were another attempt at silencing not by covering up
  evidence but by seeking to interrupt the circulation of narratives". FA's page frames it the same way: "a war that
  is as much about control over narrative as over land".
- **For Powerlines.** The lines cross a landscape that was once the Great Meadow. It was flooded in the 1950s and
  drained in 2023. The Kyiv film's move (strike on infrastructure, then down through the ground's layers, then back
  up to now) is a ready-made shape for the crossing and the meadow.

### 1.4 Herbicidal Warfare in Gaza (2019, 8:50)

- **The investigation is posed as questions.** FA's page: "our investigation sought to answer the following questions:
  how do airborne herbicides travel into Gaza? How far into Gaza does it enter? What is the concentration of the
  herbicide that drifts into Gaza? And what is the damage to the farmland on the Gazan side of the border?"
  (https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/herbicidal-warfare-in-gaza)
- **Sections named like chapters.** "Staging the terrain", "Weaponising the wind", "Farm warfare", "Tracking a single
  spraying".
- **One event modelled in depth stands for a practice.** A single spraying on 5 April 2017 is reconstructed: camera
  calibration, the plane's path, a fluid-dynamics model of drift. "each spray leaves behind a unique destructive
  signature".
- **The medium is environmental.** Wind carries the harm, and a model makes visible what can't be filmed. This is the
  closest parallel to the Hum and to the drawn flow on the lines.

### 1.5 The Left-to-Die Boat / *Liquid Traces* (2012 report; film 2014, about 17–18 min, Forensic Oceanography)

- **The case.** 72 passengers left Tripoli in March 2011 and drifted 14 days in NATO's surveillance area. Only nine
  survived. The reconstruction combined "testimonies with wind and sea-current data as well as satellite imagery"
  (https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/the-left-to-die-boat).
- **The key events are numbered and lettered on a map.** FA's summary lists seven numbered events, tied to points A–E
  on the map.
- **The uncertainty is drawn.** "Our drift model provides day-by-day estimates of the vessel's location … Over time,
  the margin of error in the drifting vessel's track decreases linearly as it is constrained by the known position of
  landing." The error band is part of the picture.
- **What the data cannot do is said.** The AIS data "could not be matched satisfactorily with satellite imagery
  targets".
- **The surveillance image is turned round.** The page names this a "'disobedient gaze', one that refuses to disclose
  clandestine migration but seeks to expose instead the violence of Europe's border regime".
- **The sea as witness.** Labocine's synopsis: "the sea itself can be turned into a witness for interrogation", and the
  film is "a synthetic reconstruction" (https://www.labocine.com/films/liquid-traces-the-left-to-die-boat-case).
- **Form [unverified].** One search summary of a review describes lights on a blue sea fading until one spark is left.
  It could not be confirmed. If true, it is a direct precedent for ten lines going dark until one is lit.

### 1.6 Cloud Studies (2020, updated 2021 and 2022; 32:59)

- **Ordered by substance, not by date.** FA's page lists the films "grouped by compound": white phosphorus, glyphosate,
  carbon monoxide, fine particulate matter, methane, tear gas, water, chlorine, cement
  (https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/cloudstudies).
- **The method's limit as the premise.** "It is a basic principle of forensics that, between solid objects, 'every
  contact leaves a trace'. By contrast, clouds are the epitome of transformation". Also: "Clouds are always double. Seen
  from the outside they are measurable objects, seen from within they are experiential conditions".
- **It ends on an epigraph.** The video closes on a line from Achille Mbembe's April 2020 essay "The Universal Right to
  Breathe" (per FA's ZKM page, via search summary: https://zkm.de/en/media/14150). The epigraph text itself is not
  quoted here.
- **It is iterative.** "an iterative film that is updated periodically". That suits a record which, like the ZNPP's,
  has no end yet.

### 1.7 Triple-Chaser (2019, 10:35; FA and Praxis Films)

- **Narrated by David Byrne**, per FA's credits (https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/triple-chaser).
- **Synthetic images, stated as such.** FA "constructed a digital model of the Triple-Chaser, and located it within
  thousands of photorealistic 'synthetic' environments … In this way, 'fake' images help us to search for real ones".
  The synthetic is put on screen as method, never passed off as evidence.
- **Its centre is a strobing montage.** Reviews describe a sequence that "strobes through images" used to train the
  classifier (search summary via https://www.e-flux.com/film/381448/triple-chaser/) [secondary].
- **The ending moves to new allegations** against Kanders (Sierra Bullets) [secondary].
- **The context of its making is disclosed** on its screening booth. FA made the work "independently and without
  request, oversight, or influence by the Whitney Museum."

### 1.8 Grenfell, Zak Kostopoulos, Mariupol: testimony, synchronisation, erasure

- **Grenfell (2018, 2:36).** Hundreds of phone videos were "projection mapped" onto a wire-frame model to make "a
  continuous '3D video' of the fire". The film asks for more footage.
- **Grenfell: Situated Testimonies (2024).** Survivors used 3D models as memory aids. One testimony includes "a
  reconstruction of the soundscape that Shahrokh heard through the phone"
  (https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/the-grenfell-tower-fire-situated-testimonies). This is a reconstructed
  sound, presented as a reconstruction: a precedent for the Hum.
- **Zak Kostopoulos (2019, 6:34).** Footage from many passers-by is synchronised. The film focuses on one overlooked
  figure, "the man in the yellow t-shirt", who becomes the hook. A single detail found in the evidence can carry a
  short film.
- **Mariupol Drama Theatre (2023, 21:34; CST with Forensis).** The film moves from the theatre as a shelter, to the
  strike, to the erasure: "After the attack on civilians came the attack on evidence." It used over "100 hours of
  interviews with witnesses" and photogrammetry. Powerlines' version of erasure is the information blackout: the IAEA
  names lines only from late 2022.

### 1.9 FA's video grammar, seen from outside

Emerson Goo, MUBI Notebook, 11 May 2021
(https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/remains-to-be-seen-the-video-work-of-forensic-architecture):
- "the timeline is the master frame to which video and audio files from different sources are synchronized"
- In the Pavlos Fyssas film, "The viewer is placed in the seat of the film editor".
- FA films show maps and 3D scenes being made in "ArcGIS, Rhino, and Blender".
- "re-enactment as immersion, rather than dramatization"
- "it is not enough, for example, to simply narrate or label scenes of aerial imagery". The imagery has to be worked.

## 2. Lawrence Abu Hamdan

Sources were read through a page-summarising fetch, except two press releases read in full (MoMA, Andrew Kreps). Treat
quotes not from those as very likely verbatim, and check them before printing anything.

**Rubber Coated Steel (2016, about 21–22 min).** Running time: Vdrome gives 21'50" (https://www.vdrome.org/?p=1034).
- **The case.** The killings of Nadeem Nawara and Mohammad Abu Daher in May 2014. Abu Hamdan's audio analysis showed
  live rounds disguised to sound like rubber bullets.
- **Form: a tribunal "in absentia".** Vdrome: "The piece acts as a kind of tribunal in absentia for these murders", and
  "Emotions, dead bodies, loud sounds, ammunition sounds, and even the voice itself are all removed from the video." It
  is set in an empty shooting range, where spectrograms take the place of targets.
- **Text instead of voice.** Peripheral Review: the transcript is "presented line by line as a series of subtitles",
  with struck-out and redacted lines
  (https://peripheralreview.com/lawrence-abu-hamdans-many-silences-rubber-coated-steel-2016).
- **Ending.** After "Judge: Can't they hear me?", "Still the transcript reads, '[Inaudible].'" (verified). The review's
  thesis: "to be silent and to be silenced are very different things".
- **The artist on structure.** Art Monthly interview, June 2017
  (https://www.artmonthly.co.uk/magazine/site/article/lawrence-abu-hamdan-interviewed-by-chris-mccormack-june-2017):
  - "The video follows a structure that I borrowed from a 1988 Harold Pinter play called Mountain Language."
  - "The crescendo is not to finally hear the witnesses' voices after they have been pushed out and redacted."
  The withheld thing is withheld to the end on purpose.
- **Earshot** (Portikus, 2016) was the show around it: "The video tribunal does not preside over the voices of the
  victims but rather seeks to amplify their silence" (https://www.portikus.de/en/exhibitions/199_earshot).

**Saydnaya (the missing 19db) (2017), an audio work of about 12–13 minutes in a dark room.**
e-flux review: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/242197/lawrence-abu-hamdan-s-earwitness-theatre
- **Opening.** A repeated test tone and a list of places, ending on Saydnaya.
- **Form.** Survivors' testimony is cut with the artist's explanations.
- **The title is a measurement of a silence.** The 19 dB drop in how loudly prisoners whispered before and after 2011.
  One number names the work and is its evidence.
- **The earwitness.** "The only way to access this place was through the memories of survivors" (Art Monthly, above).

**Walled Unwalled (2018, about 20 min).**
- **Form.** One continuous performance-lecture "monologue connecting three narratives" (Kyllo v. United States, the
  Pistorius trial, Saydnaya). Recorded at the East Berlin Funkhaus and "filmed through the studio's glass walls" (MoMA
  press release, read in full:
  https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/MoMA_LAH_PressRelease_FINAL.pdf).
- Tate: "monologues, projected images, moving walls and the performance of different sounds"
  (https://tate.org.uk/whats-on/tate-modern/lawrence-abu-hamdan-walled-unwalled).
- **Structure.** Three cases braided by one idea, the wall as a medium sound passes through. The artist opens and
  closes studio doors between the monologues (Madamasr, via search summary) [secondary].

**Air Pressure (A Diary of the Sky) (2021–) and The Diary of a Sky (2024).** The closest precedent for the Hum.
- **A record of events as sound.** MoMA release: research "conducted between May 2020 and May 2021, into the aerial
  soundscape of Lebanon, documenting 2,412 instances of Israeli fighter jets and drones. Through chronological
  sequencing and live audio processing, the performance analyzes the relationship between background noise and
  atmospheres of violence."
- **A database behind it.** AirPressure.info. Artnet quotes: "over half of the last 15 years there has been an Israeli
  combat vehicle in the Lebanese sky" (https://news.artnet.com/art-world/lawrence-abu-hamdan-air-pressure-2201490).
- **The Diary of a Sky (2024, 45 min, essay film).** IDFA: "The visuals in this essay film consist entirely of shots of
  that airspace" (https://www.idfa.nl/en/film/8b1b03ed-ae9c-4a87-9304-7ac9d20530f4/the-diary-of-a-sky). WORM: "an
  atmospheric symphony of violence over Beirut", pairing military flights with "the hum of generators during blackouts"
  (verified, https://worm.org/production/lawrence-abu-hamdan-the-diary-of-a-sky/).
- **Lesson.** The date log is the score: chronological sequencing, sound as the evidence, and an image restrained to a
  single kind of view.

**45th Parallel (2022, 15 min).** "a four-act monologue" in one building straddling the Canada–US border
(https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/536270/jesse-jonesthe-towerlawrence-abu-hamdan45th-parallel). The shape is one
place on a line, one voice, a legal case, and acts.

**Takeaways:**
- Sound is the evidence and the image is restrained.
- The measured absence (19 dB, [Inaudible]) is the climax.
- Text carries the testimony where voice would add theatre.
- Silence is a subject, not a gap.

## 3. Josh Begley, *Best of Luck with the Wall* (2016)

Sources:
- Begley's essay in The Intercept, 26 Oct 2016: https://theintercept.com/2016/10/26/best-of-luck-with-the-wall/
- Field of Vision's page ("7 min."): https://fieldofvision.org/best-of-luck-with-the-wall
- Field of Vision's interview: https://fieldofvision.org/interview-with-josh-begley
- YouTube upload, 6:41: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sIe9p7tslpg
- Observed from contact sheets of every second.

**Construction (Begley, The Intercept).** "I wrote a small computer script to download satellite imagery for the
entire border. I ended up with about 200,000 images. Using a command-line tool called ffmpeg, I programmatically
stitched the images together, and then worked with Laura Poitras and her team at Field of Vision to edit them into a
short film."

**Sound.** There is an original score. The Intercept: "Jace Clayton, the artist and author known as DJ /rupture,
developed an original score for the piece." The end credits (observed) read "original score by Jace Clayton (DJ
/rupture) & Andy Moor (The Ex)". Begley in the interview: "There were elements of the soundscape that he was playing
live."
- **[unverified, probably wrong]** No source found says Trump's voice or a speech is on the soundtrack. YouTube has no
  automatic captions for the upload, which weakly suggests there is no speech.
- Trump is in the framing, not the audio. The Intercept's standfirst: "President Trump just announced that he intends
  to fulfill his campaign pledge to build a wall along the U.S. Mexico border. This is what that border looks like."
  (Note: the standfirst says "President" on a piece dated before the 2016 election, so it was evidently updated later.)
- The title answers a slogan ("build that wall"), so the film can stay wordless.

**Structure (observed, 6:41 upload):**

| Time | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Field of Vision ident on black |
| 0:06 | Open water: the Pacific, top-down |
| about 0:09–0:12 | One small white line of text over the sea: "The U.S.-Mexico border is 1,954 miles long" |
| 0:13 onward | The surf line and the first fence at Tijuana / San Diego; travel eastward begins |
| about 0:37–0:41 | Title, after about 30 s of travel (a cold open); "OF" flashes over imagery, then "BEST OF LUCK WITH THE WALL" on black |
| 0:41 to about 5:55 | One continuous travelling shot east, cut from stills at several zoom levels |
| end of the travel | The Rio Grande meanders and its mouth; the frame goes out over the surf of the Gulf |
| about 6:01–6:04 | Over the open sea, one small line of text: "Made with 200,000 satellite images downloaded from Google Maps" |
| then | The sea fades to black; credits on black, then a short silence (silencedetect: about 6:39–6:41) |

The interviewer confirms the bookends: "There's the title card at the beginning and the title card at the end".

**Takeaways:**
- **Bookend the one move with the same element at each end.** Open water, then land, then open water. One fact-card in
  at the start (scale) and one method-card out at the end (sources). Nothing else is written.
- **The opening card is a number, not an argument.** "1,954 miles long" sets the scale the travel then makes you feel.
  Begley's question was whether one could "travel the whole 1,954 miles in, say, six minutes?" The length of the film
  is the proposition.
- **Pace follows the data, not the drama.** Begley: "In some cases the speed of the film does map to the resolution of
  the images". They chose to "skip through some of the lo-res images and focus in on the high-res images". Variable
  speed is honest about the source's coverage.
- **The seams show (observed).** Mosaic tile edges, black-and-white tiles beside colour, and jumps in zoom are left
  visible. No line is drawn over the border; the fence or the river is the line. Along the Rio Grande the frame follows
  the river's turns rather than keeping north up.
- **The purpose is stated in prose, not in the film.** "The southern border is a space that has been almost entirely
  reduced to metaphor. It is not even a geography. Part of my intention with this film is to insist on that geography."
  The essay carries the argument and the film carries the experience.
- **The ending doesn't resolve.** The Arts Fuse: "The film and the border don't so much end as just float off into the
  Gulf of Mexico" (https://artsfuse.org/178846/film-review-best-of-luck-with-the-wall-mapping-how-lost-we-are/).
- **The length is a choice.** Begley: "This is probably the most compressed version that will exist … a response to
  where people's attention spans are on the Internet." A longer gallery "variant" exists (EMAF 2018,
  https://asquare.org/best-of-luck-variant).

## 4. Essay film: Schuppli, Steyerl, Farocki

### 4.1 Susan Schuppli

**Material Witness (MIT Press, 2020).**
- **The idea.** Materials as witnesses: "nonhuman entities and machinic ecologies that archive their complex interactions
  with the world", and "Materials can, in fact, bear witness". Quoted in a review in Interfaces:
  https://journals.openedition.org/interfaces/5289
- **Chapters named after trial proceedings.** Thirteen of them. The titles below come from a library-catalogue snippet
  and were not fetched [unverified]: Opening Statements, Discovery, Hostile Witness, Hearsay, Motion to Strike, Damages,
  Toxic Tort, Cross-Examination, Expert Witness, Burden of Proof, Failure to Appear, Closing Arguments, Convictions.
- **Why it matters here.** A legal procedure is used as the table of contents.

**Trace Evidence (2016, about 53 min).**
- **Form.** A "video trilogy", one part per element: geological (Oklo, Gabon), meteorological (Chernobyl fallout found at
  Forsmark, 1986) and hydrological (caesium-137's "7,600 kilometre five year journey" from Fukushima).
  https://research.gold.ac.uk/19876 ; https://www.savvy-contemporary.com/en/events/2017/n-31-traceevidence/
- Savvy: "This 19 day gap is a subject matter that allows Schuppli's film to work at the threshold of visibility".
- **Lesson.** Organise by the medium the evidence travels through: ground, air, water. For Powerlines that could be
  steel, water and light.

**Can the Sun Lie? (2014).**
- Shown in *Forensis* (HKW, 2014). The title is a 19th-century US court's question about whether photographs can be
  evidence.
- The work is about the Arctic sun setting in the wrong place, attributed to warming air.
- Length (about 13 min) and structure are unconfirmed (https://research.gold.ac.uk/18391).

**Atmospheric Feedback Loops (2016/17, about 18 min).** A vertical 35 mm film about the Cabauw atmospheric tower:
"collecting data since 1970" (https://2021.photomonth.com/?p=2974). A tower of sensors is the protagonist.

### 4.2 Hito Steyerl, *How Not to Be Seen: A Fucking Didactic Educational .MOV File* (2013, 15'52")

Running time 15'52" per the Reina Sofía (https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/exhibition/hito-steyerl); listings vary from
14 to 16 minutes.
- **Numbered lessons in an instructional frame.** Five lessons, per Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hito_Steyerl):
  1. "Make something invisible for a camera"
  2. "Be invisible in plain sight"
  3. "Become invisible by becoming a picture"
  4. "Be invisible by disappearing"
  5. "Become invisible by merging into a world made of pictures"
  MoMA reportedly says four sections [discrepancy].
- **Opening on a calibration target.** Andrew Kreps release, read in full: it "begins with a sweeping shot of photo
  calibration targets in the California desert utilized by the military and acts as an instructional film". Proposals
  include "becoming smaller than the pixels of high-resolution satellite surveillance (1 foot)"
  (https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/6029/docfile/original-32d8880a5cb3b1f1b4eb558754d86462.pdf).
- **The narrator states the rule.** ArtFCity
  (https://artfcity.com/posts/2014/07/hito-steyerl-is-not-completely-invisible/): "Resolution determines visibility".
- **Ending: the apparatus disappears.** Rhizome, 2013 (https://old.rhizome.org/editorial/2013/may/31/hito-steyerl-how-not-to-be-seen):
  "After these tactics are outlined, the film crew making this educational video also disappears", and "happy
  low-resolution pixels take over the production".
- **For Powerlines.** The 10 m pixel is literally the film's resolution: wires don't show, and a pylon is a few pixels.
  One explicit "what 10 m can show" moment makes the drawn lines honest. Take the device, not the parody.

### 4.3 Harun Farocki

**Inextinguishable Fire (1969, 25 min).** https://www.harunfarocki.de/films/1960s/1969/inextinguishable-fire.html
- **The opening statement is about how to show.** In the official translation: "When we show you pictures of napalm
  victims, you'll shut your eyes. You'll close your eyes to the pictures." / "Then you'll close them to the memory. And
  then you'll close your eyes to the facts."
- **Then one bodily demonstration of scale.** "A cigarette burns at 400 degrees" / "Napalm burns at 3,000 degrees"
  (Artnet's translation: https://news.artnet.com/art-world/harun-farockis-inextinguishable-fire-70883).
- **Lesson.** Face the problem of representation in the first minute, with one physical comparison, not a lecture.

**Images of the World and the Inscription of War (1988, about 75 min).**
https://www.harunfarocki.de/films/1980s/1988/images-of-the-world-and-the-inscription-of-war.html
- **Recurring images as the structure.** A wave pool, the 1944 aerial photographs of IG Farben in which Auschwitz was
  photographed but not seen, the Meydenbauer photogrammetry anecdote, and Algerian women photographed for identity
  cards. These "return throughout" (https://filmref.com/?p=2408).
- **The central idea.** The aerial reconnaissance photograph holds what its makers didn't look for. A satellite pass is
  the same kind of image.
- Accounts of how it opens and ends conflict [unverified].

**Eye/Machine (2000–03) and soft montage.**
- Opening subtitles of Eye/Machine I: "Images like these could be seen in 1991 – of the war against Iraq / Operational
  images" (https://greenenaftaligallery.com/exhibitions/harun-farocki3).
- Farocki on soft montage: "One image doesn't take the place of the previous one, but supplements it, reevaluates it,
  balances it" (https://cielvariable.ca/en/issues/ciel-variable-78-collecting-photography/harun-farocki-one-image-doesnt-take-the-place-of-the-previous-one-cheryl-simon/).
- **Lesson.** Two images side by side (before and after, day and night, observed and modelled) let the viewer compare.
  Nobody has to say what changed.

**Serious Games (2009–10) and Parallel I–IV (2012–14).**
- Serious Games IV: "Nothing and no-one casts a shadow here"

## 7. General craft: the 7–10 minute nonfiction short, intertitles, silence

### 7.1 Model films for structure

**Powers of Ten (Charles and Ray Eames, 1977, 9 min).** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_Ten_(film) (verified)
- **Opening.** "an overhead view of a man and woman picnicking in a park at the Chicago lakefront".
- **One rule.** Out "at a rate of one power of ten per 10 seconds". Back "at a rate of a power of ten per 2 seconds to
  the picnic", then on in to "quarks in a proton of a carbon atom at 10−16 meters".
- **One readout.** The Eames Foundation: "With an animated image, a narration, and a dashboard, it gives a clue to the
  relative size of things" (https://eamesfoundation.org/?p=4324).
- **Narration.** By the physicist Philip Morrison.
- **The prototype had two readouts.** *A Rough Sketch* (1968) had two clocks comparing the viewer's time with Earth
  time. The 1977 film dropped them for one readout.
- **Lessons.**
  - A whole film can be one rule you can see.
  - The return trip over ground already seen goes five times faster.
  - It ends somewhere new: inside, not back at the start.
  - The zoom-out in Powerlines is already this form.

**Koyaanisqatsi (Reggio, 1982).** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koyaanisqatsi
- **No narration.** Thirteen passages, each marked by a change in the music.
- **The title is explained only at the end.** The definition card ("crazy life", "life out of balance", … "a state of
  life that calls for another way of living") comes after the images have done their work.
- **Bracketed.** It closes on the pictograph it opened on.

**Sans Soleil (Marker, 1983).** Script: https://www.markertext.com/sans_soleil.htm
- **A cold open about how to watch.** "The first image he told me about was of three children on a road in Iceland, in
  1965." He wants it "alone at the beginning of a film with a long piece of black leader; if they don't see happiness
  in the picture, at least they'll see the black."
- **Narration as reported speech** ("he wrote") keeps a distance between the voice and its claims.

**Blockade (Loznitsa, 2006, 52 min). The precedent for a made soundtrack.**
Senses of Cinema: https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2017/soviet-cinema/blockade-2006/
- **The sound is added.** "Loznitsa adds a naturalistic sound track to the originally silent footage."
- **It never stops, except once.** "In fact, the film does not give us a minute of silence." The only real silence is
  before the 1946 epilogue: "These few seconds of absolute silence make the last sequence function as an epilogue."
- **Ordered by decline.** Loznitsa "chose to structure the film alongside the vector of decay of life and
  intensification of death".
- **The sound is never labelled in the film.** On Loznitsa's later *Babi Yar. Context*, MUBI: "The footage is real, the
  sonic backdrop isn't" (https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/can-the-archive-speak-sergei-loznitsa-and-the-sound-of-history).
  Powerlines' rule (the Hum said to be synthesized) goes further than Loznitsa, and that is the right side to be on.

**La Jetée (Marker, 1962, about 28 min).** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jet%C3%A9e
- Made almost entirely of still photographs. The one moving shot, a woman's eyes opening, is the climax.
- **Lesson.** A single break of the film's own rule is the strongest moment it has. For Powerlines that is the tilt
  from the top-down map into 3D.

**Others, from secondary sources:**
- **Austerlitz (Loznitsa, 2016).** "There are 39 shots in 94 minutes, not counting title cards"
  (https://thefilmstage.com/reviews/venice-review-austerlitz/). Fixed, long holds.
- **Shoah.** Present-day sites held in long takes against testimony.
- **13 Lakes (Benning, 2004).** Thirteen fixed shots of about ten minutes each.
- **Nostalgia for the Light (Guzmán, 2010).** Two scales of searching rhyme: the astronomers' sky and the desert's
  bones (https://avclub.com/searching-for-the-past-in-bones-and-stars-1798278357).

### 7.2 Craft writing on the short documentary

**IDA, "Does Length Matter? The Liberating Limitations of Short Films"** (Suz Curtis, 17 May 2016).
https://www.documentary.org/feature/does-length-matter-liberating-limitations-short-films
- "You really only have time to say one or two things, maximum." "Every minute and every shot counts."
- Yu: "Circle your wagons around something more representative of the whole, rather than going out and surveying
  everything."
- Argott: "the bare minimum we need to arm the viewer to understand this issue", and then "the emotional heart of it,
  the lasting feeling".

**20 Days in Mariupol (Chernov, 2023), editor Michelle Mizner.** https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/20-days-in-mariupol/
- "The film is in three act". "We put a lot of thought into these transition points between the acts". The transitions
  were "memory sequences".
- "We also would cut to some quiet moments to let the audience just process what they had seen".
- "We can't include every single day".

**NYT (above).** "10 minute-ish" videos, a question resolved "revelation by revelation". The arc is built from
revelations in the evidence, not from a literary three-act shape.

### 7.3 Intertitles and on-screen text

**Godard.** Walker Art Center, "Godard's Intertitles" (2009): https://walkerart.org/magazine/godards-intertitles2
- Masculin féminin (1966, "15 faits précis") uses "purely typographic intertitles". "Letters drop in and out to reveal
  new words, as in the the closing title, when 'Féminin' becomes 'Fin.'"
- **Vivre sa vie (1962), "un film en douze tableaux"** [secondary]. Each numbered card tells what is about to happen, so
  suspense moves from *what* to *how*. That suits a record whose ending is known.

**Adam Curtis.** "bold, multicolored Helvetica intertitles—just one of many nods to Godard" (MUBI, 2021:
https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/all-eyez-on-me-adam-curtis-s-can-t-get-you-out-of-my-head).

**Begley (above).** Exactly two text lines in the whole film: a fact at the start, the method at the end.

**FA (above).** Section titles on its pages ("Weaponising the wind", "Drift") work as chapter headings. In the films,
text mostly labels evidence: times, sources, "blurred" faces.

**Abu Hamdan (above).** Subtitles as the whole testimony, with redactions and "[Inaudible]".

## 8. Structural principles for *Powerlines*

Fifteen principles drawn from the films above. Each has an application to the current material; these are options to
weigh, not decisions.

1. **Open on one exact moment or one number, not on background.**
   - Precedents: *77sqm* opens on "5 p.m. on the 6th of April 2006". Begley's only opening text is "1,954 miles long".
     *Saydnaya* opens on a test tone.
   - Powerlines: open on the plant with one card, for example the number of lines, or the date. Background comes after
     the hook.

2. **Let the sound arrive before or without the image, and make it the record.**
   - Precedents: Air Pressure's "chronological sequencing" of 2,412 events; The Diary of a Sky's generator hum over
     images of sky only; Blockade.
   - Powerlines: the Hum's full chord heard over black, or over the units, before any line is drawn. Then each line's
     voice goes as the record cuts it. The chord is the baseline that every later silence is measured against.

3. **Pose the question once, early, and answer it in order.**
   - Precedents: Herbicidal Warfare's four questions; *77sqm*'s list of questions; the NYT's "we often start with a
     question … by the end we resolve that question."
   - Powerlines: one question in a card or line, for example how a plant with ten lines came to hang on one. Each
     chapter answers part of it.

4. **State the method and the made-ness once, early, in one plain card. Put the rest in the record.**
   - Precedents: Triple-Chaser's "'fake' images help us to search for real ones"; Grenfell's reconstructed soundscape,
     named as one; Browne moves full method to a "methodology section". Loznitsa never labels his sound, and is
     criticised for it.
   - Powerlines: one card near the start covering what is observed (passes, OSM positions, dated reports), what is drawn
     (lines, flow, zone), what is synthesized (the Hum, the 3D) and what is inferred (the bed). Sources go in
     film.json and the end credits.

5. **One visible rule, one readout.**
   - Precedent: Powers of Ten's constant rate and single dashboard; the Eameses cut their second clock.
   - Powerlines: the date stepping between reported changes is the film's clock. Keep one counter (the date, with the
     loss count beside it). Later passes over known ground can run faster.

6. **Grade every claim, and draw the uncertainty.**
   - Precedents: *77sqm*'s "not possible / does not exclude / also possible"; the Left-to-Die drift model's visible
     margin of error; CIR's "highly likely"; MIS's "some uncertainty over a shadow".
   - Powerlines: dotted lines where the reports don't say which line; "proposed, never agreed" on the zone; inferred
     bed heights said to be inferred. Keep the analyst's register, never the campaigner's "conclusive".

7. **One case in depth stands for the whole: name the microcosm.**
   - Precedents: *77sqm* ("seventy-seven square metres … a microcosm"); one spraying in Gaza; one man in a yellow
     t-shirt; IDA's "something more representative of the whole".
   - Powerlines: the crossing, two lines over the drained bed, is the microcosm of the plant's ten lines. Say so once,
     then give it the most time.

8. **Make the turn a change of axis, from across the land to down through it, or from space to time.**
   - Precedents: the Kyiv TV Tower film ("needle probes piercing through layers of historical strata", down to the
     1924 map and the filled ravines); Farocki's aerial photograph that held what no one looked for.
   - Powerlines: the dam's breach is the natural midpoint turn. The water goes, the bed and tower foundations appear,
     and under them is the Great Meadow, flooded in the 1950s and now regrowing. The film turns from a record of lines
     into a record of the ground.

9. **Ration silence: one true silence is a hinge.**
   - Precedents: Blockade "does not give us a minute of silence" except before its epilogue; Rubber Coated Steel ends
     on "[Inaudible]"; Saydnaya's measured silence.
   - Powerlines: the Hum already falls silent at each complete loss. Consider making only one of them a long, held,
     black silence (the longest, or the last), and keep the others short. Otherwise silence stops meaning anything.

10. **Few chapter cards, and let them say what happens.**
    - Precedents: Vivre sa vie's twelve tableaux that announce their outcome; Steyerl's five numbered lessons;
      Schuppli's chapters named after trial proceedings; FA's section heads ("Weaponising the wind").
    - Powerlines: three to five cards in the film's white-capitals style. They can be dated or numbered, for example
      TEN LINES, THE WATER GOES, ONE LINE. The ending is known, so let the suspense be how.

11. **Bookend: end on the element you began with, now changed.**
    - Precedents: Begley goes from sea, to the border, to sea, with one card in and one card out. Koyaanisqatsi closes
      on its opening pictograph. Powers of Ten returns to the picnic before going inward.
    - Powerlines: if it opens on the units, or on the full chord, or on the ground at night in 2021, end on the same
      thing now: one line lit, one voice, the 2026 night.

12. **Show the resolution limit once, then trust it.**
    - Precedents: Steyerl's calibration target ("Resolution determines visibility"); Begley's speed that "does map to
      the resolution of the images" and his seams left visible.
    - Powerlines: one held moment at 10 m showing that wires do not show and towers are a few pixels. That is why the
      lines are drawn from OpenStreetMap. After it, the drawing is understood.

13. **Break the film's visual rule exactly once.**
    - Precedent: La Jetée's single moving shot.
    - Powerlines: if everything else is top-down and north-up, the tilt into the 3D crossing is the one break. Don't
      preview it, and don't repeat it with a second 3D tilt elsewhere. (The south flight and the unrolled tape are then
      variants to choose between, not additions.)

14. **Point by darkening, and set two images side by side rather than narrating the change.**
    - Precedents: Browne: "we sometimes obscure a lot of the frame just to focus in on a piece of evidence". Farocki's
      soft montage: "supplements it, reevaluates it, balances it".
    - Powerlines: the dimmed plate under lit lines is this already. The night-lights triptych (2021, 2022, 2026) and the
      reservoir full against drained can be shown side by side, or as a held dissolve. Then no caption has to say
      "darker" or "drained".

15. **End on a held, present-tense image, with no summary. Give the meaning once, briefly, and the method last.**
    - Precedents: Begley "float[s] off into the Gulf" with only the method card; *77sqm* ends on one line of meaning
      ("the continuation of violence by other means") after the father's words; Cloud Studies ends on an epigraph; BBC
      Verify closes on people unable to return.
    - Powerlines: end on the last reported state (11 September 2026, one line), held with its one voice. Then at most
      one line of meaning, then the sources. The record isn't over, so the ending shouldn't pretend it is (Cloud
      Studies is "updated periodically").

**Where these principles pull against each other:**
- Principle 2 (sound first) and principle 1 (an exact moment first) can be combined: the Hum over black, then one
  dated card.
- Principle 9 (ration silence) pulls against the current rule that every complete loss is silent. The record's rule can
  stay; what changes is how long each silence is held.
- Principle 13 (one break) bears on keeping both 3D directions and the unrolled tape in the final cut.
