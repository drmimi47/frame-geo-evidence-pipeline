# Bellona's 2024 report on restarting the ZNPP, read against the film

Source: `ZNPP-restart-eng.pdf` (repo root, dropped in by the user, 44 pages). Read page by page on 5 November 2026.
Page numbers below are the report's own, which are the same as the PDF's (the cover is page 1).

## 1. What the document is

- **Title:** *The potential restart of the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant. Analysis of technical and political
  aspects.* Report, 2024 (p. 1-2).
- **Authors:** Dmitry Gorchakov and Alexander Nikitin. **Publisher:** Bellona Foundation, Vilnius (p. 2). The same
  Gorchakov wrote the Bellona piece of 2 October 2025 that the project already cites for "under 0.8 MW per core"
  (`research_notes/Understanding the ZNPP site/nuclear_safety.md`), which says it uses "data from Bellona's 2024
  restart report": this is that report.
- **Date:** 2024, with no month. The latest access date in the footnotes is 26 June 2024 and the newest events are
  of May-June 2024, so it was written in June-July 2024. Everything after mid-2024 (the 2025 month on diesel, the
  truces, the lines south, the licences, the pond below 14 m) postdates it.
- **What Bellona is:** "an international environmental NGO", founded in 1986 "as a direct action protest group",
  offices in Oslo, Brussels, Berlin and Vilnius (p. 2). Not a party to the war, but not neutral: it calls the seizure
  "a state act of nuclear terrorism" (p. 5), says "only the legal owner of the plant, Ukraine, has the right to take a
  decision about the plant's future" (p. 43), and hopes "the start of the plant does not go ahead" (p. 43). It also
  hopes "that Ukraine does not resort to methods such as bombing the nuclear power plant and its critical
  infrastructure" (p. 43). Treat it as an outside analyst: its figures are estimates (`[estimate]` in the project's
  tiers), its facts are borrowed from the sources below.
- **Purpose:** to say what Russia would have to do to restart any unit, how long it would take, and which actions to
  watch: "real actions that are taken, and not words and rumors" (p. 42).
- **Its sources (74 footnotes):** IAEA updates (155-228) and the Russian mission's communications to the IAEA
  (INFCIRC/1077, March 2023; **INFCIRC/1208, 17 May 2024**); Energoatom's pond-level posts; Russian state media and
  business press (Interfax, TASS, Izvestia, Kommersant, Rossiya-24 via Rosatom's YouTube); the WSJ; Russian ministry
  decrees and Rostekhnadzor guidance; Soviet/Ukrainian engineering literature (Protasov 2013 on the pond, Pismenetskiy
  2008 on decay heat, Andrushchenko 2010); Energoatom's 2015 environmental impact summary (the 2014 water balance);
  Greenpeace CEE (17 May 2024); ISW's front-line map (10 June 2024); imagery from Sentinel (Sentinel Hub EO Browser),
  Google Earth Pro, and commercial SkySat and Pleiades Neo bought through SkyWatch; a Red Army 1939-41 map.
- **Its images:** the cover is an illustration that looks machine-generated (a collage with a hand on a switch and
  garbled lettering): never reuse it. The satellite figures are Google Earth, SkySat and Pleiades Neo (commercial, not
  open data) except figs. 6 and 7 (Sentinel). The film can make its own versions of the useful ones from its passes.

## 2. Key facts and claims

### Restart conditions: water

- **Normal operation.** Six VVER-1000 units, "each of which generates 3000 MWt" (p. 11). Through each unit's
  condensers "up to 170,000 m³/h is pumped" (p. 12); in all "21 million m³ of water being pumped through the power
  units of the plant every day, i.e. almost half of the total volume of the cooling pond" (p. 13). The pond discharges
  "> 12000 MW of heat" at full power (fig. 2, p. 13).
- **The pond.** "Its volume is 47.0 million m³, with a water surface area of 8.2 km², average depth of 5.0 m, and
  maximum depth of 13.6 m. It is separated from the Kakhovka reservoir by a 400m-wide alluvial sand dam" (p. 14).
- **The pre-war water balance (2014).** "the cooling pond was replenished from the Kakhovka reservoir by 350 million
  m³, discharged back about 250 million m³ and evaporated about 70 million m³" (p. 14); for stable work the inflow
  "should exceed the volume of water evaporating from its surface by approximately four times" (p. 15); Table 1:
  pre-war replenishment 950,000 m³/day (p. 22).
- **Cold shutdown needs very little.** Decay heat "as of June 2024 comes to at least 6*(1+2,4) = 20 MW" for all six
  units with their pools (p. 16); at the dam's collapse "not more than 25 MW" (p. 17); hot shutdown "about 40 MW"
  (p. 17). The essential service water system: "six spray ponds of 50*120 m ... and another 6 of 37*70 m ... + two
  storage reservoirs", about 55,000 m², about 150,000 m³ (p. 17). Cold shutdown needs "750-1000 m³ of water per day"
  (p. 17; Table 1, p. 22).
- **The wells.** "By the end of September 2023, a total of 11 wells had been drilled at the plant site, mainly in the
  area of the spray ponds, providing a total of 250 m³/h or 6,000 m³/day of water" (p. 18, citing IAEA Update 185),
  enough "even when two of its units are operating in hot shutdown mode. The remaining water was directed to the
  cooling pond" (p. 18). Fig. 4 (p. 18): Pleiades Neo, 16 May 2024, the spray ponds with "estimated well locations".
- **The pond falling.** Fig. 5 (p. 19), Energoatom's monthly averages: 16.65 m (June 2023), 16.48, 16.21, 16.05,
  15.71 (Oct), 15.65, 15.59, 15.62 (Jan 2024), 15.59, 15.54, 15.45, **15.35 m (May 2024)**; "decreased by almost 1.4
  meters, from 16.67 to 15.3 meters". Rates: "almost 70 cm (an average of almost 8 mm per day)" July-September 2023,
  "30 cm (less than 2 mm per day)" over winter, "3 mm per day" April-May 2024 (p. 20). Forecast: "almost 0.8 - 1 meter
  by the end of 2024, to a level of not more than 14.5 m" (p. 20). Fig. 6 (p. 19): Sentinel, 20 June 2023 against 25
  May 2024, the pond's banks drained: "The width of the drained areas in the southern part of the pond reaches 70 m
  ... From the outer part of the dam to the north and west ... 200 m"; "the drained area may reach 1-2% of the total
  water surface area".
- **What refilling the pond needs:** "around 100,000 m³/day or over 4,000 m³/hour ... more than 10 times more
  powerful than the existing network of 11 wells" (p. 20).
- **Whether to refill it.** Against: "a possible drop in the water level in the cooling pond or even its complete
  draining may be an additional guarantee against starting the plant" (p. 20); "the water level inside is now at least
  7-8 meters higher than the water level and ground surface outside, which could contribute to increased water seepage
  through it or even the risk of washing away the dam" (p. 20-21, "may be purely theoretical"). For: a reserve if the
  wells fail; Ukraine's side warns the wells could lead to "tilting or partial collapse of the power units" (p. 21).
  Draining "may affect its existing ecosystem ... with large plants growing on the pond bottom, as is the case within
  the drained area of the Kakhovka reservoir" (p. 21). Conclusion: "replenishing the level is a necessary condition to
  start the plant units in power mode" (p. 21); direct-flow cooling from the river through the channels is
  "theoretically possible" but needs new sluices and dams "as well as the provision of a much larger water supply from
  the Dnipro" (p. 21).
- **Russia's pumping station.** Rossiya-24, 15 March 2024: "It is planned to build special pump stations for this
  purpose on the bank of the Dnipro" (p. 23). The Russian mission's note of 14 May 2024 (INFCIRC/1208): "The operating
  organization has decided to construct a pump station for the cooling pond. It is planned to provide the average flow
  rate of ZNPP cooling pond up to 5 m³/s (18 000 m³/h)" (p. 23). Bellona: of 432,000 m³/day "only around 86,000
  m³/day can compensate for evaporation" (p. 23), enough for "not more than one power unit at full capacity in the
  summer period ... Or for a maximum of two to three power units in the cold weather period" (p. 24); summed up as
  "sufficient to start one to three ZNPP power units depending on the time of year" (p. 41).
- **Where it could go.** Two places, both drawing on "the newly formed bed of the Konka River, a left tributary of the
  Dnipro" (p. 24, agreeing with Greenpeace of 17 May 2024): the old intake of the thermal plant's supply canal, a pipe
  "of around 300 meters in length", its outer end "at a height of around 10 m above sea level", "at least 5-6 meters
  beneath the reservoir's surface" before the breach and now above the water (p. 26), about 5 km from the nearest unit
  and "an easy target for attack" (p. 27); or the outer north side of the pond's dam, where the Konka's bed "runs
  against the dam" and is about 300 m wide, "just 2.5 km away from ZNPP power units" (p. 27-28). As of late spring 2024
  "no large-scale construction work of any kind could be observed" (p. 28). Scale: 5 m³/s against the Dnipro's
  "average flow rate at the river mouth was 1670 m³/s" (p. 28).

### Power lines and the grid

- Before 2022: "four 750 kV lines and six 330 kV lines" (p. 28). The 750 kV, north to south: Dniprovska "length of
  around 110 km"; Zaporizka "around 190 km"; South Donbass "over 270 km"; Kakhovka "around 150 km" (p. 28). From the
  thermal plant's 330 kV switchyard: Ferosplavna 1 and 2 "around 40 km", Zaporizhzhia, Molochansk, Melitopol,
  Kakhovska-330 (p. 29); and "two overhead lines from power units 4 and 5 and from ZNPP OSY-750" into the 330 kV yard
  (p. 29). Fig. 10 (p. 29): the plant's lines on a June 2022 Google image, 750 kV blue, 330 kV yellow, both
  switchyards marked.
- "From September 11, 2022 the plant closed down the last reactor and switched to the mode of consuming electricity
  from the grid for its own needs" (p. 30).
- "complete blackout on more than eight occasions ... forced to provide for its own needs using 20 reserve diesel
  generators" (p. 30).
- **The last two lines:** "From the end of 2022 and until mid-2024 ... it is connected to the Ukrainian energy system
  by just two lines going to the north, to the opposite bank of the Dnipro: one 750 kV line from the ZNPP OSY-750 to
  the Dneprovskaya-750 substation and one 330 kV line from the Zaporizhzhia TPP OSY-330 to the Ferrosplavnaya-1
  substation" (p. 30).
- **The only lever.** "Ukraine lacks the ability to take any steps to influence the state of the ZNPP. The only
  possibility for any influence is through the grid that supplies electricity for the needs of the ZNPP and its
  security systems" (p. 6).
- **Russia's grid.** Rosatom, 25 November 2022: to "restore the switchyard of the Zaporizhzhia TPP to create a
  reserve power source" (p. 31). INFCIRC/1077 (March 2023): faulty equipment at the Melitopol, Kakhovka and Molochansk
  330 kV substations dismantled; "over a year later the OSY ZTPP had not been connected to the Russian energy system"
  (p. 31). Enerhodar was on the Russian grid by 16 April 2024 ("An emergency power cut has occurred on a high-voltage
  line from Melitopol"), "probably by the 330 kV line through the Molochansk and Melitopol substations" (p. 31,
  Bellona's inference). Fig. 11 (p. 32): SkySat and Pleiades Neo of the thermal plant's 330 kV switchyard, 30 August
  2023, 30 October 2023, 11 March 2024, 4 May 2024: earthworks from autumn 2023, foundations and supports in March
  2024, "already installed part of new equipment in May 2024"; "evidently the line for connection with Molochansk
  and/or Melitopol" (p. 32). The IAEA has been refused access to that switchyard since 15 June 2023 (p. 32-33): "all
  works to restore this key energy hub ... are being carried out without observation by the IAEA" (p. 33).
- **Where the power could go.** "Of the four existing lines only two go to occupied territories" (p. 33: Kakhovska and
  Pivdennodonbaska). Crimea: "Only two 330 kV lines and one 220 kV line with a total capacity of no more than 1 GW"
  (p. 34); Kherson and Crimea at most 1.4 GW; "only the line from Melitopol with a capacity of no more than 300-400 MW
  ... remains more secure" (p. 34). Donbas: past South Donbass "only two 330 kV lines (maximum 600-800 MW)" toward
  Mariupol and one 330 kV toward Donetsk (300-400 MW) (p. 34). "Russia currently is unable to supply more than 300 MW
  from the Zaporizhzhia NPP to Crimea and more than 400 MW to Donbass, which is less than the full capacity of just one
  ZNPP unit" (p. 35). The occupied territories need about 2,000 MW, 1,400 made locally (p. 36): "starting more than two
  NPP units is not even theoretically worthwhile for Russia" (p. 36). Fig. 12 (p. 33): a simplified map of the plant's
  750 and 330 kV lines and substations across the occupied south, on ISW's front line of 10 June 2024.

### Fuel, licences, staff, money, timelines

- **Fuel.** Four-year cycle, "around 42 fuel assemblies out of 163" replaced a year (p. 38). In March 2022 "four of its
  units used Westinghouse fuel: unit 5 entirely, and units 1, 3 and 4 partially. Only units 2 and 6 still solely used
  Russian fuel" (p. 38). A reload takes "at least a week or even several months" and "cannot pass unnoticed by IAEA
  inspectors" (p. 39); fresh fuel may have to come from Russia, through a war zone (p. 39).
- **Licences.** Rostekhnadzor exercises since late 2023 and, in April 2024, a visit "to check the work on extending
  the operation period and personnel certification" (p. 8).
- **Staff.** Pre-war "about 11,500"; Russia told the IAEA (INFCIRC/1208) 4,500 employees and 940 applications
  (p. 10). From 1 February 2024 Ukrainian staff without a Russian contract were barred (p. 10).
- **State.** Unit 1 repairs from March 2024; "From mid-April 2024 unit 4 was put into cold shutdown mode, meaning
  that all reactors are in subcritical condition" (p. 10); "Since April 2024 all six units have been in cold shutdown"
  (p. 15); "from October 2022 to April 2024, one or even two power units ... worked in hot shutdown mode" (p. 17).
- **Politics.** Likhachev, October 2023: "we do not see any immediate prospects for starting" (p. 8); the WSJ
  (12 April 2024): Putin told Grossi a restart of some units was being considered (p. 9); after the Kaliningrad meeting
  of 28 May 2024 Grossi said a restart "is not feasible" at present (p. 9).
- **Money (for I7).** Rosatom has spent "around 20 billion rubles" and plans "over 90 billion rubles more in
  2024-2026" (p. 36); one unit would bring "about 15 billion rubles" a year at 2 rubles/kWh, fuel "about 2-3 billion
  rubles per year per unit" (p. 37); "the possible economic effects ... are incommensurate with the nuclear risks"
  (p. 37).
- **Forecast.** "not more than one unit, most likely unit 2 or 6, and not before the end of 2024" (p. 42); "in the
  next one to two years we may face a situation when the ZNPP is restarted" (p. 42). The order to watch: "the
  construction and launch of a new pump station, connection of the ZNPP to the Russian energy grid and fuel reloading
  in one of the reactors" (p. 42).

### Safety

- "The current state of the plant, when all its units are in cold shutdown mode, is the safest possible under the
  current circumstances" (p. 40).
- "the risks from the front line situated 30 kilometers away are increasingly becoming all too real" (p. 8). See the
  conflict below.
- Figs. 8 and 9 (p. 26-27) outline "trenches and firing positions" round the old intake and on the pond's dam. **Out
  of bounds for the film** (military positions; the project's rule on the trench site applies in spirit).

### The reservoir and the dam

- The reservoir fed the pond through its sand dam and sluices (p. 13-15, fig. 3); after the breach the old intake pipe
  "protrudes above the water surface" (p. 26).
- Fig. 7 (p. 25): a Red Army general-staff map of 1939-41 over a July 2023 Sentinel image: the Konka's and the
  Dnipro's old beds, "Future location for Zaporizhzhia power plants and the city of Enerhodar" on the empty steppe,
  and the two possible pumping-station places.
- Fig. 6 (p. 19) labels the bed "Drained and overgrown Kakhovka Reservoir" by 25 May 2024.
- The report says nothing about rebuilding the dam, the 12.7 m level, or the forest.

### Other shots

- **`new-lines-south`** (about 80 km Berdiansk-Mariupol, 86 km Melitopol-Henichesk). Not covered: the lines were
  built in 2025-26. Bellona **adds** why they would be needed: the grid south can take no more than about 300 MW to
  Crimea and 400 MW to Donbas, "less than the full capacity of just one ZNPP unit" (p. 35), and the first step was
  already under way in 2024 at the thermal plant's 330 kV switchyard, "evidently the line for connection with
  Molochansk and/or Melitopol" (p. 32). Bellona puts one 330 kV line at 300-400 MW (p. 34); the 86 km line is 150 kV,
  so it carries less, but there's no source for how much less: don't put a figure on it.
- **`cooling-pond`** (its `tells:`). It still has the "up to 80,000 m³/h" that the corrections table removed; Bellona
  backs the 18,000. "Fell more than 3.2 m" doesn't follow from the sourced series: 16.67 m (June 2023, Energoatom via
  Bellona) to 13.54 m (August 2025, OECD-NEA) is 3.1 m, and to 12.27 m (October 2026, HUR) 4.4 m. Bellona's fig. 6 is
  the method the shot needs, the drained banks on Sentinel against Energoatom's levels (p. 19).
- **`diesel`.** "20 reserve diesel generators" (p. 30): **agrees** with the "4 of the 20" in the ending report. Its
  "more than eight occasions" by mid-2024 (p. 30) **conflicts** slightly with the record: the IAEA's count was eight
  (the eighth on 2 December 2023, none in the first half of 2024). Keep the record's count.
- **`dniprovska-repaired`.** Bellona **agrees** that the two lines left from the end of 2022 were Dniprovska-750 and
  Ferosplavna-1 (p. 30). That independently backs the project's reading of the IAEA's "the 750 kV line" and "the 330
  kV back-up line" (CLAUDE.md). It says nothing of 2026.
- **`margin`.** Bellona's June 2024 figure, at most 1 MW per core and about 20 MW for all six units with their pools
  (p. 16), **agrees** with the 2025 "under 0.8 MW a core" (its own update). It **adds** the contrast: more than
  12,000 MW of heat into the pond at full power (fig. 2) against about 20 MW now, roughly six hundred times less.
- **Line lengths (the zoom-out).** Against the traced lengths in `network.json`: Zaporizka 750 "around 190 km" vs
  190.1 km, **agrees**; Pivdennodonbaska "over 270 km" vs 272.0 km, **agrees**; Dniprovska "around 110 km" vs
  129.0 km, **conflicts**; Kakhovska 750 "around 150 km" vs 185.2 km traced and 186 km official, **conflicts**;
  Ferosplavna "around 40 km" vs 34.3 km, **conflicts** mildly. Keep the traced lengths. Bellona dates the Kakhovska
  line to "late 2020", but its footnote reads "Unian (December 23, 2012)" (p. 34, fn. 50). The project's own check
  cites UNIAN, 23 December 2020, so the footnote's year is a typo.
- **The plant's line names.** CLAUDE.md says the unnamed ways were "named by Bellona's map of the plant's lines and
  where they end". Fig. 10 (p. 29) is very likely that map, so the project already relies on this report. Its order
  and names **agree** with the config.
- **The front line.** "the front line situated 30 kilometers away" (p. 8) **conflicts** with the project's sources,
  for which the front runs along the bed opposite the plant (`reports/Kakhovka bed nature sounds.md`). Bellona
  probably means the land front to the east. Don't use it.
- **The south lines' states (zoom-out, 3D south `others`).** Bellona infers that a 330 kV line from Melitopol fed
  Enerhodar in April 2024 (p. 31) while the thermal plant's switchyard was not yet connected to Russia's grid. A line
  drawn unlit there means *not connected to the plant*, not necessarily dead. Keep the captions to "cut" and
  "connected", as the wording rules already do.
- **Staff.** "about 11,500" before the war (p. 10), against "about 11,000" in the ending report: a small **conflict**.
  Say "about 11,000-11,500" or name the source.

## 4. Ideas for the film

1. **"Three things before a restart" (a card or a slow checklist, `if it restarts`).** Bellona's own watchlist, in
   its order: "a new pump station, connection of the ZNPP to the Russian energy grid and fuel reloading in one of the
   reactors. These are the processes that should be monitored ... and not words and rumors" (p. 42). Each line is
   text only (claimed or reported), with its state as of the record's end, from the project's sources: the pumping
   station (Russia's 2024 note; Likhachev's later claim of construction, a snippet), the wire (the lines south, the
   Melitopol-Henichesk line stalled), the fuel (units 2 and 6 licensed or applied for; four units on Westinghouse
   fuel). Nothing drawn as built. It fits the ending report's "if Russia restarts: a checklist of conditions with
   states and sources". It's also an honest beat about forecasts: Bellona's 2024 "not before the end of 2024" and
   "in the next one to two years" (p. 42) had not come true by 11 September 2026.
2. **The pond's shoreline, measured (`cooling-pond`).** Bellona's fig. 6 is a precedent and a check for the
   waterline method: Sentinel shorelines of 20 June 2023 and 25 May 2024 against Energoatom's monthly levels (fig. 5),
   with the drained bank 70 m wide in the south and 200 m on the outer north and west. With the project's own passes
   it becomes one outline per clear pass, each labelled with its date and its reported level (Energoatom 2023-24 via
   Bellona; OECD-NEA 2024-25; HUR 2026, said to be a party's). The drawn levels between reports are inferred, and the
   shot says so. Bellona's p. 21 gives the shot its rhyme: the pond's own drained banks may grow over "as is the case
   within the drained area of the Kakhovka reservoir", a small forest at the plant's own water.
3. **Before the plant (the 1941 sheet).** Fig. 7's idea, made from the project's own material: the Red Army sheet the
   bed model already uses, with the empty steppe where the plant and Enerhodar would stand and the Konka's old bed,
   dissolving to a post-breach Sentinel pass where the Konka has come back into that bed against the pond's dam. A
   caption: "Russia says it will pump from here, a channel of the river that came back" (attributed to the 2024 note,
   via Bellona). The pumping station's possible places are shown only as a region of at least about 3 km, never at
   the pipe or the dam: Bellona calls them "an easy target for attack" (p. 27), so the safety rule applies in spirit.
   No trenches (figs. 8, 9).
4. **The only lever (a line for chapter I or the margin).** "The only possibility for any influence is through the
   grid that supplies electricity for the needs of the ZNPP" (Bellona, 2024, p. 6). It says in one sentence why the
   lines are the film's subject. Quote it as Bellona's.
5. **The river is not short of water.** "5 m³/s ... the average flow rate at the river mouth was 1670 m³/s" (p. 28):
   the pumping station would take about 0.3% of the river. The reservoir was the plant's intake, not its supply. It
   could be one figure in `water-river` or `water-wells`.
6. **Heat, then and now (`margin`).** "> 12000 MW of heat" into the pond at full power (fig. 2) against about 20 MW
   for the whole plant in 2024 (p. 16): two bars, one six hundred times the other, both labelled with their dates and
   with "Bellona's estimate".
7. **Who watches the switchyard.** The thermal plant's 330 kV switchyard, where Ferosplavna-1 starts, has been rebuilt
   since autumn 2023 "without observation by the IAEA" (p. 33). A caption over the plant's area in the 3D south film
   or the zoom-out, never a pin on the works (they are repairs, so a region of at least about 3 km: the plant and the
   thermal plant together). Bellona's SkySat and Pleiades chips aren't open data; at 10 m, the project's own Sentinel-2
   passes might show the bare earthworks of 2023-24 as a derived reading.
8. **For I7, if it stays:** one number at most, for example "Rosatom says it has spent about 20 billion rubles on the
   plant and Enerhodar" (p. 36, Likhachev via Bellona).

## 5. Proposed changes to the water cards

Only two cards need changes. The other seven hold against Bellona. Their `tells:` could add Bellona 2024 as a further
source (`water-river`: Russia's own plan draws from the river, p. 23-28; `forest-or-reactor`: the same).

**`water-wells`:** fix the attribution and add what the station would buy. Proposed `card:`

    Since April 2024 the six reactors have been in cold shutdown.
    Wells on the site, about 250 cubic metres an hour,
    keep the fuel cool. They could not run a reactor.

    To run again, the plant would need the river:
    a pumping station of up to 18,000 cubic metres an hour,
    as Russia described one to the IAEA in May 2024,
    enough for one reactor in summer, Bellona estimates,
    or the reservoir back.

`tells:` add "Russia's permanent mission to the IAEA, note of 14 May 2024 (INFCIRC/1208): 'up to 5 m3/s (18 000
m3/h)', the pond's average make-up flow, quoted in Bellona 2024, p. 23; Bellona: one unit in summer, two to three in
winter (p. 24, 41); 11 wells, 250 m3/h, by September 2023 (p. 18, IAEA Update 185)." Replace "Greenpeace's ~6,000 m3 a
minute ... only as its estimate, if at all" in `needs:` with "not used: it is twice Bellona's per-unit condenser flow
(170,000 m3/h, p. 12)".

**`water-pond`:** say these are levels above sea level, not depths, and give the start. Proposed `card:`

    Without the reservoir, the plant's cooling pond is sinking.
    Its surface, in metres above sea level:
    16.65 in June 2023, when the dam was destroyed,
    14.34 in September 2024,
    12.27 in October 2026, as Ukraine's military intelligence reports it.

    At about 12 m, by the IAEA's figures, its pumps would stop.

`tells:` add "16.65 m, Energoatom's June 2023 monthly average (Bellona 2024, fig. 5, p. 19)." If a shorter card is
wanted, drop the 16.65 line and keep "above sea level". The last line stays until S1's open item (the IAEA's own
wording of the 12 m limit) is settled.

An optional line for `water-pond` or `water-wells`, the other side of the falling pond (Bellona, p. 20):

    A falling pond is also a brake: without it, no reactor can run.

It is the film's paraphrase of Bellona's "an additional guarantee against starting the plant". If used, it needs
Bellona named in the `tells:` and the source line.

Not a water card, but the same shot family: **`cooling-pond`** `tells:` should become "The pond fell from 16.67 m
(June 2023, Energoatom via Bellona) to 13.54 m (August 2025, OECD-NEA) and, by HUR's figure, 12.27 m (October 2026);
a slow fall, not a leak; Russia's planned station of up to 18,000 m3/h against about 250 m3/h from the wells." This
removes the unsourced 80,000 and the "more than 3.2 m".
