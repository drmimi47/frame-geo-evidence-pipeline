# How Ukraine's electricity system has replaced (and could replace) the ZNPP's fifth, 2022–2026

Research notes, 4 November 2026. Web sources only, checked through search and fetch. Some primary pages (VoxUkraine,
Green Deal Ukraina PDFs, Mezha) refused fetching (HTTP 403), so their figures here come from search-result
summaries and are marked as such. Ukraine has not published a full generation mix since 2023 "for security reasons"
(KSE Institute, below), so 2024–2026 shares are partial, monthly or compiled estimates.

**Scale check (for the film's wording):** the ZNPP was about a fifth of Ukraine's electricity, roughly 40% of its
nuclear output, and up to about 42 TWh a year. It was Europe's largest nuclear station by capacity (5,700 MWe net),
not "Europe's power".

## 1. Generation mix before and after (2021–2026)

### Takeaway
Ukraine generated about 156 TWh in 2021 and about 97–105 TWh a year in 2023–2024. Nuclear's *share* stayed near half
(49–54%) even without the ZNPP, because everything else fell with it. Hydro lost the Kakhovka plant and DniproHES's
machines. Thermal was largely destroyed in 2024. Renewables grew to about 11% in 2025. There is no official full
2024–2026 mix.

### Cited Findings
- **ZNPP pre-war:** about 20% of Ukraine's electricity, up to about 42,000 GWh a year, and about 40% of all
  Ukrainian NPP output; more than 1.23 trillion kWh generated by December 2021 — [NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant/); also [Anadolu, 2022: "20% of Ukraine's electricity before the war"](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/energy/nuclear/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-plant-disconnects-from-ukrainian-electricity-grid/36248)
- **ZNPP capacity:** six units of 950 MWe net, 5,700 MWe in all; Europe's largest station from the end of 1995. All six
  units have been in cold shutdown since September 2022, under occupation since March 2022 (WNA, updated 23 October 2026) — [World Nuclear Association, Ukraine profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine)
- **2021 total generation:** 156 TWh, no source split given (WNA) — [WNA profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine)
- **2023 mix (WNA):** 105 TWh in all. Nuclear 52.0 TWh (49%), coal 23.3 TWh (22%), hydro 12.4 TWh (12%), gas 11.0 TWh
  (10%), solar 4.6 TWh (4%), wind 1.0 TWh, biofuels and waste 0.7 TWh. 2023 consumption was 82.4 TWh — [WNA profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine). The EIA gives a similar nuclear figure (52 TWh) but a total of about 103 TWh — [EIA Country Analysis Brief, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- **Nuclear share 2022 vs 2023:** 55% in 2022, falling to 50.7% in 2023 (Statista and WNA, via search summary) — [ExPro search context / Statista](https://expro.com.ua/en/tidings/the-share-of-resin-the-structure-of-electricity-generation-in-march-2025-was-almost-9)
- **2024 nuclear:** 49,850 GWh, a 52% share; 15 operable reactors with 13,107 MWe, including the ZNPP's six, which
  produce nothing — [WNA Reactor Database, Ukraine](https://world-nuclear.org/nuclear-reactor-database/summary/Ukraine)
- **2024 mix (compiled estimate, not official):** about 96.9 TWh net. Nuclear about 53.6%, renewables about 16.2%,
  fossil about 30.7% — [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/electricity/ukraine-electricity/). [Measured World](https://measuredworld.com/countries/ukraine/energy) gives the same split (54% nuclear, 16% renewables).
- **2024 RES share:** almost 11% of the energy balance — [Ecopolitic](https://ecopolitic.com.ua/en/news/in-2024-the-share-of-res-in-ukraines-energy-balance-amounted-to-almost-11)
- **2025 nuclear:** the per-reactor 2025 figures in WNA's database sum to roughly 50.9 TWh. This is the search tool's
  sum, so treat it as approximate — [WNA Reactor Database](https://world-nuclear.org/nuclear-reactor-database/summary/Ukraine)
- **H1 2026 nuclear:** 26.82 TWh in January–June 2026, 0.16 TWh more than H1 2025 (Energoatom, via NV) — [NV](https://english.nv.ua/business/ukraine-s-nuclear-power-plants-increase-electricity-generation-in-2026-50628168.html)
- **2025 monthly mix (March 2025):** nuclear 52%, thermal and CHP 32%, RES almost 9% — [ExPro](https://expro.com.ua/en/tidings/the-share-of-resin-the-structure-of-electricity-generation-in-march-2025-was-almost-9)
- **2025 RES, full year (regulator NEURC):** 11% of generation. Within it: solar 78%, wind 12%, biogas and biomass 8%,
  small hydro 2% — [Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4121716-renewables-accounted-for-11-of-ukraines-electricity-generation-in-2025-regulator.html)
- **Data secrecy:** KSE Institute says updated information on the generation structure is not publicly available for
  security reasons; its latest breakdown is for 2023 — [KSE Institute, Energy sector priority projects 2025 (Feb 2026)](https://institute.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/energy_sector_priority_projects_in_ukraine_eng_2025.pdf)
- **Remaining nuclear fleet:** the three operating NPPs (Rivne, Khmelnytskyi, South Ukraine) are 7.7 GW, 50% of
  generation capacity (IEA, 2025) — [IEA, Ukraine's Energy Security: a pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **2024 capacity (compiled estimate):** 44.85 GW installed. About 29.2% nuclear, 26.4% renewables, 41.8% fossil.
  Installed is not available: much of it is damaged — [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/electricity/ukraine-electricity/)

### Inferences
- Nuclear without the ZNPP ran at about 50–52 TWh a year in 2023–2025, close to its 2023 level, and was again about
  half of a smaller total. The ZNPP's roughly 40 TWh was not replaced by another source. Total generation fell by
  about a third (156 → ~100 TWh), in line with demand (section 2).
- If the ZNPP's ~40 TWh were added back at 2025's level of demand (about 80 TWh), it alone would be close to half
  of what Ukraine consumes. That is a back-of-envelope figure, not a published one; the grid could not take it as it
  stands (section 7).

### Gaps
- No official full-year 2024 or 2025 mix by source (coal, gas, hydro, solar, wind in TWh). KSE says it is withheld.
  Ember's and Energy Map's pages were not reached in this session.
- No sourced 2021 nuclear total in this session (Energoatom's 2021 output). The ZNPP's 2021 TWh comes only from NS
  Energy's "up to 42,000 GWh".
- The 156 TWh (2021 generation) and 125.7 TWh (2021 consumption, section 2) differ in scope: exports, losses, own use.
  No source here reconciles them.

## 2. How much of the gap was closed by demand falling?

### Takeaway
Most of it. Consumption fell from 125.7 TWh (2021) to 86.1 TWh (2022) and to 80.2 TWh (2025): 45.5 TWh lost, more
than the ZNPP's yearly output. The fifth wasn't so much replaced as matched by what the war took from demand. The
problem moved from energy over a year to capacity at the winter peak: about 18 GW of peak demand against 10.5–12 GW
of working conventional capacity in the worst weeks of 2025/26.

### Cited Findings
- **Consumption (NEURC data on Energy Map, DiXi Group, 30 June 2026):** 2021 125.7 TWh; 2022 86.1 TWh (−31.5%);
  2023–2024 between 80 and 83 TWh; 2025 80.2 TWh, 36.2% below 2021 (64% of 2021) and 3.6% below 2024. Non-household
  consumption fell 41.5% (to 51.1 TWh), household 24.1% (38.3 → 29.1 TWh) — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-2025-ukraine-consumed-only-64-of-the-electricity-volume-recorded-in-2021/)
- **Causes named by DiXi (Oksana Zuieva):** destroyed energy and industrial infrastructure, lost territory,
  displacement and emigration, reduced business activity, stabilisation outages, and distributed generation lowering
  draw from the grid — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-2025-ukraine-consumed-only-64-of-the-electricity-volume-recorded-in-2021/)
- **Jan–Apr 2023:** consumption 32.4 TWh, 18.9% below 2022 and 33.5% below 2021 (48.8 TWh). Generation fell 19.4% — [Interfax-Ukraine](https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/economic/918158.html); [UNDP Energy Damage Assessment, June 2023](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2023-06/undp-ua-energy-damage-assessment.pdf)
- **OECD:** electricity demand dropped by around a third as a direct result of the war — [OECD Competition Market Study, 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/06/competition-market-study-of-ukraine-s-electricity-sector_045239a1/f28f98ed-en.pdf); the draft NECP 2025–2030 says 30–35% below 2021 — [Energy Community](https://www.energy-community.org/dam/jcr:9d144283-08ed-410b-a670-7fd15c7782f2/1_NECP_EnMachineTranslation.pdf)
- **Peak demand:** 18 GW in winter 2023/24; 16.5 GW in 2024/25 (mild weather); 18 GW or more possible in a cold
  winter (IEA, 2025) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **Dispatchable capacity:** about 38 GW before 2022; 19 GW lost in 2022; down to 12 GW after the spring 2024 attacks;
  3 GW restored before winter 2024/25; 17.6 GW expected by the Ministry of Energy going into October 2025 — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **Winter 2025/26 (Green Deal Ukraina, via search summary):** working conventional capacity 10.5–12 GW against an
  estimated peak load of 18 GW — [Green Deal Ukraina, Lessons from last winter (2026)](https://greendealukraina.org/assets/images/reports/lessons-from-last-winter-10-measures-long-version.pdf)
- **Historical peak:** about 28 GW in 2014, on consumption of 134 TWh (Wikipedia, secondary) — [Electricity in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_in_Ukraine)

### Inferences
- In energy over a year, the 45.5 TWh lost from consumption (2021 → 2025) exceeds the ZNPP's roughly 40 TWh. Without
  the war's fall in demand, Ukraine would have had to find a fifth of its supply elsewhere. With it, the remaining
  plants and imports roughly cover what is left, except at the winter peak and after each wave of strikes.
- Industry lost most (−41.5%). The ferroalloy plants beside the ZNPP are one case (section 7).

### Gaps
- No sourced pre-war (2021/22) winter peak demand in this session. A figure of about 24 GW often quoted was not
  confirmed; check Ukrenergo's data or the UNDP report's chart of the 24 December 2021 peak.
- No published breakdown of how much of the demand fall comes from lost territory, how much from displacement, and
  how much from closed industry.

## 3. The other nuclear plants and Khmelnytskyi 3 and 4

### Takeaway
Rivne, Khmelnytskyi and South Ukraine (nine units, 7.7 GW) have run near full output, about 50 TWh a year. Since
autumn 2025, Russian strikes on the substations that carry their power have forced repeated cuts, about half the
fleet's output at once on 7 February 2026. Khmelnytskyi 3 and 4 are not under way:
- **Equipment:** a law to buy the Belene VVER-1000 equipment was signed in March 2025, but Bulgaria reversed course
  in April 2025.
- **Law:** a separate law is still needed before completion can start.
- **AP1000s:** the units planned with Westinghouse are a 2030s prospect.

### Cited Findings
- **Load factors:** no plant-by-plant figures found. National nuclear output of ~49.9 TWh (2024) on ~7.7 GW in
  operation implies about 74% (my calculation from the WNA and IEA figures above, not a published figure).
- **Output cuts, 17 and 19 November 2025:** Khmelnytskyi and Rivne cut output after a substation was damaged on the
  night of 17 November. On 19 November all three plants reduced output (IAEA). Earlier damage in August had
  disconnected Khmelnytskyi and Rivne from one of their two 750 kV lines — [Ukrainska Pravda, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/11/20/8008183); [RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/three-ukrainian-nuclear-plants-reduce-output-1763642662.html)
- **Ministry of Energy explainer:** why NPPs must reduce output after attacks on the grid — [Ministry of Energy of Ukraine](https://mev.gov.ua/en/storinka/why-ukrainian-nuclear-power-plants-are-forced-reduce-output-after-russian-attacks)
- **7 February 2026:** strikes on the high-voltage substations linking all three NPPs to the grid. "We lost half the
  capacity immediately after the attack" (Ukrenergo CEO Vitaliy Zaichenko, via the Kyiv Independent); one reactor
  shut down entirely — [Switchgear Magazine, 10 Feb 2026](https://switchgear-magazine.com/tm-news/business/russian-strikes-halve-ukraines-nuclear-output/); [Ukrainska Pravda, 7 Feb 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/02/07/8019846)
- **Khmelnytskyi 3 and 4, history:** work stopped in 1990 with unit 3 75% complete and unit 4 28% complete. In 2015
  parliament repealed the 2012 law on building them. WNA still lists them as under construction (suspended) — [WNA profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine)
- **Belene purchase:** the Rada supported buying equipment from Bulgaria's Belene NPP on 11 February 2025, and
  Zelensky signed the procurement law on 13 March 2025 — [Energoatom](https://energoatom.com.ua/en/news/prezident-ukrayini-pidpisav-zakon-shhodo-zakupivli-obladnannia-dlia-dobudovi-blokiv-na-xaes); [UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/procurement-of-equipment-in-bulgaria-for-two-power-units-of-khmelnytskyi-npp-zelenskyy-signed-the-bill). Bulgaria reversed its decision in April 2025 (WNA) — [WNA profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine)
- **US fuel:** Westinghouse confirmed it can supply a compatible fuel design for the Belene V-466V reactors at
  Khmelnytskyi 3 and 4 — [Energoatom](https://energoatom.com.ua/en/news/westinghouse-oficiino-pidtverdiv-shho-bolgarski-reaktori-vver1000-z-aes-belene-iaki-planujetsia-vstanoviti-na-energoblokax-3-ta-4-xmelnickoyi-aes-zmozut-praciuvati-na-amerikanskomu-palivi); [Interfax-Ukraine](https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/economic/1046032.html). This was disputed by expert Olga Kosharna, who said TVEL fuel or a newly developed Westinghouse fuel would be needed (via search summary of the same coverage).
- **October 2025:** Energy Minister Hrynchuk said Ukraine can complete units 3 and 4 without the Bulgarian reactors.
  The government programme set an updated feasibility study for 31 October 2025 and a draft siting, design and
  construction law by the end of 2025 — [Ukrainska Pravda, 7 Oct 2025](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/10/07/8001654/)
- **April 2026 (via search summary of Mezha, fetch refused):** a separate law is still needed for completion to start.
  Zelensky said on 30 March 2026 that Ukraine is ready to keep working to obtain the two units — [Mezha, 7 Apr 2026](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/ukraine_plans_to-4/)
- **AP1000 plans:**
  - September 2021: agreement for four AP1000s.
  - November 2021: a contract for two AP1000 units at Khmelnytskyi, about $5 bn each.
  - June 2022: the plan rose to nine units.
  - January 2023: the Cabinet approved project documentation for Khmelnytskyi 5 and 6, targeting 2030–2032.
  - December 2023: an equipment purchase agreement for unit 5.
  - Source: [WNA profile](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine); see also [World Nuclear News, "Completion of Khmelnitsky 3 begins"](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Completion-of-Khmelnitsky-3-begins-in-Ukraine)

### Inferences
- Even if the Belene equipment were installed, two VVER-1000s (about 2 GW) would replace about a third of the ZNPP's
  capacity, and not before the late 2020s at best. No source gives a date for either unit to start.
- The constraint on nuclear since late 2025 is the grid, not the reactors. Output is cut when the 750/330 kV
  substations are hit, the same weakness that cut the ZNPP off (the film's own record).

### Gaps
- No 2026 confirmation that a Khmelnytskyi 3/4 completion law was adopted, nor of Bulgarian talks resuming. The
  "nine units" Westinghouse agreement of May 2026 seen in a search title was not opened or confirmed.
- No per-plant load factors (Energoatom does not seem to publish them in wartime).

## 4. Imports from the EU after synchronisation (16 March 2022)

### Takeaway
Imports are a buffer, not a replacement. Even in the record year (2024, 4.4 TWh) they were about a tenth of the
ZNPP's annual output. The firm import limit from the EU rose to 1.7 GW (summer) and 2.1 GW (winter) from December
2024, with an agreement for 2.3 GW from December 2025, and it is rarely used in full. Net imports were 4.2 TWh in 2024
and about 1.3 TWh in 2025, and Ukraine was a net exporter in summer 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **2024:** record imports of 4,436 GWh (IEA) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **Firm import capacity:** 2.1 GW in winter and 1.7 GW in summer since December 2024; export capacity raised from
  550 MW to 650 MW — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **2.3 GW:** an agreement with ENTSO-E to raise the maximum import capacity from 2.1 GW to 2.3 GW from December 2025.
  Even 2.3 GW would not meet the system's needs in full — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-november-ukraine-recorded-the-highest-electricity-imports-of-the-year/)
- **Use:** Ukrenergo's head said in October 2025 that Ukraine uses 30–50% of the import capacity; average use was 27.4%
  in November 2025 — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-november-ukraine-recorded-the-highest-electricity-imports-of-the-year/)
- **Monthly peak:** December 2025 imports of 639.5 GWh, the highest since July 2024 — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-december-ukraine-imported-the-largest-amount-of-electricity-compared-to-other-months-of-2025/). October 2025 was about 360 GWh, 2.5 times September — [Odessa Journal](https://odessa-journal.com/ukraine-increased-electricity-imports-25-times-in-october)
- **Net exporter:** June to September 2025, with September 2025 exports at 635 GWh (IEA) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **2025 (VoxUkraine, via search summary, fetch refused):** gross imports about 6.5 TWh, exports about 5.2 TWh, net
  imports 1.3 TWh, against 4.2 TWh in 2024 — [VoxUkraine](https://voxukraine.org/en/from-exports-to-imports-how-electricity-flows-between-ukraine-and-its-neighbors-have-changed)
- **Prices:** ferroalloy producers say the imported power they buy costs more than in developed European countries — [GMK Center, 24 Feb 2026](https://gmk.center/en/opinion/ferroalloy-plants-operated-at-minimum-capacity-in-2025-at-10/)

### Inferences
- At 2.1 GW running flat out all year, imports could at most bring about 18 TWh, under half the ZNPP's output. In
  practice they bring 1–4 TWh net. They matter at the peak and after strikes, not over the year.

### Gaps
- 2022 and 2023 import and export volumes (the first months after synchronisation: imports authorised from late June
  2022, exports from summer 2022, suspended after October 2022) were not sourced in this session.
- No systematic figures on import prices compared with Ukraine's day-ahead market prices.

## 5. Distributed generation (gas units, solar, batteries)

### Takeaway
About 0.8 GW of distributed gas generation was added in 2024 and about 0.76 GW in 2025, around 1.7 GW in all. Solar
grew by about 0.8 GW (2024) and 1.5 GW (2025), and 1–3 GWh of batteries were added in 2025. Together that is a few GW
of mostly daytime or peaking capacity, against the ZNPP's 5.7 GW running round the clock. It cushions the peaks; it
does not replace the plant's energy.

### Cited Findings
- **Gas, 2024:** 835 MW of distributed generation put into operation in 2024, for 967 MW in total (Ministry of
  Energy) — [ua-energy.org, 31 Dec 2024](https://ua-energy.org/en/posts/31-12-2024-812032b8-5207-4ac4-ba71-8bb818b96e8b)
- **Gas, 2025:** 762 MW of new distributed gas generation in 2025, from municipal, private and state operators — [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/ukraine-adds-762-mw-distributed-gas-generation-in-2025-with-growing-solar-projects/)
- **Solar (Solar Energy Association of Ukraine estimates):** about 800 MW in 2024 and about 1.5 GW in 2025. Estimates
  because self-consumption systems need not be registered. Cumulative solar about 8.5 GW — [pv magazine, 26 Jan 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/26/ukraine-deploys-1-5-gw-of-solar-in-2025/)
- **Batteries 2025:** more than 1 GWh (SEAU), almost 3 GWh (SolarPower Europe). The sources conflict. For 2026, SEAU
  expects at least 1.5 GW of solar and more than 3 GWh of storage — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/26/ukraine-deploys-1-5-gw-of-solar-in-2025/); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/markets/ukraine-targets-15-gw-solar-3-gwh-storage-2026)
- **Utility storage:** DTEK has 200 MW / 400 MWh across six sites (September 2025) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **Wind:** more than 700 MW in development; the Tyligulska wind farm expansion (nearly 400 MW) is due in late 2026 — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **IEA policy work on distributed solar and storage:** [IEA, Policy options to accelerate distributed solar PV in Ukraine](https://www.iea.org/reports/policy-options-to-accelerate-distributed-solar-pv-in-ukraine); [pv magazine, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/08/iea-sets-distributed-solar-storage-policy-path-for-ukraine/)

### Inferences
- 1.5 GW of solar in Ukraine yields perhaps 1.6–1.8 TWh a year, at an assumed capacity factor of about 12–14% (my
  assumption, not sourced). Matching the ZNPP's ~40 TWh with solar alone would take roughly 30 GW, plus storage. The
  "fifth" is far beyond what distributed generation has built so far.

### Gaps
- No sourced total of all distributed generation for 2023 or 2026 to date. Gas pistons and turbines are not given
  separately.
- The terms of the "energy independence" programmes for homes and businesses (grants, loans, numbers of systems)
  were not researched in this session.

## 6. Russian attacks on generation and the grid; blackouts by winter

### Takeaway
- **Capacity lost:** half the dispatchable capacity in 2022 (19 of 38 GW), then most of the thermal and much of the
  hydro in spring 2024 (Zmiivska and Trypilska destroyed, DniproHES's machines destroyed). A further wave from
  October 2025 disabled about 8.5 GW.
- **Kakhovka:** the HPP (about 335–357 MW, by source) went with the dam on 6 June 2023.
- **Blackouts:** scheduled outages every winter since November 2022. In 2025/26 the national average reached almost
  12 hours a day (8 February 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **Capacity:** about 38 GW of dispatchable capacity before 2022; 19 GW lost in 2022; 12 GW after the spring 2024
  attacks (IEA) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **Heat:** at least 18 thermal and CHP plants, more than 800 boiler houses and 354 km of heating pipes damaged by the
  end of 2024. Trypilska TPP was hit by 19 drones on 7–8 September 2025. More than 3,100 power-sector disruptions
  from March to September 2025, and almost 6,900 drones launched in September 2025 (IEA) — [IEA pre-winter assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)
- **22 March 2024:** all units of Centrenergo's Zmiivska TPP (Kharkiv region) destroyed. DniproHES was hit: the dam
  held, but its equipment was destroyed, and restoration will take years. Trypilska TPP was destroyed on 11 April
  2024, leaving Centrenergo with no generation. About 9 GW of capacity was lost across the attacks of early 2024.
  DTEK reported 80% of its capacity damaged by the end of March 2024 — [Wilson Center, Ukraine Quarterly Digest Jan–Mar 2024](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/ukraine-quarterly-digest-january-march-2024); [NV](https://english.nv.ua/nation/russians-attacked-the-destroyed-tpp-of-centrenergo-50483091.html); [Cyprus Mail / Reuters, 30 Mar 2024](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/03/30/ukraine-says-russian-drone-missile-attacks-damage-power-facilities/)
- **Kakhovka HPP:** capacity reported as 334.8–335 MW (six units), 343.2 MW nameplate plus 192 MW of regulating
  capacity, and 357 MW (Wikipedia). The brief's 351 MW is not confirmed by any source found. Rebuilding would take
  6–7 years after de-occupation (Ukrhydroenergo) — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/kakhovka-plant-6-7-year-to-rebuild); [Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3871965-kakhovka-hpp-can-be-rebuilt-in-67-years-after-deoccupation-ukrhydroenergo.html); [Kakhovka Dam, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Dam); about 5% of hydro capacity — [CEPA](https://cepa.org/?p=19583)
- **Winter 2022/23:** scheduled hourly outages introduced in November 2022. Ukrenergo announced scheduled and
  emergency shutdowns across Ukraine on 17 November 2022, with emergency outages again on 22 January 2023 — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3666297-emergency-blackouts-applied-due-to-colder-weather-in-kyiv-two-other-regions.html); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/news-feed/ukrenergo-ukraine-will-face-energy-deficit-throughout-winter)

### Inferences
- What hurts in 2024–2026 is not the ZNPP's missing energy alone but the loss of flexible thermal and hydro capacity
  that balanced nuclear. The nuclear that is left cannot follow the load, and its own lines are targets.

## 7. What the lost fifth means: regions, industry, and a possible return

### Takeaway
The ZNPP and the Zaporizhzhia TPP sat at the heart of the south-east's heavy industry: the ferroalloy plants at
Nikopol and Zaporizhzhia and the metallurgy of the Dnipro bend. The ferroalloy plants have run at minimum or stopped
since 2023. In 2025 they ran at about 10% of capacity, and they stopped on 19 January 2026 for lack of power.

The plant's return is now a negotiating item:
- **Russia:** the Russian regulator issued ten-year licences for units 1 (23 December 2025) and 2 (February 2026),
  and Russia talks of restarting units 2 and 6 first.
- **US and Ukraine:** the US floated joint operation in late 2025, and Ukraine proposed a 50/50 split of the power
  under a US–Ukraine venture.
- **Energoatom:** a restart "in its current state is technically impossible": no ultimate heat sink since the dam,
  weak grid links, lapsed equipment life.

No source found says how Ukrenergo would integrate it if it came back.

### Cited Findings
- **Ferroalloys:**
  - The ferroalloy plants have been shut since 19 January 2026, after an electricity shortage. Nikopol had already
    cut output from about 10,000 to 3,500 tons, which made production unprofitable.
  - Ferroalloys are the most energy-intensive branch of the steel industry (electric arc furnaces), with electricity
    more than 30% of production cost before the war.
  - The plants ran at about 10% of capacity in 2025.
  - Source: [GMK Center, 24 Feb 2026](https://gmk.center/en/opinion/ferroalloy-plants-operated-at-minimum-capacity-in-2025-at-10/)
- **Production fall:** Ukrainian ferroalloy producers cut output 57% y/y in 2023 — [GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/news/ukrainian-ferroalloy-producers-reduced-production-by-57-y-y-in-2023/)
- **Nikopol Ferroalloy Plant (NFP):** suspended production in November 2023 for the winter, to protect its furnaces
  against loss of power and water. It sits across the reservoir from occupied Enerhodar and is shelled daily — [GMK Center, 7 Nov 2023](https://gmk.center/en/news/nfp-suspends-the-production-of-ferroalloy-products-in-the-winter-period/); [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3783845-nikopol-ferroalloy-plant-suspends-production-due-to-russian-shelling.html)
- **Zaporizhzhia Ferroalloy Plant:** halted from March 2023 (power prices, weak ferroalloy prices), resumed in May,
  halted again in November 2023 — [Odessa Journal](https://odessa-journal.com/the-ferroalloy-plant-in-zaporizhzhia-has-resumed-production)
- **The ZNPP's last link:** the Ferosplavna line, named after the Nikopol ferroalloy substation, was the plant's last
  link to Ukraine's grid when it was cut in 2022 — [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/energy/nuclear/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-plant-disconnects-from-ukrainian-electricity-grid/36248)
- **Russian licences:**
  - Rostekhnadzor issued a ten-year operating licence for unit 1 on 23 December 2025, and one for unit 2 in February
    2026. Unit 6's application has been filed, with units 3–5 to follow in 2027.
  - A major overhaul, including the reactor vessels' metal, is planned for 2026.
  - The licences apply to the reactors in cold shutdown.
  - Ukraine's Energy Ministry calls them legally null and void.
  - Sources: [NucNet, Feb 2026](https://www.nucnet.org/news/russia-s-nuclear-regulator-issues-10-year-operating-licence-for-zaporizhzhia-2-2-5-2026); [World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/russian-regulator-issues-operating-licence-for-second-zaporizhzhia-unit); [Ukrainska Pravda, 27 Dec 2025](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/12/27/8013619)
- **Russian restart timetable (April 2025, plant director Chernichuk):**
  - Units 2 and 6 first (Russian fuel in their cores), and only after "the end of hostilities".
  - Cooling water is gone with the Kakhovka dam: using the Dnipro "is not one day or one month".
  - Lines must be restored and customers decided; the plant's output could free Rostov NPP's power for the North
    Caucasus. There is no rail line for fuel.
  - Energoatom's reply: a restart "in its current state is technically impossible" (weak grid links, exhausted fuel
    and equipment design life, "unqualified unlicensed personnel", no ultimate heat sink); safe operation requires
    ending the occupation.
  - Source: [World Nuclear News, 4 Apr 2025](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/russia-outlines-zaporizhzhia-restart-timetable-ukraine-rejects-plan)
- **Russia's grid plans:** Russia would need to build several new lines to connect the plant to its own grid
  (Greenpeace's Shaun Burnie) — [Yahoo / Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.yahoo.com/news/russia-plans-restart-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-171904468.html). A Russian official said preparatory work on a line is under way but not feasible now (Bellona, via search summary) — [Bellona](https://etc.bellona.org/?p=111684)
- **Peace talks (December 2025 – January 2026):** the US proposed joint operation by Ukraine, the US and Russia, about
  a third each with the US as chief manager. Ukraine counter-proposed a US–Ukraine venture: 50% of the electricity to
  Ukraine and the US deciding where the other 50% goes. A US official spoke of nearing a "50-50 split of the power" — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/amp/rubric-polytics/4072994-zelensky-americans-propose-znpp-be-jointly-operated-by-ukraine-us-russia.html); [United24](https://united24media.com/latest-news/zelenskyy-proposes-50-50-us-ukraine-management-of-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-in-new-peace-plan-14519); [NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/zelenskyy-puts-forward-alternative-plan-for-future-of-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-station-12-2-2025); [Reuters explainer via The Star, 27 Dec 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2025/12/27/explainer-what-lies-ahead-for-ukraine039s-contested-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)
- **IAEA:** there is a "common view" that restarting in the current military situation would be inadvisable (2025,
  via search summary) — [NEI Magazine](https://neimagazine.com/news/newsfuture-of-zaporozhye-npp-remains-unclear-9717311)
- **Off-site power in 2026:** reconnected on 13 June 2026 after repairs under an IAEA-brokered local ceasefire (Reuters,
  via U.S. News) — [U.S. News / Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-13/ukraines-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-plant-reconnected-to-grid-after-iaea-brokered-ceasefire). On 11 September 2026 the damaged 330 kV line was restored after a local ceasefire, while the 750 kV line "has been damaged for some time" (IAEA via Dutch ANVS) — [ANVS](https://english.autoriteitnvs.nl/latest/news/2026/09/11/power-supply-to-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-restored). This matches the project's record: only Ferosplavna-1 connected on 11 September 2026.

### Inferences
- The ZNPP's fifth fed a region that has itself largely lost its demand: the ferroalloy plants beside it are shut,
  and the front runs through the oblast. A returned ZNPP would, in the short run, produce far more than its
  surroundings use. It would need the 750 kV lines westward, Dniprovska among them, rebuilt and defended. That is a
  reasoned inference for the film's speculative section, not a grid operator's statement, and must be shown as such.
- Every restart path found needs cooling water that has not existed since 6 June 2023, and lines that are now on the
  front.

### Gaps
- No Ukrenergo or Energoatom statement found on how the grid would take the ZNPP back (which lines, how many units,
  how fast). No published estimate of the time to restart after a return to Ukraine.
- No data on which oblasts drew the ZNPP's output before the war. The 750 kV lines ran to Dniprovska, Zaporizka,
  Pivdennodonbaska and Kakhovska (see the project's own network config), but no source quantifies the flows.
- Reporting on a "30-day blackout" of the ZNPP in 2026 conflicts across outlets on dates; it was not reconciled here
  (the project's own record in `film.json` is the reference for the film).
