Researched 29 October 2026 (web search plus fetches of the pages marked "fetched"). The verification tags are:
- **fetched**: I read the page.

- **403/timeout**: the page would not load.

Every URL below was returned by a search tool; none was typed from memory. Where the evidence was thin I say so.

## Which 4–6 candidates are strongest? (synthesis across the questions below)

### Takeaway
Seven candidates are concrete and sourced. Each mirrors the plant at a smaller scale: one building, one household or one animal population depending on a single supply line, a generator, or a water source that the war cut.
1. The Nikopol dog shelter "Chance for Life", whose water ran out when the reservoir drained.
2. The fish of the ZNPP cooling pond, which died when the reactors went cold.
3. Enerhodar, the town that goes dark when the plant's lines are hit.
4. The Zaporizhzhia regional children's hospital, running on generators and later on solar panels.
5. Nikopol's invincibility point (пункт незламності) with three generators.
6. The ninth-floor balcony in Nikopol that faces the six reactors.
7. Nikopol under drone nets (2026).

Zaporizhzhia's underground schools are a strong institutional extra. One documentary already covers some of this ground (Bonadyha's Nikopol film, below).

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate A: the "Chance for Life" dog shelter, Nikopol (water on one line).**
  - It is the Nikopol Animal Shelter "Chance for Life" (Нікопольський притулок для тварин «Шанс на життя»), founded and run by Olena Voshun (55, a lawyer by profession, working with street dogs since 2017). It holds about 300 dogs in 130 enclosures, and she estimates about 1,000 abandoned dogs in the city — [LB.ua, Olena Struk, 21 Oct 2023, "Острів собак"](https://lb.ua/society/2023/10/21/580380_ostriv_sobak_dekotrim_lyudyam.html) (fetched)
  - The shelter has no centralized water supply. After the Kakhovka dam was destroyed it lived first on its own basin reserves and needed about 300 L a day. Residents, other cities and charities then delivered water, and staff collected rainwater. Later the city linked its water to the old Dnipro riverbed, and the shelter keeps tanks. Shelling debris has landed on the grounds, the dogs learned to run to their kennels at blasts, and her own home was hit four times — [LB.ua](https://lb.ua/society/2023/10/21/580380_ostriv_sobak_dekotrim_lyudyam.html) (fetched)

### Inferences
- **A (the shelter)** mirrors the plant most precisely at a creature's scale. A body of animals whose survival depends on one water source, which is the Kakhovka reservoir the plant also used for cooling. It goes from reserves (like the plant's diesel days) to outside deliveries to a new connection to the old riverbed. Being "next to the sewage treatment works" can be looked up on OpenStreetMap (Nikopol's wastewater plant), so the site could be placed on the satellite passes the project already has. The founder is a public-facing volunteer who publishes on Facebook, which makes consent tractable. **Strongest candidate.**
- **B (the fish)** is the cleanest "creature" mirror: cooling water without the reactors' heat. But it rests on one Ukrainian report and its environmental claim is not independently verified (no IAEA confirmation found), so it should be shown as reported, not observed.
- **C (Enerhodar)** inverts the main story: the town that housed the plant's workers depends on the same lines and substations ("Luch") as the plant. It is under occupation, so filming new footage is impossible and identifiable residents must not be shown. Use public statements from officials (Orlov) and satellite imagery. Night-light (VIIRS) would be the natural forensic layer, but the project has put that on hold.
- **D (the children's hospital)** maps directly onto the plant's diesel generators and the cooling the reactors need: dialysis and intensive care that must never lose power. It is an institution, so there are few consent problems. The solar roof can perhaps be seen in high-resolution imagery (not checked).
- **E (the invincibility point)** is a public building with a known capacity (120), its generators counted, and a named director. It is a "one-building" version of the plant's off-site power loss. It needs the Suspilne video to be found and dated.
- **F (the balcony)** is the most cinematic: a line of sight from a domestic window to six reactors, about 11–12 km. It can be placed on a map from a photograph by matching the reactor skyline. The people are private elderly individuals, so use the published photographs and quotes only, or find the building without identifying the flat.
- **G (the nets)** shows the 2026 state of the city: its streets literally wired over. It needs fresh footage, which locals post, but ethical care is needed with drivers and volunteers.
- **Splat (3D reconstruction from video) potential:** realistic only for A (if the volunteer's own walkaround video of the enclosures exists on Facebook), E (a Suspilne video report inside the point) and possibly G (street footage under the nets). None of the sources confirms an orbiting camera move, so this has to be checked by watching the videos.

### Gaps
- No orbiting or turnaround footage was confirmed for any candidate; the Facebook and Suspilne videos were not watched.
- The Suspilne invincibility-point report and the drone-on-Voshun's-house report are undated in my results (the latter returned 403).
- No street addresses or coordinates were sourced. The shelter's position is "next to the sewage treatment works" only; Kling's building is not identified.

## Hospitals, maternity wards, water stations, bakeries, schools, invincibility points on generators

### Takeaway
Institutional cases are well documented in Zaporizhzhia: the children's hospital on generators and solar, the emergency hospital's 96 kW generator, and underground schools from December 2024. Nikopol has an invincibility point on three generators, and its water came back through a new main pipeline in 2024. I found no specific Nikopol or Marhanets hospital, maternity ward or bakery story.

### Inferences
- Nikopol's water is itself a microcosm at city scale: a city and a plant that both drank from the same reservoir, both lost it on 6 June 2023, and both were put on a replacement connection. The new pipeline is a single, mappable "line", like the plant's last 750 kV line.
- An underground school is the architectural opposite of the plant: a building designed to survive the threat. That suits an architecture studio, though it is less personal.

### Gaps
- No specific Nikopol or Marhanets hospital, maternity ward, bakery or water pumping station on generators was found. It needs searches in Ukrainian on Суспільне Дніпро and the Nikopol city council's Telegram.
- No underground school in Nikopol or Marhanets was found (schools there were reported closed in 2026, per CNN above).

## Iodine distribution, drills and evacuation planning

### Takeaway
Iodine was handed out around the plant in late August 2022, and pharmacies in Nikopol had queues. Specific drill locations and footage were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Distribution:** potassium iodide tablets were issued in Ukrainian-controlled Zaporizhzhia city (about 45 km from the plant) around 26 August 2022, to be taken only if a leak occurred. Enerhodar's exiled mayor Orlov said 25,000 tablets were sent to the town from the regional reserve — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6562924), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/russia-ukraine-war-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-radiation-fears-iodine-rcna45041), [RFE/RL via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2022/08/ukraine-220827-rferl01.htm) (search summaries only)

- **Queues in Nikopol:** long queues for iodide pills after the plant was seized — [Kyiv Independent, 25 Aug 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/life-on-the-front-line-of-russias-new-nuclear-brinkmanship/) (fetched, quoting Mykyta Sayenko)
- **A drill at the plant:** Babel says an explosion drill was held at the plant at the end of June 2023 — [Babel](https://babel.ua/en/texts/96028-nikopol-is-a-city-where-alarms-are-constantly-howling-preparing-for-an-explosion-at-the-znpp-and-waiting-for-clean-water-from-volunteers-photo-report-about-those-who-stays-there) (fetched; no further detail given)

### Inferences
- A single iodine pharmacy queue, or a distribution point in a Zaporizhzhia building, could be a strong held image. Sourcing it needs agency photo archives (AP/Reuters/AFP from 26–28 Aug 2022).

### Gaps
- No primary Ukrainian State Emergency Service (ДСНС) source for the regional drills or the evacuation plan was found. Distribution addresses and footage were not found.

## Existing artistic and documentary projects (avoid duplication)

### Takeaway
One directly overlapping film exists: Anastasiia Bonadyha's 20-minute documentary on Nikopol (Ji.hlava 2025). Texty.org.ua's data-journalism piece already maps the occupation. I found no photo-essay series or exhibition dedicated to Nikopol and Enerhodar.
