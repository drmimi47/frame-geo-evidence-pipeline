## 1. What it is

**The project.** *Los Datos de la Coca: el registro del cultivo y su destrucción* (*The Data of Coca: the registry of
its cultivation and destruction*), by Plano Negativo, 2025. It is a web investigation in three parts, each with its
own media:
- **Part I, Guaviare** ("Coca Eradication, Deforestation & Cattle"): the film *El Píxel y la Parcela / The Pixel and
  the Plot*, maps, three archives and a remote-sensing study;
- **Part II, UNODC Data** ("a War of Pixels"): a paper-like essay with named authors, figures and downloadable data;
- **Part III, Falta de Luz**: an earlier film.

The page closes with Team, Showing and Press. The footer's navigation runs in reverse: "Press | Showing | Team |
P3 | P2 | P1 | Intro".

**The film.** *The Pixel and the Plot* (2025). The page says 17 min. Vimeo gives 1,044 s, about 17:24
([vimeo.com/1143207042](https://vimeo.com/1143207042), public, uploaded 3 December 2025; captions in Spanish only,
machine-made).
- Directed by Hannah Meszaros Martin.
- Script by Meszaros Martin and Camilo García.
- Animation by Camilo García and Sofia Prado.
- Andrés Jurado edited it, narrated it and did the sound design.
- Script consultation by Oscar Pedraza, Paulo Murillo Sandoval and Andrés Jurado.
- Camera by Meszaros Martin and Alejandro Jaramillo. Field producer: Gerald Bermúdez.
- The remote sensing is by Paulo Murillo Sandoval (Universidad del Tolima) and Pedro Sánchez. Jamon Van Den Hoek
  (Oregon State) co-wrote Part II.

**The companion film.** *Falta de Luz* (Hannah Meszaros Martin, 2020). The page says 23 min; Vimeo gives 1,385 s
([vimeo.com/1143150367](https://vimeo.com/1143150367), public). Edited by Manuel Correa and Meszaros Martin, sound
design by Emil Olsen, score by Mhamad Safa.

**The studio.** Plano Negativo is co-directed by Hannah Meszaros Martin, Nadia Méndez and Oscar Pedraza. One press
item calls it a lab founded in 2023 by Forensic Architecture and Colombia's Truth Commission
([hsbnoticias](https://www.hsbnoticias.com/?p=320493)). The site itself only thanks both of them, so treat that origin
as the press's account.

**Funding.** The Porticus Foundation, the Antipode Foundation, the Mellon Foundation, and UC Santa Cruz's Center for
Creative Ecologies.

**Venue.** The work was shown as the installation *Data Tierra* at the 24th Bienal de Arte Paiz, Antigua, Guatemala,
from 5 or 6 November 2025 to 15 February 2026. The two pages give different start dates.

**Press.** In January 2026, the findings in Part II were covered by:
- Noticias UNO ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mm5AUHifOZQ) and
  [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDcUHiaqgNY));
- [RTVC](https://www.rtvcnoticias.com/justicia/seguridad-y-orden/monitoreo-de-la-onu-ha-sobreestimado-cultivos-coca-hasta-un-901);
- [Semana](https://www.semana.com/nacion/articulo/ong-advirtio-presuntas-falencias-en-los-calculos-de-cultivos-ilicitos-en-el-pais/202628/);
- [El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/judicial/falta-transparencia-en-la-informacion-investigadores-cuestionan-datos-de-cultivos-de-coca/);
- [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/colombia/gobierno-dejara-medir-cocaina-cifras-onu-riesgo-respaldo-internacional-FP32861163).

The claim they reported: the UN's SIMCI coca census overstated cultivation by "93–901%" in four Amazon departments in
the early years of Plan Colombia.

**Subject.** The forced eradication of coca in Colombia's Amazon (Guaviare, Putumayo, Caquetá, Meta), mainly by aerial
herbicide spraying, and the data that records it. The project's thesis: "Data creates narratives, policies and laws.
Data clarifies, but also obstructs and obscures … Data shapes the earth."

Its central problem is a dataset that is withheld. The project asks: "How can we analyze information that is known to
be partial, delivered incompletely, and that institutions actively keep opaque?" The film works from three
fragments:
- **one FOIA release** (2018, to the US State Department), which contained a single spraying test over the Larandia
  base;
- **a PDF of 3,012 undated "centroids of fumigation lines"**, given by the Ministry of Defence (DIRAN) to the Truth
  Commission in 2020–21;
- **the DynCorp papers.**

With those fragments it reconstructs, by satellite, what became of the sprayed ground. Of the points, 8% are coca
in 2024, 58% are cattle and 34% are forest. Its conclusion: eradication belongs to a continuum of destruction that
ends in deforestation by cattle.

The site's numbers do not quite add up. It gives the 2024 counts as 247 + 1,746 + 1,019 = 3,012, which is all the
points, yet it also says no coca was detected at 974 of them.

The film has these qualities:
- **Shape.** It is a bookend, and inside it a ladder of scale: one document, then a second document, then the points,
  then one point, then the cells, then the department, then back to the document.
- **Pace.** The pace is slow and even: one voice, plain present-tense description, the first person plural ("we
  asked", "we could not").
- **Voice and images.** The narration stops for found footage and for the analytical animation, so both get time
  without a voice.
- **Hinges.** Each new scale is motivated by a limit of the last one.

**Falta de Luz (2020) for comparison**, as the page describes it. It is in four parts:
1. fragments of a DEA herbicide test in Georgia (1983), "bracketed with a black screen and the video noise that is
   slowly devouring the images";
2. US news footage of outraged farmers, "cut and disrupted" by testimony recorded in Putumayo;
3. "the black screen dissolves into another black screen", into a DEA agent's camcorder in a destroyed cocaine lab,
   where a Colombian officer asks "¿Falta de luz?" ("Need light?") and the agent says "No" as the image is lost in
   the grain (the title comes from that line);
4. an aerial sequence ending on eradicators who vanish under the canopy.

In it the archive's decay is itself a register of erasure.

## 3. How it marks data against interpretation

**Every figure says who made it.**
- Their own work is tagged "(Plano Negativo, 2025)".
- Official material is tagged by its maker and year: "(UNODC, 2011)", "(DIRAN, 2021)", "(US Department of State,
  2024)", "(El Tiempo, 2003)".

**The source's own material is shown as received.**
- Documents are presented "with their original file names" (e.g. "F-2019-00271 - Final Response+ar.pdf"), with the
  note that "no explanatory information was given".
- UNODC's figures are reproduced, and the page says when they are too low-resolution to read.

**The limits are spoken in the film, not kept in a footnote.** "We don't have a capture date"; "the points are only
points"; "it is not possible to draw the direct consequences". The essay keeps the same graded verbs: "indicates a
possible connection", "we hypothesize".

**Accuracy is published.** The deep-learning model's accuracy is given ("greater than 68% for coca plots"), against
SIMCI's claimed 89%. The comparison data can be downloaded.

**What can't be classified is left blank** ("Cells that could not be classified have no colour"), not filled.

**The method's spaces are separate.** The film carries the story and the dense method lives on the page beside it
(the archives, the UNODC essay, the data). The film never has to show every source.

## 4. Visual language (from the captioned stills; not seen in motion)

- **Satellite imagery** as stacked detection layers over Landsat 5, 7 and 8 and PlanetScope, in false colour
  (infrared, red, green).
- **A fixed colour code.** Fumigation centroids white, coca green, cattle red or peach, deforestation grey. The
  shades run in time from 2000 to 2024.
- **Pixels made 3D.** The detection pixels are extruded into voxel columns with time running downward ("by
  transforming the pixels of each image into 3D objects, land transformation over time becomes more legible"). The
  3D is the data itself, not a model of the landscape.
- **A resolution ladder.** 30 m, then 10 m, then 3 m over the same ground, with a comparison of 30 m against
  2.5 m pixels on a PlanetScope mosaic.
- **One close-up of the real thing.** A coca plant in infrared, filmed in Putumayo.
- **Documents on screen.** Scanned documents and the DIRAN letter, read out verbatim.
- **A grid of cells.** 900 m cells with one unclassified cell among the classified, as its own still.

## 5. Techniques worth borrowing, and where in *Powerlines*

**1. Open (and close) on one record, described before it is explained.**
- *In the film.* The opening spends nearly two minutes on one file: what is visible, then questions, then what it
  lacks (no date), then why this one. At the end it returns to the same file and names what it still withholds.
- *In Powerlines.* The cold open is already a bookend ("One line of ten", heard first, `cold-open` and `brief-7`).
  The rhyme is a statement, though, not a record. One option is to make the first card one dated source sentence,
  quoted as published, with its update number and date: the IAEA's or Energoatom's report of the record's last
  state, already in the record's sources. Then a line on what it names and what it doesn't. The return in the coda
  would then say what the record still lacks (§7).
- *Rules.* The text must be the source's exact words.

**2. Teach how to see, using the medium's limit as the rule.**
- *In the film.* "Nothing can be seen below a pixel" turns the sensor's limit into the film's premise. After that
  line, every map is understood as a reading.
- *In Powerlines.* The project has the same truth: at 10–30 m a wire does not show and a pylon is a few pixels, so
  the lines are drawn from OpenStreetMap, never detected. That could be a 20–25 s demonstration between the title and
  Chapter I, in place of `brief-8`'s three-line method card:
  - the 10 m Sentinel-2 pass at the crossing, zoomed until its pixels show and no tower is visible;
  - the WorldView-2 inset (`crossing-pylons.png`, 23 August 2024, Vantor via Esri), where the towers appear;
  - the OSM line drawn on, said to be drawn.
  The three words observed, drawn and inferred are then shown, not only stated.
- *Rules.* Pixels are only resampled, and each layer is credited.

**3. Make each limit the hinge to the next scale.**
- *In the film.* "The points are only points", so the film turns to one point in depth. "Not at the local level", so
  it turns to the region. The film moves forward by saying what the data can't do.
- *In Powerlines.* Each chapter turn could be one stated gap, which is also a sourced fact:
  - *I to II:* the IAEA named no line before late 2022 (the dotted stretches);
  - *II to III:* no elevation model of the drained bed exists, so the bed is inferred by the waterline method (said
    so);
  - *III to IV:* where repairs were made is shown only as a region, by choice;
  - *IV to V:* "brightness is not electricity".
  These lines sit on the chapter cards (`ch-*`) or on the shot just before each. They do the work of the method card
  across the film instead of once.

**4. Let found footage play first, and identify it after.**
- *In the film.* About 68 s of the FOIA video runs before the voice says what it is.
- *In Powerlines.* `tower-cut` already runs without words. The borrowing is to name it once, after it ends, in one
  plain line: people's own videos of the region's lines, published on YouTube 2022–2026. Name the publication years,
  not capture dates (collection rule 4).

**5. Give the analytical animation time without a voice or a caption.**
- *In the film.* The clustering runs for about 35 s with no voice.
- *In Powerlines.* Captions are already kept to holds. The lesson is to protect the unrolled run (`unrolled-run`) and
  the record's long stretches in `zo-war` from extra cards. The data is the sentence.

**6. Tag each figure with its maker.**
- *In the film.* Each figure is tagged "(Plano Negativo, 2025)" or "(UNODC, 2011)".
- *In Powerlines.* The coda's cards already attribute every claim. A short, consistent maker tag in the corner would
  help wherever the film shows someone else's figure as published. Examples: `seepage-paper` (Vyshnevskyi & Shevchuk
  2024, fig. 11) and the pond levels (Energoatom via Bellona, OECD-NEA, HUR). Use one style throughout, and the same
  for the film's own drawings ("drawn: Powerlines").

**7. Stack observations through time in 3D, as data rather than landscape (optional).**
- *In the film.* The voxel column, with time running downward.
- *In Powerlines.* The unrolled lines (`unrolled-*`) already make time a spatial axis. A literal analogue under the
  crossing: each clear pass's own water mask after 6 June 2023 stacked as a layer, the water's retreat read
  downward. It is wholly observed, unlike the inferred bed heights, and could sit beside or before `3d-south-drain`
  as the evidence the bed is built from. A prototype only; check it against the story before building.

### What not to borrow

- **The length and the slow ekphrasis.** Two minutes on one document, at 17:24 overall, would be a fifth of the
  final film's ceiling. Borrow the move (look, question, gap), compressed to 15–25 s.
- **Narration as the backbone.** The film is carried by one voice. *Powerlines* is carried by type and the Hum.
  Adding a voice would change its mode, and a voice over the Hum competes with the record. If a voice is ever tried,
  it should be one line, not a narrator.
- **Field verification at specific points.** The film shows sprayed points confirmed on the ground and by GPS
  centroid. *Powerlines* must never show where lines were damaged or repaired, or where crews went, finer than about
  3 km, and never the Dniprovska substation's place (the safety rule). Their pin-per-point grammar is exactly what is
  ruled out here.
- **Pixel classification as the headline.** Their deep-learning land cover is the core of their claim. Here frames
  and passes aren't classified, and nothing is labelled that the project can't stand behind. The water masks
  (NIR/SWIR under 3%) are as far as it goes.
- ***Falta de Luz*'s decaying image.** Video noise "devouring the images" is an effect laid on the pixels. Over
  satellite imagery it breaks the rule (pixels are only resampled or mixed, never generated or degraded). Over found
  footage it would aestheticise damage. The black-into-black dissolve, a cut with no image, is fine.
- **The campaign register of the essay's close** ("the Colombian public deserves full transparency"). The film itself
  stays measured, and *Powerlines* should keep the analyst's voice (see `Forensic film structures`, principle 6).

## 6. How it fits the existing notes

This note confirms several principles in `research_notes/Forensic film structures/notes.md` §8:
- 1, open on one exact moment;
- 4, the method once;
- 6, grade every claim;
- 7, one case in depth;
- 11, bookend.

It adds two that are not there:
- **the medium's limit as an early, shown rule** (§5.2 above);
- **the stated gap as the hinge between chapters** (§5.3).

The scale ladder is the reverse of *Powerlines*' zoom-out. *The Pixel and the Plot* climbs from one point to the
region. The zoom-out descends from the six units to 172 km and then pushes in to the crossing. Both work. The point is
to choose one direction per chapter and say why.

## 7. Proposal: two structural lessons applied

These keep to the rules: nothing generated, nothing inferred shown as observed, every drawn fact sourced in
film.json.

### A. The opening: a record, then the rule of seeing (replacing `brief-8`)

1. **Cold open (about 12 s).** Black, one line's voice of the Hum, as now. Over it, one sentence quoted as published
   from the record's source for its last state (11 September 2026), with the publisher, update and date in the
   corner. Then one line in the film's type on what the sentence leaves out. Candidates, each to be checked against
   the record's sources before use:
   - that it names a line by voltage, not by name;
   - that it gives no place.
2. **Title** (as now).
3. **`tower-cut`** (as now): the towers as people filmed them, from the ground. It ends with one identifying line,
   per §5.4.
4. **New, about 20 s: "Nothing can be seen below a pixel", made from the project's own material.** It follows the
   steps in §5.2, ending on the method in three words. It pairs with `tower-cut`: seen from the ground, then unseen
   from orbit. It reuses `gm-pylons` and the zoom-out's 10 m close-up, so nothing new is needed but a short edit and
   one card.
5. **Chapter I** (as now).

Net time is about +10 s against the current open, since `brief-8`'s 7 s card is absorbed.

### B. The ending: return to the record and name its gaps (the coda tightened)

The coda now runs the speculative `under-the-crossing`, then nine cards on the plant's future and water, then
`brief-7`. *The Pixel and the Plot* ends with about 30 s of return and about 2 minutes of silence, not with an
argument. Applied here:

1. **`under-the-crossing`** stays: speculative, said so.
2. **`water-today`** keeps the turn to the conditional. The conditional cards are cut to the two or three the story
   needs. Candidates are `point-12` (the plant tied to the dam) and `forest-or-reactor`, with the others parked, not
   deleted. That is the user's call.
3. **New, about 15 s: the return.** The cold open's sentence again, now with the whole record behind it, and two or
   three lines on what the record does not hold, each a sourced fact:
   - before late 2022 the reports don't say which lines were cut (the dotted stretches);
   - the protection zone's size was never published;
   - where the lines were cut and repaired is shown here only as regions, by choice.
   This is the film's version of "details that remain silenced in the archive". It is stated, not dramatised, and it
   turns the safety rule into content.
4. **`brief-7`**: "By September 2026, one line of ten remained." Then a held silence (the one long silence the
   forensic notes ration, principle 9), then `credits` with every source.

Together A and B make the bookend a record, opened and then accounted for. The method is shown once at the start, and
the gaps are stated at the end and at each chapter turn (§5.3). The film's argument stays where it is now: the lines
carry time.
