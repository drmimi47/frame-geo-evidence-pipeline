# Two thin lines keep six reactors safe

The occupied Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant (ZNPP) has not generated electricity since 11 September 2022. Since then its last lines have carried power the other way, **into** the plant. The amount is tiny: Energoatom gives 40–50 MW (March 2026) and 11 MW on reconnection (September 2026), about **0.1–0.3% of Ukraine's winter peak** ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html); [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/08/8052353/)). So keeping the plant cool does not take power from Ukrainians in any way that matters to the grid. What does hurt them is that the plant's **6 GW, about a fifth of Ukraine's pre-war electricity**, are gone ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/35398)). Russia's strikes on everything else have deepened that loss, and the substations that feed the plant also serve an operating nuclear plant and the people of Nikopol district.

The "meltdown" framing is right in principle and wrong in timing. After four years in cold shutdown each core gives off well under 1 MW of heat. A total blackout would start a countdown of **days to weeks**, not the hours of Fukushima ([Bellona](https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/2025-10-is-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plants-longest-blackout-a-scheme-for-russia-to-siphon-off-its-power)).

The IJES article on the Kakhovka reservoir is closed access, and its abstract does not mention the ZNPP. Its authors' two open-access 2024 papers do show the cooling pond from satellites, and neither shows or claims a leak. The verifiable record is a slow fall of **more than 3.2 m by September 2025**, which the IAEA put down to use and evaporation. No verifiable source reports seepage through the dike ([IAEA Update 312 via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/09/ukraine-250904-iaea01.htm)).

VIIRS Black Marble night lights are worth one short, labelled regional beat: the river as a border between light and dark. They are not evidence about the plant. NASA stops delivering new Suomi NPP data on 1–2 November 2026, so any series should be built on NOAA-20's VJ146 products ([NASA Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/black-marble)).

This briefing explains why, step by step, and then gives a timeline, the key numbers, a glossary, the cast, and ranked next steps.

## The plant draws a small town's load while its 6 GW absence shapes the grid

### Which way the power flows

Before the war the ZNPP pushed power out. It had six VVER-1000 pressurised-water reactors of roughly 1,000 MWe each (about 950 MWe net, 5,700 MWe in total), and in 2020 it made nearly half of Ukraine's nuclear electricity and more than a fifth of all its electricity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant)). Four 750 kV lines connected it to the grid. Two crossed what is now Ukrainian-held territory and two crossed what is now occupied ([Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/06/russia-building-power-lines-to-restart-occupied-ukrainian-nuclear-plant)). Separate 330 kV lines ran from the neighbouring thermal plant's switchyard. One of them, Ferosplavna-1, became the plant's backup.

The reversal came in three steps:
- **25 August 2022:** the plant was cut off from Ukraine's grid for the first time in its history.
- **5 September 2022:** with the last line cut, Unit 6 ran "in island mode", powering only the plant itself ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/09/5/7366161/index.amp)).
- **11 September 2022:** Energoatom shut Unit 6 down ([The Conversation, Meshkati](https://theconversation.com/cold-shutdown-reduces-risk-of-disaster-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-but-combat-around-spent-fuel-still-poses-a-threat-190516)).

Since then the 750 kV Dniprovska line and the 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 line have carried power **from** Ukraine's interconnected system **to** the plant. It runs pumps, ventilation, instruments and lighting, and keeps the safety systems ready. The project's convention of drawing the flow "toward the plant since September 2022" matches this.

### How much power the plant draws

There are three figures, and all come from parties or are IAEA-relayed:
- **40–50 MW.** Energoatom's chief Pavlo Kovtoniuk said in March 2026 that Ukraine supplies the plant with 40–50 MW daily. He added, "If you count the cost of this electricity, then these are indeed losses" ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)). This is an operator figure.
- **11 MW.** When Ferosplavna-1 was reconnected on 7 September 2026, Energoatom said "the plant's current consumption is 11 MW" ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/08/8052353/)).
- **20–22 MW.** The best independent figure comes from the month-long blackout. On 7 October 2025, eight diesel generators were supplying 20–22 MW for cooling and other safety functions ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)). This is IAEA-relayed, and it covers essential loads only.

Compare these with demand. The IEA put Ukraine's winter peak at about **18.5 GW** and its summer peak at about **12 GW** ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security-and-the-coming-winter/executive-summary)).

The researchers' own arithmetic, not a published figure:
- 40–50 MW is 0.22–0.27% of the winter peak.
- 45 MW sustained is about 1.1 GWh a day, about 0.4 TWh a year. That is roughly 0.4% of Ukraine's 2023 generation of 102.6 TWh ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)).
- At the 2025 average day-ahead price of UAH 5,292.62/MWh ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)), that is about **UAH 2.1 billion (≈ $50 million) a year**. It is real money, but not a quantity of electricity anyone would notice in their home.

No source says how the supply is billed. The two Energoatom figures were not reconciled. They may reflect falling decay heat, or the larger one may include site and town loads.

### Where the real harm to the grid comes from

The real harm is the hole the plant left. Energoatom counts **155 TWh not produced** from the occupation to 27 February 2026, and UAH 598.9 billion of foregone benefit ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)). The EIA earlier cited $5.2 billion of lost revenue ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)).

The rest of the system was then destroyed around that hole:
- In the war's first year, 19 GW were occupied, destroyed or damaged.
- After the spring 2024 strikes, available capacity fell to "just 12 GW" ([IEA PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cec49dc2-7d04-442f-92aa-54c18e6f51d6/UkrainesEnergySecurityandtheComingWinter.pdf), snippet).
- Strikes from March 2024 took out Zmiivska, Trypilska, Burshtyn and Ladyzhyn thermal plants, and more than 1 GW at DniproHES ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/35398)).
- On 7 February 2026 Russia's "worst-ever" attack on the substations linking the three operating nuclear plants cut nuclear output by about half ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraine-repairs-nuclear-substation-but-situation-remains-critical-after-mass-attack/)).
- That month the shortage reached "at times 5–6 GW" ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)).

The plant's 6 GW is the same size as that worst deficit. This is a counterfactual worth saying carefully. If the ZNPP were producing for Ukraine, it alone could roughly cover the worst winter shortfall. But no study divides Ukraine's outage-hours between "ZNPP lost" and "strike damage". The fair framing is that the plant's loss is a permanent structural hole, and the strikes are the swings that turn it into rolling blackouts.

### Where the plant and civilians share the same nodes

The plant's supply touches people directly where its lines meet substations that also serve civilians:
- **Dniprovska.** On 22 May 2026 a fire from military action at the Dniprovska 750 kV substation partly disconnected the operating **South Ukraine NPP** from external power ([Ми Україна](https://weukraine.tv/novyny/pivdennoukrajinsku-aes-chastkovo-vidkljuchili-vid-zovnishnoho-elektropostachannja-cherez-pozhezhu-na-pidstantsiji-dniprovska)). The same damage has kept the repaired Dniprovska line from reconnecting ever since ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/06/26/8041243/)).
- **Nikopolska.** On 2–3 June 2026 a drone strike on the "Nikopolska" substation tripped Ferosplavna-1 for about 20 minutes ([Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4129961-znpp-briefly-loses-external-power-after-drone-strike-on-nikopol-substation.html)).
- **Nikopol district.** On 5 August 2026, attacks left more than 55,000 subscribers there without power ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1191029.html)).
- **The ferroalloy plants.** Nikopol's ferroalloy plant, after whose load the Ferosplavna substation is named, has been shut since 19 January 2026. Electricity is over half its production cost ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/ukraines-ferroalloy-exports-nearly-halt-in-early-2026-amid-energy-crisis/), snippet).

This gives the strongest "grid and people" fact for narration. One strike can do three things at once: black out a Nikopol district, drop the occupied plant onto diesel, and remove a 750 kV path from a working Ukrainian reactor.

### What "keeping it from meltdown" means today

The physics answers the meltdown question:
- **Before the war:** a reactor at full power makes about 3,000 MW of heat.
- **One hour after a shutdown:** decay heat is still about 1–1.5% of that, about 30–45 MW. That is why Fukushima's cores melted within hours to days ([Wikipedia, Fukushima Units 4–6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_units_4,_5_and_6), snippet; textbook figures).
- **Now:** Bellona's conservative estimate is **under 0.8 MW per core** and **no more than 2.5 MW per unit including its spent-fuel pool**. With all cooling lost, the water already in the buildings would take **"at least two to three weeks"** to boil away ([Bellona, 2 Oct 2025](https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/2025-10-is-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plants-longest-blackout-a-scheme-for-russia-to-siphon-off-its-power)). This is an outside estimate.
- **The IAEA's own wording:** in a complete station blackout "it would take considerably longer before nuclear fuel could be damaged or potentially melt" than at an operating reactor ([IAEA Update 365](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-365-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine), snippet).
- **A cross-check:** the researchers' textbook Way–Wigner estimate gives about 0.2 MW per core after four years, the same order as Bellona's. The formula is known to run low at long times.

The danger is real, but it comes as a sequence, not a flash: the last line lost, then the diesel generators, then their fuel, then a slow heat-up and boil-off, most plausibly in the pools.

That sequence has been tested:
- **The diesel fleet.** The site has **20 emergency diesel generators**, three per unit plus two bunkered ones, and a legal minimum of **10 days' fuel** ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)).
- **One month on diesel,** 23 September to 23 October 2025 ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)).
- **Eighteen days on diesel,** 20 August to 7 September 2026. During it the fuel fell to the 10-day minimum, the plant ran cooling pumps on and off with two generators, and Grossi warned of a possible station blackout "within days" ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-09/article-8377/zaporizhzhia-restores-offsite-power-following-iaeabrokered-local-ceasefire/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/29/ukrainian-nuclear-power-plant-without-external-power-for-over-a-week)).
- **Raising the pools,** August 2026: operators began raising the water in all six spent-fuel pools, with the IAEA watching, to "increase the time available before water could begin to boil off" ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/water-levels-to-be-increased-in-zaporizhzhias-used-fuel-ponds)). The margin itself is the safety measure.
- **The latest reports.** By 25 September 2026 only 4 of the 20 generators were set to start automatically ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)). HUR says no diesel has been delivered since March 2026 and a prolonged blackout burns about 150 t a day ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)). Both are party-relayed.

### How the framing changes over time

The meltdown framing was much closer to true in 2022 than it is now:
- **September 2022 to April 2024:** one or two units were kept in **hot shutdown** at times, to make steam and heat for Enerhodar. A hot reactor melts faster if its cooling fails ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-2671872736)).
- **15 April 2024:** all six units reached cold shutdown together for the first time ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)).
- **A Russian restart** would bring the hours-scale margins back. Each core would go from under 1 MW to about 3,000 MW of heat.

The word "Ukrainian" in "fed in from the Ukrainian grid" is also only half the point. The physics needs power, not Ukrainian power. Russia has told the IAEA it plans to connect the plant to its own grid ([Bellona](https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/2025-10-is-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plants-longest-blackout-a-scheme-for-russia-to-siphon-off-its-power)), and it is building lines south to do so (covered below).

## The power in the lines comes from the whole Ukrainian system

### There is no source plant

The Dniprovska and Ferosplavna-1 lines do not have a source plant. Both hang off nodes of Ukraine's meshed 750/330 kV transmission system, which has been synchronised with continental Europe since 2022 and permanently since the end of 2023 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)).

In a meshed alternating-current grid, power is pooled. What enters the plant at any moment is a share of everything generating in the system. Tracing it to a particular plant would need load-flow data, and Ukrenergo has restricted its grid maps since 2022.

### What the pool is made of

You can say confidently what the pool contains:
- **Nuclear: about half or more.** Nuclear made **50.6% of Ukraine's 2023 generation** (51.9 of 102.6 TWh), coal 21%, gas 12%, hydro 10% and other renewables 6% ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)). Nuclear's share is estimated at 55–60% in 2025 ([DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/analytic/winter-outlook-2025-electricity/), snippet).
- **Three operating nuclear plants.** That nuclear share now comes from Rivne, Khmelnytskyi and South Ukraine.
- **Imports from the EU.** The import cap rose to 2.45 GW for Ukraine and Moldova from January 2026. February 2026 set a record of **1.26 TWh of imports**, 49% of it from Hungary, 19% from Romania, 18% from Slovakia and 13% from Poland ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)).
- **What remains of the rest.** Surviving thermal and hydro plants, damaged DniproHES among them, plus solar and distributed generation.

### What the two end nodes are

The 750 kV **Dniprovska substation** is a major node, so the national backbone feeds it directly. Ukrenergo's page gives:
- 750/330 kV, 1,998 MVA;
- **three 750 kV lines and six links to the 330 kV network**;
- two 1,000 MVA autotransformers.

([Ukrenergo](https://ua.energy/energo_substation/ps-750-kv-dniprovska/), snippet.) It also carries part of South Ukraine NPP's external supply, as the 22 May 2026 fire showed. Its location is given only as "more than 100 km north-west" of the plant ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/06/26/8041243/)) and "about 100 km north" ([Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/%d0%bd%d0%be%d0%b2%d0%b8%d0%bd%d0%b8/6515/russia-is-deliberately-sabotaging-electricity-supply-lines-at-zaporizhzhia-npp-as-new-greenpeace-satellite-analysis-exposes-moscows-disinformation-on-ukrainian-attacks/)). It is an active target, so the film should keep it at that level of generality.

**Ferosplavna-1** is a 330 kV line from the thermal plant's switchyard in Enerhodar to the Ferosplavna substation on the Nikopol side ([ua-energy.org](https://ua-energy.org/uk/posts/ukraina-znovu-vidnovyla-robotu-linii-zviazku-750-kv-zaes-dniprovska), snippet). That substation is a regional, industrial node, built round the ferroalloy works.

### How to say it

The accurate sentence for the film is: "The power comes from Ukraine's grid as a whole: about half of it nuclear from the three plants still running, the rest thermal, hydro, solar and imports from the EU." Proximity makes South Ukraine NPP and the Dnipro-region plants likely large contributors at the Dniprovska node. That is an inference, and the film should not draw it as an arrow from a named plant.

## The IJES article argues for rebuilding the reservoir and says nothing verifiable about a leak

### What the article is

Viktor Vyshnevskyi (National Aviation University, Kyiv) and Serhii Shevchuk (Central Geophysical Observatory, Kyiv), "The destruction of the Kakhovka dam and the future of the Kakhovske reservoir", *International Journal of Environmental Studies* 81(1): 275–288, published online 23 February 2024, with 20 references ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/00207233.2024.2320033)). It is **closed access**. Unpaywall and OpenAlex find no repository copy, and Taylor & Francis and ResearchGate refused automated reading ([Unpaywall](https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1080/00207233.2024.2320033?email=research.notes@gmail.com)). This briefing therefore cannot describe its figures, captions, data sources or body text.

### What is known from the abstract

The full abstract, reassembled from OpenAlex, reads: the dam's destruction "terminated the operation of irrigation canals, industrial and drinking water supplies across southern Ukraine". The authors "argue that it should be restored with minor changes", and that "the normal retention level and the forced level of the reservoir can be lowered", allowing for reduced water abstraction and climate change, with "some improvements … to the hydropower plant" ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1080/00207233.2024.2320033)). A ResearchGate summary, seen only as a snippet, says the paper weighs several options and calls rebuilding the identical structure "undesirable" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378447473_The_destruction_of_the_Kakhovka_dam_and_the_future_of_the_Kakhovske_reservoir)).

So the article is a hydrologists' case for **restoring the reservoir at a lower level**. That bears directly on the Great Meadow's future, covered below. Its abstract does not mention the ZNPP or its cooling pond. That does not rule out a figure of the pond inside the paper, but nothing verifiable supports one.

### How to get the full text

Ask a university library for a Taylor & Francis copy, or write to Vyshnevskyi. His address is given in his 2024 open-access papers ([JLE 2024 PDF](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/jlecol-2024-0014.pdf)).

### The pond images the student may be remembering

They are most likely from the same authors' two open-access papers in the *Journal of Landscape Ecology* 17(2), 2024:
- **The thermal paper**, "The Impact of the Kakhovka Dam Destruction on the Water Temperature…" ([PDF](https://sciendo.com/pdf/10.2478/jlecol-2024-0008)). It has Landsat 8/9 surface-temperature maps of 15 June 2019, 18 June 2023 and 5 August 2023 (Figs. 9–11), with "2 – the cooling pond of Zaporizhzhia NPP" labelled. It says that once the plants stopped, "the water temperature in the both cooling ponds approached natural conditions". There is nothing on leakage or the dike.
- **The paper on natural processes in the drained reservoir** ([PDF](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/jlecol-2024-0014.pdf)). Its Fig. 8 is a Sentinel-2 series from 5 June 2023 to 9 June 2024. In it the pond stays dark, as water, while the reservoir around it drains and turns green. The only cooling-pond drawdown its text discusses is Chornobyl's.

Two other papers by the same group may also be the source. One is the earlier, similarly titled *Water International* paper of July 2023 ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1080/02508060.2023.2247679)). The other is the open-access *Earth's Future* paper with Peter Gleick ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023EF003910)). Neither was read in full.

### The cooling pond: a slow fall, not a leak

The verifiable record shows steady decline.

**June 2023, right after the breach:**
- The pond stood at 16.67 m on 11 June 2023, "stable", according to Energoatom ([Babel](https://babel.ua/en/news/95015-skhemy-project-showed-satellite-images-of-the-tributary-to-the-cooling-pond-of-the-znpp)).
- Energoatom's then-head Petro Kotin said the pond is "separated from the Kakhovka reservoir" ([Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/zaes-kakhovske-vodoskhovyshche/32453385.html)).
- On UNOSAT's Sentinel-1 radar map of 9 June 2023 the pond appeared stable (snippet only) ([ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/map/ukraine/satellite-detected-water-extent-over-zaporizhzhia-power-plant-zaporizka-oblast-ukraine-09-june-2023-imagery-analysis-09062023-published-10062023-v1)).

**The levels since then:**
- Mid-2023: the IAEA described a fall of about 1 cm a day from use and evaporation ([IAEA Update 176](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-176-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine), snippet).
- The levels then went 15.61 m (April 2024), 14.34 m (September 2024), 14.11 m (March 2025) and 13.54 m (11 August 2025) ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)).
- In September 2025 the IAEA said the pond had dropped "just over 3.2 metres" since the dam's destruction, "almost 60 centimetres in the past three months alone" ([IAEA Update 312 via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/09/ukraine-250904-iaea01.htm)).
- In October 2026 HUR claimed **12.27 m on 12 October 2026**. This is a party claim, not verified by the IAEA ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)).

The researchers' arithmetic turns this into a rate: about 0.4 cm a day on average over 27 months, rising to about 0.65 cm a day in summer 2025. Faster loss in summer is the signature of evaporation.

**What the operator did about it.** In August 2025 the operator built an **isolation dam** across the channel that supplies service water to the main transformers. The dam holds that channel at about 14 m, "about 2 metres above the threshold at which the water pumps would no longer be able to operate", and mobile pumps fill it from the pond. The IAEA was refused access ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-team-seeks-access-to-new-dam-at-zaporizhzhia-plant)). The reactors and pools themselves are cooled by about 11 groundwater wells feeding 12 sprinkler ponds, at about 250 m³/h ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)).

**Satellite measurements of the pond.** Greenpeace's contractor, McKenzie Intelligence Services, measured the pond's surface falling from **9,599,617 m² on 3 July 2022 to 8,662,141 m² on 12 August 2025**. The discharge channel shrank by a further 63,797 m² in summer 2025. They also saw the new dam "effectively creating a smaller, separate cooling pond" ([Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/5438/new-level-of-militarization/)). This is advocacy-commissioned work, and the sensor is not named.

### Why a leak is a hypothesis and nothing more

**No verifiable source reports seepage or a breach of the dike.** Ukrainian-language searches for seepage through the dam came up empty. *(Update, NEXT.md S1: the user read the full text of the closed-access IJES article, Vyshnevskyi and Shevchuk 2024, doi 10.1080/00207233.2024.2320033, which states that the emptying "isolated the cooling pond and its water level slowly subsided through evaporation and seepage of water through the dyke; there now being a 10 m difference between the pond level and the adjacent branch of the river". So a peer-reviewed paper does claim seepage, and the earlier note that the article does not mention the pond is wrong for its full text. Read in full since (the user's screenshots of all 14 pages, research_notes/Understanding the ZNPP site/Vyshnevskyi and Shevchuk 2024 pages/): p. 284 adds "The satellite images (Figure 11) reveal many places where seepage is taking place". Figure 11 is one Sentinel-2 image of 5 July 2023 with wet patches circled at the dike's foot, four weeks after the bed drained, when it was still wet. So the seepage is inferred from one image, not measured, and the "10 m" has no date; set against SWOT, it is the pond against the river at Nikopol (about 6 m), while beside the plant the river stood about 8-9 m. No breach is reported. See NEXT.md S1.)*

There is a physical reason to keep the question open. CESBIO's Olivier Hagolle, citing France's IRSN, wrote in June 2023 that the pond's dikes "can withstand a Dnieper level of 10 m". He added that the river level near the plant would soon be "well below 10 m but the cooling pond remains full" ([CESBIO](https://www.cesbio.cnrs.fr/multitemp/?p=23522)). With water six or more metres lower outside the dike, seepage through an earth embankment is physically possible. A leak is therefore a **testable hypothesis, not a finding**. The test is described under the next steps below.

## Night lights can show a divided river but cannot show the plant

### The verdict

Black Marble is worth a short sequence, well under a minute, as regional **context**. The honest story is the two banks. A data newsroom found that through 2024 the occupied territories had not lost their lights, and some had brightened, while Ukrainian-held areas went dark. In July–August 2026 that reversed: Melitopol, Berdiansk and much of Crimea blacked out after Ukrainian drone strikes on power nodes. Texty called it "the first time since the full-scale invasion" that the occupied side was worse off ([Texty.org.ua, 4 Sep 2026](https://texty.org.ua/articles/118222/usf-disabled-267-power-nodes-in-occupied-territories-blackouts-visible-from-space/)). Texty's analysis did not mention Enerhodar or the ZNPP. The film's geography fits this story exactly: occupied Enerhodar and Kamianka-Dniprovska on the south bank, held Nikopol and Marhanets on the north, the drained reservoir between them.

What night lights **cannot** carry is any link from the plant's line cuts to a town going dark:
- **The towns are not fed by the plant.** Cities draw on the whole grid, and the plant has generated nothing since September 2022.
- **The plant is too small to resolve.** The site is about 1 × 1.5 km, two or three sensor footprints, and its light merges with the thermal plant's and Enerhodar's.
- **Most outages are missed.** Most complete losses lasted hours and fell between overpasses.
- **No one has published such a test.** Nothing was found that measures Enerhodar, Nikopol, Marhanets or the ZNPP site.

### Why darkness here does not mean a power cut

The overpass, at about **01:30 local solar time**, falls in the darkest hours of wartime policy ([NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/IntotheBlack/page3.php), snippet). By the researchers' calculation, 01:30 solar time at 34.6° E is about 01:10 Kyiv winter time and 02:10 Kyiv summer time, and 02:10 year-round on occupied Enerhodar's Moscow clocks.

**Nikopol has been under a "full blackout" regime since 13 December 2023.** Outdoor lights are off by order, and indoor lights must be masked ([ZN.ua](https://zn.ua/war/v-nikopole-vveli-rezhim-polnoho-zatmenija.html)). That makes Nikopol useless as a measure of grid supply.

NASA itself notes that darkness may reflect "a lack of power or deliberate choices to keep lights off" ([NASA EO](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tracking-night-lights-in-ukraine-150002)). The NPR and Payne Institute work found the causes, which include streetlights turned off, destroyed plants and people leaving, cannot be separated in the data ([Payne/NPR](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/?p=38041)). Night-light maps of Ukraine also circulate as fakes: VoxUkraine debunked a "2025 blackout" image ([VoxUkraine](https://voxukraine.org/en/false-a-satellite-image-of-ukraine-during-blackouts-in-2025), snippet). So every frame needs its product and date on screen.

### The technical recipe

**Product and series:**
- **NOAA-20's VJ146 series as the backbone.** Its monthly composite VJ146A3 is for trends; its daily VJ146A2 is for single nights. The series runs from January 2018.
- **Suomi NPP's VNP46 series only for history from 2012.** NASA's Black Marble page says "Delivery of new Suomi NPP data ceases on Nov. 2, 2026" ([NASA Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/black-marble)). A separate Earthdata alert gives 13:00 UTC on 1 November 2026, "subject to change" ([Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-data-product-delivery-cease-november-1-2026), snippet). Either way, that is three or four days from this briefing. What ends is the delivery of new data; nothing found says the archive will be withdrawn.

**Tile and resolution:**
- **One tile covers every town on the list:** h21v04, 40–50° N, 30–40° E. This was confirmed by a NASA CMR query of 29 October 2026, which also found VJ146A3 granules up to September 2026 ([NASA CMR](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?short_name=VJ146A3&version=2&point=34.6,47.5&sort_key=-start_date&page_size=2)).
- **Grid:** 15 arc-seconds, about 464 m north–south by 313 m east–west at this latitude.
- **What the sensor really sees:** a footprint of about 742 m, so lights less than about 1 km apart blur together ([Black Marble User Guide C2.0](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-05/BlackMarbleUserGuide_Collection2.0.pdf)).

**Quality filters:**
- **Layer:** use `DNB_BRDF-Corrected_NTL` with `Mandatory_Quality_Flag = 0` and a clear cloud mask. Use the snow-free layers, and compare within one view-angle class (near-nadir), because dense city cores look brighter at nadir ([User Guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-05/BlackMarbleUserGuide_Collection2.0.pdf)).
- **Never use the `Gap_Filled` layer.** It carries forward the last good value, up to 254 days old. A cloudy night after a blackout can show the lights from before it. It also counts as "filled" under the project's rule against filled pixels.
- **Avoid June and July.** At 47.5° N the 01:30 sun sits 102–108° below the zenith from about late May to mid-July, which Collection 2 flags as poor quality. Treat June–July monthly composites as weak, and never build a claim on a June–July night.
- **Consequence for the dam:** the night of the dam's destruction (6 June 2023) falls inside that window, and the overpass came before the breach. **VIIRS will not show the dam's destruction.** Sentinel-2 already does.

**Volume and tools:**
- Monthly A3 from January 2021 to September 2026 is about 5 GB. Daily A2 is about 50 GB per satellite.
- The World Bank's `blackmarblepy` downloads, mosaics and georeferences the tiles, and needs a free NASA Earthdata personal access token ([GitHub](https://github.com/worldbank/blackmarblepy)). In this project the user adds that token to `.env` themselves; it is never printed or committed.

**Scale on screen.** The 172 km frame of the zoom-out is the natural scale. Black Marble is about 370 pixels across it, upscaled about 5× to a coarse but honest grid of light, resampled only, never sharpened. The 25 km crossing frame would be only about 54 pixels across, which is too coarse.

### The cheapest useful test

Daily VJ146A2 or VNP46A2 data for Enerhodar around 6–7 September 2022 is the cheapest useful test. On those days the town lost electricity and water, and the "Luch" substation was hit ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/09/07/7366411/)). Expect a null result. "At this resolution the plant cannot be told apart from its town" is itself an honest line.

### A sharper alternative

China's SDGSAT-1 Glimmer Imager (10 m panchromatic, 40 m colour) is free to registered scientists, with a quota of 20 images ([CAS](https://english.cas.cn/newsroom/news/202209/t20220923_320728.shtml), snippet). Whether it has scenes over Enerhodar and Nikopol, and its licence terms for a film, are unchecked.

### On-screen wording

> "Night lights, NASA Black Marble (NOAA-20 VIIRS), monthly composite, [month year], about 01:30 local. Darkness can mean a cut, a curfew, lights switched off for safety, or people gone; the satellite cannot tell which."

For Nikopol, add:

> "Since December 2023 Nikopol's outdoor lights are off by order."

## How to say it accurately

These sentences are safe, confident and sourced. Each claim to avoid sits beside the reason and the better phrasing.

| Avoid | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Without Ukrainian power the plant would melt down within hours." | Four years into cold shutdown, decay heat is under 1 MW per core. Bellona estimates at least two to three weeks with no cooling at all, and the IAEA says "considerably longer" than at an operating reactor. | "Outside power is the plant's first line of defence. If every layer failed, nothing would happen in hours; a countdown of days to weeks would begin, most dangerously in the spent-fuel pools." |
| "Ukraine keeps the plant alive at a cost to its people." | The draw is 11–50 MW (Energoatom), about 0.1–0.3% of peak. | "Ukraine pays a small, continuous cost to keep someone else's seized reactors cool. What its people lost is the plant's 6 GW, more than a fifth of their electricity." |
| "The lines carry the plant's power." | Since 11 September 2022 they carry power in. | "Lines built to carry gigawatts out now carry a few tens of megawatts in." |
| "The cooling pond is leaking." | No verifiable source reports seepage. The fall is attributed to use and evaporation. *(Update: the IJES article's full text attributes it to evaporation and seepage through the dyke; see the update under "Why a leak is a hypothesis".)* | "The pond has fallen more than 3.2 m since the dam was destroyed (IAEA, September 2025). Whether any water seeps through its dike has not been shown." |
| "The IJES article shows the pond leaking." | The article is closed access, and its abstract does not mention the pond. *(Update: its full text, read by the user, says the pond subsided "through evaporation and seepage of water through the dyke", with a 10 m difference to the river; inferred from one Sentinel-2 image of 5 July 2023, its Figure 11, not measured; NEXT.md S1.)* | "The same hydrologists' 2024 satellite figures show the pond full and cooling while the reservoir drained around it." |
| "The cities went dark because of the plant." | Darkness has many causes, and Nikopol's lights are off by order. | "On this bank the lights went out. On that bank they stayed on, until 2026 (Texty). The satellite cannot say why." |
| "A meltdown would set off a catastrophic chain reaction." | This phrase is in the fetched text of a 2022 *Conversation* article ([Meshkati](https://theconversation.com/cold-shutdown-reduces-risk-of-disaster-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-but-combat-around-spent-fuel-still-poses-a-threat-190516)). It is wrong: melted fuel does not cause a nuclear explosion. | "Damaged fuel could release radioactivity. It cannot explode like a bomb." |
| "Russia withdrew from Protocol I." | In 2019 Russia revoked only its Article 90 declaration accepting the Fact-Finding Commission ([Swiss depositary notification](https://www.dfae.admin.ch/dam/eda/fr/documents/aussenpolitik/voelkerrecht/genevenotifications/191030-GENEVE_e.pdf), snippet). | "Russia and Ukraine are both bound by Additional Protocol I, whose Article 56 protects nuclear power stations." |
| "This is Fukushima again." | Fukushima's reactors lost power within an hour of running at full power. | "At Fukushima the margin was hours. Here it is weeks, and it shrinks each time the diesel runs low." |
| "Dniprovska was destroyed." | The line was repaired in June 2026. A damaged substation keeps it from reconnecting. | "Dniprovska was repaired but cannot be switched back on." |
| "26 blackouts." | Counts differ by source and date, and "blackout" blurs a loss of off-site power with a station blackout. | "26 complete losses of off-site power by August 2026 (the project's count from IAEA updates); the plant fell back on diesel each time." |
| "The IAEA zone" (as if it exists). | It was proposed on 6 September 2022 and never agreed. | "A protection zone was proposed and never agreed. No size was ever published." |

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cold shutdown** | The reactor is subcritical and its coolant is at atmospheric pressure and below about 93 °C (the NRC definition) ([Meshkati](https://theconversation.com/cold-shutdown-reduces-risk-of-disaster-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-but-combat-around-spent-fuel-still-poses-a-threat-190516)). |
| **Hot shutdown** | Subcritical, but kept hot and pressurised. Used at the ZNPP until April 2024 to make steam and heat for Enerhodar. |
| **Decay (residual) heat** | Heat from the radioactive decay of fission products after fission stops. About 6–7% of full power at shutdown, about 1% after hours, a tiny fraction after years. |
| **Loss of off-site power** | Every grid line lost; the diesel generators take over. |
| **Station blackout** | Off-site power and the diesels both lost, leaving only batteries. |
| **Defence in depth** | Independent layers of safety (lines, diesels, mobile pumps, water inventory, containment), so no single failure causes harm. |
| **Seven pillars (2022) and five principles (2023)** | The IAEA's wartime safety frame. Pillar 4 and principle 3 are about off-site power, so the film's two lines are one of the IAEA's own named conditions ([NEI Magazine](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/iaea-chief-unveils-vital-guidelines-to-prevent-nuclear-accident-at-zaporizhia-npp-10905039/)). |
| **Cooling pond / sprinkler ponds / wells** | The large pond beside the plant; the 12 small spray ponds that now cool the reactors and pools; the groundwater wells that fill them. |
| **Meshed AC grid** | An interconnected network in which power is pooled and cannot be traced to a single plant. |
| **Autotransformer** | The large transformer linking the 750 kV and 330 kV networks at a substation such as Dniprovska. |
| **Article 56** | The rule of Additional Protocol I protecting "works and installations containing dangerous forces" (dams, dykes, nuclear generating stations). Article 56(5) asks parties to avoid placing military objectives nearby ([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/escalating-military-use-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)). |
| **DNB / Black Marble** | VIIRS's Day/Night Band, and NASA's corrected night-light products made from it. |
| **Waterline method** | The project's way of inferring heights by recording where the water's edge was on each clear satellite pass, at the level reported that day. |

## What to look into next, ranked by payoff for the film

These are ordered by how much new, sourced material each would add for the effort, given tools the student already has.

1. **The cooling pond as a measured series.** Run the project's Sentinel-2 waterline method on the pond, the intake channel behind the 2025 dam and the discharge channel, as three separate polygons, on every clear pass from 2022 to 2026.
   - **Checks:** test the result against Greenpeace and McKenzie's three dated areas and the IAEA's 3.2 m.
   - **Why it works:** at 10 m the pond is about 96,000 pixels, and summer 2025's loss of about 105,000 m² is about 1,050 of them, which is easily detectable. The pond's shallow margins make area a sensitive proxy for level, so an area-to-level curve is feasible.
   - **The same passes can test the leak hypothesis.** Compare moisture and vegetation (NIR/SWIR) on the drained bed just outside the north and west dikes with bed farther away. A persistent wet or green plume that grows as the pond falls would be a finding. Its absence would also be worth saying.
   - **Outcome:** this is the one place the film can produce evidence rather than quote it.

2. **The IAEA's updates as a dataset, set against Russia's own figures.** More than 360 numbered updates hold counts of losses, ceasefires, wells, pond levels and diesel days. Russia files its own figures as IAEA information circulars, for example pond levels of 15.4 m in May 2024 ([INFCIRC/1227](https://iaea.org/sites/default/files/publications/documents/infcircs/2024/infcirc1227_rus.pdf), snippet). Two official accounts filed with one agency make a natural split screen.
   - **Reconcile the loss count first.** The project, WNA and NEA agree on 26 by August 2026, but Greenpeace says 14 since February while NEA says 14 since April, and a 27th was reported and never confirmed.

3. **Point 12 of the peace plan, which could reflood the Great Meadow.** The December 2025 draft joins "control over the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant and the restoration of the Kakhovka Hydroelectric Power Plant" in one point ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/zelensky-reveals-20-point-peace-plan-draft-backed-by-ukraine-us/)).
   - **The two sides of the argument.** The IJES authors argue for restoring the reservoir at a lower level. Ecologists call the regrown willow forest the largest floodplain forest in Europe and oppose rebuilding ([Yale e360](https://e360.yale.edu/features/ukraine-war-wilding)).
   - **The legal hold.** A March 2024 law bars construction on the former reservoir until five years after martial law ends ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir)).
   - **Why it matters to the film.** A restart would also need the water back: 250 versus 80,000 m³/h. So the studio site appears in the peace plan as well as in the war. This is the film's strongest forward-looking turn.

4. **Article 56, said precisely.** Both states are bound by Additional Protocol I. Tom Dannenbaum argues that using the site as a firing base breaches Articles 56(5) and 58 and could amount to the ICC war crime of using human shields. This is an argument, not a ruling ([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/escalating-military-use-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)).
   - **Institutional condemnation:** IAEA Board resolutions and the July 2024 UNGA vote.
   - **The legal limit:** a protected zone needs both parties' agreement under Article 60. That is why the IAEA's zone was never agreed.
   - **The quiet parallel:** the ICC's energy-grid warrants ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/icc-arrest-warrants-shoigu-gerasimov-rcna158758), snippet) fit a film told through lines.

5. **The Nikopolska versus Ferosplavna naming question.** The project's OpenStreetMap trace ends Л-243/Л-244 (Ferosplavna 1 and 2) at the Ferosplavna-330 substation. The great-meadow corridor then reaches a separate Nikopolska-330, where Л-211 turns east. But Energoatom and the IAEA named the "Nikopolska substation" as the strike that tripped Ferosplavna-1 on 2–3 June 2026 ([Eastern Herald, citing the IAEA](https://easternherald.com/2026/06/03/znpp-power-outage-17th-time-nikopolska-drone-iaea/)).
   - **Possible explanations:** different naming conventions, a strike further along the system, or a reporting error.
   - **What to do:** settle it against Ukrenergo's naming before captioning the 17th loss.

6. **Greenpeace and McKenzie's Dniprovska tower.** Their October 2025 analysis found a damaged Dniprovska tower about 1.9 km north-east of Unit 6 still upright, with no craters ([Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/4460/the-750-kv-power-line-at-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant-shows-no-signs-of-major-damage-new-satellite-investigation-by-greenpeace/)). Their September 2026 analysis found no craters supporting Russia's claims about the Ferosplavna switchyard.
   - **Check:** both against the corridor's saved Overpass towers and the project's own passes.
   - **Treat:** as advocacy-commissioned imagery, and never mark damaged towers.

7. **The other direction of the lines.** Russia's new lines (about 80 km between Berdiansk and Mariupol, and about 86 km between Melitopol and Henichesk) are the plant's possible future outputs ([Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/novyny/6346/high-resolution-satellite-imagery-reveals-russias-latest-doomed-attempt-to-connect-the-occupied-zaporizhzhia-npp-to-the-temporary-occupied-and-russian-grid/)). Tracing them in OSM, if mapped, would let the zoom-out show the plant pulled between two unsynchronised grids.

8. **Licences as territory.** Ukraine's licence for Unit 1 ran to 23 December 2025, and Russia's licence for Unit 1 was issued that month. A short "paper sequence" could follow the plant's legal identity: Soviet, then Ukrainian, then "federal property", then a Rostekhnadzor licence.

9. **One clean night-light test.** Check whether a flag-0, clear, low-moon night exists near 6–7 September 2022 for Enerhodar. Then decide whether the two-banks sequence earns its time.

## Conclusion

Put together, the research reverses the film's starting intuition in a useful way. The lines matter not because they carry much power, since they carry almost none, but because they are the plant's **first layer of safety**, and the IAEA names off-site power as one of its own conditions for a safe plant. What is at stake is time, not energy: weeks of margin in cold shutdown, eroded by diesel running out, fewer staff, and a pond falling about half a centimetre a day. A restart would collapse that margin back to hours. That gives the film a precise, defensible tension. The quiet, nearly empty lines are the thinnest point of a safety margin, not a lifeline draining the country.

The same research shows that the Great Meadow is not a backdrop to the plant but its counterweight in the political future. The peace draft ties the plant's control to rebuilding the dam. The hydrologists behind the IJES article want the reservoir back, lower. A restart needs the water back. And the willow forest now growing on the bed exists only because the water is gone. The film's most honest and original contribution will be measurements made with its own tools: the pond's shoreline series, a tested and probably null leak hypothesis, and a sourced record of the lines. Each will say plainly what the imagery cannot show.
