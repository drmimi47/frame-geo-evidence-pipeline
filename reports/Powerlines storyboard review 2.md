# Powerlines storyboard review 2

5 November 2026. The cut `storyboard` as saved in `libraries/conductive-seam/storyboard/storyboard.blend`: 41 shots,
8:26 to the byline, 9:05 in all. I looked at one to three frames rendered from every shot, every card's text and
timing, which shots carry sound, and where each shot sits in its source film. The findings are ranked by how much
each would change the film. Length isn't counted against anything here (the cut is still a prototype), but
pacing within a shot is.

The first 1:46 and the flight are the film's spine and hold up. The weak parts are the stretches between them:
cards and stills that tell rather than show, and a run of silence through most of the second half.

## 1. Silence already means something in this film, and the cards break it

In the Hum's grammar, silence means every line is lost (the complete losses; the diesel sound was removed so that
silence would carry it). In the storyboard, every card and every still is silent: 23 of 41 shots. Running from
5:57 to 7:35, that is **98 seconds of silence in one stretch** (nl-monthly-chart through water-choice), and it falls
at the very time the record says one line is still lit. Viewers who have learned in the first two minutes that
silence means a blackout will read the water chapter as one.

**Move:** keep the Hum running under every card and figure, at the state of the date being spoken of. After the
flight that means a single voice: Ferosplavna-1 alone. Save true silence for the losses. Under the water chapter,
the bed's recordings (the birds and the frog already credited) can come in under that one voice, so the sound
moves from the line to the land before the forest scene. For the storyboard, a placeholder will do: the 3D's
end-state hum laid under the silent stretches on a sound channel.

## 2. The night lights were cut out of the zoom-out, where they already sat in date order

The zoom-out film was rendered with its night holds in place: chord 40–44.2 s, **night 2021 44.2–52**, the war
52–68.4, **night 2022 (5 September) 68.4–76.2**, the zone (6 September) 76.3–81.1. The cut takes these out and
regroups them at 5:24 as one study, so time now jumps from 11 September 2026 back to 2021, then to 2022. It also
shows 2022 twice (zo-night-2022, then zo-night-2022.001 under the overlay: 11 s of the same night).

The last review grouped them on purpose. There is a new reason to undo it: the flight now ends on 11 September
2026, the very date zo-night-after opens on. Each night can now sit at its own date:

- **2021:** straight after zo-chord, as the source film has it. The full chord, then the region lit at night.
- **2022:** zo-war → front-2022 (3 September) → zo-night-2022 with nl-sep2022 over it (5 September) → zo-zone
  (6 September). The dates run in order, and this restores the first 81 s of the zoom-out (known good to about a
  minute) almost unbroken. Drop zo-night-2022.001, which only repeated it.
- **2026:** zo-night-after straight after 3d-south-plant. The flight fades on 11 September 2026, and the zoom-out
  picks up at the same date at the crossing and pulls back to the dark region. This is the film's own "one line of
  ten", seen from above, and it is the only place left where that count lands at full scale.
- nl-monthly-chart follows the 2026 night as its summary, or is parked. It still lacks a source on screen for why
  each place is dark.

The cost: the night lights stop being a chapter of their own. The gain: time runs forward through the whole
record, and one repeated shot goes.

## 3. The cards are read faster than they can be read

At about 3 words a second (after the 0.8 s fade-in and 0.6 s fade-out), a card reads comfortably. Most run faster:

|---|---|---|---|
| brief-2 | 40 | 7 s | **7.1** |
| brief-4 | 25 | 6 s | **5.4** |
| brief-5 | 24 | 6 s | **5.2** |
| water-choice | 57 | 14 s | 4.5 |
| brief-3 | 16 | 5 s | 4.4 |
| water-cooling | 58 | 15 s | 4.3 |
| forest-or-reactor | 40 | 11 s | 4.2 |
| water-dam | 47 | 13 s | 4.1 |
| the-gap | 21 | 8 s | 3.2 |
| one-fifth | 14 | 6 s | 3.0 |
| end-card-b, end-final | 11, 10 | 7, 5 s | 2.0, 2.8 |

Twelve cards take 103 s, a fifth of the film before the byline. The film is meant to carry graphics and cinematography
as much as words, so the fix is fewer words on fewer cards, not longer cards.

**Move:** turn six cards into captions over the pictures they describe (`caption`, at the foot of a shot), and cut
the words to what the picture doesn't already say:

| Card | Over | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| one-fifth | zo-night-2021 | "more than a fifth of Ukraine's electricity", over the region lit by it |
| brief-3 | fog-crackle's last seconds | "What they bring is time.", over the line singing |
| the-gap | repair-regions | the crews, over where they were sent |
| water-cooling (the pond's level only) | section-block | the pond is what that shot opens up |
| forest-or-reactor | under-the-crossing | the forest, over the forest |
| water-dam | waterline-reprise | the 12.7 m proposal, over the 12.7 m line drawn |

That leaves six cards: brief-2, brief-4, brief-5, water-choice, end-card-b, end-final. Cut brief-2 to about 20
words; its second sentence repeats what share and brief-3 then show.

## 4. Lines said twice

- **"The choice has not been made."** (the end of water-choice, 7:21) and **"Whether the water comes back has not
  been decided yet."** (end-final, 8:21) say the same thing a minute apart, and the first takes the force out of the
  last words. Cut the line from water-choice.
- **Shutdown and cooling** are said twice: by brief-2 at 1:46 ("shut down, but their fuel must still be cooled")
  and again by water-cooling at 6:03 ("cold shutdown. Wells on the site keep the fuel cool"). Keep only the new
  facts in water-cooling: the pond sinking, and the 12 m at which its pumps would stop.
- **12.27 m and 12.7 m** come within a minute of each other (the pond's level in October 2026, the proposed level
  of the water). Read once off a card, they look like the same number. Drop the 12.27 (say "falling toward 12 m"),
  or round it on screen to "about 12.3 m" and keep the exact figure in the film's sources.
- **section-block** opens on 6 September 2022, the first of section-dniprovska's three dates. Keep it as the way
  into the region, but trim it so the date isn't read twice.

## 5. The ending: let the pictures carry it

The current order is forest (30 s) → the 12.7 m line drawn (9 s) → "The towers stood in water. / Now they stand in
a forest." → "Whether the water comes back…". The card about the forest comes after the picture has moved on to the
water's return, so it steps backward.

**Move:**

1. **under-the-crossing**, with the forest caption: grown since June 2023, willows up to 9 m.
2. **end-card-b:** "The towers stood in water. / Now they stand in a forest."
3. **waterline-reprise**, held longer, with the proposal as its caption (12.7 m, enough to run both plants again).
   The two voices follow as captions: the ecologist's and the village head's.
4. **end-final:** "Whether the water comes back / has not been decided yet."

That runs picture, statement, the same picture with the alternative drawn in, then the open question. water-choice
as a full card then becomes optional: its engineers' and ecologists' options can go to the film's notes or be
parked.

Two cautions for the speculative scene:

- **It must say what it is on screen.** It is a modelled scene, not imagery, and a caption has to say "a modelled
  view, not filmed". At the moment only the storyboard's label says "speculative", and that label isn't in the film.
- **Vasyliuk should be asked before any screening.** His recordings are under CC BY, so credit is enough by the
  licence, but this is already in the shot's needs.

## 6. The flight: pacing inside it

The flight is one unbroken 2:32 (2:31–5:04), and that is right. Cutting into it for the truces would break the
film's best sequence. Two things inside it:

- **The record stops at 4:11 and the date holds on 11 September 2026 for the last 52.5 s** (3d-south-plant: the
  curve to the units, the rise, the fade). This is the longest stretch in the film where nothing new is told. It
  works if the plant's arrival is a held moment on purpose. It sags if it is just the camera finishing its path.
  Either give the plant a caption at its closest point (the six units in cold shutdown, on one line), or bring the
  rise in sooner.
- **The truces fall inside the flight but are told after it.** brief-5 ("Since October 2025 the war has paused seven
  times") comes at 5:04, after the flight has carried the date through those months to September 2026. Name the
  truces in the flight as the date passes them, as the unrolled film does ("TRUCE 3 · NOVEMBER 2025"): small, dated,
  like the 3D's other captions. brief-5 and the-gap can then shrink to one card.

## 7. One meaning per mark

The film is building a visual vocabulary, and a few marks have started meaning two things:

- **Hatching** means "no good-quality value" in the night lights (5:52), and "the reservoir on 5 June 2023" in the
  repair map 30 s earlier (5:18). The unrolled film, now parked, uses it for the truces. Pick one meaning. My
  suggestion is a faint flat lighter grey for the old water, keeping hatching for "no data".
- **front-2022's occupied area is solid saturated red**, the strongest colour in the first act. Red was taken off
  the cut lines because it clashed with the towers' paint and read as alarm. A darker, desaturated red at low
  opacity, or the same red as an outline only, would match the repair map's outline language. DeepStateMAP's
  credit and licence on screen are still open as well.
- **The repair map is the only figure on a mid-grey ground, with six hues used nowhere else.** That is fine for a
  figure that stands alone. To tie it in, the sections (section-dniprovska) could reuse its colour for the same
  stretch: the pink of "the crossing" where the section crosses the river.

## 8. Smaller things seen in the frames

- **tower-cut** opens on a small clip on black (its first footage isn't full frame). Decide whether the opening
  montage is framed on purpose, as windows on black, or full frame, and keep to it.
- **share** and **nl-monthly-chart** are dense at their lengths (7 s and 6 s). share's headline reads, but its
  labels don't. nl-monthly-chart's lines aren't named in the frame.
- **seepage-paper** reads as a slide: a journal figure with small text. If it stays, zoom into the part the claim
  rests on (the dike and the seepage) and name the paper in a caption.
- **brief-3 and brief-4** are back to back (11 s of cards). This is solved if brief-3 becomes fog-crackle's caption
  (section 3).
- **The card animation** (fade and 12 px rise) is the same on every card, the end cards included. For
  end-card-b and end-final, a slower fade with no rise would let the last words settle.
