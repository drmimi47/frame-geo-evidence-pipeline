## 4. Other evidence on the cooling pond's level, water loss and the dam in its channel

### Takeaway
The verifiable record is a slow, steady fall in the pond's level, not a leak or breach:
- Stable at about 16.6–16.7 m in June 2023.
- About 1 cm/day by mid-2023, which the IAEA attributed to use and evaporation.
- Down "just over 3.2 metres" by September 2025, including about 60 cm in June–August 2025.
- Ukrainian intelligence claimed 12.27 m on 12 October 2026 (not verified by the IAEA).

The operator built an isolation dam in the cooling-water channel in August 2025 to hold that channel at about 14 m, and fills it with mobile pumps. Safety cooling comes from groundwater wells (about 11) feeding the sprinkler ponds. Greenpeace's contractor measured the water area falling from 9.60 km² (July 2022) to 8.66 km² (August 2025).

## 5. Seepage through the dike, how the level is held, and where to look on Sentinel-2

### Takeaway
No source I found reports seepage or a breach through the dike between the pond and the drained reservoir. Officially the pond is a separate basin (Kotin), "designed to stay above the level of the Kakhovka reservoir" (WNA). Its dikes, per IRSN via CESBIO, withstand an outside level down to 10 m.

Losses are attributed to use and evaporation (IAEA, 2023). The level was formerly topped up from the Zaporizhzhia TPP's discharge ("hot") channel, which itself drew on the reservoir. Since the reservoir drained there is no gravity supply, so the level falls. The operator now protects one channel with an isolation dam and mobile pumps.

The visible changes in satellite imagery are:
- the supply ("tributary") channel shallowing (Skhemy, June 2023);
- the discharge channel's water area shrinking (Greenpeace/MIS, 2025);
- the new dam across the intake channel (from August 2025);
- the pond's margins exposed (SPF, October 2023).

### Cited Findings
- Separation and design: [Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/zaes-kakhovske-vodoskhovyshche/32453385.html); [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy); [CESBIO/IRSN](https://www.cesbio.cnrs.fr/multitemp/?p=23522)
- Losses "use and evaporation", about 1 cm/day, mid-2023 (IAEA; search summary): [IAEA Update 176](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-176-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- Kotin allowed that "якщо буде йти вода з цього ставка-охолоджувача" (if water goes out of this pond) it would need topping up. This is a hypothetical about evaporation and use, not a report of leakage. — [Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/zaes-kakhovske-vodoskhovyshche/32453385.html)
- Ukrainian-language searches for "фільтрація через дамбу" (seepage through the dike) at the ZNPP pond found nothing. — [search context: Ekonomichna Pravda, 3.2 m](https://epravda.com.ua/biznes/naslidki-ruynuvannya-kahovskoji-grebli-riven-vodi-u-stavku-oholodzhuvachi-na-zaes-vpav-na-3-2-metra-811248/)
- Where to look: the pond is the enclosed basin on the south (left) bank at Enerhodar, west of the town, with the six units on its south-east side. In the JLE 2024 Sentinel-2 Fig. 8 panels it shows as a dark rectangle-like basin on the south shore. The thermal plant's discharge channel and the plant's intake (cooling-water supply) channel are its connections. Greenpeace places bunkers by the "sluice gates" and on the pond's "western and northern banks". — [JLE 2024](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/jlecol-2024-0014.pdf); [Greenpeace](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/5438/new-level-of-militarization/)

### Inferences
- Seepage through an earth dike is physically possible when the outside water falls 6+ m below the pond. But it is not claimed in any source I could verify. A "leak" in a satellite image would need, for example, a persistent wet or green plume on the drained bed outside the north dike, growing while the pond falls. Treat it as unproven.
- If the student wants to test it, the place to check is the strip of drained bed immediately north and west of the pond's dikes. The test would compare NIR/SWIR moisture or vegetation outside the dike with the bed further away, on several clear passes after June 2023. This is the same moisture approach the project already uses for wet channels.
- The coordinates should be taken from the project's own OpenStreetMap data (the pond's water polygon and the plant's site), not from this note. I did not verify exact coordinates of the dam or channels.

### Inferences
- **What the resolution can detect.** At 10 m, the pond's about 9.6 km² is about 96,000 pixels.
  - Sentinel-2 at 10 m: the 2025 summer loss of about 105,000 m² is about 1,050 pixels, clearly detectable.
  - Landsat at 30 m: the same loss is about 117 pixels, detectable but sensitive to shoreline mixed pixels.
- **What the area loss implies.** About 0.94 km² of area lost for about 3.2 m of level means the margins are gently sloping shallows. Area is therefore a sensitive proxy for level, and a hypsometric curve (area against reported level) is feasible.
- **Method choices.** Use the same NIR/SWIR < 3% or MNDWI threshold on every pass, and mask the units, the TPP and the channels separately. Report the pond, the intake channel (behind the 2025 dam) and the discharge channel as separate polygons, as Greenpeace did.
- **Dates to match.** Greenpeace's 3 July 2022 and 12 August 2025 should be checked against Sentinel-2 acquisitions over tile 36TWT/36TXT. That tile ID is my assumption, to be checked in STAC. Exact-date matches would suggest MIS used Sentinel-2; otherwise they likely used commercial imagery.
