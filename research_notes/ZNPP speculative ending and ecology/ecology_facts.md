# Ecology of the drained Kakhovka reservoir bed, the Great Meadow (Velykyi Luh), the ZNPP cooling pond and the power-line crossings, 2023–2026

Research notes, compiled 4 November 2026. Scope: the former reservoir from Zaporizhzhia to Nova Kakhovka, with emphasis on the reach by Enerhodar / Nikopol / Marhanets where the ZNPP's 750 kV line (to Dniprovska) and the thermal plant's 330 kV line (to Ferosplavna) cross the drained bed.

Source-quality conventions used below:
- **[Peer-reviewed]**: a journal paper (abstract or press release read; full text not always accessed).
- **[Scientist, media]**: a named scientist quoted in a news piece; not a published method.
- **[Ukrainian state / operator]**: Energoatom, HUR, State Ecological Inspectorate etc.; a party to the war, often not independently verified.
- **[Occupation authority]**: Russian-installed officials; claims, unverified.
- **[Unverified / local]**: residents, local news, wiki pages.

Several pages (Science news, Suspilne, ResearchGate, Rubryka, Ukrainska Pravda Life, UAnimals) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read in full; where findings come only from search-result summaries of them, that is noted.

## 1. The new willow/poplar forest on the drained bed: species, growth, height, area, remote sensing, the national park debate, invasive species

### Takeaway
Within weeks of the dam's destruction (6 June 2023) the bed seeded itself with native white willow (Salix alba, and the hybrid S. × rubens) and black poplar (Populus nigra); by autumn 2026 trees reach 7 m generally and 9 m at measured plots, and the most careful satellite estimate (Sentinel-2, Pichura et al. 2025) is ~48,000 ha of woody cover within ~135,000 ha of overgrown bed; media figures range wildly (33,000 to 150,000 ha) and should not be quoted without the method. Invasive species were feared but have not dominated; the reservoir rebuild vs. "Velykyi Luh" restoration debate is live, with ecologists against refilling and residents and the occupation administration for it.

### Cited Findings

**Timing and why willows won**
- The dam was destroyed 6 June 2023; poplar and willow shed seed in late May–early June, so the drained mud was seeded at exactly the right moment. Mulenko (Khortytsia National Reserve) said a breach in March or August would have been far worse and let more invasive species in. [Scientist, media] — [Kyiv Independent, 7 Jun 2024](https://kyivindependent.com/deer-and-boar-could-roam-forests-in-the-kakhovka-reservoir-in-five-years/); [Irish Times, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/11/21/to-flood-this-place-again-would-be-a-crime-ukraines-great-meadow-springs-back-to-life-after-destruction-of-dam)
- Mulenko: "It could have been a desert. But that's not what happened." Seeds carried by wind, birds and animals; willows ~4 m tall on ground that had been under 3 m of water. [Scientist, media] — [Irish Times, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/11/21/to-flood-this-place-again-would-be-a-crime-ukraines-great-meadow-springs-back-to-life-after-destruction-of-dam)
- UNCG (Vasyliuk et al.) named white willow (Salix alba) as the leading seedling species at Kamianska Sich; vegetation began recovering about a month after drainage; the Oskil reservoir (Kharkiv oblast, drained early 2022) was the comparison case. — [UNCG, "It is time for Velykyi Luh to revive", 22 Aug 2023](https://uncg.org.ua/en/it-is-time-for-velykyi-luh-to-revive/)
- Species measured at the Askania-Nova plots in 2026: reddish willow (Salix × rubens) and black poplar (Populus nigra). — [Kherson Online, 12 Oct 2026 (citing Askania-Nova Biosphere Reserve's Facebook)](https://khersonline.net/dovkillya/verby-na-dni-kakhovskoho-vodoskhovyshcha-syahnuly-9-metriv/)
- The Revelator reports the forest is "mainly white willow (Salix alba)"; Vasyliuk hypothesised that organic matter from dead animals and molluscs fertilised it (not yet studied). — [The Revelator, 12 Nov 2024](https://therevelator.org/new-forest-ukraine/)

**Growth and heights (a time series a film could plot)**
- June 2023: willow seedlings with 2–4 leaves (max 6), 2–3 cm tall (max 10 cm). 19 October 2023: over 2 m, tallest above 2.5 m, record 3.08 m (≈4.5 months of growth). Species on Moisiienko's study plots: 11 in June 2023 → 69 in October 2023. [Scientist, media] — [Vgoru, interview with Ivan Moisiienko, 6 Jan 2024](https://vgoru.org/analitika/ekocid-yak-naslidok-pidrivu-kahovskoyi-ges-ivan-mojsiyenko-pro-ekologichni-vtrati-i-prognozi-yaki-ne-spravdilis)
- Three weeks after the breach, willow seedlings under 1 cm; ~3 m by October 2023; some willows and poplars ~7 m in 2025 (Anna Kuzemko, botanist). — [Hmarochos, 3 Sep 2025](https://hmarochos.kiev.ua/2025/09/03/bilshist-prognoziv-ne-spravdylysya-vcheni-kazhut-shho-na-misczi-kolyshnogo-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-vidnovylys-rusla-rik-i-zyavyvsya-oseter/)
- Mulenko: willows 1.5 m after ~4 months, "twice the normal growth rate". — [Irish Times, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/11/21/to-flood-this-place-again-would-be-a-crime-ukraines-great-meadow-springs-back-to-life-after-destruction-of-dam)
- October 2023 thickets to 2 m, some trees >3 m; a willow reached 4.7 m in under a year; spring 2024 willow growth on the left bank up ~30% on the previous year; Academician Yakiv Didukh: willows here grow about twice as fast as elsewhere. — [UWEC Work Group (Viktoria Hubareva), 4 Jul 2024](https://uwecworkgroup.info/one-year-after-the-terrorist-attack-at-kakhovka-hydropower-plant-1b-trees-instead-of-desert-and-willow-forests-unique-to-the-continent/)
- June 2024: saplings up to 5 m, about 20 plants per m²; the Academy of Sciences noted at least 100 species of herbs and mosses. — [Kyiv Independent, 7 Jun 2024](https://kyivindependent.com/deer-and-boar-could-roam-forests-in-the-kakhovka-reservoir-in-five-years/)
- 2026: tallest trees ~7 m, trunks about as thick as an adult's wrist; some trees died in winter 2023, none newly in 2024 or 2025; tree density fell by at most half; ~400 plant species (Vasyliuk via TSN). — [Texty, 28 May 2026](https://texty.org.ua/fragments/117544/na-misci-kolyshnoho-kaxovskoho-vodosxovyshcha-vyris-unikalnyj-dykyj-lis-foto/)
- Institute of Botany (NAS Ukraine): mean tree height over 5 m, individual willows 7 m and more (search summary of the source). — [Bukinfo](https://bukinfo.com.ua/ekologiya/vysota-derev-perevyshchuye-p-yat-metriv-a-okremi-verby-vyrosly-bilsh-nizh-na-sim-na-misci-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshcha-vyris-naybilshyy-molodyy-lis-yevropy); [Kavun.city](https://kavun.city/articles/434034/do-kolishnogo-kahovskogo-vodoshovischa-povenulis-osetri-virosli-topoli-j-verbi)
- **October 2026: willows measured at 9 m**, nearly 2 m more than the year before; the team (Ivan Moisiienko, Nadiia Skobel, Oleksii Vasyliuk for fauna; Mykhailo Mulenko and Iryna Mikhina of Khortytsia reserve) measured height, diameter, stand density, biomass increment and branching, plus relevés. Parts of previously accessible plots have become dangerous. — [Kherson Online, 12 Oct 2026](https://khersonline.net/dovkillya/verby-na-dni-kakhovskoho-vodoskhovyshcha-syahnuly-9-metriv/)
- Some 2026 media say "up to 10 m" (search summary, not verified). — [Ukrainska Pravda Life (403, summary only)](https://life.pravda.com.ua/society/yak-zminivsya-velikiy-lug-pislya-pidrivu-kahovskoji-ges-317748/)
- Moisiienko (Oct 2026): species on the bed 343 in 2025, at least 400 now; the forests, three years old, already have tree, shrub, herb and moss layers; six protected plant species recorded, among them Carex secalina (Red Book of Ukraine) (search summary); first oak seedlings found by Mulenko. Water bodies cover ~12% of the bed. — [Donetskoblenergo repost of Ukrinform/UNIAN interview, Oct 2026](https://donetskoblenergo.dn.ua/2026/10/14/kabani-povernylisia-ie-oleni-hto-jive-y-lisi-na-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovisha-naykovec/)

## 2. The historic Great Meadow before flooding (1955–1958) and what is returning

### Takeaway
Velykyi Luh was a 100+ km mosaic of channels, reed lakes, marshes, meadows, floodplain forest and dunes between the Dnipro and the Konka, the heartland of the Zaporozhian Cossacks (six of eight Sichs), flooded in stages from 1953–1958; what is returning is a willow-poplar floodplain forest and old channels (Kamianka river, Oriliv island), not the old meadow itself, and scientists say so.

### Cited Findings
- Over 100 km east–west and 20–25 km north–south; a mix of channels, reed-covered lakes and marshes, meadows, floodplain forests, shrubland and high sand dunes; >250,000 ha lost under the reservoir including floodplains, Cossack sites, kurgans, villages, forests. — [Texty, 23 Jan 2024](https://texty.org.ua/projects/111574/karta-velykoho-luhu-pyat-sichej-stavka-monholskoho-hana-ta-inshi-cikavi-miscya/)
- Six of the eight Zaporozhian Sichs lay on the Great Meadow (Tomakivka, Bazavluk, Chortomlyk, Mykytyn Rih, Kamianka, Nova Sich/Pidpilnenska); Sirko's winter grange Hrushivskyi Kut flooded in the 1950s; Tomakivka partly flooded in 1953; Oriliv island vanished in 1954 and is re-emerging since 2023. — [Texty, 23 Jan 2024](https://texty.org.ua/projects/111574/karta-velykoho-luhu-pyat-sichej-stavka-monholskoho-hana-ta-inshi-cikavi-miscya/)
- Trees named historically: age-old oaks at Tomakivka, tall pears, willow thickets (верболози) round the Ivanivski dunes, willow cut for wicker at Velyki Hyrla; oak predominated in the Hylaea forests downstream; perch, pike and bream in the lakes by Zolota Balka; animals drowned in the flooding and some, including wolves, were shot. — [Texty, 23 Jan 2024](https://texty.org.ua/projects/111574/karta-velykoho-luhu-pyat-sichej-stavka-monholskoho-hana-ta-inshi-cikavi-miscya/)
- Historic fauna listed: wolves, deer, roe deer, foxes, hares, badgers, beavers, otters, wild horses in autumn (Tomakivskyi island); fish: sturgeon, sterlet, beluga, catfish, pike, carp. Floodplains >400 km², ~110 km from Khortytsia to Skalozubova island, 3–25 km wide; endemic Dnipro birch survives on islands and dunes; flooded 1955–1957 (the page also says 1958). [Unverified / wiki; cites V. P. Suprunenko] — [uk.wikipedia: Великий Луг](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%9B%D1%83%D0%B3)
- ~80,000 ha between the Dnipro and the Konka, called "Kinski plavni" or Velykyi Luh (search summary). — [Zbruc](https://zbruc.eu/node/115613)
- Flooded 1955–1958; ~90 villages and ~37,000 people affected (historians' hypothesis); 6,730 ha of gardens flooded in 1957; reserve proposals for the site from the 1920s; sturgeon and predatory fish disappeared after filling; Kherson fish catch fell from 9,000 t (1956) to 1,300 t (1966); local endemic cornflowers Centaurea appendicata and C. konkae at risk. — [UNCG, 22 Aug 2023](https://uncg.org.ua/en/it-is-time-for-velykyi-luh-to-revive/)
- Great Meadow ~100,000 ha before flooding; Soviet approval of the HPP 20 Sept 1950. — [EOS, 17 Oct 2025](https://eos.com/blog/life-after-kakhovka-dam-destruction-a-new-beginning/)
- By the 1950s only ~20% of the area remained forest after collectivisation (search summary of European Wilderness Society). — [European Wilderness Society](https://wilderness-society.org/from-destruction-to-renewal-the-ecological-revival-of-velykyi-luh-after-the-kakhovka-disaster/)

## 3. Fauna: fish kills, birds, mammals, insects, reptiles, Red Book species, mines and war damage

### Takeaway
The drainage killed fish and molluscs on a vast scale (official counts in the tens of thousands of fish, billions of mussels); since 2024 the bed has filled with songbirds (nightingales, reed warblers), raptors (white-tailed eagle), herons and new pelicans (2026), and mammals by track (jackal, fox, polecat, wild boar); large mammals cannot yet move through the thickets; sturgeon spawn near Khortytsia again; the area is a mined front line, which limits all fieldwork.

## 4. The ZNPP cooling pond: fish, biological reclamation, algae, falling level, die-offs

### Takeaway
The pond (8.2 km², 47 million m³, cut off from the reservoir by a dam) was run as a managed warm-water fishery: silver and bighead carp against algal bloom, black carp against zebra mussels, grass carp against weed, tilapia against filamentous algae on intake plates, channel catfish as predators; Energoatom reported a mass die-off of the warm-water fish in October 2022 when cold shutdown let the pond cool to ~13 °C; since the dam's loss the pond has fallen from ~16–17 m to 12.27 m (HUR, 12 Oct 2026); no reports of fish deaths there in 2023–2026 were found.

### Cited Findings
- **[Peer-reviewed] Marenkov 2018**, *Ukrainian Journal of Ecology* 8(2):140–148: pond area 8.2 km², volume 47.05 million m³, mean depth 5.87 m, max 13.5 m, coastline 11.2 km; formed by cutting off part of the Kakhovka reservoir. 18 fish species recorded over its history, 14 in autumn 2017; 43% native, 57% introduced. Ameliorative species: silver carp, bighead, black carp, grass carp, carp, Mozambique tilapia, channel catfish, brown bullhead; others include bream, bleak, white bream, Prussian carp, roach, rudd, stone moroko, wels catfish, pumpkinseed, zander, mullet, monkey goby. — [CyberLeninka](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/ichthyofauna-of-the-zaporizhia-nuclear-power-plant-cooling-pond-enerhodar-ukraine-and-its-biomeliorative-significance)
- Same paper: roles — silver carp controls phytoplankton bloom; black carp eats Dreissena and Melanoides/Tarebia snails; grass carp reduces overgrowth; tilapia eats filamentous algae (Cladophora, Ulothrix) on intake and outlet channel plates; carp works benthic organic matter. Stocking 2007–2016 incl. ~1.5 million silver carp, ~179,700 grass carp, ~571,600 carp; silver carp ~2.4–2.6 kg at four years. ### Inferences
- The pond is a fish community designed as plant machinery: algae, mussels and weed were controlled by stocked species. With the plant cold, the "biological reclamation" lost its warm water (2022), and with the reservoir gone, its water source; a film could say this as context from sources.
- The level series is from a party to the war (HUR) and should be labelled as such; the IAEA's own 2026 level figures were not found.

### Gaps
- No report of fish deaths, algal blooms or exposed-shore vegetation in the pond 2023–2026; no follow-up confirming the scale of the 2022 die-off.
- Conflict on species: Energoatom names tilapia and "Asian catfish"; Marenkov's species list has channel catfish (American) and wels catfish, no "Asian catfish" — probably a loose name for channel catfish (inference).
- No occupation-authority (Rosatom/ZNPP management) statement on the pond's ecology found.

## 5. Birds and power lines: nesting on towers, electrocution and collision, diverters, cormorants on the crossing towers

### Takeaway
The crossing towers of both lines over the former reservoir carried one of Ukraine's few great-cormorant colonies on man-made structures: hundreds of nests per tower in a 2012 survey (Sydorenko & Siokhin 2016); whether the colony survived the drainage is undocumented. No published collision/electrocution data for Ukraine's 330/750 kV lines were found; stork-nest platforms and diverters are a distribution-grid (DTEK, 6–150 kV) practice.

- Branta (Azov-Black Sea Ornithological Station): lines of 35–110 kV "not so dangerous for most birds"; studies should target 6–10 kV; collision killed 125 birds on three control sites; collision more likely where birds concentrate. — [Branta 17](https://branta.org.ua/en/branta-issues/branta-17/17-08.html)
- DTEK Dnipro Grids: 18–30 line outages a year from bird collisions; ~170 (later reported 200) stork-nest platforms in Dnipropetrovsk oblast, ~40 raptor nest boxes, 700+ bird-protection markers and covers (utility press releases). — [DTEK Grids](https://grids.dtek.com/en/media-center/press/mezhdunarodnyy-den-zaschity-bioraznoobraziya-kak-spetszaschita-naoporakh-lep-pomogaet-energetikam-dtek-seti-spasat-zhizni-aistov/); [NV (partner content)](https://nv.ua/ukr/ukraine/events/dtek-merezhi-pikluyetsya-pro-ptahiv-ukrajini-u-proekti-lelechenki-video-foto-50183669.html)

### Inferences
- Electrocution is mainly a distribution-line (6–35 kV) problem: at 330/750 kV the clearances exceed a bird's span, so collision with the wires (especially earth wires) over water and wetland is the relevant risk; this is general knowledge, not a sourced finding for these lines.
- The cormorant colony depended on fish in open water around the caissons; with the water gone in June 2023 its food and isolation from predators went too. Whether the birds still nest on the towers over the drained bed and river channel is unknown — a satellite or drone image could show guano-whitened crossarms, but nothing published was found.

### Gaps
- No post-2012 counts and no 2023–2026 report on the crossing colony; no photographs located.
- No Ukrenergo bird-protection programme or diverter installation on 330/750 kV lines found.
- No stork/raven/falcon nesting records on these specific lines.

## 6. Pollution on the exposed bed (heavy metals), dust, and the effect of vegetation

### Takeaway
The bed holds about 1.3–1.7 km³ of silt laid since the 1950s with an estimated 83,300 t of heavy metals (Shumilova et al., Science 2025), much of it from Zaporizhzhia, Nikopol and Marhanets industry; UNCG found 94% of 119 samples above Ukrainian permissible limits (As, Pb, Sr, Zn, 7–17×); predicted toxic dust storms have not happened because the bed overgrew, and the willows are thought to hold metals in place — a reason ecologists give against cutting or reflooding.

### Cited Findings
- **[Peer-reviewed] Shumilova, Sukhodolov, Osadcha, Grossart et al., Science (press release 13 Mar 2025):** 18 km³ storage, 16.4 km³ released over ~2 weeks; 1,944 km² of bed exposed; ~1.3–1.7 km³ of silt since the 1950s; ~83,300 t of heavy metals (lead, cadmium, nickel) in the exposed sediment; <1% released during drainage; erosion and seasonal flooding can mobilise them into rivers and food webs; proposed two temporary 15 km barriers to separate the main channel from the two largest wetlands. Shumilova: "In modern warfare, rivers continue to be used not only as the frontlines, but increasingly as weapons." — [IGB](https://www.igb-berlin.de/en/news/water-war-long-term-environmental-consequences-destruction-kakhovka-dam-ukraine)
- The Washington Post's coverage cited >90,000 t (conflicts with 83,300 t). — [Spokesman-Review reprint, 13 Mar 2025](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/mar/13/destruction-of-ukraine-dam-triggered-toxic-time-bo/); spring 2024 floodwater covered nearly 900 km² — [Portside reprint of Science news](https://portside.org/2025-03-16/wartime-destruction-ukraine-dam-has-set-time-bomb)
- **UNCG (2 Dec 2024):** 119 soil samples (bottom sediments, temporarily flooded and dried zones), analysed at Canterbury Christ Church University; 94% exceeded maximum permissible concentrations; main contaminants As, Pb, Sr, Zn at 7–17× MPC; pH 7.0–8.6 limits migration but favours accumulation; mobile forms can be taken up by plants; agricultural use of the bed "impossible"; contamination does not prevent natural recovery; sources: industry and mining in Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kamianske, Nikopol, Marhanets. — [UNCG](https://uncg.org.ua/en/high-levels-heavy-metal-contamination-at-former-kakhovka-reservoir-bed/)
- **Arnika (Czech NGO) with Ukraine's Environmental Inspectorate (Dec 2023 / extended Oct 2024):** 11 sediment samples; highest metals at one site (Cd, Cr, Pb, As, Ni, Cu, Sn); Cd up to 12.17 mg/kg and Cr up to 402.55 mg/kg (search summary of the ResearchGate listing); higher metals near ash landfills; some means and maxima below national limits. — [Arnika report](https://arnika.org/en/publications/first-research-of-the-contamination-of-the-sediments-from-kakhovka-reservoir-extended-version); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378123208_First_research_of_the_contamination_of_the_sediments_from_Kakhovka_reservoir)
- NICOLE Foundation / UNEP assessment (Apr 2024–Feb 2025): 30 sediment samples from the bed and 8 downstream near Kherson; metals present consistently, plus PAHs and petroleum hydrocarbons. — [NICOLE](https://www.nicolefound.org/programmes/kakhovka-reservoir-sediment-assessment,-ukraine)
- Dust: Moisiienko (Jan 2024): dust storms will not occur because the bed is overgrowing fast, and even bare it is a smaller dust source than the surrounding fields; toxic dust risk "close to zero", though bare patches may still raise dust. — [Vgoru](https://vgoru.org/analitika/ekocid-yak-naslidok-pidrivu-kahovskoyi-ges-ivan-mojsiyenko-pro-ekologichni-vtrati-i-prognozi-yaki-ne-spravdilis)
- UWEC: researchers found the soil dense and unlikely to blow. — [UWEC, 4 Jul 2024](https://uwecworkgroup.info/one-year-after-the-terrorist-attack-at-kakhovka-hydropower-plant-1b-trees-instead-of-desert-and-willow-forests-unique-to-the-continent/)
- Kuzemko: "no desert formed and no dust storms arose"; willows absorb heavy metals; cutting or reflooding would release them. — [Hmarochos, 3 Sep 2025](https://hmarochos.kiev.ua/2025/09/03/bilshist-prognoziv-ne-spravdylysya-vcheni-kazhut-shho-na-misczi-kolyshnogo-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-vidnovylys-rusla-rik-i-zyavyvsya-oseter/)
- Rylsky: dust storms not expected now the area is almost entirely tree-covered; pesticide and metal levels exceed norms "in some areas only"; Mulenko: trees act as a natural filter. — [Irish Times, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/11/21/to-flood-this-place-again-would-be-a-crime-ukraines-great-meadow-springs-back-to-life-after-destruction-of-dam)
- Fires on the bed produce smoke that pollutes the air (State Ecological Inspectorate). — [Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/ua/news/1098863-na-vysohlomu-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-postijno-spalahuyut-pozhezhi-povitrya-zabrudnyuye-dym)

### Inferences
- UNCG's "94% above MPC" and Rylsky's "in some areas only" differ in framing; the Arnika data suggest strong spatial variation (hot spots near industrial outfalls and ash landfills), which fits the Nikopol–Marhanets–Enerhodar reach where the lines cross. This is an inference; no sample was located specifically under the lines.
- Willow uptake of metals is asserted by scientists in interviews; no measured plant uptake on the bed was found (UNCG lists phytoremediation as future research).

### Gaps
- No measured airborne dust (PM10) study from the bed found.
- No sample results tied to the Enerhodar ash dumps or the ZNPP/ZTPP outfalls.
- Shumilova et al. full text and exact title not accessed (Science paywall/403).
