# Human stories of the people who repair, guard or operate the ZNPP's last power lines (Enerhodar crossing, 2022–2026)

Researched 29 Oct 2026. Reading status is marked on each item: **[read]** means the page was fetched and read (through a fetch tool that summarises and quotes, so check quotes against the page before using them on screen); **[snippet]** means it was seen only in a search result. YouTube metadata came from yt-dlp (`--skip-download`, metadata only, nothing downloaded). The iaea.org press releases and the globalsecurity.org mirrors refused automated fetches (HTTP 402 or a bot check), so the IAEA's own wording is quoted here only through the news outlets that carried it.

Safety: no tower, crew or military positions are given or inferred below. Where a source itself gives a distance or a general area (for example "about 3 km from the site perimeter" or "tall towers near the demarcation line on the Dnipro"), it is quoted as published and nothing more is derived from it.

## Q1. Is there any interview, video report or photo essay with the crews who repaired Dniprovska or Ferosplavna-1 under the IAEA-brokered ceasefires (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
No. I found no interview, named worker, worker's quote, video report or photo essay from the crews who worked under any of the ceasefires between Oct 2025 and Sep 2026. Every account I found comes from the IAEA (Grossi), Energoatom, the occupation management or Rosatom, and the crews appear only as "technicians", "a Ukrainian team" or "specialists from both Ukraine and Russia". The only published images are an IAEA post on X with a repair photo (Jan 2026) and Energoatom stock photos of the plant.

### Cited Findings
- **Oct 2025, first ceasefire (after the outage that began 23 Sep 2025).** Repairs were in two phases. Dniprovska 750 kV had been damaged "in an area under Russian control" and Ferosplavna-1 330 kV "lies in Ukrainian-controlled territory". The occupation management credited "close cooperation" between the IAEA and Rosatom and said the Russian Defence Ministry "will play a key role in ensuring the safety of the repair work". Grossi: "Both sides engaged constructively with the IAEA to enable complex repair plan to proceed." The article quotes no plant staff or repair workers. — [Al Jazeera, 18 Oct 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/18/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-repairs-begin-in-ukraine-as-ceasefire-zones-set) [read]
- The IAEA's 18 Oct 2025 update (as quoted in search snippets): "following de-mining activities, work started to repair two cables of the 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 line… after completion of the necessary de-mining of the specified area on the other side, repairs are due to begin on the damaged section of the 750 kV Dniprovska line." Main-line power was restored on 23 Oct. Ferosplavna-1 was delayed because "additional damage… was discovered at another location closer to the plant itself but outside of the initially agreed ceasefire zones." — [IAEA Update via globalsecurity mirror, 18 Oct 2025](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/10/ukraine-251018-iaea01.htm) [snippet: page blocked]
- **Nov 2025, Ferosplavna-1.** Repairs began on the morning of 8 Nov "approximately three kilometres from the perimeter of the ZNPP site", "after this territory was demined the previous day", "under the supervision of the IAEA team". The line had been down since 7 May 2025, and this gave the plant back-up grid power for the first time in six months. The crew is called only "техніки" (technicians), with no company named. The byline is Alla Pavlyuchenko. — [Mind.ua, 9 Nov 2025](https://mind.ua/news/20297101-zaes-znovu-pidklyuchili-do-rezervnogo-zhivlennya) [read]. A 7 Nov IAEA update (per a search snippet) says technicians repaired "a damaged cable between two pylons" with an IAEA team monitoring. — [IAEA Update 326 mirror](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/11/ukraine-251107-iaea01.htm) [snippet]
  - Inference: "3 km from the site perimeter" would put the damage on the occupied (south) side, so the crew could have been occupation-run ZNPP or ZTPP staff rather than Ukrenergo. No source I read says who they were.
- **Jan 2026, Ferosplavna-1 (cut 3 Jan, repaired under a ceasefire agreed in late Dec or January).** Liga.net reports the work "is being carried out by a Ukrainian team", with IAEA experts on site. The photos are Energoatom's (the plant) and **an IAEA post on X showing the repair work**. No workers are named or quoted, and mines and drones are not mentioned. Author: Olena Mazun. The page also says the ceasefire was "confirmed on April 4", which looks like an editing error. — [Liga.net, 18 Jan 2026](https://news.liga.net/en/politics/news/repair-of-a-backup-power-line-near-zaporizhzhia-npp-begins-photo) [read]. The IAEA team "travelled… to the frontline to observe the work" (search summary). — [IAEA Update mirror, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2026/01/ukraine-260116-iaea01.htm) [snippet]
- **Feb–Mar 2026, Ferosplavna-1 (cut 10 Feb, ceasefire 27 Feb, reconnected about 5 Mar, 23 days off).** Only IAEA statements were found, and no crew accounts. — [Ukrainska Pravda, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/03/05/8024118) [snippet]; [NTD clip u2jt8wLW700, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u2jt8wLW700) [metadata]
- **June 2026, Dniprovska 750 kV.** The ceasefire took effect on 5 Jun 2026. The damage is "on tall towers near the demarcation line on the Dnipro River". **"Technical specialists from both Ukraine and Russia will carry out the repairs"** after "extensive demining". — [Ukrinform, 5 Jun 2026](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4130748-iaea-secures-ceasefire-for-repairs-to-power-line-supplying-znpp.html) [read]. The IAEA began monitoring mine clearance on Sunday 7 Jun. — [Counter-IED Report, 8–9 Jun 2026](https://counteriedreport.com/tag/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant/) [read, excerpt only]
- **Injuries at the June 2026 repair site (occupation side, unverified).** The ZNPP's occupation representatives said Ukrainian forces struck "the place where repair work is being carried out on the ZNPP line". After a drone attack on 4 Jun, "five servicemen received wounds of varying severity" in the area of the Dniprovska line. No workers or deminers were reported hurt. These are Russian-side claims and nothing independent confirms them. — [M24.ru, 7 Jun 2026](https://www.m24.ru/news/07062026/908241) [read]. Eastern Herald separately reports that Ukraine struck a Russian mine-clearance team inside the ceasefire zone, prompting Grossi to demand compliance. — [Eastern Herald, 5 Jun 2026](https://easternherald.com/2026/06/05/grossi-znpp-ceasefire-compliance-ukraine-attack-mine-clearance-dniprovska/) [snippet]
- **26 Jun 2026.** The line was repaired after "extensive mine clearance along the front line" but stays off because its connecting substation, "more than 100 km northwest of the ZNPP", was damaged in late May. "IAEA staff experts who visited the facility this week observed severe damage to essential equipment." Who repaired the line is not said, and the workers are not praised or named. — [Ukrainska Pravda, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/06/26/8041243/) [read]. Per NEI Magazine, Grossi called it the most challenging of the six ceasefires he had negotiated. — [NEI Magazine](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/znpp-powerline-repairs-complete/) [snippet]
- **Aug–Sep 2026, Ferosplavna-1 (the 7th ceasefire).** IAEA staff were "on site monitoring the demining and repair work". Diesel stocks for the emergency generators were down to "little more than a week of supplies". Grossi: "nuclear power plant staff must be able to carry out their essential work without being attacked or placed at risk." Crews are not named or quoted. — [World Nuclear News, 7 Sep 2026](https://wnn.world-nuclear.org/articles/local-ceasefire-in-place-for-zaporizhzhia-power-line-repairs) [read]
- **The closest crew voice on these lines (pre-ceasefire, Feb 2024).** Ukrenergo's Facebook post on repairing the 330 kV back-up line to the ZNPP site (damaged 20 Feb 2024) quotes the crew, unnamed: "If it were not for the constant shelling by the Russians, we would have done it faster." "We waited a long time for the enemy to stop firing artillery." "[W]e had to clear the way for the equipment through the damaged landing, and only then start repairing." "All of this was constantly interrupted by enemy fire." Access came only with military permission, in "short periods of relative calm". — [Ukrinform, 24 Feb 2024](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3831789-ukrenergo-restores-znpp-power-supply-reliability.html) [read]. The original Ukrenergo Facebook post was not located.

### Inferences
- Both parties and the IAEA seem to keep the crews anonymous on purpose: both sides' workers are targets, and the June 2026 work used specialists from both countries. A crew interview is therefore unlikely to appear while the war goes on. The student's human layer would have to come from proxies: the Ukrenergo crew quotes of Feb 2024, the IAEA's observers, or crews on nearby lines.
- The IAEA's photo on X (Jan 2026) is the one documented image of the crews at work. It needs to be found on @iaeaorg and its licence checked before use.

### Gaps
- No interview, podcast, photo essay or TV report with any ceasefire repair crew (Oct 2025 to Sep 2026) was found in Ukrainian, Russian or English.
- Not established: which organisation repaired each break (Ukrenergo, ZNPP or ZTPP staff, Rosatom contractors), crew sizes, hours worked, or the weather. No source gives numbers of workers.
- Who repaired Ferosplavna-1 "on the northern bank" in Aug–Sep 2026 (that framing comes from the brief and was not confirmed in what I read), and its reconnection time.
- The IAEA's own update texts could not be fetched (blocked). They may hold more detail on the crews and should be read directly in a browser.

## Q2. Ukrenergo, Energoatom and DTEK channels: named workers in Nikopol / Marhanets district, including workers killed or wounded repairing lines
