# The last lines crackle where no one listens

**No published story ties a house, family or named villager to the Dniprovska (750 kV) or Ferosplavna-1 (330 kV) lines, their towers, their ceasefire repair zones or their outages.** The researchers searched Ukrainian, English and Russian sources, YouTube and the trade press. Every account of the seven IAEA-brokered repairs (October 2025 to September 2026) is institutional: the IAEA, Energoatom, Ukrenergo, Rosatom and wire copy. The crews are always "technicians" or "a Ukrainian team". The nearest human material is in Marhanets and Nikopol, and none of it concerns these two lines. In Marhanets, strikes have hit homes and local distribution lines together. In Nikopol, residents watch the plant across the bed. A Ukrenergo crew's unnamed words from February 2024, "we waited a long time for the enemy to stop firing artillery", are the closest thing to a line worker's voice. A human perspective therefore has to be gathered first-hand, remotely, with consent, and without a composite "house by the line". The physics of the lines' sound gives the film something firmer, and it supports the film's argument that what is at stake is time, not energy. **Corona noise follows voltage, not load.** A line held at 750,000 volts crackles and hums at 100 Hz in rain and fog just as loudly when it carries 11 MW into the plant as when it carried a gigawatt out. In dry weather it is close to silent, and from Nikopol, about 10 km away, it is inaudible in any weather. The synthesized Hum's choice to sound a line by whether it is *connected*, not by how much it carries, is the physically honest one. The war's sound at the site reaches the record as evidence in three forms: the IAEA team's dated logs of explosions heard, a Ukrainian NGO's method of reading a drone's engine note as evidence of intent, and the dam's destruction recorded by infrasound arrays at 02:34:50 Kyiv time on 6 June 2023. Each can enter the film as text, waveform or caption without breaking the decision, made with the user, to play no sounds of fighting.

**Evidence grades used throughout.** [read] = the page was read in full by a researcher. [snippet] = seen only in a search-engine summary, so check it before it goes on screen. [meta] = YouTube metadata read with yt-dlp; nothing was watched or downloaded. [party] = a claim by Energoatom, Rosatom, the occupation management, HUR or an army spokesperson. [advocacy] = Greenpeace/McKenzie Intelligence Services or similar commissioned work. [peer-reviewed]. [conference poster] = a formal scientific output that was not peer-reviewed. [anecdote] = a single witness as reported. [own calc] = arithmetic by the researchers, not a source. Most IAEA pages and mirrors returned HTTP 402/403, so the IAEA's wording reaches this report through World Nuclear News, Ukrainska Pravda, Mind.ua and similar outlets.

## No family's story reaches the towers, so the nearest voices are a town away

### The plain answer to question 1

There is no article, video report, photo essay or social video that tells a household's story about these two lines. In particular, nothing was found about a house beside the right-of-way, a family affected by a ceasefire repair, villagers who noticed a truce, or anyone living under the crossing towers. Searches for the north-bank villages along the routes, including Maryivka and Dobra Nadiia, returned no coverage at all. That held for shelling, the garden plots, towers on private land and demining. The truces themselves exist only in wire and agency copy that quotes no residents ([Ukrainska Pravda, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/01/17/8016600) [snippet]; [ANS, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-09/article-8377/zaporizhzhia-restores-offsite-power-following-iaeabrokered-local-ceasefire/) [snippet]). The crews are anonymous on both sides, apparently on purpose. In June 2026 "technical specialists from both Ukraine and Russia" repaired Dniprovska after "extensive demining" ([Ukrinform, 5 Jun 2026](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4130748-iaea-secures-ceasefire-for-repairs-to-power-line-supplying-znpp.html) [read]). ### How a human perspective could still be gathered responsibly

The gap can be closed only by original, remote reporting, and the routes are concrete:

- **Suspilne Dnipro.** Its Marhanets and Nikopol correspondents, Roman Mykhalchuk and Anton Sirenko (named in the [meta] credits above), already film in the hromada.
- **The Marhanets city council.**
- **Francis Farrell at the Kyiv Independent**, for a follow-up on Kling and Lyubov Harmash.
- **The press offices of Ukrenergo and DTEK Dnipro Grids**, about crews who worked the Nikopol-side lines.
- **The IAEA press office**, for the January 2026 photo and its licence.
- **The Telegram channels**: Serhii Lysak's oblast channel, the Nikopol district administration and "Типовий Марганець". These are where a village-level line such as "a house damaged near X" would appear. They are leads only, because Telegram is outside the collection's pipeline.

The question to ask is narrow and checkable: what did the night of a reconnection, or of a truce, sound and feel like in Marhanets? No source yet answers it.

The safety rules are stricter here than for imagery, because the risk falls on named people.

- **Interview only remotely.** Do not travel to the bank or ask anyone to film there.
- **Record consent for each use.** Use only the names people choose.
- **Never publish house numbers, streets, coordinates, or a view from a window or garden that could locate a home.** Truth Hounds documents a 2024 Nikopol case in which a resident's TikTok, reposted in a shelling channel, is believed to have preceded a drone strike on the flat that injured an 81-year-old ([Truth Hounds, *Killzone*, Nov 2025](https://dev.truth-hounds.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/killzone-how-russian-drones-are-devastating-the-river-dnipros-right-bank.pdf) [read]).
- **Many Nikopol and Marhanets residents have relatives under occupation.** Harmash's mother and sister are in Vodiane.
- **Do not contact anyone inside Enerhodar.** Quote Enerhodar residents only under the pseudonyms already published.
- **Never pair a line worker's face with a work location.**
- **Do not stand in a generic "house near the line" composite for a story the record does not hold.** The absence is itself a finding the film can state: the people who keep these lines alive are kept nameless by both sides, and the people nearest them have not been asked.

### 1. The ceasefire for one span

This is now the centre of the list, because it is the lines' own story told at human scale. Each repair since October 2025 has needed a negotiated local truce, demining, a crew working under observation, reconnection, and later another cut. In October 2025 Dniprovska was damaged "in an area under Russian control" and Ferosplavna-1 "lies in Ukrainian-controlled territory". Grossi said "both sides engaged constructively" ([Al Jazeera, 18 Oct 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/18/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-repairs-begin-in-ukraine-as-ceasefire-zones-set) [read]). Ferosplavna-1 had been down since 7 May 2025. It was repaired on the morning of 8 November 2025 "under the supervision of the IAEA team", and that gave the plant back-up power for the first time in six months ([Mind.ua, 9 Nov 2025](https://mind.ua/news/20297101-zaes-znovu-pidklyuchili-do-rezervnogo-zhivlennya) [read]). The June 2026 Dniprovska repair was the one Grossi reportedly called the most challenging of six ([NEI Magazine](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/znpp-powerline-repairs-complete/) [snippet]). The line was mended but could not be switched on, because its substation "more than 100 km northwest" was damaged ([Ukrainska Pravda, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/06/26/8041243/) [read]). Russian outlets claimed a drone strike wounded five servicemen near the work ([M24, 7 Jun 2026](https://www.m24.ru/news/07062026/908241) [party, unverified]). The seventh truce, in September 2026, came with "little more than a week" of diesel left ([WNN, 7 Sep 2026](https://wnn.world-nuclear.org/articles/local-ceasefire-in-place-for-zaporizhzhia-power-line-repairs) [read]). The new human layer is thin but real: the February 2024 crew quotes and the IAEA's "heard several rockets" on a repair day (table above). It works best as the record's longest held moment, with the Hum returning on one voice. Never mark which towers or spans were repaired. The published phrase "tall towers near the demarcation line on the Dnipro" stays text.

### 2. The crossing towers standing in the bed, and their cormorants

The two crossings are the 330 kV line from the thermal plant (1977) and the 750 kV line from the nuclear plant (1984). Their towers are up to about 126 m tall, on caissons sunk in the reservoir and now standing on the drained bed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enerhodar_Dnipro_Powerline_Crossing)). A 2012 survey counted great cormorants nesting on four towers of the 330 kV crossing, up to 320 nests on one, and on two of the 750 kV crossing ([Sidorenko & Siokhin 2016](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/nesting-of-great-cormorants-phalacrocorax-carbo-on-man-made-structures-in-ukraine) [peer-reviewed; from the earlier notes]). Nobody knows whether the colonies survived the drainage. The sonic research adds two things. The Stockbridge dampers already modelled "2 m from every clamp" exist to absorb the wind's vibration of the spans, so they are a visible sign of the lines' wind voice (section 4 below). And in dry weather the birds of the new forest are probably louder than the line [own inference]. Ukrenergo's 2015 aerial of the towers in water ([YouTube yd6nkeCuomc, 11 Sep 2015](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yd6nkeCuomc) [meta]) is **pre-2022 and cannot enter the collection without the user's decision**. No post-2023 footage of the towers in the bed was found.

### 3. The shore where the lines come ashore, near Marhanets

The student's site keeps its earlier findings and its safety rules unchanged. A zig-zag fire trench with two dug-in emplacements is absent in Esri's capture of 1 October 2021 and present, freshly dug, on 29 August 2022 ([Esri Wayback configuration](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/config.maptiles.arcgis.com/waybackconfig.json), primary metadata read by the researcher). A fire burned the steppe beside it between the Sentinel-2 passes of 16 and 18 August 2024 ([Planetary Computer, Sentinel-2 L2A](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-2-l2a), own measurement); its cause is unknown. On 20 August 2026 the IAEA "preliminarily assessed" Ferosplavna-1's damage to be "on the northern bank" ([WNN, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/local-ceasefire-in-place-for-zaporizhzhia-power-line-repairs) [read]). That bank runs for tens of kilometres, so the film must not imply the break was here. The new research found **no coverage at all of the villages and garden plots near the landfall**, so this entry stays satellite stills only.

The rules bind it. Describe the site only as "on the north bank near Marhanets, where the lines come ashore". Publish no coordinates and no pin. Use dated imagery from 2024 or earlier. Make no operational interpretation. Mark no towers.

### 4. The line that sings in fog whatever it carries (new, sonic)

This is the most useful new microcosm, because it is the lines' own voice and it carries the film's argument. Corona noise is set by the voltage gradient at the conductor's surface, not by the current. "Even a line that is energized but not carrying load may emit corona noise" ([CPUC El Casco RDEIR, 2008, citing EPRI](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/aspen/elcasco/recircdeir/D%209%20Noise%20Rev3.pdf) [read]). A 750 kV line feeding 11 MW into a cold plant therefore crackles in the mist exactly as it did when it exported a gigawatt. Its sound marks its *availability*, the margin, not its energy. Section 4 below gives the measurements and what they mean for the Hum. As a microcosm it needs no access, no footage and no private person.

### 5. Launches beside the reactors, filmed from where the lines land

On the night of 2–3 September 2022 a camera on the Nikopol bank filmed rocket launchers firing near a power unit. CIT placed the launch site west of the thermal plant by matching that plant's chimneys, the chimneys of the plant Ferosplavna-1 leaves from ([The Insider, 3 Sep 2022](https://theins.ru/news/254676)). CIR's "Danger Zone" placed the launches between the two plants ([CIR](https://info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant) [read]). Kling heard what the camera saw: "Sometimes Russians launch (shells) from behind them, from Enerhodar." A Grad takes "about fifteen seconds" to reach Nikopol ([Kyiv Independent, 25 Aug 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/life-on-the-front-line-of-russias-new-nuclear-brinkmanship/) [read]). This is the plant used as cover for fire, which is the literal claim to keep here. Whether the launchers stood inside the fence remains unresolved, and the film must say so. Never reveal a filmer's position.

One sonic fact belongs in this entry. Even a 735 kV line falls to about 40 dBA 100 m beyond its right-of-way in bad weather, and to 25 dBA or less in fair weather ([BAPE DQ17.1](https://archives.bape.gouv.qc.ca/sections/mandats/ligne_chamouchouane_bout-de-lile/documents/DQ17.1.pdf) [snippet, utility estimate]). At 10–12 km **no one in Nikopol has ever heard these lines** [own inference]. What they hear across the water is fire. No follow-up on Kling or Korolkova after 2022 was found.

### 7. The new forest under the wires: nightingales and drones

The willow and poplar forest that grew on the bed was about 2 m tall in summer 2024, up to 7 m in 2025 and 9 m by 2026 ([TSN, 7 Jun 2026](https://tsn.ua/en/exclusive/kakhovka-hpp-blown-up-three-years-ago-what-unique-great-meadow-looks-like-now-3100838.html) [read]). It now has a documented sound. TSN's crew recorded "the astonishing singing of hundreds of nightingales echoing alongside the hum of frontline drones": thrush and common nightingales, said botanist Anna Kuzemko, "when there are hundreds, it is simply breathtaking" (same source). The zoologist Oleksii Vasyliuk describes "nonstop singing in the forest" from nightingales and reed warblers, many spiders, almost no bees or butterflies, and jackals and foxes hunting rodents ([Texty, 1 Jul 2026](https://texty.org.ua/fragments/117772/xto-zaselyv-dno-kaxovskoho-vodosxovyshcha-zooloh-opysav-tvaryn-novoho-lisu/) [read]). Suspilne's *Great Meadow. The Unexplored Zone* has a chapter "Nature to the sounds of war" at 33:30 ([YouTube DGIFNa_viuc, 28 May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DGIFNa_viuc) [meta; unheard]). "Hundreds" is the observers' description, not a count. No acoustic monitoring of the bed exists. The forest is also cover, so plots stay unlocated.

### 8. Marhanets: houses and local lines hit in one strike (new, human)

## The lines are loud only when wet, whatever they carry

### What the measurements say

High-voltage line noise is **corona**: air ionising at the conductor surface. It has two parts. One is a broadband crackle and hiss, about 0.5–10 kHz and strongest around 2–3 kHz. The other is a tonal hum at twice the grid frequency, which on Ukraine's 50 Hz grid means **100 Hz, with a weaker 200 Hz** ([TransGrid/WSP, Nov 2025](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/llsbcso3/audible-coronal-noise-study-investigating-audible-noise-from-transmission-lines-in-australian-climates.pdf) [read]; [Hydro-Québec synthesis, ch. 12](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf) [read]). It is above all a wet-weather sound. The closest analogue to Dniprovska is Hydro-Québec's 735 kV Saint-Césaire–Hertel line, measured before and after it was energised at the right-of-way edge, 40 m from the centreline. With the line on, mean background levels rose **11 dBA in rain and 14 dBA in snow**. In dry weather they did not change at all: "a slight crackle of the line is perceived" ([Duchassin et al., *Canadian Acoustics* 35(3), 2007](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/1960/1707/2097) [peer-reviewed]).

Once precipitation starts, noise climbs over **about 15 minutes to about 53 dBA in rain or 48 dBA in snow**. After it stops, noise takes **about 40 minutes** to fall below 40 dBA. The tones were never strong enough to count as tonal noise under Québec's rule ([Hydro-Québec](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf) [read]). At 330 kV the Australian measurements found levels of 25–60 dBA across rain rates, with no consistent trend. International models overpredicted at that voltage. Fog and mist at dawn gave "a modest but consistent increase" even with no rain ([TransGrid/WSP 2025](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/llsbcso3/audible-coronal-noise-study-investigating-audible-noise-from-transmission-lines-in-australian-climates.pdf) [read]). The Bonneville Power Administration (BPA) treats corona noise as a concern "primarily for… 345 kV and above" ([BPA Audible Noise Policy](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/foia/foia-2024/202400429F.pdf) [read]).

| Condition | 735/750 kV, ROW edge (~40 m) | 330 kV | Source and grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry, fair | No measurable rise; faint crackle (utility estimate 30–40 dBA) | Quieter still | Duchassin 2007 [peer-reviewed]; BAPE [snippet] |
| Rain | ~53 dBA, rising over ~15 min | 25–60 dBA, no clear trend | Hydro-Québec [read]; TransGrid [read] |
| Snow | ~48 dBA | — | Hydro-Québec [read] |
| Fog, mist, dew | Raised; the "most critical period" is just after rain | Modest, consistent rise | CPUC/EPRI [read]; TransGrid [read] |
| 100 m beyond the ROW | ~40 dBA bad weather, ≤25 dBA fair | — | BAPE [snippet] |

The physics of load settles the user's question. Corona "can occur on any AC electrical line, **regardless of the amount of current (load)**" ([CPUC El Casco RDEIR §D.9](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/aspen/elcasco/recircdeir/D%209%20Noise%20Rev3.pdf) [read]). A line energised at 750 kV but carrying 11–50 MW therefore crackles and hums as much in rain as it did at full export. The one second-order effect runs the other way. Load current warms the conductor, and "conductor heating due to current flow will discourage formation of water droplets during fog" (same source), so a nearly idle line may stay wet, and noisy, a little longer after rain [inference from a qualitative statement]. The Ukrainian 750 kV Environmental Impact Assessment for the ZNPP–Kakhovska line says only that corona makes low-frequency noise, "with humidity always increasing the effect", and gives no dBA figure ([EBRD 2009 non-technical summary](https://www.ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/english/pages/project/eia/40147u.pdf) [read]). No measurement of any Soviet or Ukrainian 750 kV line was found.

Three more mechanisms complete the picture.

- **Transformer hum** comes from magnetostriction. It sits at 100 Hz and its even harmonics, "present as soon as the transformer is energised", and it "varies with voltage but not with load". Winding noise does follow current, rising about 12 dB per doubling ([Hydro-Québec, pp. 21, 24](https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/data/developpement-durable/pdf/12_AmbianceSonore.pdf) [read]). The switchyards' core hum would therefore continue while their winding noise fell away.
- **A coil hears something different.** An induction coil, as in Christina Kubisch's *Electrical Walks*, responds to the *magnetic* field, which comes from current, at **50 Hz** with odd harmonics. Corona and transformer hum is an *acoustic* sound at **100 Hz**. When an energised line carries no current, the magnetic field falls away while the electric field stays ([WHO Fact Sheet 322](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/lands/EMF_WHO_EMF-Fact-Sheet-2007a1.pdf) [read]; [NI Assembly paper](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/globalassets/documents/raise/publications/2008/environment/9608.pdf) [snippet]). The magnetic voice will not fall in proportion to MW, because long 750 kV lines carry capacitive charging current. A rough estimate is about 200 A per 100 km against about 8–40 A of active current for 11–50 MW [own calc; the line's length and reactors are unsourced].
- **A de-energised line is electrically silent but not mute.** Wind sheds vortices off the conductors at about 3–150 Hz in steady 1–7 m/s winds, and Stockbridge dampers are tuned to absorb that vibration ([Mechanical Sciences 12, 2021](https://ms.copernicus.org/articles/12/863/2021/) [peer-reviewed]). For an assumed 25 mm sub-conductor the vibration falls at about 7–52 Hz, mostly below or at the edge of hearing in air but audible by contact microphone [own calc].

**A line tripped only at the plant end can stay energised from the far substation and keep crackling**, so the record's "cut" does not always mean physical silence.

### What this means for the synthesized Hum

The current Hum, as `hum.py`'s statement describes it, sounds a line while the record says it is connected, stops it when cut, and sets the level by how many lines are connected rather than by MW. **The research supports that design.** Connection, not flow, is what the physics follows, and it is also what the film argues matters. Six adjustments follow, each a design decision for the user rather than a code change made here:

1. **Retire the plan's "inversion heard as loudness" for the crackle layer.** `docs/znpp-heart-plan.txt` expects the post-2022 lines to be "a whisper". That holds only for a magnetic, coil-like layer, and even there not in proportion to MW, as the coil item above explains. A connected line in 2026 should crackle like a connected line in 2021. If a coil layer is added, it belongs at 50 Hz, kept apart from the 100 Hz corona hum.
2. **Let the crackle lead and the hum sit under it.** In wet weather the crackle is the dominant sound, and the plan already notes that 100 Hz is hard to place and that small speakers barely reproduce it.
3. **Make the 330 kV voice clearly quieter and drier than the 750 kV voice.** Ferosplavna-1 is below the voltage at which utilities treat corona as a problem, and the Australian 330 kV data are weak and scattered.
4. **Weather as the honest modulator.** If the Hum swells or fades for anything other than connection, weather is the physical reason: rain and mist up, dry days near silence. Rises should take minutes and falls about three times longer, the 15-minute and 40-minute asymmetry. Tying it to the actual weather on each date would need a dated weather source, which was not researched here. Otherwise it is a convention and should be said to be.
5. **Say what silence means.** The statement could add that a line's silence means "not connected, by the record", not that the wire itself was silent. It could also say that real lines are almost inaudible in dry weather and inaudible from the far bank. This keeps the Hum labelled as synthesized and as a reading of the record.
6. **Keep the reconnection swell a drawing convention.** The swell (`fade_on_s`) and any cut transient are drawn, not heard at the line. The real momentary sound of a line switching is a breaker in a substation, "momentary, but very infrequent" ([SRP Exhibit I](https://www.srpnet.com/assets/srpnet/pdf/grid-water-management/grid-management/improvement-projects/hip/cec/SRP-HIP-CEC-Final_ExhibitI.pdf) [read]).

**No recording exists of the Enerhodar crossing, any Ukrainian 750 kV line or the ZNPP's switchyards** on Freesound, aporee or Bandcamp, or in web searches of YouTube. The aporee and Freesound sites were not searched directly. That absence is itself a reason the Hum must be synthesized and say so. If a recorded reference is ever set beside it, it has to be an EHV line elsewhere, credited with its place and date as "a 750 kV line", never "this one".

## War sound around the bed enters the record as text, waveform and testimony

The research on war sound divides into what was measured and what was reported. The only peer-reviewed Ukrainian study of wildlife and war disturbance tracked 19 greater spotted eagles in spring 2022. Their deviation was greatest where military activity was heaviest, and one diverted about 250 km. The authors *suspected* noise and light but measured neither ([Russell et al., *Current Biology* 2024](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/95248/1/1_s2.0_S0960982224005190_main.pdf) [peer-reviewed, snippet]). A Ukrainian ornithologist counters that the loss of civilian air traffic mattered more ([UWEC Work Group](https://uwecworkgroup.info/has-the-war-forced-eagles-to-alter-their-migration-routes-in-ukraine/) [anecdote/expert]). Janine Natalya Clark's interviews with more than 30 Ukrainian scientists produced the sharpest anecdote. At Tuzlivski Lymany, near Odesa, drone noise in 2024 reportedly made a flamingo colony abandon its nests. Interviewees also said cormorants, herons and storks have grown less sensitive to war sound ([The Conversation, 9 Jul 2025](https://theconversation.com/sound-recordings-can-give-us-an-animal-eye-view-of-the-war-in-ukraine-260519) [read; anecdote]). Nothing from either source concerns Kakhovka. For the bed itself the evidence is the ecologists' descriptions in entry 7. Nightingales and drones are documented *together* in the same recording ([TSN](https://tsn.ua/en/exclusive/kakhovka-hpp-blown-up-three-years-ago-what-unique-great-meadow-looks-like-now-3100838.html) [read]). No frog chorus, no dB measurement and no acoustic monitoring of the bed or the Great Meadow was found. The nearest systematic survey is 506 sites in Polesia ([BTO](https://www.bto.org/our-work/science/publications/papers/acoustic-monitoring-enables-multi-taxa-conservation-assessment-and-prioritisation-over-large-scales) [peer-reviewed, snippet]).

In Nikopol, sound is how people survive. In 2022 residents distinguished oblast alerts, which they mostly ignored, from town shelling alerts. They read the "dull, empty boom" of outgoing fire through a Telegram code: "the farmers are working" ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/life-on-the-front-line-of-russias-new-nuclear-brinkmanship/) [read]). In 2023 "sirens blare for hours", "a quiet night is a rarity", and a shell from Enerhodar arrives before the siren ends ([Voxeurop, 21 Dec 2023](https://voxeurop.eu/en/ukraine-nikopol-martyr-victory-town/) [read]). An Oslo researcher describes the siren as splitting life into danger and relative safety, and creating "acoustic communities" ([UiO, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.uio.no/english/research/research-news/articles/2025/the-soundscape-of-war-shapes-the-daily-lives-of-uk.html) [read]). An official spokesperson likened the Shahed's engine to "a moped or chainsaw" ([Babel, 28 Sep 2022](https://babel.ua/en/news/84880-the-oc-south-told-how-to-recognize-the-iranian-shahed-136-kamikaze-drone-in-the-sky) [party, read]). Ukraine's acoustic detection networks, Sky Fortress (about 9,500 phone-and-microphone poles by 2024: [Defense One](https://defenseone.com/defense-systems/2024/07/ukraines-cheap-sensors-are-helping-troops-fight-waves-russian-drones/398204) [read]) and Zvook, are documented only as systems. Zvook's machine learning is trained "to distinguish missile sounds from insects, voices, vehicles, and animals" ([Euromaidan Press, 2023](https://euromaidanpress.com/2023/03/03/hear-and-destroy-ukrainian-artificial-intelligence-project-zvook-helps-shoot-down-russian-missiles/) [read]). In machine listening, then, the bed's nightingales are the noise to be filtered out. That is an observation the film may make. It is not a claim about any sensor, and **nothing here says, or should imply, where any sensor is**.

No source describes what a ceasefire sounded like in Nikopol. During the declared May 2025 truce the raion was still hit ([Fakty, 9 May 2025](https://fakty.com.ua/ua/proisshestvija/20250509-obstril-nikopolskogo-rajonu-dnipropetrovskoyi-oblasti-9-travnya-2025-naslidky-vorozhoyi-ataky/) [snippet]). Any silence the film holds is therefore an editing choice and should be labelled as one, the way the Hum is. Nor was any recording of the full reservoir at the crossing from before June 2023 found: no waves, ferry or shore. The "before" can only be narrated, or synthesized and said to be.

### Precedents that place the Hum in a tradition

The lineage is well established and gives the Hum's honesty a frame.

- **The wire as an instrument.**
  - *Alan Lamb's Primal Image / Beauty*: contact-microphone recordings of kilometre-long telegraph wires sung by the wind, Western Australia, 1981–88 ([Forced Exposure, Room40](https://forcedexposure.com/Catalog/RM.4240CD.html)). This is the voice of a de-energised line.
  - *Alvin Lucier's Music on a Long Thin Wire* (1977): AC current in a magnetic field makes a wire sing, the physics of line and transformer hum ([DRAM notes](https://www.dramonline.org/albums/alvin-lucier-music-on-a-long-thin-wire/notes)).
- **The field made audible.** Kubisch's *Electrical Walks* (2003–) use headphones with built-in coils ([christinakubisch.de](https://christinakubisch.de/?p=123)).
- **Listening to dangerous places.**
  - Peter Cusack's "sonic journalism" in *Sounds from Dangerous Places* (2012), whose Chernobyl disc includes "sounds of work, electricity and radiometers" ([Grünrekorder](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?p=9070)).
  - Jacob Kirkegaard's *4 Rooms* (2006), which drew out the resonances of empty rooms in Pripyat ([Touch](https://touch33.net/?p=339)).
- **Forensic listening.**
  - Lawrence Abu Hamdan's audio ballistics with Forensic Architecture in the Beitunia killings ([Forensic Architecture](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/the-killing-of-nadeem-nawara-and-mohammed-abu-daher)).
  - *Saydnaya (the missing 19db)*, which reconstructs a prison from earwitness testimony and the drop in how loudly detainees dared whisper ([Sharjah Art Foundation](https://sharjahart.org/sharjah-art-foundation/projects/saydnaya-the-missing-19db)).
  - *Earwitness Inventory*: objects made to stand in for described sounds, labelled as such ([Domus 2018](https://domusweb.it/en/art/2018/09/21/earwitness-theatre-non-neutral-sound-evidences.html)).
  - *Air Pressure (A Diary of the Sky)*, which turns 22,111 overflights logged from UN filings and citizens' phone videos into "atmospheric violence" ([ArtDependence](https://artdependence.com/articles/lawrence-abu-hamdans-air-pressure-a-diary-of-the-sky-curated-by-irene-calderoni-and-amanda-sroka)).
  - Susan Schuppli's *Material Witness* (MIT Press, 2020) supplies the theory ([Goldsmiths](https://research.gold.ac.uk/28197)).
- **Ukrainian work.**
  - *Blackout Tape* (2025): ten artists recording without electricity during the blackouts ([A Closer Listen](https://acloserlisten.com/2025/06/14/various-artists-blackout-tape/)).
  - Clark's online exhibition of 43 war-environment recordings ([Birmingham](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/news/2025/a-new-online-exhibition-immerses-users-in-the-sounds-of-the-russia-ukraine-war)).
  - Anna Khvyl's wetlands talk at Izolyatsia ([Izolyatsia](https://izolyatsia.org/en/project/zazemlennia-invasion/wetlands/)) and Zhanna Kadyrova's *The Forest*, a boat at the vanished water level ([CTM 2026](https://www.ctm-festival.de/festival-2026/programme/exhibition/the-forest)). Both touch the drained reservoir.
  - No Ukrainian artist working with power lines or the 50 Hz grid was found.

What sets the film apart is that its score is a sourced, dated record of connections. Abu Hamdan's discipline of saying what is measured, what is reconstructed and what is re-enacted is the model for its labels.

## Conclusion

The search for a human story ended in a structural finding rather than a scene. The two lines that hold the plant's margin are the most negotiated objects on this front, the subject of seven truces, yet both sides keep the people who mend them nameless. No one who lives near them has been asked about them. The film can state that gap honestly and point to where it could be filled. Inventing a household would trade the project's credibility for a moment of feeling.

The sound research does more for the argument than the user's original framing did. Because corona follows voltage and ignores load, the lines' real voice tracks exactly what the film says matters: whether a line is there, energised and available, not how much it carries. A line feeding 11 MW to a cold reactor sounds in the fog like one exporting a gigawatt, and it falls silent only when the margin is gone. That makes the synthesized Hum the honest rendering of the record's states, not a dramatisation of energy, provided the film says three things. The sound is made, not recorded. Its silence belongs to the record. And from the bank where the lines come ashore, no one has ever heard them.
