# The ZNPP, its last two lines, and Ukraine's grid and people (2022 to October 2026)

Research notes, 29 October 2026. Every claim has a URL. "[snippet]" means the claim was seen only in a search-engine
summary, not on the fetched page; treat it as weaker. Official figures (Energoatom, Ukrenergo, IAEA, IEA, EIA, World
Bank) are marked as such; NGO, think-tank and media estimates are marked as estimates. Where my own arithmetic is used,
it is under "Inferences".

Short answer for the student: **the power flowing into the plant through Dniprovska / Ferosplavna-1 is tiny for the grid
(tens of MW out of a 12–18.5 GW peak), so it is not what hurts Ukrainians. What hurts is the 6 GW the plant no longer
gives (about a fifth of pre-war electricity), on top of Russia's strikes on the rest of the system, and the fact that the
same substations that feed the plant (Dniprovska 750 kV, Nikopol) are nodes that also serve the operating South Ukraine
NPP and the people of Nikopol district.** The lines carry no "ZNPP power": since 11 September 2022 they carry power
from Ukraine's interconnected system into the plant.

## 1. Direction and size of the flow in the two lines

### Takeaway
Since September 2022 the lines carry power *into* the plant for cooling, pumps and monitoring: Energoatom's head put it at
40–50 MW (March 2026) and Energoatom reported 11 MW on reconnection (7 September 2026). Against a winter peak of about
18.5 GW this is roughly 0.1–0.3 %: negligible for the grid's balance, though Energoatom calls its cost a real loss.

### Cited Findings
- Energoatom CEO Pavlo Kovtoniuk (4 March 2026): "Україна щодня підтримує постачання електроенергії Запорізькій АЕС,
  яке коливається від 40 до 50 МВт за годину" (Ukraine supplies the ZNPP daily with 40–50 MW); "If you count the cost of
  this electricity, then these are indeed losses." Same article: ZNPP "still remains on one line; Ferosplavna-1 is not
  repaired" (at that date). Official (operator statement). — [Interfax-Ukraine, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)
- Energoatom on reconnection, 7 September 2026, 19:02, via 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 after an outage since 20 August 2026
  (almost three weeks on diesel): "The plant's current consumption is 11 MW." — [Ukrainska Pravda, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/08/8052353/); [Ukrinform, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4161836-ukraine-restores-external-power-supply-to-zaporizhzhia-npp.html)
- Note on units: "MW per hour" in the Ukrainian quote is a loose usage; MW is a rate (power). 40–50 MW sustained is about
  1 GWh a day (see Inferences).
- History of the flow reversal: on 25 August 2022 the plant was disconnected from Ukraine's grid for the first time in
  its history (Energoatom); units were reconnected and generated again for days; on 5 September 2022 the last line was cut
  and unit 6 ran "in island mode" supplying only the plant's own needs; the plant has not generated electricity since
  11 September 2022. — [Ukrainska Pravda, 25 Aug 2022](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/08/25/7364779/); [Ukrainska Pravda, 5 Sep 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/09/5/7366161/index.amp); search summary of Interfax-Ukraine for the 11 Sep 2022 date [snippet]
- In island mode in September 2022, unit 6 ran "at a critically low capacity level (from 114 to 140 MW)" powering only
  the plant's own needs (this was a reactor running, not the cold-shutdown load). [snippet] — [Energoatom (via search result)](https://energoatom.com.ua/en/news/zaporiz-ka-aes-povnistju-zupynena)
- All six reactors in cold shutdown since April 2024, but they and the spent fuel pools still need constant cooling. — [Euromaidan Press, 21 Oct 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)
- When the lines are lost, diesel generators take over: in a prolonged blackout they burn about 150 t of fuel a day
  (HUR, Ukrainian military intelligence); no diesel deliveries since March 2026 (IAEA, 16 Oct 2026); since late
  September 2026 only 4 of 20 emergency generators start automatically. — [Euromaidan Press, 21 Oct 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)
- HUR says Russia rejected Ukraine's proposal to supply power through the 750 kV Dniprovska line (October 2026). —
  [Euromaidan Press, 21 Oct 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)
- Context for "is this a burden": winter 2024/25 peak demand expected 18.5 GW, summer about 12 GW (IEA, official
  estimate). — [IEA, Ukraine's Energy Security and the Coming Winter (2024), exec. summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security-and-the-coming-winter/executive-summary)
- Russia's 2022 counter-offer: Russian Deputy PM Khusnullin said the plant would supply Russia and Ukraine "so long as
  Ukraine paid Russia"; Ukrenergo called supply from it to Russia "physically impossible". — [Bellona, May 2022](https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/2022-05-russia-says-ukraine-could-pay-for-power-from-besieged-zaporizhzhya-plant)

### Inferences
- 40–50 MW is 0.22–0.27 % of an 18.5 GW winter peak and 0.33–0.42 % of a 12 GW summer peak; 11 MW is about 0.06 % of
  the winter peak. Sustained 45 MW ≈ 1.1 GWh/day ≈ 0.39 TWh/year ≈ 0.4 % of Ukraine's 2023 generation (103 TWh, EIA
  below) and about 1 % of what the ZNPP itself used to produce in a year (~40 TWh). In grid terms it is a rounding error;
  the plant's draw is roughly the size of one small town's load.
- Ukraine could cut the plant off to "save" power, but that would be the nuclear-safety lever in reverse: the flow is a
  safety supply, paid for by Ukraine, to a plant Russia holds. That is the "hostage" structure in one picture: Ukraine
  pays a small, continuous cost to keep someone else's seized reactors cool.
- The 11 MW vs. 40–50 MW difference probably reflects different moments (decay heat falling over years of cold shutdown,
  fewer systems running after a blackout, or the 40–50 MW figure including Enerhodar/plant-site loads). Not resolved by
  the sources.

### Gaps
- No Ukrenergo, IAEA or independent figure for the plant's cold-shutdown auxiliary load was found; the two numbers are both
  Energoatom's. Whether the 40–50 MW includes supply to Enerhodar's town or the thermal plant site is not stated.
- No source on who pays (Energoatom vs. the market), the tariff, or any billing arrangement for this supply.
- No expert statement found that explicitly calls the draw "negligible" or a "burden" for the grid; the inference above is
  mine from the numbers.

## 2. The real impact on Ukraine's grid: losing 6 GW, and how Ukraine compensated

### Takeaway
The ZNPP was about 20–25 % of Ukraine's electricity (≈40 TWh/yr) and ~43 % of nuclear capacity. Losing it was the
largest single loss, but by 2024–26 the deficits that cause blackouts (up to 5–7 GW at winter peaks) come mainly from
Russia's strikes on thermal and hydro plants, substations and the links from the three remaining nuclear plants. Ukraine
compensated with the other three NPPs (Rivne, Khmelnytskyi, South Ukraine; nuclear now 50–60 % of generation), EU
imports (cap rising from 1.7 to 2.45 GW), repaired units, distributed generation and demand cuts (rolling outages).

### Cited Findings
**The ZNPP's pre-war weight**
- In 2021 the ZNPP was 43 % of Ukraine's nuclear generation capacity and its operating capacity exceeded 20 % of total
  generation of the Integrated Power System (UNDP damage assessment, June 2023). — [UNDP, Energy Damage Assessment, Jun 2023](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2023-06/undp-ua-energy-damage-assessment.pdf) [snippet]
- Pre-invasion output about 40 TWh/yr, 20–25 % of Ukraine's electricity (estimate). — [Green Deal Ukraina, S. Nies, "Building Nuclear Transparency"](https://greendealukraina.org/assets/images/reports/building-nuclear-transparency.pdf) [snippet]; NS Energy gives "up to 42,000 GWh … about 20 % of national output" — [NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant/) [snippet]
- ZNPP 6 GW nameplate, "once supplied about a fifth of Ukraine's power"; the neighbouring Zaporizhzhia TPP (2.8 GW) has
  also been occupied since March 2022. — [Kyiv Post, 6 Jul 2024](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/35398)
- EIA: ZNPP combined capacity 5,700 MW (net); "largely not generating electricity, which has led to an estimated $5.2
  billion in lost revenue for Energoatom". — [EIA Country Analysis Brief: Ukraine, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- Energoatom: lost output from the occupation as of 27 February 2026 = 155.02 million MWh (155 TWh); foregone benefit
  UAH 598.9 billion (company calculation via Energoreforma). — [Interfax-Ukraine, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)

**Capacity losses overall (the ZNPP is one part)**
- Losses in the first year of war from occupation, destruction or damage: 19 GW; after the spring 2024 strikes capacity
  fell "to just 12 GW" (IEA). — [IEA 2024 report PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cec49dc2-7d04-442f-92aa-54c18e6f51d6/UkrainesEnergySecurityandtheComingWinter.pdf) [snippet]
- About half of generation capacity occupied, destroyed or damaged in 2022–23; by mid-2024 only about a third of pre-war
  capacity remained; roughly half of large network substations damaged; summer 2024 generation fell more than 2 GW below a
  12 GW peak; winter 2024/25 deficit could reach 6 GW (≈ Denmark's peak) even with 1.7 GW of imports. — [IEA exec. summary, 2024](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security-and-the-coming-winter/executive-summary)
- Strikes from March 2024 removed more than 9 GW: Zmiivska TPP 2.3 GW (22 Mar 2024), Trypilska TPP 1.8 GW (11 Apr 2024),
  Burshtyn + Ladyzhyn TPPs 2 GW, DniproHES over 1 GW (22 Mar 2024). — [Kyiv Post, 6 Jul 2024](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/35398); DniproHES "lost 50 % of its generating capacity" — [EIA, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- Wikipedia's crisis article (secondary) counts the ZNPP's occupation as "a loss of 6 GW of generation capacities" and
  totals 43.5 GW lost over four years, with 25–30 GW permanently destroyed or badly damaged (estimates). — [Wikipedia, Ukrainian energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_energy_crisis) [snippet]

**The deficits and blackouts (caused mainly by strikes)**
- 7 February 2026: Russia's "worst-ever" attack on the substations linking the three operating NPPs to the grid cut
  nuclear output by about 50 % and forced one reactor to shut down; Ukrenergo CEO Vitaliy Zaichenko: "We lost half the
  generation capacity right after the attack." — [Kyiv Independent, 9 Feb 2026](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraine-repairs-nuclear-substation-but-situation-remains-critical-after-mass-attack/)
- After those strikes, 4–4.5 queues of scheduled outages nationwide. — [Ukrainska Pravda, 7 Feb 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/02/07/8019842/) [snippet]
- Zaichenko (28 Jan 2026): the shortfall cannot be covered by existing generation or imports; Ukrenergo auctions added
  423 MW of new capacity over the past year. — [Ukrainska Pravda, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/01/28/8018311/)
- February 2026: grid shortage "at times 5–6 GW" (Interfax). — [Interfax, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)
- January 2026 in Kyiv: nearly a million without power on 25 January; ~3,000 buildings without heating after the 20 January
  attack; blackouts of over 48 hours at times in subzero temperatures. [snippet] — [Kyiv Post energy coverage](https://www.kyivpost.com/topic/energy?page=14); BBC (via Nikvesti): Ukrainians "can spend up to 20 hours a day without electricity or heating" — [Nikvesti](https://nikvesti.com/en/news/public/bbc-ukrainians-spend-20-hours-day-without-electricity-heating-winter) [snippet]
- DiXi Group: nuclear output could be constrained by 1.5–2.0 GW if 750 kV substations are damaged (Winter Outlook 2025). —
  [DiXi Group, Winter Outlook 2025: Electricity](https://dixigroup.org/en/analytic/winter-outlook-2025-electricity/) [snippet]
- Distributed generation fell short: a former Ukrenergo chief says about 700 MW built against ~7,000 MW needed (Aug 2026). —
  [Euromaidan Press, 11 Aug 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/08/11/ukraines-distributed-generation-program-has-failed-it-needed-7000-mw-of-small-plants-to-outlast-russian-strikes-but-built-only-700-says-former-grid-chief/) (headline-level)

**Compensation**
- Nuclear became the backbone: ~55 % pre-war, ~55–60 % in 2025 (DiXi estimate); 50.6 % in 2023 (EIA). — [DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/analytic/winter-outlook-2025-electricity/) [snippet]; [EIA, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- Grid synchronised with continental Europe after the invasion (emergency), made permanent end of 2023; trade capacity
  raised from 1,200 to 1,700 MW; imports from Europe rose 94 % after interconnection (Ukrenergo estimate). — [EIA, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- Import cap 2.1 GW from December 2024, 2.45 GW for Ukraine + Moldova since January 2026 (≈2.1 GW commercially available
  to Ukraine); February 2026 imports a record 1,262,800 MWh (1.26 TWh), five times February 2025 (244,200 MWh); Hungary
  49 %, Romania 19 %, Slovakia 18 %, Poland 13 %; 89.5 % use of the 2.1 GW. — [Interfax, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)
- Imports were 4.1 TWh in 2022 and 3.3 TWh in 2023; exports 6.1 TWh both years. — [EIA, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)

### Inferences
- The ZNPP's 6 GW is about the same size as the IEA's whole projected winter deficit (6 GW, 2024/25) and Interfax's
  5–6 GW shortage (Feb 2026). It is fair to say: "if the ZNPP were producing for Ukraine, the plant alone could roughly
  cover the worst winter deficit" (6 GW × typical availability), but that is counterfactual; it also needs the 750 kV
  lines and substations Russia keeps hitting, and the cooling water lost with the Kakhovka dam (section 6).
- Attribution: by 2024–26 the deficit is driven by strikes (thermal, hydro, CHP, substations). The ZNPP loss is a
  structural, permanent hole underneath; the strikes are the swings that turn it into outages. I found no study that
  apportions the deficit between "ZNPP lost" and "strike damage".
- 155 TWh lost (Energoatom) ≈ 1.5 years of Ukraine's whole 2023 output (103 TWh).

### Gaps
- No source quantifies what share of 2022–26 outage-hours is attributable to the ZNPP's loss.
- ENTSO-E synchronisation date 16 March 2022 (given in the brief) was not re-verified in this pass; EIA confirms the
  emergency synchronisation and its permanence.
- No authoritative national count of people without power or average outage hours for winter 2025/26 was found.

## 4. Where the power in the Dniprovska and Ferosplavna-1 lines comes from

### Takeaway
There is no "source plant" for these lines: they hang off two nodes of Ukraine's interconnected 750/330 kV system
(the Unified Energy System, synchronised with continental Europe). Power in a meshed AC grid is pooled; what flows into
the plant at any moment is drawn from whatever is generating in the system: mostly the three operating NPPs (South
Ukraine is the nearest, and its external supply partly passes through the same Dniprovska substation), plus remaining thermal and
hydro in the Dnipro region and EU imports. The Dniprovska 750/330 kV substation is a major node (three 750 kV lines,
six 330 kV links, two 1,000 MVA autotransformers).

### Cited Findings
- Ukrenergo's own page: PS 750 kV "Dniprovska", 750/330 kV, 1,998 MVA, Dnipropetrovsk oblast; connected to the 750 kV
  network by **three lines** and with **six links to the 330 kV network**; two 750/330 kV autotransformers of 1,000 MVA
  each; max generation connectable to the 750 kV buses 300 MW; a reconstruction contract (2020). [snippet; page returned
  403 to fetch] — [Ukrenergo, ПС 750 кВ «Дніпровська»](https://ua.energy/energo_substation/ps-750-kv-dniprovska/); reconstruction contract with a European consortium (Dalekovod/GE, €31.7 m) — [ExPro](https://expro.com.ua/novini/kompanya-ukrenergo-pdpisala-kontrakt-z-vropeyskim-konsorcumom-na-rekonstrukcyu-ps-750-kv-dnprovska); [StrategEast](https://www.strategeast.org/ukrainian-ukrenergo-croatian-german-dalekovod-general-electric-sign-contract-worth-e31-7-million/) [snippets]
- The Dniprovska substation also carries external power for the **South Ukraine NPP**: a fire at the 750 kV Dniprovska
  substation from military action on 22 May 2026 led to South Ukraine NPP being "partially disconnected from external
  power" at the grid operator's request (IAEA, reported by weukraine.tv). — [Ми Україна, 22 May 2026](https://weukraine.tv/novyny/pivdennoukrajinsku-aes-chastkovo-vidkljuchili-vid-zovnishnoho-elektropostachannja-cherez-pozhezhu-na-pidstantsiji-dniprovska)
- The Dniprovska substation is "more than 100 km north-west" of the ZNPP (IAEA via UP) / "about 100 km north" (Greenpeace).

  occupied areas (damaged in fighting) (NYT/Greenpeace via Moscow Times). — [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/06/russia-building-power-lines-to-restart-occupied-ukrainian-nuclear-plant)

- The IAEA named the Nikopol end the "Nikopolska substation" for the 2–3 June 2026 strike. — [IAEA on X](https://x.com/iaeaorg/status/2062046271766945950); [Eastern Herald, 3 Jun 2026](https://easternherald.com/2026/06/03/znpp-power-outage-17th-time-nikopolska-drone-iaea/)
- Ukraine's grid is synchronised with continental Europe (ENTSO-E), so imports flow into the same system that feeds these
  nodes. — [EIA, June 2025](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/countries_short/Ukraine/Ukraine.pdf)
- Topology sources: Ukrenergo publishes substation pages (above); the project's own config traces the lines from
  OpenStreetMap (power=line ways). OpenInfraMap renders OSM power data. ENTSO-E publishes a transmission map (not checked
  here for Ukraine's detail).

### Inferences
- Physically, the 11–50 MW into the plant is a share of the whole system's flow; it is most accurate to say "the power
  comes from Ukraine's grid, in which the three remaining nuclear plants supply about half, plus thermal, hydro, solar and
  imports from the EU". Given proximity and the 750 kV link at Dniprovska, South Ukraine NPP and the Dnipro-region plants
  (e.g., Prydniprovska and Kryvorizka TPPs) are likely large contributors to the local node, but power flow is not
  traceable to a plant without load-flow data.
- Film framing: the 750 kV Dniprovska line is the plant's tie into the national backbone; Ferosplavna-1 is a 330 kV tie
  into a regional, industrial node at Nikopol (ferroalloy plant load). Both endpoints are substations that matter to
  Ukrainians independently of the ZNPP.

### Gaps
- I did not find Ukrenergo or ENTSO-E data naming the three 750 kV lines at Dniprovska (e.g., whether one runs to South
  Ukraine NPP directly) or load-flow figures; Ukrenergo has restricted grid maps since 2022 for security.
- No confirmed status of Prydniprovska / Kryvorizka TPPs in 2026 was gathered in this pass.
- DniproHES damage date (22 Mar 2024) is cited; its 2026 status was not checked.

Dniprovska substation (12 Oct 2022 missile; 22 May 2026 drones, partly disconnecting South Ukraine NPP; 750 kV section

## 6. Russia's plan to connect the ZNPP to its grid, and the political stakes

### Takeaway
Russia is building lines in occupied territory (≈80 km Berdiansk–Mariupol, seen May 2025; ≈86 km Melitopol–Henichesk,
Jan–July 2026, disrupted by Ukrainian drone strikes) to tie the plant into the occupied/Russian grid; Rosatom says the
grid is one of the conditions for restart, and has issued a Russian licence. Peace talks (late 2025–2026) put US
operation or control of the plant on the table; Ukraine rejects any Russian role; experts say splitting output between
unsynchronised grids is impractical. A restart would also need cooling water lost with the Kakhovka dam.

### Cited Findings
- May 2025: satellite images (Greenpeace, analysed by the NYT) show ~80 km of new lines and pylons from occupied Berdiansk
  to Mariupol, where a large substation connects to the plant farther west; Rosatom's Likhachev said replacing the grid is
  one of four key steps before restart; Greenpeace's Shaun Burnie: "the first physical evidence of … Putin's plan for
  restarting"; possible eventual connection to Russia's Rostov grid. (The Moscow Times page's URL carries 2026/08/06 but the
  content is dated 27 May 2025.) — [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/06/russia-building-power-lines-to-restart-occupied-ukrainian-nuclear-plant); [Boston Globe/NYT, 27 May 2025](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/05/27/world/satellite-images-suggest-russian-plan-restart-seized-ukrainian-nuclear-plant)
- July 2026: Greenpeace's satellite analysis (Jan–Jul 2026) shows a new high-voltage line along the M18 between Melitopol
  and Henichesk, nearly complete in May, disrupted by Ukrainian drone strikes incl. on the Henichesk substation in early
  July; intended to connect the plant to occupied territory incl. Crimea and the Russian grid; "86 km" of new line by

### Inferences
- Losing the plant permanently = the 6 GW / ~40 TWh hole becomes structural; Ukraine's grid has already been rebuilt
  around its absence (imports, three NPPs, new nuclear units planned at Khmelnytskyi and South Ukraine per EIA). Getting it
  back = not quick power: years of restart, a cooling-water problem, depleted staff, and 750 kV links (Dniprovska
  substation, the lines through occupied land) to rebuild.
- If Russia connects it to its own grid, the plant's lines to Ukraine would become a frontier between two unsynchronised
  systems (Ukraine is in ENTSO-E, Russia's south is not), which is why "sharing" output is technically hard.

### Gaps

- No confirmed status of any Russian line energised to the plant as of October 2026.
- The FT 28 Oct 2026 report is unverified here.

## 7. The economic dimension

### Takeaway
Energoatom counts 155 TWh not produced and UAH 598.9 bn of foregone benefit by February 2026 (EIA cited $5.2 bn lost
revenue earlier); Ukraine pays continuously for the plant's own power; wholesale prices are among Europe's highest and
capped; electricity-hungry industry such as Nikopol's ferroalloy plant has shut.

### Cited Findings
- 155.02 million MWh underproduced; UAH 598.9 bn foregone benefit (Energoatom, 27 Feb 2026); Kovtoniuk: the supply to
  the plant is "indeed losses". — [Interfax-Ukraine, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)

  21 days; price cap UAH 15,000/MWh (18 Jan–31 Mar 2026). — [Interfax, 4 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)

  [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/ukraines-ferroalloy-exports-nearly-halt-in-early-2026-amid-energy-crisis/) [snippet]; [GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/opinion/ferroalloy-plants-operated-at-minimum-capacity-in-2025-at-10/) [snippet]

  [PRM.ua](https://prm.ua/en/90-6-billion-is-needed-to-restore-the-energy-sector/) [snippet]; [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/02/23/updated-ukraine-recovery-and-reconstruction-needs-assessment-released)

### Inferences
- At the 2025 average DAM price (UAH 5,292.62/MWh), 45 MW for a year (~394 GWh) would be roughly UAH 2.1 bn (≈ $50 m)
