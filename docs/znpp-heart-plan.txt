THE END RESULT: ONE FILM, AT MOST 10 MINUTES (decided with the user, 25 October 2026)
======================================================================================================
The project ends in one developed film of at most 10 minutes: a high-quality video exploration in the manner of
computational design visualisation, showing the project's intricate parts. Narrative and storytelling, graphics,
data and cinematography carry equal weight. The collection, the satellite passes, the corridor and zoom-out films,
the Scene Viewer and the site are its research and its sequences, not the deliverable.
- 10 minutes is a ceiling, not a target: every sequence earns its time; a draft that runs long is cut, not sped up.
- Story first: what each sequence tells and where it sits (chapters, turns, held moments, silence), then how it looks.
- Cinematography on purpose: camera moves, holds, pacing, sound and type decided and recorded, never defaults.
- The evidence rules hold on screen (HONESTY RULES below): every drawn fact sourced in film.json, nothing inferred
  shown as observed, no generated imagery, the synthesized and the drawn said to be so.
- Quality: final renders at 60 fps (tests at 30), 1920x1080 or larger, sound mixed with the picture; end credits for
  the imagery (Copernicus), OpenStreetMap (ODbL), GeoNames and the record's sources.

======================================================================================================
THE DIRECTION
======================================================================================================
The Zaporizhzhia NPP (ZNPP) as the contested heart of the territory; its 750 kV and 330 kV lines as arteries.
The inversion is the story: the heart has stopped beating and the arteries now feed it. Before September 2022 the
plant sent power out to Ukraine; since then power flows in across the front line to keep the shut-down reactors
cooling, with diesel generators as the last resort.

INTERVENTION (chosen 21 October 2026): THE HUM
======================================================================================================
An immersive sound architecture of the plant's lines, made from real sounds of live lines.

THE INSTRUMENT AT THE HEART (updated 21 October 2026, the user's idea)
- The listener is dropped into the ZNPP itself, in an abstracted, imagined form: an invented site where every line
  from the plant ends close enough to be heard at once. Labelled as an imagined site, never a model of the real plant.
- The result is a symphony of hums: all lines live is loud and full; one line left is a single thin voice; none is
  silence (then the diesel generators). How many arteries are left is heard, not read.
- The object: a new musical instrument hooked up to all the lines, transmitting their status audibly to someone
  inside the plant. Each line is a voice (a string); its timbre by voltage (750 kV vs 330 kV), its level by what the
  record says it carried.
- The lines arrive at the instrument from their real bearings (the 750 kV lines from their real directions, the
  330 kV from the thermal plant's switchyard), so the symphony keeps the geography: you hear where each artery goes.
- Its listener: the people inside the plant (its staff), who would know by ear how many lines are left. A related
  image: a hospital's heart monitor, where the sound is the status and the flatline is the alarm.
- A render / animation: the instrument stays in one spot, immovable and permanent, while the region around it
  changes through time (see HISTORY below). It is silent before the lines exist, gains a voice as each line is
  built, plays the full chord in the plant's working years, then falls apart into single voices and silence in the
  war as lines are cut and reconnected.
- NO sounds of fighting or war (decided with the user: too sensitive). Only the lines, the generators, and the
  silence between.

Why it works
- An outage stops being a date on a chart; a voice drops out. It answers "why care about the lines" through the body.
- One place holds all the lines: the instrument gathers what is spread over hundreds of km into one listening point.
- The real site can't be visited (Enerhodar occupied, the bank shelled), so the heart is reached only as an imagined
  place, and says so.

The physics (check before using)
- Ukraine's grid runs at 50 Hz; the hum (transformers, corona) is at twice that: 100 Hz.
- Corona crackle follows VOLTAGE: a connected line crackles (louder in rain, louder at 750 kV than 330 kV); a cut
  line is silent.
- The magnetic hum follows CURRENT (how much power flows; recorded with an induction coil). Before 2022 the 750 kV
  lines carried up to gigawatts out (roughly): loud. Since shutdown, a much smaller draw for cooling flows in: a
  whisper. The inversion is heard as a change in loudness.
- The DIRECTION of flow can't be heard, only its size. Say so; don't invent a sound for it.
=> Two layers per line: crackle (connected or cut) and magnetic hum (how much flows).

Two kinds of silence (the key design decision)
- A dead line: the record says it was cut.
- A gap in the record: no source says either way.
They must sound different (e.g. dead silence vs a filtered noise floor). The record is complete only for the ZNPP's
own lines (the IAEA names each line and date); other lines are left out or given the "unknown" texture.
Possible theme: "the record is silent, not the line".

Other listening spots (earlier idea, now secondary: possible extra stations out along the lines)
- Under the crossing pylons on the drained bed (km 5.2): two 330 kV circuits plus the 750 kV line overhead.
- Nikopol embankment: the lines heard faintly across the water, facing the plant.
- Ferosplavna-330 substation: a node, with transformer hum, where Ferosplavna-1 arrives.
- Under Dniprovska (750 kV): the main line, the last one left in the long single-line periods.

HISTORY: what the instrument "hears" around it over time (all to check against sources)
- Before the lines: the Great Meadow (Velykyi Luh) floodplain, then the Kakhovka reservoir filled in the 1950s
  (dam 1956). No hum: the instrument is silent.
- The user recalled a coal mine before the plant: TO CHECK. What I know: the Zaporizhzhia thermal plant at Enerhodar
  (1970s) burned coal and gas brought in; the mines nearby are the Nikopol manganese basin on the north bank
  (Marhanets, Pokrov), open pits. Check what stood on the site itself.
- Enerhodar founded about 1970 for the thermal plant; its 330 kV lines first voices.
- The ZNPP's six units, about 1984-1995; its 750 kV lines join as they were built (line build dates: to find).
- The working years: the full chord.
- 2022 on: seizure (4 March 2022), the grid supply stops (September 2022), outages, single voices, silence, diesel.
- 6 June 2023: the dam destroyed, the reservoir drains (the setting changes; the instrument stays).
- Sounds of a period are only sounds the record supports (the lines, generators). Nothing invented to stand for an
  era, and no fighting.
- Imagery for the changing surroundings can be real for every era (USGS / archive, free):
  WWII German aerial photographs (1940s, before the flooding), Corona spy satellite (declassified, 1960s-1972),
  Landsat (1984 on: the ZNPP's construction visible), Sentinel-2 (2017 on). Each labelled with its date and source.

Time
- March 2022 to October 2026: about 28 minutes at a day a second (too long); about 4 minutes at a week a second, but
  outages of a few hours vanish. The full history (1950s on) needs a much faster scale for the early decades.
- Options: event-driven time (slows round each outage, then speeds up), or a minimum audible length for every event
  (honest only if a visible clock says so). Either way, show the date while it plays.

Sounds
- These lines can't be recorded. Record other 330 kV and 750 kV lines, or use licensed field recordings (Freesound
  has some; check each licence). Label every recording with its place and date: "a 330 kV line", not "this one".
- If possible, record under a real line: an ordinary mic for crackle, an induction coil for the magnetic hum.
- No sounds of fighting or war at all (decided with the user). When the last line goes, the real sequence is the
  diesel generators starting (20 of them): a labelled recording of a large diesel generator.

Making it architecture, not just a soundscape
- The instrument itself is the designed object: where each line terminates, how the voices are arranged by their
  real bearings, the room or structure a listener stands in at the heart. To design.
- On the web: the instrument at the plant; each line plays from its real direction through headphones (the
  browser's Web Audio API), with the date running. Could become a view in the site.
- As an installation: a room built as the instrument. Speakers for each line arriving from its real bearing; each
  falls quiet when the record says its line was cut; the listener stands where the plant would be.
- A render / animation: the instrument fixed in place while the surroundings change through the eras (HISTORY).
- Tied to the linear zone (below): the instrument is where the zone's lines are gathered and heard. TO CONFIRM
  whether the zone stays as the framing.

Playback for the final presentation (discussed 21 October 2026; low-tech, to test)
- Main: a ring of 4 wired speakers round the reviewers (north, east, south, west), lines panned by real bearing,
  from a pre-rendered 4-channel file. Needs a 4-output USB audio interface; borrow speakers / interface from the
  school's media or AV lab if possible. Bluetooth speakers can't be kept in sync.
- Fallback: a stereo pair (lines panned west-east), or a binaural (headphone) render, which works on any headphones.
- AirPods head tracking: only through Apple's own apps / a Dolby Atmos mix (e.g. Logic Pro); not from a browser;
  one pair for several reviewers is slow, and headphones cut reviewers off from each other. Not the main plan.
- The 100 Hz hum itself is hard to place by ear (low sounds aren't localised) and small speakers barely play it:
  direction comes from the crackle and the harmonics, so every voice needs those.
- Pitch / timbre: line n on the nth harmonic of 100 Hz (all real multiples of the hum), so all lines make a full
  chord and each cut takes a harmonic out; 750 kV lower and denser, 330 kV higher. A design choice, labelled.
  Listeners won't count ten voices, but will hear full / fewer / one / none.
- Note: since late 2022 the record is mostly one or two lines (Dniprovska 750 kV, Ferosplavna-1 330 kV backup:
  check), so the war years are a duet that flickers; the full chord is the pre-war years.

Precedents (know them; set yourself apart)
- Christina Kubisch, Electrical Walks: headphones that make electromagnetic fields audible.
- Jacob Kirkegaard, 4 Rooms: recordings in empty rooms in the Chernobyl zone.
- Lawrence Abu Hamdan: earwitness testimony, forensic listening.
- Alvin Lucier, Music on a Long Thin Wire.
What sets this apart: the score is the line record (built, connected, cut). Kubisch lets you hear fields; this
plays a sourced history of a contested grid, gathered into one instrument.

Suggested first step for the Hum
A rough browser prototype of the instrument: every ZNPP line as a voice from its real bearing, played through the
plant's outage record 2022-2026, placeholder sounds until there are recordings. Tests the feeling (does one line
vs all read clearly by ear?) cheaply, like the low-resolution film. The longer history comes after.

FRAMING ARGUMENT (chosen earlier on 21 October 2026; may frame the Hum): THE PROTECTION ZONE SHOULD BE A LINE, NOT A CIRCLE
======================================================================================================
- In 2022 the IAEA (Grossi) proposed a nuclear safety and security protection zone around the plant. It was never
  agreed. (Check against sources: proposed after the IAEA's September 2022 mission; I believe it was replaced in
  May 2023 by five principles put to the UN Security Council; whether a boundary was ever published.)
- A zone round the plant protects the wrong thing: the plant's safety depends on lines running many km away, across
  the front, where the outages happen.
- The proposal: the zone the diplomacy never drew, as a linear zone along the plant's lines, including the local
  ceasefire windows that repairs need. Architecture as treaty (reference: Keller Easterling, Extrastatecraft).
- Other candidate parts along it (not chosen): the Datum (a band on the crossing towers at the old reservoir level,
  about 16 m above sea level: check), the Meadow Line (the cleared strip under the lines kept as meadow habitat), the
  Pulse (lights on only while power flows to the plant: film or post-war only), the Bypass (distributed generation
  along the corridor, sited where VIIRS shows the lights fell most).
- In the film: red is only ever what is real (the OSM lines); proposals in a second colour (e.g. dashed white).

======================================================================================================
HONESTY RULES
======================================================================================================
- VIIRS shows lights falling, not why: curfews, light discipline, rolling outages, clouds, snow and moonlight also
  dim it. Say "lights fell after...", never "this strike caused...".
- Line-by-line damage across Ukraine is mostly secret in wartime. Close focus on damage stays on the ZNPP's own lines
  (the IAEA names them); damage elsewhere is shown through the lights.
- OpenStreetMap is today's map and may be stale in occupied areas. Cross-check against a Ukrenergo or ENTSO-E grid
  map if one can be found.
- The country scale needs its own map projection (the corridor's grid only holds over about 30 km), with a crossfade
  where the two scales meet.
- Immersive pieces are intervention, not evidence: they may be invented, but are labelled as reconstruction or
  proposal. Recordings are labelled with where and when they were made.
