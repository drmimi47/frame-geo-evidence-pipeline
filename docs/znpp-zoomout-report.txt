THE CONDUCTIVE SEAM: WHAT THE ZOOM-OUT SAYS, AND WHAT TO DO NEXT
Report, 22 October 2026, on draft 3 of the zoom-out
(libraries/conductive-seam/satellite/networks/znpp/films/film-20261022-005221/film.mp4, 64 s).
A report on an early draft (the film it names was a test render, since removed); the end result is now one film of at
most 10 minutes: see THE END RESULT in docs/znpp-heart-plan.txt. Read with docs/znpp-heart-plan.txt. Facts marked [check] still need a source before they go in the film or on a slide.

======================================================================================================
1. WHAT EXISTS NOW
======================================================================================================
- 0-7 s: the Zaporizhzhia NPP on Sentinel-2. The six units are outlined one by one and numbered, under the plant's
  full name.
- 7-15 s: the camera starts pulling back. No lines yet.
- 15-56 s: all nine lines leave together at one speed. Each lands at its own substation, so a line's length becomes
  its arrival time (Ferosplavna at 34 km lands first, Pivdennodonbaska at 271 km last).
- On the way out: the satellite imagery gives way to a dark map. The Great Meadow draws itself in. The rest of the
  750/330 kV grid appears faintly, and the towns come in.
- 56-64 s: a hold on the whole network, about 470 km wide, then a fade.
- Look: deep blue lines, brightest at the plant and fading toward their ends. White capitals on half-black boxes.
- Accuracy: every line is traced in OpenStreetMap to the substation the config names. Kakhovska 750 is checked
  against its official length, and the stepped stretches against the mapped towers.

What it says well: the plant is not a point. It is the knot of a network that reaches across the south and east of
Ukraine, and that network's size is the first surprise (271 km to Pivdennodonbaska, Zaporizhzhia city 190 km away by
wire). The opening on the units, then the pull-back, makes "the heart" literal without saying it.

What it does not say yet: almost everything that makes the project an argument rather than a utility map:
- that the network is contested;
- that it has been cut again and again;
- that the plant now depends on it rather than feeding it;
- that the Great Meadow lies between the plant and the only lines still holding;
- that the safety zone everyone talks about is the wrong shape.
At the moment it is a beautiful explainer of a 2021 grid. The next steps below are about turning it into the project.

======================================================================================================
2. THE BIGGEST THEMATIC ISSUE: WHICH WAY DOES THE POWER FLOW?
======================================================================================================
The new fade (bright at the plant, dim at the ends) reads as radiance: a source pouring power out to the region.
That was true before the war: six units of about 950 MW each, more than a fifth of Ukraine's electricity (checked 5 November 2026: 22-28% by the published figures).
Since September 2022 the reverse is true:
- the reactors are shut down, all six in cold shutdown since 2024 [check the date];
- the plant draws power in through these lines to run the pumps that cool its fuel and spent-fuel pools;
- every time the last line is cut, it falls back on diesel generators. The IAEA counted 26 such complete losses of
  off-site power by August 2026 (already checked for the notes).
The heart is not pumping. It is on life support, and the lines are the drip.

That is the strongest idea the project has. The film can make it felt with one gesture:
- A. Two passes. First the lines grow out with the current fade (the plant as source: "before"). Then, after a
  caption with the date, the gradient flips: bright at the substations, dim at the plant, with a slow pulse travelling
  inward ("after"). The same lines, the opposite meaning.
- B. Grow them inward from the start: tips leave the substations and converge on the plant. The zoom-out becomes a
  closing-in. It is less spectacular, but it is the true direction now.
I'd do A: the reversal is the point, and showing both directions is more honest than choosing one.

A smaller point about the fade. It is currently by share of each line's own length, so the 34 km Ferosplavna line
fades as fast as the 271 km one. Fading by distance from the plant instead would give one field of brightness
radiating from the heart (or converging on it), the same for every line. It reads as reach rather than as decoration.
Worth trying; it is a one-line change.

======================================================================================================
3. TIME IS MISSING: THE LINES SHOULD GO GREY ONE BY ONE
======================================================================================================
The film shows one moment: today's map, 2025 imagery. But the project's subject is attrition:
- ten lines before the war (750 kV and 330 kV);
- one or two holding by 2026;
- Dniprovska lost on 24 March 2026, repaired in June, its substation too damaged to reconnect (already in the plan,
  with sources).

The reference image you chose has the answer built in: among the blue lines it has grey ones. Use that grammar:
- blue = connected, grey = disconnected.
- After the full network is drawn, run a dated sequence from the IAEA's updates. A line turns grey on the day it is
  lost and blue again if it is restored.
- Show a quiet counter: the date, and "complete losses of off-site power: n".
This stays inside the evidence rules: every change has an IAEA (or Ukrenergo or Energoatom) source with a date, and
film.json records it. It is the cross-referenced, non-speculative version of "what if all lines go dark". It also
makes the speculative part land harder: the audience will already have watched them go dark one at a time, for real.

Work needed:
- A table of line status changes, 2022–2026, from the IAEA's updates (they name lines by destination, as the film
  does).
- A `status` track in the config: line, date, state, source.
This is the most valuable next build.

======================================================================================================
4. THE GREAT MEADOW IS THE FRONT LINE: SAY SO
======================================================================================================
Your studio's site is not just nearby. Since November 2022 the Dnipro, and with it the former reservoir, has been the
line of contact between the plant (occupied, south bank) and Nikopol and Marhanets (north bank).
- The two lines that still hold, Dniprovska 750 and Ferosplavna-1 330, are the two that cross that bed going north.
- The lines that run south-east all run into occupied territory.
So the Great Meadow is the gap the plant's life support has to cross. That ties the studio brief to the whole
project in one sentence, and the film can show it:
- Add the line of contact as its own element (a thin white dashed line, not blue), from a dated public source, with
  the date in the caption. Check it against more than one source.
- After the network is drawn, push in to where Dniprovska and Ferosplavna cross the drained bed. This is the deferred
  "zoom into the two lines", and it is where the Great Meadow film (and its pylon inset at km 5.2) already lives.

Representation of the meadow itself:
- OpenStreetMap's "Великий Луг" is the whole drained reservoir down to the dam. The historical Great Meadow was the
  upper reach, flooded when the reservoir filled in 1955–56.
- Consider three dated outlines: the historical meadow (from a pre-1955 map [find one]), the reservoir (1956–2023)
  and the drained bed now (2023–), with its new willow forest visible in the Sentinel-2 close-up.
- That is the studio's site told as layers of time, without leaving the film's language.

======================================================================================================
5. THE CIRCLE AND THE LINE (THE FRAMING ARGUMENT, NOT YET IN THE FILM)
======================================================================================================
The plan argues that the plant's protection zone should be a line, not a circle. The film can make that argument
without a word:
- On the close-up, draw a thin dotted white circle round the plant: the shape of the zone that was proposed. The
  IAEA's 2022 proposal named no radius [check], so label the radius as illustrative.
- As the camera pulls back, the lines run straight out of it, and keep going for 100–270 km.
- The circle protects the reactors. What actually keeps them cool is out there, on pylons in the meadow and in
  occupied fields.
Your reference image even has a dotted white arc with the blue lines crossing it. It is the same picture.

======================================================================================================
6. LOOK AND TASTE
======================================================================================================
- Hierarchy. With every text boxed, the wide frame has about 25 boxes of nearly equal weight. Rank them:
  - line names: the strongest box;
  - lengths: inside the same box, smaller (as now);
  - towns: smaller type, a lighter box (around 30%), or none at all once the frame is wide. They are only there for
    orientation.
  - the plant's name: the only bold text in the frame.
- Voltage. 750 vs 330 kV is now only a width difference of 2.6 vs 1.7 px, and hard to see. Two blues would make the
  two systems readable at a glance, as two families of voices (which the Hum needs anyway): the 750 kV lines deep
  blue, the 330 kV lines a paler, greyer blue.
- Crowding. South-east of the plant, Zaporizka 750, Zaporizka 330, Pivdennodonbaska and Melitopolska run together
  for the first 40 km and their labels bunch mid-film. Options: name a shared corridor once ("4 LINES") until the lines
  part, or hold labels back until each line is clear of the others.
- The ground. The satellite imagery fades to a flat dark map at about 50 km. The dark map is honest but empty.
  VIIRS night light (on hold) would give that ground a meaning: where the electricity actually reaches, and where it
  went out. When you're ready, it is the natural next layer: the lines as wires, the night lights as what they feed.
- Tempo. 15 s on the plant before any line appears is right for a presentation (it lets you speak), but in a 5-minute
  film the growth (40 s) is the only motion. Give each later section its own single gesture (the greying, the push in,
  the silence) rather than more camera moves.
- The ending. It ends on a static diagram. End on the heart again: the camera returns to the plant at the end of the
  greying sequence, or holds on the meadow crossing.

======================================================================================================
7. SOUND: LET THE FILM PREVIEW THE HUM
======================================================================================================
The intervention is sound, and the film is silent. Even a rough sketch would connect them in the presentation:
- Each line is a voice that enters as its line leaves the plant. Its pitch or timbre comes from its voltage, its
  loudness from its brightness.
- In the greying sequence a voice drops out when its line goes grey.
- The base is Ukraine's 50 Hz grid (in sync with Europe's since March 2022); a transformer's hum is at 100 Hz.
- No war sounds (as decided): only the instrument thinning out.
- Play it on the speaker-ring layout from the plan, or binaural on headphones.
The film then becomes the score for the Hum, and the review sees the instrument and the map as one work.

======================================================================================================
8. HONESTY: WHAT THE DRAWING CLAIMS WITHOUT MEANING TO
======================================================================================================
- A continuous bright line says "working". OpenStreetMap shows lines as mapped, not as live. Until the status
  sequence exists, the end caption should say so ("the network as mapped, not as working").
- OSM may be stale in occupied areas. There are reports of new lines planned or built to tie the plant into the
  Russian grid [check: IAEA, Reuters]. If they're documented, they belong in the film as the contested future of the
  network: drawn in a different style, dated and sourced. If they aren't, leave them out.
- The "Pivdennodonbaska" name is still not found in IAEA reporting. Keep it, but take it from Bellona's map or the
  operator's records, and say which.
- Speculation stays visibly apart: a change of ground or a title card for "what if all the lines go dark", so the
  audience never confuses the speculative section with the sourced sequence before it.
