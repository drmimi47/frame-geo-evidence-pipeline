# Russia's counterclaims about the ZNPP and the attribution problem (March 2022 to November 2026)

Researched 9 November 2026. The IAEA's own site (iaea.org) returned HTTP 402 to automated fetches, so IAEA wording is
taken from mirrors (globalsecurity.org reposts of IAEA updates) and trade press (World Nuclear News, NucNet, ANS),
which quote the updates. Where only a search summary or a secondary write-up was available, that is said. Party
claims are marked **[RU claim]** (Russian MoD, Kremlin, Rosatom, the occupation administration, Russian diplomats,
Russian state media) and **[UA claim]** (Energoatom, Ukrainian government, military, NSDC). None of these is
evidence of who fired unless an independent body says so.

## Dated record of major incidents: each side's account and independent attribution

### Takeaway
In almost every major incident (the March 2022 assault, the August–November 2022 shelling, the April 2024 drone
strikes, the August 2024 cooling-tower fire, the September 2025 line cut, the May 2026 turbine-hall strike) Russia and
Ukraine blamed each other and the IAEA described the damage without naming who fired. Independent attribution exists
for only a few events, all from journalists or OSINT groups, not from the IAEA, UN or OSCE: NPR's video analysis of the
March 2022 assault (Russian forces fired on the site), and the Centre for Information Resilience's geolocation of an MLRS
launch beside the plant and of a shell fired into Enerhodar from Russian-held ground (September 2022).

### Cited Findings

- [RU claim] Russian MoD spokesman Igor Konashenkov said a Russian National Guard patrol was fired on near the plant at
  about 2 a.m., returned fire, and the attackers set the training centre alight as they withdrew; he called it a
  "monstrous provocation" and said Kyiv wanted to pin radioactive contamination on Russia — [Inquirer/Reuters](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1563482/russia-blames-attack-at-nuclear-power-station-on-ukrainian-saboteurs); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/214091/Russia+called+the+Incident+at+the+Nuclear+Power+Plant+a+%E2%80%9CMonstrous+Provocation%E2%80%9D)
- [RU claim] Russia's UN envoy called claims that the Russian military attacked the plant a lie — [Sputnik via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2022/03/ukraine-220304-sputnik02.htm)
- [UA claim] Enerhodar mayor Dmytro Orlov: "As a result of continuous enemy shelling ... the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power
  plant is on fire"; the IAEA, citing Ukrainian authorities, said essential equipment was not affected and no release
  was detected — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/3/4/russia-ukraine-war-fire-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-put-out)
- Independent: NPR's analysis of the plant's security-camera footage (11 March 2022) found Russian forces repeatedly
  fired heavy weapons toward the reactor area during the assault — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/03/11/1085427380/ukraine-nuclear-power-plant-zaporizhzhia)
  (the strongest independent attribution in this record: the footage shows the attacking column, which was Russian).

- 5–7 August and onward: [RU claim] Russia's Defence Ministry said Ukrainian forces damaged the plant; Russia's UN

  Russian forces shelled the plant and nearby territory. Reuters said it could not verify the battlefield reports — [CBC/Reuters, 27 Aug 2022](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6564311); [Algemeiner/Reuters](https://www.algemeiner.com/2022/08/27/russia-and-ukraine-accuse-each-other-of-shelling-around-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/); [Moscow Times, 11 Aug 2022](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2022/08/11/russia-ukraine-trade-blame-in-fresh-strike-on-nuclear-plant-a78555/pdf); [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/article-4226/accusations-and-dire-warnings-swirl-over-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant)

**1–6 September 2022: the IAEA mission (ISAMZ) and its report**
- The IAEA's 6 September 2022 report recorded "the presence of Russian military personnel, vehicles and equipment at
  various places at the ZNPP, including several military trucks on the ground floor of the Unit 1 and Unit 2 turbine

- Independent (OSINT): the Centre for Information Resilience (7 September 2022) geolocated footage filmed from the
  Dnipropetrovsk side on the night of 2–3 September that appears to show an MLRS firing from between the nuclear plant
  and the thermal plant, contradicting the Russian Telegram channel Rybar's claim that the launch was across the
  reservoir; CIR calls the footage "allegedly" showing firing and its trajectories approximations — [CIR, Danger Zone](https://www.info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)
- Independent (OSINT crater analysis, Enerhodar not the plant): CIR traced a shell that struck central Enerhodar near the

  concluding the shell "highly likely" came from Russian-controlled ground; CIR did not attribute shelling of the plant

- CIR also cited drone footage of 2 August 2022 showing a convoy with anti-aircraft guns and APCs entering the plant
  near reactor 2, and Maxar imagery of 29 August 2022 showing military equipment under the cooling pipes at unit 5 —
  [CIR](https://www.info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)

**July 2023: the mines claims**

**27 November 2023: rockets fired from near the plant**
- IAEA experts "heard several rockets that appeared to have been fired from close to the plant"; "the distinctive sound

  The IAEA did not say which side fired — [WNN, 27 Nov 2023](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/IAEA-reports-rockets-fired-from-near-Zaporizhzhia)

### Inferences
- For a film, the safe formula for nearly every strike on the site is: "[date]: [what was hit, per the IAEA]. Russia
  blamed Ukraine; Ukraine denied it [and blamed Russia]. The IAEA did not say who fired." The two incidents where
  stronger wording is supported are the March 2022 assault (NPR: Russian forces fired on the site) and Russian
  launches from beside the plant (NYT via VOA, CIR's geolocated MLRS, the IAEA's "fired from close to the plant").
- The power-line record splits by bank: damage on the Ukrainian-held side (Nikopol, Dniprovska's far end) is usually
  attributed by Ukraine and accepted by the IAEA as drone or military activity there; damage near the plant or over the
  river is contested, and the IAEA locates it ("1.5 km from the site", "over the Dnipro") without naming a party.
- Russia's claims about strikes on Enerhodar and its substations rise sharply in 2026 (Likhachev's "dozens every day");
  none found was independently verified. These are where Ukrainian strikes are most plausible (Enerhodar is a Russian
  rear area with military presence), but plausibility is not attribution and the film should not say they happened as
  described.

### Gaps
- The IAEA's own update texts (iaea.org) could not be fetched (HTTP 402); wording comes from mirrors and trade press.
- The NYT's August 2022 investigation and ISW's August 2022 update were not read in the original.
- No OSINT crater or debris analysis of shelling of the plant itself (as opposed to Enerhodar) was found; none of the
  April 2024 or May 2026 drone strikes has an independent attribution that I found.
- No Russian/Rosatom statement on the cause of the 23 September 2025 line cut was found, though Russian officials are
  widely reported to have blamed Ukrainian shelling; not confirmed here.
- The date of the chief engineer's reported death, the Raduga substation injuries and the Enerhodar substation outages
  could not be fixed from the pages found.
- A November 2026 Ferosplavna-1 trip (the plant briefly on diesel; IAEA said it did not know the cause; the plant's
  management cited "automatic protection") appeared in a search summary citing Reuters, but no URL was retrieved.
- The Jamestown article (403) and a Morning Star headline "UN condemns Ukrainian drone strike" (April 2024, paywalled)
  were not read; the latter's framing is not supported by the IAEA's statements found, which named no side.

## Russian denials of troops or heavy weapons at the plant, and what the IAEA saw

### Takeaway
Russia has consistently denied heavy weapons at the plant (Shoigu, Peskov, 2022). The IAEA confirmed Russian military
personnel, vehicles (including trucks inside the unit 1 and 2 turbine halls) and armoured vehicles, but said it did not
see heavy weapons such as MLRS, tanks or artillery on the site; its access has been restricted (turbine halls' western
side, unit 3–4 roofs).

### Cited Findings
- [RU claim] Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu said Russia has no heavy weapons on the plant's territory or in the surrounding
  areas (August 2022) — [Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/en/news/879813-shoigu-states-that-russian-army-did-not-place-heavy-military-equipment-at-zaporizhzhia-npp); [Defense Post, 18 Aug 2022](https://thedefensepost.com/2022/08/18/russia-weapons-ukraine-nuclear/amp/)
- [RU claim] Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov (December 2022) denied heavy weapons there and said IAEA staff present day and
  night could confirm it — [CGTN, 13 Dec 2022](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2022-12-13/Peskov-denies-Russian-heavy-weapons-at-Zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-1fJv98U2SwU/index.html); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/4041141/kremlin-no-heavy-weapons-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant?page=3)
- Grossi said Russian armoured vehicles and a security presence were at the plant, but monitors did not see prohibited
  weapons such as MLRS, tanks and artillery — reported in [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/4041141/kremlin-no-heavy-weapons-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant?page=3)
- IAEA, 6 September 2022: Russian military personnel, vehicles and equipment, including military trucks on the ground
  floor of the unit 1 and 2 turbine halls — [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/09/ukraine-russias-military-activities-at-nuclear-plant-risk-safety-in-region/)
- The IAEA team was barred from the western side of the turbine halls and could not confirm what was there — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3906936-iaea-reports-presence-of-russian-armed-troops-military-equipment-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant.html)
- Russia's own video of the IAEA's September 2022 visit showed military trucks marked "Z" — [CIR](https://www.info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant); see also [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3906936-iaea-reports-presence-of-russian-armed-troops-military-equipment-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant.html)
- July 2024: Russia denied the IAEA access to the roofs of units 3 and 4 and parts of the cooling system, where it wanted
  to check reports of mines and explosives — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/14/ukraine-says-russian-military-trucks-are-at-zaporizhzhia-power-plant/)

### Inferences
- The film can say as observed by the IAEA: Russian troops, vehicles and trucks in the turbine halls (2022), mines at the
  perimeter (2023). It should not say heavy weapons were on the site (the IAEA did not see them), but can say rockets were
  heard fired from close to the plant (IAEA, November 2023) and that OSINT geolocated an MLRS launch between the nuclear
  and thermal plants (CIR, September 2022).

### Gaps
- No IAEA statement after 2022 confirming or denying heavy weapons on site was retrieved.

## Ukraine's denials and its "false flag" counter-accusation

### Takeaway
Ukraine has denied every Russian claim of striking the plant (HUR in April 2024, the Southern Defence Forces in May
2026) and accuses Russia of shelling the site and Enerhodar itself, of using the plant as cover to fire on Nikopol, and of
staging provocations (Ukraine's own words; "false flag" is the framing in Ukrainian and some Western commentary). Ukraine
has supplied no more independent proof than Russia has; the independent evidence that exists (NPR, CIR, NYT, IAEA
observations of military use) tends to support Ukraine's account of Russian military use of the site, not of who struck
it in each incident.

### Cited Findings
- [UA claim] April 2024: HUR said Ukraine was not involved in the drone strikes — [Cyprus Mail/Reuters](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/04/08/russia-says-ukraine-struck-nuclear-plant-kyiv-denies-attack)
- [UA claim] May 2026: "The Russian Federation continues to use the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant as a tool for nuclear

## How the IAEA (and other bodies) handle attribution, and how the mutual accusation functions

### Takeaway
The IAEA has, throughout, refused to attribute any attack on the plant; Grossi's stated reason (19 April 2024) is that the
agency is "not commentators" and needs "indisputable evidence" of where a strike came from, which drones flying varied
paths on a front line rarely give. No UN or OSCE body was found to have attributed a specific strike (the OSCE's
monitoring mission left Ukraine in 2022). Commentators read the mutual accusation as part of Russia's use of the plant for
"nuclear blackmail" and information war; Russia's claims (Ukrainian strikes, Kyiv's provocations) mirror Ukraine's, so the
unattributed record leaves each audience its own story.

### Cited Findings
- Grossi, 19 April 2024: "We are not commentators. We are not political speculators or analysts, we are an international
  agency of inspectors"; attribution needs "proof, indisputable evidence"; a drone "could be manned from one side and

### Inferences
- The IAEA's non-attribution is itself a fact worth stating in the film ("the IAEA, on site since September 2022, has
  never said who fired"), and fits the film's evidence rule: what is not verified is not shown as observed.
- The structure of the dispute is symmetrical in claims but not in evidence of military use: independent sources confirm
  Russian troops, vehicles, mines and launches near the site; none confirms Ukrainian strikes on the site, and none
  confirms Russian strikes on it either.
