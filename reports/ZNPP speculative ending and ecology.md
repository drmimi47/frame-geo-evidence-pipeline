# Water ties the reactor to the forest

Ukraine never replaced the Zaporizhzhia plant's fifth of its electricity. The country shrank around the gap instead. Consumption fell from **125.7 TWh in 2021 to 80.2 TWh in 2025**, a loss of 45.5 TWh, which is more than the plant's 40–42 TWh a year ([DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-2025-ukraine-consumed-only-64-of-the-electricity-volume-recorded-in-2021/); [NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant/)). So the gap shows up at the winter peak: about 18 GW of demand against 10.5–12 GW of working conventional capacity in the worst weeks of 2025/26, and shortages of 5–6 GW, about the size of the plant's 5.7 GW. Every future for the plant meets one of two limits, water or wire, and the water limit runs through the Great Meadow. A restart needs far more cooling water than the wells give (about 250 m³/h). It can come only from a large pumping station on the river or from a rebuilt reservoir. A rebuilt reservoir would drown part of the willow-poplar forest that has grown on the bed since June 2023. The reservoir's hydrologists propose refilling to 12.7 m and say that at that level "both Zaporizhzhya power stations" could run again (Vyshnevskyi & Shevchuk 2024, p. 286). The December 2025 peace framework puts the plant and the dam in one point. Ecologists call refilling "another crime". The film does not have to invent the fork for its ending: it is already in the sources. The ecology gives the film a second, observed record to set against the lines going dark: forest heights measured from 3 cm to 9 m, a cormorant colony that nested on the crossing towers, nightingales recorded 15 km from the crossing, and citizen-science observations that stop at the shoreline. The project's existing material needs several corrections before any of this reaches the screen. The most important are the unsourced "80,000 m³/h", the peace plan's point numbers, and the forest's conflicting heights.

## Falling demand, not new supply, swallowed the plant's fifth

Before the war the ZNPP made about 20% of Ukraine's electricity, up to about 42,000 GWh a year and about 40% of the country's nuclear output, from six VVER-1000 units of 950 MWe net ([NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plant/); [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine)). It has generated nothing since Unit 6 shut down on 11 September 2022. Energoatom counts **155 TWh not produced** up to 27 February 2026 ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)). Nuclear's *share* of generation hardly moved, because everything else fell with it. The three remaining plants (Rivne, Khmelnytskyi and South Ukraine, 7.7 GW) produced about 52 TWh in 2023 and 49.85 TWh in 2024, about half of a total that had dropped from 156 TWh in 2021 to roughly 100 TWh ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine); [WNA reactor database](https://world-nuclear.org/nuclear-reactor-database/summary/Ukraine); [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)). Nuclear output in the first half of 2026 was 26.82 TWh, slightly up on 2025 ([NV](https://english.nv.ua/business/ukraine-s-nuclear-power-plants-increase-electricity-generation-in-2026-50628168.html)). Since 2023 Ukraine has published no full mix by source, "for security reasons" ([KSE Institute](https://institute.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/energy_sector_priority_projects_in_ukraine_eng_2025.pdf)). Any on-screen national figure after 2023 is therefore partial, and the film should say so.

**Demand closed most of the gap.** Non-household consumption fell 41.5%. DiXi's analyst names destroyed industry, lost territory, displacement and outages as the causes ([DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-2025-ukraine-consumed-only-64-of-the-electricity-volume-recorded-in-2021/)). The plant's own neighbourhood shows it most clearly. The ferroalloy plants that gave the Ferosplavna line its name ran at about 10% of capacity in 2025 and **stopped on 19 January 2026** for lack of power. Electricity had been more than 30% of their cost before the war ([GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/opinion/ferroalloy-plants-operated-at-minimum-capacity-in-2025-at-10/)), and an IndexBox snippet puts it at more than half. The fifth was not so much replaced as matched by what the war took from demand, and part of that demand was the plant's own region.

**What fills the hole is fragile and peaking.** Imports reached a record 4.4 TWh in 2024 but fell to about 1.3 TWh net in 2025, according to a VoxUkraine summary, which this research saw only as a search snippet. Ukraine was even a net exporter in summer 2025 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment); [VoxUkraine](https://voxukraine.org/en/from-exports-to-imports-how-electricity-flows-between-ukraine-and-its-neighbors-have-changed)). The import limit rose from 2.1 GW to 2.3 GW from December 2025 and is often less than half used ([DiXi Group](https://dixigroup.org/en/in-november-ukraine-recorded-the-highest-electricity-imports-of-the-year/)). Interfax gives 2.45 GW for Ukraine and Moldova together from January 2026 ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)). Even 2.1 GW running all year would bring under half the plant's energy. Distributed gas added about 0.8 GW in 2024 and 0.76 GW in 2025, and solar about 0.8 GW and 1.5 GW ([ua-energy.org](https://ua-energy.org/en/posts/31-12-2024-812032b8-5207-4ac4-ba71-8bb818b96e8b); [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/ukraine-adds-762-mw-distributed-gas-generation-in-2025-with-growing-solar-projects/); [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/26/ukraine-deploys-1-5-gw-of-solar-in-2025/)). That is a few GW of mostly daytime or peaking capacity, set against 5.7 GW that ran round the clock. Meanwhile the strikes removed what used to balance nuclear. Ukraine had about 38 GW of dispatchable capacity before 2022. It lost 19 GW in 2022, fell to 12 GW after spring 2024 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/ukraines-energy-security/a-pre-winter-assessment)), and lost another 8.5 GW from October 2025 ([The Ukrainian Review](https://theukrainianreview.info/power-outages-as-the-new-reality-the-state-and-prospects-of-ukraines-energy-system/)). On 8 February 2026, outages averaged almost 12 hours a day, according to a Green Deal Ukraina summary seen only as a search snippet ([Green Deal Ukraina](https://greendealukraina.org/assets/images/reports/lessons-from-last-winter-10-measures-long-version.pdf)). The surviving nuclear plants are now exposed through the same weakness that isolated the ZNPP: their substations. The strike of 7 February 2026 on those substations cut nuclear output by half at once ([Switchgear Magazine](https://switchgear-magazine.com/tm-news/business/russian-strikes-halve-ukraines-nuclear-output/)).

**Where it "would come from" is mostly the 2030s.** Khmelnytskyi 3 and 4 (about 2 GW) have stalled. The law to buy Bulgaria's Belene reactors was signed in March 2025, then Bulgaria reversed course, and in April 2026 a separate completion law was still needed ([Energoatom](https://energoatom.com.ua/en/news/prezident-ukrayini-pidpisav-zakon-shhodo-zakupivli-obladnannia-dlia-dobudovi-blokiv-na-xaes); [WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine); [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/ukraine_plans_to-4/), snippet). The AP1000 units 5 and 6 target 2030–2032. Rebuilding the Kakhovka hydro plant (about 335–357 MW, depending on the source; the project brief's 351 MW is confirmed by none) would take 6–7 years after de-occupation ([Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3871965-kakhovka-hpp-can-be-rebuilt-in-67-years-after-deoccupation-ukrhydroenergo.html)). A back-of-envelope figure, not a published one: matching 40 TWh with solar would take roughly 30 GW plus storage. The forecast for winter 2026/27 is 8–10 hours a day without power in Kyiv in the base case and up to 20 in a severe one ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/articles/2026/10/24/8059524/)).

Two framings for one beat are both sourced, and they pull in different directions. The **energy framing**: the fall in consumption is larger than the plant ever made, so the plant's absence is hidden inside a country that uses a third less. The **capacity framing**: the worst winter shortfall, "at times 5–6 GW" in February 2026 ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116449/)), is the same size as the plant. No study divides outage hours between "ZNPP lost" and "strike damage", so the film must not do it either. Project note N3 already limits the country scale to "one short beat from published outlooks at most". The best beat is two bars, 2021 and 2025 consumption, with the plant's 40 TWh laid against the difference. The caption says only what the sources say. One complication belongs in that beat: Ukraine still supplies the seized plant with 11–50 MW (Energoatom), about 0.1–0.3% of peak ([Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html); [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/08/8052353/)). The lines built to carry gigawatts out carry tens of megawatts in.

## Every future for the plant runs out of water or wire

Published scenario work on the plant is thin. No RUSI, Carnegie, CSIS, Atlantic Council, Razumkov or NEA foresight study was found. What exists is policy analysis: Brookings in 2023 ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-to-do-about-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant/)), Sokolski in the *Bulletin* in 2025 ([Bulletin](https://thebulletin.org/2025/05/zaporizhzhia-hurdle-or-catalyst-for-a-peace-deal-in-ukraine/)), and expert round-ups ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)). A blog figure of a "60% probability" attributed to RAND has no source and must not be used. The honest frame for each scenario is therefore **a party's stated position plus the physical conditions it would need**, never a probability.

### If the war goes on: a margin that thins, with dated rates

The plant has been in cold shutdown since April 2024. Each core now gives off well under 1 MW, so a total loss of power starts a countdown of days to weeks, not hours ([Bellona](https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/2025-10-is-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plants-longest-blackout-a-scheme-for-russia-to-siphon-off-its-power)). The measurable trends are all moving the wrong way, and each can be drawn as a projection from its own dates.

**Losses of off-site power are accelerating.** The 9th came on 4 July 2025. The 26th was 20 August to 7 September 2026, eighteen days on diesel. WNN counted ten losses in summer 2026 alone ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy); [WNN](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/local-ceasefire-in-place-for-zaporizhzhia-power-line-repairs)). A projection "at the rate so far" therefore depends entirely on its window. Eight losses in the three years to mid-2025 and ten in one summer give very different futures, so the film must state the window it uses.

**Diesel is running low.** Stocks reached the 10-day regulatory minimum on 28 August 2026 ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)). By late September only **4 of the 20 generators** would start automatically ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)). HUR says no diesel has been delivered since March and that a long blackout burns about 150 t a day; both are unverified ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)).

**The cooling pond keeps falling.** It went from 14.34 m (September 2024) to 14.11 m (March 2025) and 13.54 m (August 2025) ([OECD-NEA](https://oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_66130/ukraine-current-status-of-nuclear-power-installations)). HUR's readings, a party's figures, then run 12.86 m (July 2026), 12.55 m (end of August) and **12.27 m on 12 October 2026** ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/21/nuclear-safety-risk-grows-russian-occupied-nuclear-plants-cooling-pond-keeps-sinking-russia-runs-short-of-diesel-and-staff-ukraine-says/)). The IAEA's 2025 reporting implies a pump cut-off of about 12 m: an isolation dam holds the transformers' service-water channel at about 14 m, "two metres above" the level at which the pumps stop ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-team-seeks-access-to-new-dam-at-zaporizhzhia-plant)). The rates in these series, worked out here and not published anywhere, are about 0.13 cm a day over winter 2024/25 and 0.6–0.7 cm a day in summer 2025 and summer 2026. From 12.27 m, the pond would reach 12 m within about six weeks at summer rates, or around spring 2027 at winter rates. That range is the honest drawing. It is also close enough to the present that the film's projection could be overtaken by a report before release, so it needs a last check.

**Staff numbers are falling.** The figures conflict: about 11,000 before the war, about 7,500 (Greenpeace Ukraine, July 2026), and about 5,000 with only 1,450 on site (HUR, October 2026, unverified). Staff were also killed in 2026 ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/ukraine-russia-war-and-nuclear-energy)).

Two qualifications keep this angle honest. First, a falling pond is not a catastrophe in cold shutdown. About nine to eleven groundwater wells (200–250 m³/h) and twelve sprinkler ponds cover almost all shutdown cooling, by the IAEA's account ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Groundwater-extraction-begins-at-Zaporizhzhia)). So the 12 m line marks a margin, not a deadline. Second, ageing pushes whoever holds the plant toward costly refurbishment or decommissioning. Engineers warn of boric-acid crystallisation and steam-generator damage after years of low-temperature cooling, with replacements costing more than $1 billion a unit ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)). No published study models decommissioning ZNPP. The literature offers only defuelling until the war ends, or indefinite cold shutdown "as was done for Krško" ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-to-do-about-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant/)).

### If Russia restarts it: the paperwork moves, the water does not

**The licences are moving.** Rostekhnadzor licensed Unit 1 for ten years in December 2025 and Unit 2 on 18 February 2026. Unit 6 has applied, and Units 3–5 are to follow so that all six meet Russian rules by 1 January 2028 ([NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/russia-s-nuclear-regulator-issues-10-year-operating-licence-for-zaporizhzhia-2-2-5-2026); [www1.ru](https://www1.ru/en/news/2026/02/27/vse-energobloki-zaporozskoi-aes-proidut-licenzirovanie-po-rossiiskim-trebovaniiam-do-2028-goda.html)). Ukraine calls them null and void.

**So is the wire.** Russia has built about 80 km of line from Mariupol to Berdiansk, verified by the NYT, and 201 km from Melitopol to Mariupol by September 2025 (Greenpeace). Since January 2026 it has been building an 86 km 150 kV line from Melitopol to Henichesk, which stalled after Ukrainian drone strikes in July ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-builds-power-lines-to-connect-occupied-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-to-its-grid-nyt-reports/); [UNITED24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukrainian-drone-strikes-halt-russias-bid-to-connect-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-to-its-grid-20958)). Experts say several more lines would be needed.

**Rosatom says the restart waits.** It will not restart while fighting continues (February 2026). In March 2026 Likhachev said "the necessary equipment is ready", floated supplying "including to Ukraine", and pitched 6 GW for data centres with "new partners… the United States, among others" ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/rosatom-boss-warns-increased-military-110059643.html)).

**Fuel is a problem of its own.** Four units hold Westinghouse fuel, which Rosatom has no licence for. Converting them "could take years" ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)). That is one reason the Russian-appointed director names Units 2 and 6 first ([WNN](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/russia-outlines-zaporizhzhia-restart-timetable-ukraine-rejects-plan)).

**Water is the binding constraint.** The wells cool the fuel in shutdown "but not enough to do so if they are restarted" ([Beyond Nuclear](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)). Rosatom's 2024 submission to the IAEA described a pumping station of up to **18,000 m³/h** into the pond. Greenpeace estimates that one running reactor circulates about **6,000 m³ a minute** (about 360,000 m³/h) ([Greenpeace Ukraine](https://www.greenpeace.org/ukraine/en/news/3301/greenpeace-urges-iaea-to-prevent-russian-restart-of-zaporizhzhia-reactors/)). The two figures measure different things: make-up water to the pond, and flow through the condensers. Both sit two to three orders of magnitude above the wells.

**The safety argument is the sharpest of all.** A restart would take each core from under 1 MW of heat back to about 3,000 MW, and the margin from weeks back to hours. Grossi has said "we are not in a situation of imminent restart" ([Beyond Nuclear](https://beyondnuclear.org/zaporizhzhia-restart-greenpeace-warns-of-perils/)).

### If it returns to Ukraine, or to someone else

The peace drafts changed between versions, and the film must name which draft it quotes.

**The 28-point draft (November 2025), point 19:** the plant "launched under the supervision of the IAEA", with its output split 50:50 between Russia and Ukraine ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/11/21/trumps-28-point-ukraine-plan-in-full-what-it-means-could-it-work)).

**The 20-point framework (dated 23 December 2025), point 12:** control of the ZNPP and restoration of the Kakhovka HPP, in one point. The US proposed a three-way split led by Washington, a third each. Kyiv proposed a US–Ukraine venture with half the output to Ukraine. Point 12 remained unresolved ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/zelensky-reveals-20-point-peace-plan-draft-backed-by-ukraine-us/); [Yale copy of the text](https://politicalscience.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-12-23-revised-peace-plan-announced-by-zelenskyy.pdf)).

**Russia's position:** in February 2026 Russia rejected US control. Its Foreign Ministry calls the plant Russian property that cannot be handed to anyone ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-rejects-calls-to-return-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)). On 28 October 2026 the US and Russia were reported, via an FT snippet, to be discussing a US entity managing the plant ([CDM](https://cdm.press/news/business/2026/10/28/washington-and-moscow-float-bringing-u-s-oil-companies-back-into-russia-as-part-of-ukraine-peace-push)).

**The time a return would take:** Ukraine estimates "up to two years" for all six units, given a solid peace and full control. Sokolski notes that "no one has ventured how much it would cost", and that the lines, the transformers and the mined site all need work ([Bulletin](https://thebulletin.org/2025/05/zaporizhzhia-hurdle-or-catalyst-for-a-peace-deal-in-ukraine/)). Energoatom would restart only after demining and demilitarisation, with 27 safety programmes agreed with its regulator ([Energoatom](https://energoatom.com.ua/en/news/perezapusk-zaporizkoyi-aes-pid-kontrolem-rosiiskoyi-federaciyi-je-nebezpecnim-ocilnik-ukrayinskoyi-energogeneruiucoyi-kompaniyi)). Ukraine's own licences began expiring in 2025; Unit 1's ran to 23 December 2025.

**Joint or split operation:** Greenpeace's Jan Vande Putte said of splitting the plant: "Maybe on paper you can develop a plan to split it, but practically it's not realistic." Splitting one plant's output between two unsynchronised grids would also need major modifications ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/splitting-europes-largest-nuclear-plant-between-russia-ukraine-wont-work-experts-say/)).

### The dam and the plant together: the fork is already in the sources

This is the angle the research most strengthens, because three independent kinds of source tie the plant to the reservoir.

**The hydrologists.** Vyshnevskyi and Shevchuk's 2024 paper (held locally as page images, for reference only) proposes a temporary dam that raises the water to the dead-volume level of **12.7 m** (11.4 km³, "a quarter of the annual flow of the Dnipro"). They write that "both Zaporizhzhya power stations and Kryvorizka power station… will also be able to operate at this level". The normal level would later be lowered from 16.0 m to "say, 15.5 m" (p. 286). The same 12.7 m is the reservoir level below which, the IAEA said in June 2023, pumping into the plant's supply could not continue ([IAEA Update 162](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-162-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). The paper and the IAEA agree on the number from different directions. That number is about the reservoir. It is not the pond's roughly 12 m pump cut-off, and the two must not be drawn as one line.

**The diplomats.** The 20-point framework's point 12 joins the plant to the dam, and Zelensky said the dam must be rebuilt for the plant's safe operation ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/66845)).

**The operator.** Ukrhydroenergo's first phase is a temporary restoration to raise the water for irrigation and the plant's cooling, followed by full reconstruction: 5–6 years to build, 7–8 with survey and demolition, $1–1.2 billion ([TSN](https://tsn.ua/en/ato/how-long-will-it-take-to-rebuild-the-kakhovka-hydroelectric-power-plant-the-general-director-of-ukrhydroenergo-explained-2631618.html)). Zelensky put it at about €2 billion ([Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/4072957-zelensky-says-restoration-of-kakhovka-dam-will-cost-eur-2b.html)).

**Against refilling.** A Ukrainian engineer's "do not restore" option would need new pumping stations, "including for supplying the cooling reservoir at the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant" ([Hydropower & Dams](https://www.hydropower-dams.com/news/future-possibilities-following-the-destruction-of-kakhovka-dam-and-hydro-plant/)). So the forest and a restart are not strictly exclusive: a large river pump could cool a reactor without a reservoir. The ecologists' positions run from Nefedov's "to flood this place again would be another crime" ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/11/21/to-flood-this-place-again-would-be-a-crime-ukraines-great-meadow-springs-back-to-life-after-destruction-of-dam)) to Moisiienko's August 2026 compromise. That compromise would leave about 200,000 of the former 215,000 ha wild and put about 15,000 ha into small reservoirs on tributaries: "we won't block the main channel" ([Intent](https://intent.press/en/news/ecology/2026/restoring-the-kakhovka-hydroelectric-power-plant-or-velykyi-luh-what-the-scientist-proposed/)). Peer-reviewed scenario papers set refilling against restoring the meadow and against a partly watered system ([Pichura et al. 2025](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/jlecol-2025-0023)), or propose a cascade of small reservoirs with fish passes ([Benatov et al. 2024](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/67a091a020478411b0259d25)). Shumilova et al. propose two temporary 15 km barriers to keep the main channel apart from the largest wetlands ([IGB](https://www.igb-berlin.de/en/news/water-war-long-term-environmental-consequences-destruction-kakhovka-dam-ukraine)). The Malokaterynivka village head speaks for many residents: "I just want there to be water again." Soroka adds that refilling over decaying biomass could produce a swamp, and that demining the bed makes reconstruction impossible during the war and its first years ([MOST](https://most.ks.ua/en/news/url/vidnovlennja-velikogo-lugu-v-jogo-kolishnomu-vigljadi-na-mistsi-kahovskoji-ges-ne-bude-ekspert/)). The earlier briefing also reports a March 2024 law barring construction on the former bed until five years after martial law ends. Its source is secondary ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir)), so it needs the law's text before use.

## Draw the future in dashes, outlines and quotes, never in light

No precedent combines forensic grammar with a future chapter. The research-fiction studios mark speculation outside the frame. Superflux's statement for *Mitigation of Shock* is "not a prediction, nor a render" ([Superflux](https://superflux.in/index.php/work/mitigation-of-shock/)). Liam Young calls *The Great Endeavour* "built entirely from scientific papers" and "data dramatisation as opposed to data visualisation" ([Koozarch](https://koozarch.com/interviews/the-great-endeavour-carbon-removal-infrastructure-at-a-planetary-scale)). Both render their futures whole, which the film's rule against generated imagery forbids. Forensic Architecture stays in the past tense. Territorial Agency is the useful bridge, because its composites leave the areas with no data visible ([STARTS Prize](https://starts-prize.aec.at/de/?p=3587)). The research on uncertainty displays settles one design choice. Viewers routinely read the hurricane cone's width as the storm's size ([NOAA review](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/general/lib/lib1/nhclib/Bibliographies/Cone%20of%20Uncertainty%20Literature%20Review_4_10_19%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf)), so a widening band of "possible cuts" would read as more damage. Animated hypothetical outcomes help untrained viewers ([Hullman lab](https://www.hullmanlab.northwestern.edu/paper%20discrete-outcome/2015/01/01/hops.html)), but the NYT's election needle shows that fast motion reads as alarm. The Bank of England has moved from a single fan chart to named scenarios ([BoE working paper 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)). A few named scenarios, slowly paced, each with its source, beat one smooth fan.

That gives one rule set to state once and keep:

- **Observed:** solid and, for power, lit.
- **Unconfirmed past:** dotted, as now.
- **Projected:** long dashes, only after a "today" mark. They must look different from the dots.
- **Proposed:** outline only, labelled "PROPOSAL · who, year".
- **Claimed:** text only, quoted and attributed, never plotted as geometry or as a date on the timeline.

Captions switch to the conditional after "today". Light stays reserved for observed power, so no future reconnection is ever animated. The project's wording rule 4 still holds inside the coda. The refill contour is "inferred × proposed": inferred bed heights, proposed level. "Speculation" is kept for the futures themselves. The Hum can carry the switch, for example by holding a single tone or falling silent in the projected stretch; that is a design choice and should be said so in film.json.

Mapped onto the storyboard's coda (new-forest, gm-meadow, ice-pools, point-12, forest-or-reactor, then "By September 2026, one line of ten remained" and credits), the research supports this order:

1. **The observed meadow first** (new-forest, gm-meadow, ice-pools), with the forest clock and the counter-record described below.
2. **The "today" stop**, the record's last state held.
3. **One card in the film's voice**: "From here on nothing is observed: the plans and the rates so far."
4. **"If this goes on"** in dashes.
5. **point-12**, rewritten to name the 20-point framework and the 28-point draft's point 19.
6. **forest-or-reactor** as the last image: the 12.7 m and 15.5 m contours drawn on the last clear pass over the regrown bed, the land below them hatched "would be under water at this level, by our inferred heights", held without resolution.
7. **"One line of ten"** returns the film to the observed, closing the rhyme with the prologue, and the credits follow.

The forest-or-reactor card needs rewording. "A restart would need the water back" overstates the case, because a river pumping station is the published alternative. "Would drown the forest" overstates it too, because 12.7 m covers only the bed below that level. A sourced version reads:

> A restart would need far more water than the wells give: a pumping station on the river, or the reservoir back. Raised to 12.7 m, as hydrologists propose, the water would cover the forest wherever the bed lies lower.

## The bed's forest is a dated clock with conflicting readings

The forest's origin is precisely dated. Willow and poplar shed seed in late May and early June, so the mud drained on 6 June 2023 was seeded at exactly the right moment. Mulenko said a breach in March or August would have let in far more invasive species ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/deer-and-boar-could-roam-forests-in-the-kakhovka-reservoir-in-five-years/)). The trees are native white willow (*Salix alba*), the hybrid *S. × rubens* and black poplar (*Populus nigra*) ([UNCG](https://uncg.org.ua/en/it-is-time-for-velykyi-luh-to-revive/); [Kherson Online](https://khersonline.net/dovkillya/verby-na-dni-kakhovskoho-vodoskhovyshcha-syahnuly-9-metriv/)). The feared invaders (black locust, *Amorpha*, box elder) have not taken over ([UWEC](https://uwecworkgroup.info/one-year-after-the-terrorist-attack-at-kakhovka-hydropower-plant-1b-trees-instead-of-desert-and-willow-forests-unique-to-the-continent/)). By October 2026 the bed held at least 400 plant species, up from 343 in 2025. They include the Red Book sedge *Carex secalina*, the first oak seedlings, and three-year-old forests that already have tree, shrub, herb and moss layers ([Moisiienko interview](https://donetskoblenergo.dn.ua/2026/10/14/kabani-povernylisia-ie-oleni-hto-jive-y-lisi-na-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovisha-naykovec/)).

The upstream readings near Khortytsia (1.5 m, then about 4 m) run well below the lower-bed maxima. TSN's "about 2 m in summer 2024" contradicts a record of 3.08 m measured nine months earlier. The defensible on-screen series uses only measured maxima at named plots: **3 cm (June 2023), 3.08 m (October 2023), about 7 m (2025, implied by the October 2026 increment), 9 m (October 2026)**. It is labelled "the tallest willows measured at study plots, not at the crossing". "Up to 10 m" stays out, because no measurement backs it. Against the crossing towers (90–100 m for the 330 kV crossing, 126 m for the 750 kV), 9 m is **under a tenth** of a tower, not "about a quarter" as an earlier note had it ([uk.wikipedia](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B8_%D0%9B%D0%95%D0%9F_%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B7_%D0%94%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE_(%D0%95%D0%BD%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%80))). The crossing itself can have a measured height. ICESat-2's ATL08 land-and-canopy product covers the tracks the project already uses in `bed.py`. Testing it for canopy heights over the crossing is the best "forest clock" the project could make itself.

**Area is worse than height.** The figures run from 33,000 ha to 150,000 ha. The most defensible is the calibrated Sentinel-2 study by Pichura, Potravka and Boiko (2025): **48,000 ha of woody cover within 135,000 ha overgrown**, measured 2023–24 ([KSAEU repository](https://dspace.ksaeu.kherson.ua/handle/123456789/12334?show=full)). The larger media numbers exceed or equal the whole bed (1,944 km², [IGB](https://www.igb-berlin.de/en/news/water-war-long-term-environmental-consequences-destruction-kakhovka-dam-ukraine)) and must count all vegetation. "The largest floodplain forest in Europe" is the scientists' consensus phrase, not a measurement, so it should be said as theirs.

The same study gives the film something its own passes can show: the forest is not uniformly thriving. Spring 2024 floods submerged up to 70% of the drained bed. By late September 2024, after July heat of 40.5–42 °C and low releases from DniproHES, 75.3% of the cover showed growth disturbance and 72.8% had lost chlorophyll function. The project's Sentinel-2 NDVI over the bed should show this as a derived reading.

**The bed is also contaminated.** It holds about 83,300 t of heavy metals in 1.3–1.7 km³ of silt laid since the 1950s, much of it from the Zaporizhzhia, Nikopol and Marhanets industry the lines once fed ([IGB](https://www.igb-berlin.de/en/news/water-war-long-term-environmental-consequences-destruction-kakhovka-dam-ukraine)). UNCG found 94% of 119 samples above permissible limits ([UNCG](https://uncg.org.ua/en/high-levels-heavy-metal-contamination-at-former-kakhovka-reservoir-bed/)). The predicted toxic dust storms never came, because the bed overgrew. Scientists hold that the willows keep the metals in place and that cutting or reflooding would release them ([Hmarochos](https://hmarochos.kiev.ua/2025/09/03/bilshist-prognoziv-ne-spravdylysya-vcheni-kazhut-shho-na-misczi-kolyshnogo-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-vidnovylys-rusla-rik-i-zyavyvsya-oseter/)). No uptake has actually been measured, so the film should say "scientists say".

**What is returning is not the old meadow.** Velykyi Luh was a mosaic more than 100 km long of channels, reed lakes, oak and pear woods and dunes, and six of the eight Sich stood on it. It was flooded in stages from 1953 to 1958 ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/projects/111574/karta-velykoho-luhu-pyat-sichej-stavka-monholskoho-hana-ta-inshi-cikavi-miscya/)). Didukh and Soroka both say the original meadow will not return. A willow-poplar forest "unique in its own way" is forming instead, with old channels such as the Kamianka back in their beds and Orliv island resurfacing ([UNIAN](https://www.unian.net/ecology/kahovskoe-vodohranilishche-ne-prevratitsya-v-velikiy-lug-uchenye-13418577.html)). The film's phrase "the Great Meadow regrowing" should become "a floodplain forest of the Great Meadow's kind".

**The forest is also a battlefield.** Recurrent fires burn on the bed ([Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/ua/news/1098863-na-vysohlomu-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-postijno-spalahuyut-pozhezhi-povitrya-zabrudnyuye-dym)). The thickets serve as cover for movement across the front ([Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/kakhovske-vodoskhovyshche-pole-boyu/33618198.html), snippet). Fieldwork reaches only a narrow strip, and teams cannot go 9–10 km in ([Moisiienko interview](https://donetskoblenergo.dn.ua/2026/10/14/kabani-povernylisia-ie-oleni-hto-jive-y-lisi-na-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovisha-naykovec/)).

## Birds, fish and recordings give the film an observed counter-record

### The cormorants on the crossing towers

This is the closest link between living things and the lines themselves. A 2012 survey found great cormorants nesting on the Enerhodar crossings: **320, 280, 200 and 100 nests** on four towers of the 330 kV ZTPP–Ferosplavna crossing, and **190 and 150** on two towers of the 750 kV ZNPP–Dniprovska crossing ([Sidorenko & Siokhin 2016, *Biosystems Diversity*](https://ecology.dp.ua/index.php/ECO/article/view/011640); counts via [uk.wikipedia](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B8_%D0%9B%D0%95%D0%9F_%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B7_%D0%94%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE_(%D0%95%D0%BD%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%80))). It was one of only eight cormorant colonies on man-made structures in Ukraine. Cormorants use such sites only where nothing else is available. The colony depended on fish in open water and on the water's isolation from predators, and both vanished in June 2023. A month later, starving cormorants were wandering Nikopol's streets ([NikopolNews](https://nikopol.nikopolnews.net/lite/nature/u-nikopoli-kakhovskoho/), local and unverified, and not tied by any source to the colony). Twelve great cormorants were logged at Marhanets on 26 August 2025 ([GBIF/eBird](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?datasetKey=4fa7b334-ce0d-4e88-aaae-2e0c138d049e&decimalLongitude=34.3,34.8&decimalLatitude=47.4,47.65&eventDate=2023-06-06,2026-11-01)). Whether the colony survived is undocumented, and no photograph of birds on these towers after 2023 was found. The project already holds a test for it: the WorldView-2 inset of 23 August 2024 at 0.5 m shows the crossing pylons. Guano whitening on the crossarms or caissons, or its absence, is checkable from that image and the Esri Wayback stack. A null result is worth saying too.

Tower positions are public in OSM, so a colony is not repair or damage information. Even so, any bird image should be placed only as a stretch of the crossing, and no bird should ever be drawn on a conductor without a photograph. On wildlife and power lines generally, no collision or electrocution data for Ukraine's 330/750 kV lines exist. Electrocution is a distribution-line problem. A Crimean steppe study found collisions worst on lines between water bodies and near wetlands ([BirdLife](https://datazone.birdlife.org/in-the-ukrainian-steppes-powerlines-are-hazardous-to-birds-during-migrations-and-in-winter)). DTEK's stork platforms and diverters are on 6–150 kV distribution lines, not Ukrenergo's ([DTEK Grids](https://grids.dtek.com/en/media-center/press/mezhdunarodnyy-den-zaschity-bioraznoobraziya-kak-spetszaschita-naoporakh-lep-pomogaet-energetikam-dtek-seti-spasat-zhizni-aistov/)).

### The cooling pond as plant machinery

The pond (8.2 km², 47 million m³) was run as a designed warm-water fishery ([Marenkov 2018](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/ichthyofauna-of-the-zaporizhia-nuclear-power-plant-cooling-pond-enerhodar-ukraine-and-its-biomeliorative-significance)). Each stocked species had a job:

- silver and bighead carp ate algal bloom;
- black carp ate zebra mussels;
- grass carp ate weed;
- tilapia grazed filamentous algae off the intake plates.

Stocking from 2007 to 2016 included about 1.5 million silver carp. The pond's hottest month averaged 28.7 °C, and in winter it never fell below 17–18 °C. Energoatom said that after cold shutdown the pond cooled to about 13 °C and the warm-water fish "died in great numbers" ([Ukrainska Pravda, 31 Oct 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/10/31/7374280/)). That is an operator claim, never confirmed. Energoatom's "Asian catfish" is probably a loose name for the channel catfish in Marenkov's species list, an inference. The pond is the reactor's own engineered ecosystem, set against the self-seeded forest across the water. Its fish stopped working when the heat stopped, and its water has been falling since the reservoir went. No 2023–2026 wildlife record or footage of the pond exists. The pond enters the film as passes and stills, with the 2022 die-off as sourced text.

### Fauna on the bed

Most of the fauna record is aural, or known from tracks. Vasyliuk's 2026 expedition found that "birds became the true masters of the willow thickets": hundreds of nightingales and reed warblers. TSN's crew with Kuzemko and Vasyliuk heard two nightingale species, thrush and common ([TSN](https://tsn.ua/exclusive/rivno-try-roky-pislia-pidryvu-kakhovskoyi-hes-iak-vyhliadaye-unikalnyy-velykyy-luh-3100838.html)). Vasyliuk's team found no hole-nesters yet, because the trees are too young, and no large mammals in the thickets. Jackals, foxes and polecats were known from their tracks. Bees and butterflies were nearly absent, because willow and poplar are wind-pollinated ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/fragments/117772/xto-zaselyv-dno-kaxovskoho-vodosxovyshcha-zooloh-opysav-tvaryn-novoho-lisu/)). The rest of the record:

- **Mammals.** By October 2026 wild boar and polecats live on the bed; deer walk the roads but not the thickets ([Moisiienko interview](https://donetskoblenergo.dn.ua/2026/10/14/kabani-povernylisia-ie-oleni-hto-jive-y-lisi-na-dni-kahovskogo-vodoshovisha-naykovec/)).
- **Reptiles.** A Caspian whipsnake (Red Book) was the species' first record on the bed.
- **Fish.** A sturgeon was seen near Khortytsia for the first time in more than 70 years ([Hmarochos](https://hmarochos.kiev.ua/2025/09/03/bilshist-prognoziv-ne-spravdylysya-vcheni-kazhut-shho-na-misczi-kolyshnogo-kahovskogo-vodoshovyshha-vidnovylys-rusla-rik-i-zyavyvsya-oseter/)).
- **Pelicans.** Great white pelicans returned in summer 2026, "absent for about 150 years" according to Maniuk ([UNIAN](https://www.unian.ua/ecology/rozhevi-pelikani-na-kahovske-vodoshovishche-vpershe-za-150-rokiv-povernulis-pelikani-novini-dnipra-13471692.html)). The footage is a 128th Brigade drone's reconnaissance with no public URL, so the pelicans can appear only as a caption. A Telegraf report of July 2025 already mentions cormorants joining pelicans' hunting there ([Telegraf](https://news.telegraf.com.ua/ukr/ukraina/2025-07-02/5913698-chervonoknizhni-ptakhi-oselilisya-na-mistsi-kolishnogo-kakhovskogo-vodoskhovishcha-video)), which conflicts with "first in 150 years" in 2026. The first-sighting date needs settling before any caption.
- **The breach itself.** It killed fish and molluscs on a vast scale: about 28,000 fish counted officially, billions of mussels, and an ecocide case opened on 8 June 2023 ([RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/amp/thousands-dead-fish-kakhovka-reservoir-ukraine-ecocide-probe-dam/32452124.html)).

### Datasets: the observations stop at the shore

The citizen-science record makes its own forensic image. Since the breach, iNaturalist has 2,996 observations within 20 km of Nikopol, but only **13 plant records and no birds or mammals** inside the bed box between the crossings ([iNaturalist API](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?swlat=47.47&swlng=34.30&nelat=47.565&nelng=34.70&d1=2023-06-06)). The real record clusters on the Marhanets shore, 10–15 km upstream on the crossing's north bank.

**Sound recordings.** Sixty-one iNaturalist sound observations by `uabird` (cc-by-nc) include Savi's warbler, little bittern, little crake, red-footed falcon, black-winged stilt, Temminck's stint and spotted redshank from 2025–26 ([iNaturalist sounds](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?swlat=47.35&swlng=34.10&nelat=47.70&nelng=35.00&d1=2023-06-06&sounds=true)). Ekaterina Kormina's xeno-canto recordings include a **thrush nightingale at Marhanets on 16 April 2025 (XC1016141)** ([xeno-canto](https://xeno-canto.org/explore?query=cnt%3AUkraine+box%3A47.3%2C34.0%2C47.8%2C35.3)).

**eBird checklists.** These hold the white storks (50, 10 August 2025) and the cormorants ([GBIF/eBird](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?datasetKey=4fa7b334-ce0d-4e88-aaae-2e0c138d049e&decimalLongitude=34.3,34.8&decimalLatitude=47.4,47.65&eventDate=2023-06-06,2026-11-01)).

**A pre-drainage baseline.** GBIF holds a 2014–2021 fish-catch dataset from the Kakhovka reservoir ([GBIF dataset](https://api.gbif.org/v1/dataset/b71a6701-ec26-4d1a-9d16-c04e5958de43)).

Plotted on the zoom-out, the points crowd the towns and stop at the shoreline. The caption "the observations stop where the shelling starts" is then a statement about access, with a source for every point. The points should be binned into cells of at least about 3 km rather than shown individually. That avoids placing private observers' homes and keeps to the spirit of the safety rule.

### Methods that make plants and animals witnesses

Forensic Architecture's Gaza herbicide case treats plants as the instrument: leaves kept "spotted marks where they were hit by toxic particles" ([Arts of the Working Class](https://artsoftheworkingclass.org/text/samaneh-moafi)). Its *Ecocide in Indonesia*, about 9.5 minutes, shows drainage as a cause of fire ([FA](https://www.forensic-architecture.org/case/ecocide-indonesia/)). It is a useful length and form for "Powerlines". Susan Schuppli's *Material Witness* frames matter as registering events, and asks that the practices which make it testify be shown ([Goldsmiths](https://research.gold.ac.uk/28197)). That is the theory behind the forest as a clock. Chernobyl gives one sourced parallel, as a line and not a prediction: without people, elk, deer and boar matched uncontaminated reserves, and wolves were seven times as abundant ([Deryabina et al. 2015 via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/10/151005132553.htm)).

For sound, the project's rule bans sounds of fighting. UNCG's "hundreds of nightingales" recording has drone noise in it, which arguably counts. The clean Marhanets recordings are the safer real biophony, licence and recordist's consent permitting (cc-by-nc needs checking for the film's use). Mixed under the Hum and credited "recorded at Marhanets, 16 April 2025", the nightingale would hold steady through every cut and complete loss while the synthesized lines fall silent. It is the one place in the film where an observed sound can enter.

## Conclusion

The research turns the coda from an epilogue into the film's argument. The lines carried the plant's power out, and now carry a trickle in. Their future, and the plant's, is bounded by water that can only come from the river or the reservoir. The reservoir is the forest's ground. A hydrology paper, a peace framework and the dam's operator independently bind plant and dam together, while the ecologists' counter-proposals and a contamination study argue against refilling. The film can therefore end on a fork made entirely of other people's documents, drawn as contours over its own imagery, without choosing a branch. The country-scale question has a counter-intuitive answer that is worth one beat. Ukraine absorbed the loss of its largest plant mainly by losing demand, including the plant's own industrial neighbours, so the plant's absence is felt at the winter peak, not over the year.

The ecology offers more than mood. It supplies a second, observed timeline that runs opposite to the record: trees measured from centimetres to 9 m, a cormorant colony stranded on the crossing towers, nightingales recorded 15 km away, and observations that stop at the shoreline. Each has a date and a source, and each sits on the "observed" side of the film's own rules. Three things to do next would let the film measure rather than quote. Test ICESat-2 ATL08 canopy heights over the crossing. Run NDVI over the bed through the 2024 heat stress. Check the 2024 WorldView-2 inset for guano on the towers. Done alongside the pond's waterline series, they let the film's strongest closing claims be its own, and the corrections above keep the borrowed ones from failing on screen.
