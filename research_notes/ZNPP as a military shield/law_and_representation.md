# ZNPP as a military shield: the law, its application, and how to show it

Research notes, 9 November 2026. Treaty text quoted verbatim from the ICRC's printed edition of the Additional Protocols
([ICRC, AP I and II, PDF](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)), the ICRC Customary IHL
Study vol. I ([PDF](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/customary-international-humanitarian-law-i-icrc-eng.pdf))
and the Rome Statute ([ICC, PDF](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)). The ICRC's online IHL
database pages render only with JavaScript and could not be read directly; the PDFs above are the same texts.

## 1. The law: AP I Articles 56, 51(7), 58; customary rules 42 and 97; the Rome Statute; the parties' status

### Takeaway
AP I gives nuclear electrical generating stations "special protection" from attack even when they are military
objectives; the protection of the station itself lapses only if it supplies power in "regular, significant and direct
support of military operations" and attack is the only feasible way to end that. Separately, parties must "endeavour to
avoid" putting military objectives near such plants (56(5)) and must not use civilians to shield military objectives
(51(7)); the shielding offence in treaty and in the Rome Statute is framed around people, not installations, which is
why "nuclear shield" is a political and analytical metaphor rather than a legal term. Both Russia and Ukraine remain
parties to AP I; Russia withdrew only its 1989 Article 90 declaration (Fact-Finding Commission) in October 2019.

### Cited Findings

**AP I Article 56: Protection of works and installations containing dangerous forces (verbatim)** — [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)
- 56(1): "Works or installations containing dangerous forces, namely dams, dykes and nuclear electrical generating
  stations, shall not be made the object of attack, even where these objects are military objectives, if such attack
  may cause the release of dangerous forces and consequent severe losses among the civilian population. Other military
  objectives located at or in the vicinity of these works or installations shall not be made the object of attack if
  such attack may cause the release of dangerous forces from the works or installations and consequent severe losses
  among the civilian population."
- 56(2): "The special protection against attack provided by paragraph 1 shall cease: [...] b) for a nuclear electrical
  generating station only if it provides electric power in regular, significant and direct support of military
  operations and if such attack is the only feasible way to terminate such support; c) for other military objectives
  located at or in the vicinity of these works or installations only if they are used in regular, significant and
  direct support of military operations and if such attack is the only feasible way to terminate such support."
- 56(3): "In all cases, the civilian population and individual civilians shall remain entitled to all the protection
  accorded them by international law, including the protection of the precautionary measures provided for in Article
  57. If the protection ceases and any of the works, installations or military objectives mentioned in paragraph 1 is
  attacked, all practical precautions shall be taken to avoid the release of the dangerous forces."
- 56(4): "It is prohibited to make any of the works, installations or military objectives mentioned in paragraph 1 the
  object of reprisals."
- 56(5): "The Parties to the conflict shall endeavour to avoid locating any military objectives in the vicinity of the
  works or installations mentioned in paragraph 1. Nevertheless, installations erected for the sole purpose of defending
  the protected works or installations from attack are permissible and shall not themselves be made the object of
  attack, provided that they are not used in hostilities except for defensive actions necessary to respond to attacks
  against the protected works or installations and that their armament is limited to weapons capable only of repelling
  hostile action against the protected works or installations."
- 56(6): "The High Contracting Parties and the Parties to the conflict are urged to conclude further agreements among
  themselves to provide additional protection for objects containing dangerous forces."
- 56(7): "In order to facilitate the identification of the objects protected by this Article, the Parties to the
  conflict may mark them with a special sign consisting of a group of three bright orange circles placed on the same
  axis, as specified in Article 16 of Annex 1 to this Protocol. The absence of such marking in no way relieves any Party
  to the conflict of its obligations under this Article." (The ICRC edition footnotes that the cross-reference should
  now be read as Article 17 of Annex I.)
- Note on wording: 56(1) says "may cause", not "will cause". The 56(5) "vicinity" is undefined: the treaty gives no
  distance.

**AP I Article 51(7)-(8) (verbatim)** — [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)
- 51(7): "The presence or movements of the civilian population or individual civilians shall not be used to render
  certain points or areas immune from military operations, in particular in attempts to shield military objectives from
  attacks or to shield, favour or impede military operations. The Parties to the conflict shall not direct the movement
  of the civilian population or individual civilians in order to attempt to shield military objectives from attacks or
  to shield military operations."
- 51(8): "Any violation of these prohibitions shall not release the Parties to the conflict from their legal
  obligations with respect to the civilian population and civilians, including the obligation to take the
  precautionary measures provided for in Article 57."

**AP I Article 58: Precautions against the effects of attacks (verbatim)** — [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)
- "The Parties to the conflict shall, to the maximum extent feasible: a) without prejudice to Article 49 of the Fourth
  Convention, endeavour to remove the civilian population, individual civilians and civilian objects under their
  control from the vicinity of military objectives; b) avoid locating military objectives within or near densely
  populated areas; c) take the other necessary precautions to protect the civilian population, individual civilians and
  civilian objects under their control against the dangers resulting from military operations."

**AP I Article 85(3)(c): grave breach (verbatim)** — [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)
- Grave breach "when committed wilfully, in violation of the relevant provisions of this Protocol, and causing death or
  serious injury to body or health: [...] c) launching an attack against works or installations containing dangerous
  forces in the knowledge that such attack will cause excessive loss of life, injury to civilians or damage to civilian
  objects, as defined in Article 57, paragraph 2 a) iii)".

**Customary IHL (ICRC study, 2005)** — [ICRC CIHL vol. I](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/customary-international-humanitarian-law-i-icrc-eng.pdf)
- Rule 42: "Particular care must be taken if works and installations containing dangerous forces, namely dams, dykes
  and nuclear electrical generating stations, and other installations located at or in their vicinity are attacked, in
  order to avoid the release of dangerous forces and consequent severe losses among the civilian population." The
  study states practice establishes it as customary "in both international and non-international armed conflicts".
  (The customary rule is weaker than Art. 56: "particular care", not an absolute bar.)
- Rule 97: "The use of human shields is prohibited."
- Related passive-precaution rules: Rule 22 ("all feasible precautions to protect the civilian population and civilian
  objects under their control against the effects of attacks"), Rule 23 ("avoid locating military objectives within or
  near densely populated areas"), Rule 24 ("remove civilian persons and objects under its control from the vicinity of
  military objectives").

**Rome Statute Art. 8(2)(b)(xxiii) (verbatim)** — [ICC](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)
- War crime in international armed conflict: "Utilizing the presence of a civilian or other protected person to render
  certain points, areas or military forces immune from military operations;"
- Ukraine became an ICC State Party in 2025 (not verified in this pass; see Gaps); Russia is not a party.

**Parties' status**
- On 23 October 2019 Russia notified the Swiss depositary that it withdrew the USSR's declaration of 29 September 1989
  accepting the competence of the International Humanitarian Fact-Finding Commission under AP I Art. 90 —
  [Swiss FDFA depositary notification](https://www.dfae.admin.ch/dam/eda/fr/documents/aussenpolitik/voelkerrecht/genevenotifications/191030-GENEVE_e.pdf);
  [WIPO Lex record of the declaration](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/parties/remarks/RU/281).
- Russia's MFA said the withdrawal does not affect Russia's participation in AP I, which "continues to remain valid";
  the Commission can now act regarding Russia only with its consent —
  [Russian MFA, 22 Oct 2019](https://www.mid.ru/en/foreign_policy/news/1473198);
  [CivilM+ comment, S. Krivenko](https://civilmplus.org/en/news/what-does-russia-s-withdrawal-from-the-jurisdiction-of-the-international-fact-finding-commission-mean-comment-by-sergei-krivenko/).
- EJIL:Talk! (Morgandi and Betin, April 2022): "both Ukraine and the Russian Federation are parties" to AP I —
  [EJIL:Talk!](https://www.ejiltalk.org/legal-implications-of-the-military-operations-at-the-chernobyl-and-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plants/).
  Contradicted by a CIDOB paper saying Russia "revoked in 2019 its previous ratification in 1989" of the Protocol —
  [CIDOB](https://www.cidob.org/en/publications/zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-taken-military-target-legal-and-technical-global);
  the depositary notification shows only the Art. 90 declaration was withdrawn, so CIDOB conflates the two.

### Inferences
- The legally precise vocabulary for the film is "special protection" (Art. 56), "military objectives in the vicinity"
  (56(5)) and "passive precautions" (58); "shield" is the vocabulary of 51(7) and the Rome Statute, which protect
  people. Calling ZNPP a "nuclear shield" is a characterisation by governments and analysts, and should be attributed,
  not stated as the law's term.
- 56(2)(b) is close to inapplicable to ZNPP since September 2022: the plant has generated nothing for the grid while in
  shutdown and depends on the lines that feed it from outside (the film's own record). This is an inference from the
  plant's state, not something the sources above argue; the EJIL:Talk! authors raise the related point that the English
  "generating" may imply a working station.
- The treaty leaves "vicinity" undefined, so any ring drawn as "the 56(5) vicinity" would be the film's own invention.

### Gaps
- Ukraine's AP I ratification date (1990) and ICC accession date were not checked against the depositary in this pass.
- The ICRC 1987 Commentary on Art. 56 was cited only second-hand (via EJIL:Talk! and Security and Defence Quarterly,
  "not defending such works or installations"); the commentary paragraphs themselves were not read.

## 2. How scholars apply it to ZNPP (2022-2026), including "nuclear shield"

### Takeaway
The core legal analyses (Dannenbaum on Articles of War; Morgandi and Betin on EJIL:Talk!) agree that Russia's stationing
of troops and weapons at ZNPP very likely breaches 56(5) and Art. 58 passive precautions, and that Ukraine's room to
respond is also tightly confined by 56(1)/(2)(c). The shielding offence is contested as applied to an installation:
Dannenbaum argues the Rome Statute crime can reach co-location with a nuclear plant because the deterrent is the risk to
distant civilians, while acknowledging intent must be proved. "Nuclear shield" and "the equivalent of a human shield" are
the terms of Ukraine's government and the US Secretary of State, not of the treaties.

### Cited Findings
- Tom Dannenbaum, "The Escalating Military Use of the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Plant", Articles of War (Lieber Institute),
  22 Aug 2022: Russia appears to be violating "its passive precautionary obligations (both under Article 56 and under
  Article 58 of Additional Protocol I) by locating non-defensive military objectives in the vicinity"; deliberate
  co-location to deter attack is "an endeavor to co-locate, rather than an endeavor to avoid it"; the 56(5) defensive
  exception is cumulative (use, purpose and armament all defensive) and Russia's deployments likely fail it; "it is
  likely that anything other than a very tightly targeted Ukrainian response would violate Article 56"; he argues the
  Rome Statute 8(2)(b)(xxiii) elements should be read to cover co-location with a nuclear plant, since fallout risk to
  distant civilians is what deters, but individual intent must be proven; a demilitarised zone needs both parties'
  agreement (AP I Art. 60), and a material breach releases the other party from the zone, though Art. 56 still applies —
  [Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/escalating-military-use-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)
- Dannenbaum, "Weaponizing Civilians: Human Shields in Ukraine" (Lieber): AP I bars using any civilians to shield
  military operations; the prohibition protects people, not objects; the mere presence of civilians near operations does
  not violate it — [Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/weaponizing-civilians-human-shields-ukraine/)
- A separate Lieber post on the March 2022 attack: an Art. 56 violation is likely if the shelling risked a release, but
  the associated war crime is less likely to be made out —
  [Lieber Institute, "The Attack at the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Plant and AP I"](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/attack-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)
- Tibisay Morgandi and Batuhan Betin, EJIL:Talk!, 15 Apr 2022: "may cause" means a risk at the time of attack suffices;
  the March 2022 seizure was "clearly" an attack; AP I Art. 49 defines attacks as "acts of violence against the
  adversary, whether in offence or in defence", so the rules bind defenders too, leaving "a dangerous grey zone" over
  what defensive operations are lawful; operationalising 56(5) is "far from straightforward"; Russia as occupying power
  (Hague Regs Arts 42-43) owes due diligence against transboundary harm; under the Vienna Convention 1963 Art. IV(3)(a)
  the operator (Energoatom) is likely exonerated for damage "directly due to an act of armed conflict" —
  [EJIL:Talk!](https://www.ejiltalk.org/legal-implications-of-the-military-operations-at-the-chernobyl-and-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plants/)
- SAIIA: Art. 56 is violated not only if a release occurs but if, at the time of attack, there was a risk of it —
  [SAIIA](https://saiia.org.za/research/legal-protection-for-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant-matters-for-nuclear-installations-everywhere/)
- Security and Defence Quarterly: special protection has been "deliberately used as a 'shield' for weapons, vehicles,
  and troops"; cites the ICRC 1987 Commentary that parties may consider not defending such facilities at all —
  [Security and Defence Quarterly](https://securityanddefence.pl/Nuclear-power-plants-in-war-zones-Lessons-learned-from-the-war-in-Ukraine,174810,0,2.html)
- Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists (Oct 2022) and Brookings argue ZNPP exposes gaps in the legal protection of nuclear
  installations in war; Brookings notes no plant had been used this way before —
  [Bulletin](https://thebulletin.org/2022/10/russian-actions-at-zaporizhzhia-show-need-for-better-legal-protections-of-nuclear-installations/);
  [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-to-do-about-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant/)
- Political uses of the shield frame: Ukraine's MFA calls ZNPP a "nuclear shield" for shelling civilian infrastructure —
  [MFA of Ukraine](https://mfa.gov.ua/en/news/komentar-mzs-ukrayini-stosovno-zafiksovanih-komandoyu-magate-artilerijskih-obstriliv-poblizu-zaporizkoyi-aes);
  Centre for Countering Disinformation, "Russia is hiding behind the nuclear shield" —
  [CPD](https://cpd.gov.ua/en/articles-en/russia-is-hiding-behind-the-nuclear-shield/);
  Blinken (Aug 2022): Russia is using the plant as "the equivalent of a human shield" —
  [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/blinken-russia-ukraine-nuclear-plant-zaporizhzhia-equivalent-of-a-human-shield/);
  Euromaidan Press, Day 160 headline "nuclear shield" —
  [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2022/08/02/russo-ukrainian-war-day-160-russians-turned-the-zaporizhzhia-npp-into-a-nuclear-shield/)
- Russia's counter-claim: its defence ministry said satellite imagery "shows that weapons, especially heavy ones, are not
  placed on the territory of this station" — [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2022/08/18/russian-vehicles-seen-inside-turbine-hall-at-ukraine-nuclear-plant/)
- The IAEA's own findings (6 Sept 2022 report, briefed to the Security Council): Russian troops and military vehicles
  inside the site, including vehicles near the turbines —
  [UN press SC/15020](https://press.un.org/en/2022/sc15020.doc.htm)
- Grossi's five principles (UNSC, 30 May 2023): "no attack of any kind from or against the plant"; the plant should not
  be used as storage or a base for heavy weapons (e.g. multiple rocket launchers) or military personnel that could be
  used for an attack from it; off-site power kept available and secure; essential systems protected; no action
  undermining the principles. Russia said they matched what it was already doing without committing; Ukraine asked to
  add withdrawal of Russian personnel —
  [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/05/1137172);
  [WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Grossi-calls-Zaporizhzhia-principles-step-in-right)

### Inferences
- The "shield" claim is really two claims of different evidential weight: (a) military objectives are located at the
  plant (documented by the IAEA: vehicles, troops), which engages 56(5)/58; (b) they are there in order to deter attack
  (intent), which is what "shield" asserts and which is inferred from pattern, not observed. A film can show (a) as
  sourced fact and must present (b) as a characterisation attributed to who makes it.
- Grossi's principles are the closest thing to an operative, agreed-in-substance standard; they are about conduct
  ("from or against the plant"), not a drawn boundary.

### Gaps
- No Just Security or ICRC Law & Policy blog piece specifically on ZNPP and shielding surfaced in searches; there may
  be some (not found is not none). No 2024-2026 academic journal article was read in full.
- IAEA reports of mines between the site's perimeters (2023) and later reports on military presence were not re-checked
  in this pass.

### Takeaway

2024, 99-9-60) have all demanded that Russia withdraw its military and other unauthorised personnel. None is binding or

## 4. Visual precedents: contested zones, military use and "shields", shown without overstating

### Takeaway
No major outlet found drew the IAEA's proposed ZNPP zone as a shape, because none was ever defined; the IAEA itself
described it only as "limited to the perimeter and the plant itself". Grossi's own metaphor was "a shield, a bubble
around the perimeter", which is the nearest source for a dome image and also its warning: it was a proposal, never
agreed. Precedents for showing unsettled zones are Forensic Architecture's mapping of Gaza's shifting "humanitarian
zone" (boundaries redrawn over time, set against where attacks fell), the IAEA's own guidance that planning circles are
first approximations whose real edges follow landmarks, and the long critique of Fukushima's concentric circles as
encoding distance, not deposition. Coverage of ZNPP's military use (NPR, CNN, Texty) relied on dated satellite images
matched to footage, with explicit "could not independently verify" language.

### Cited Findings
**The IAEA zone itself**
- 6 Sept 2022, Grossi to the UNSC: "we are proposing the establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone
  limited to the perimeter and the plant itself"; it "requires agreement by all relevant parties"; Nebenzia called it
  "not serious" — [UN press SC/15020](https://press.un.org/en/2022/sc15020.doc.htm);
  [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2022/09/07/un-agency-calls-for-safety-zone-around-ukraine-nuclear-plant)
- Grossi called for "a shield, a bubble around the perimeter of the facility" (as quoted by Ross Peel, KCL Centre for
  Science & Security Studies, 7 Sept 2022, from The Conversation); Peel: IAEA oversight of an endangered plant in war
  would be unprecedented and "legally and practically very challenging" —
  [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/why-are-the-proposals-for-a-demilitarised-zone-around-europes-biggest-nuclear-power-plant-unprecedented)

- Ukraine's Hydrometeorological Centre (State Emergency Service) daily radiological bulletins map dashed rings at 30
  km and 100 km around each NPP, ZNPP included: "Пунктирною лінією визначено 100км та 30км зону навколо АЕС" —
  [Ukrhydromet bulletin, 31 Jan 2026, PDF](https://www.meteo.gov.ua/f/radiolog/radiolog%20dovidka/%D0%86%D0%BD%D1%84%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%96%D0%B9%D0%BD%D0%B0%20%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%D0%BA%D0%B0%20%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%2031%2001%202026%20%D0%B7%D0%B0%2010-00.pdf)
- Critique of circles: Fukushima's 20/30 km concentric zones show distance, not where fallout landed; contamination
  followed wind, topography and hydrology, with hot spots far beyond the rings (Robert Jacobs; CRMS Atomic Age blog);
  the official contour map itself already showed the north-west plume —
  [Jacobs, "The Broken Maps of Fukushima"](https://www.dianuke.org/broken-maps-fukushima/);
  [CRMS Atomic Age blog](https://lucian.uchicago.edu/blogs/atomicage/2013/12/02/fukushima-radiation-exceeds-chernobyl-evacuation-levels-across-japan-via-simply-info);
  [Modern Power Systems](https://www.modernpowersystems.com/?p=8587)

### Inferences
- A dome over ZNPP for the IAEA zone visualises Grossi's own "shield, a bubble" metaphor, so it can be attributed to
  him; but it gives a never-agreed, never-sized proposal the most solid-looking form there is. If used, it should be
  bounded by the plant's own perimeter (the only extent the IAEA named), drawn as incomplete or dashed, labelled
  "proposed, never agreed, no size published", and dated (6 Sept 2022 to its setting aside), consistent with the zoom-out
  film's existing dashed circle.
- A dome is also physically misleading as a protection metaphor: nothing in the law or the proposal is a volume, and a
  hemisphere suggests a barrier to fallout or projectiles. Domes of different sizes side by side would read as degrees of
  physical protection; the zones listed in section 5 are planning, monitoring or legal categories, not barriers.
- Per the IAEA's own guidance, true-scale circles for PAZ/UPZ should be labelled "suggested maximum radius", since real
  planning zones follow landmarks.

### Gaps
- No NYT, WSJ, Reuters or Bellingcat graphic of the proposed ZNPP zone, or of ZNPP's military use, could be retrieved
  directly (search did not surface their graphics pages); this does not show none exists.
- No published critique of the dome or hemisphere metaphor for protection or exclusion zones was found (the nearest are
  the Fukushima circle critiques). "Iron Dome" and "Golden Dome" graphics were not researched.

## 6. Beyond 3D: how investigative practices present contested claims (coordinator's addition)

### Takeaway
The working toolkit is: dated timelines and counts drawn from attributable reports; side-by-side claim and counter-claim
with what was verified; annotated, dated satellite images matched to footage; document montages of the orders or
statements themselves; maps of event counts by area, not by point; and sound treated as evidence (synchronisation,
echo, weapon identification), or, when synthetic, said to be.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline and counts: Texty's ZNPP piece organises capture, shutdowns and line losses by date and maps which 750 kV
  lines were cut — [Texty](https://texty.org.ua/projects/109061/nuclear-terror-how-russia-took-nuclear-plant-and-people-enerhodar-hostage/);
  FA mapped "fifty-eight evacuation orders" (18 Mar to 1 Aug 2025) against attacks inside the zone, a count-over-time
  method — [FA, Humanitarian Violence](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/humanitarian-violence-in-gaza)
- Claim against claim, with verification stated: CNN printed Russia's MoD claim that heavy weapons were not at the site
  beside the turbine-hall footage — [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2022/08/18/russian-vehicles-seen-inside-turbine-hall-at-ukraine-nuclear-plant/);
  Reuters on the Nov 2022 shelling: both sides blamed each other and Reuters "was unable to independently verify" —
  [Reuters explainer via BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/world/europe/2022-11-21-whats-at-risk-if-an-accident-occurs-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)
- Document montage / rebuttal of an opponent's visuals: FA's ICJ assessment found "eight instances" where Israel's legal
  team misrepresented visual evidence, shown by setting the exhibits beside the analysis —
  [FA ICJ Assessment Report, PDF](https://content.forensic-architecture.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2024.02.26_ICJ-Assessment-Report_Forensic-Architecture.pdf)
- Sound as evidence: FA's audio analysis uses "synchronisation, identification of weapons, or using 'ear-witness'
  testimony and 'echo profiling' to reconstruct spaces and events through sound" —
  [FA methodology: audio analysis](https://forensic-architecture.org/methodology/audio-analysis);
  situated testimony (witnesses walking through a model) in Saydnaya and Grenfell —
  [Media Commons](https://mediacommons.org/imr/node/16228); critique of the films' persuasive "correspondence with
  reality" — [MUBI Notebook](https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/remains-to-be-seen-the-video-work-of-forensic-architecture)
- Dashed rings as a public-service convention: Ukrhydromet's 30/100 km dashed rings round NPPs —
  [Ukrhydromet](https://www.meteo.gov.ua/f/radiolog/radiolog%20dovidka/%D0%86%D0%BD%D1%84%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%96%D0%B9%D0%BD%D0%B0%20%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%D0%BA%D0%B0%20%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%2031%2001%202026%20%D0%B7%D0%B0%2010-00.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Powerlines, a claims matrix (who says / what evidence / verified by whom / date) suits the "shield" claim: IAEA
  observation of vehicles and troops (observed, by the IAEA); "nuclear shield" (Ukraine MFA, US State, characterisation);
  Russia's denial (MoD); scholars' legal reading (Dannenbaum, Morgandi and Betin, opinion). Quotes set as type should be
  the verbatim treaty and resolution text above, attributed and dated.
- The votes (Board, GC, GA) are a natural dated sequence to set as type or as a tally, not a map.
- Shelling counts by region (not points) match the project's safety rule (regions of about 3 km or more, never finer
  than the source).

### Gaps
- No NYT Visual Investigations, WSJ or Bellingcat ZNPP pieces were read directly, so their specific devices (e.g. NYT's
  claim-versus-evidence panels) are not cited here.
