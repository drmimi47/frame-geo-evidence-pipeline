# The IAEA saw trucks, heard rockets, named no one

The occupied Zaporizhzhia plant has been a military site, on the IAEA's own first-hand observation. Its team saw Russian military personnel, vehicles and trucks inside the turbine halls of units 1 and 2 in September 2022, trucks in unit 2's turbine hall again in August 2023, and anti-personnel mines between the site's perimeter fences. Whether it has been a *firing position* from which Nikopol and Marhanets are shelled is a question with three different answers depending on how close to the reactors one asks. Firing from **beside** the plant is independently supported: the New York Times reported it on the ground in mid-2022, the Centre for Information Resilience geolocated one rocket launch in the industrial strip between the nuclear and thermal plants (September 2022), and IAEA staff themselves **heard** rockets "fired from close to the plant" (November 2023) and "outgoing artillery fire from near the plant" (April 2024), without saying who fired. Firing from **inside the fence** at the north bank is a Ukrainian claim, detailed and repeated from 2022 to 2026 (Grads by the spent-fuel store, drone launch points by unit 6, ammunition in basements), but no independent group has geolocated a single such launch, the IAEA never saw heavy weapons there, and Russia denies all of it. No source says what share of the fire on Nikopol, which ran to hundreds of drone attacks a month by 2024–25, comes from the plant rather than elsewhere on the south bank. The IAEA proposed a protection zone on 6 September 2022 "limited to the perimeter and the plant itself", never agreed and never sized, recast it on 30 March 2023 as a list of things to avoid, and on 30 May 2023 "established" five principles, the first of which is "no attack of any kind from or against the plant". For *Powerlines*, the honest picture is asymmetric: rings at true scale for the zones that have published sizes, and words, not shapes, for every proposal that never had one, which includes the only zone ever meant to stop the fighting.

## Trucks in the turbine halls are observed; launches from inside the fence are not

The evidence sorts into a ladder of strength, and the film's rules need each rung kept apart. At the top is what the IAEA saw with its own eyes. Its 2nd Summary Report, released 6 September 2022 after the 1 September mission, states that "the team observed the presence of Russian military personnel, vehicles and equipment at various places at the ZNPP, including **several military trucks on the ground floor of the Unit 1 and Unit 2 turbine halls** and military vehicles stationed under the overpass connecting the reactor units" (para. 39), adding that operating staff could not freely reach the cooling ponds, roofs or water intake, and that access to the cooling ponds "was required to be granted by the military personnel at the site" (para. 47) ([IAEA 2nd Summary Report, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)). The report keeps its own sighting apart from what it was told: Ukraine's July–August claims about equipment in the turbine halls sit in para. 23, and the SNRIU's figure of **"more than 40 units of military equipment"** is in para. 29 as something the IAEA was informed of, not something it counted ([same report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)). Later first-hand observations follow the same pattern: **directional anti-personnel mines** in the buffer zone between the inner and outer perimeter, "facing away from the site", seen on 23 July 2023 and again on 1 August ([IAEA Update 175](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-175-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 177](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-177-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)); "a number of military trucks parked" in unit 2's turbine hall on 8 August 2023 ([Update 178](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-178-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)); and Russian troops on unit 6's roof engaging an approaching drone while IAEA staff stood there, 7 April 2024 ([Update 220](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-220-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)).

Just as important is what the IAEA did **not** see. On 24 July 2023 it said its experts had carried out walkdowns "across the site, **without seeing any heavy military equipment**" ([Update 175](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-175-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)), and Grossi had earlier said monitors saw armoured vehicles and a security presence but not MLRS, tanks or artillery ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/4041141/kremlin-no-heavy-weapons-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant?page=3)). That ceiling is soft. The IAEA moved only on routes the Russian side approved, waited months for roofs (units 3 and 4 only on 3 August 2023), was kept from parts of the turbine halls and from impact sites "due to security reasons", and in January 2024 Grossi told the Security Council that some areas had been closed "sometimes for many months" ([Update 177](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-177-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 221](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-221-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 208](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-208-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). Russia denied the IAEA access to the unit 3–4 roofs again in July 2024 ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/14/ukraine-says-russian-military-trucks-are-at-zaporizhzhia-power-plant/)). "Not seen" therefore means not seen where the IAEA was allowed to look.

The second rung is what the IAEA **heard**, which is where firing near the plant enters its record. On 27 November 2023 experts "heard several rockets that appeared to have been **fired from close to the plant**", whose "distinctive sound indicated they were fired from a Multiple Launch Rocket System nearby" ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/IAEA-reports-rockets-fired-from-near-Zaporizhzhia)). In April 2024 they reported "outgoing artillery fire from near the plant" and, on 13 April, "**sixteen rounds of outgoing artillery fire in less than half an hour** – some at near distance" ([Update 221](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-221-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 223](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-223-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). None of these says *from the site*, gives a distance or names the side. The plant was behind Russian lines, so outgoing fire from near it is very probably Russian, but that is an inference the IAEA declined to write.

The third rung is independent journalism and open-source work, and it is thin but real. CNN geolocated leaked video of at least five Russian military trucks, one marked "Z", inside a turbine hall (August 2022), while saying it could not tell whether they were stored or sheltering, nor when the video was shot ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/08/19/europe/ukraine-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-russian-vehicles-intl-hnk)). The Centre for Information Resilience matched a convoy with anti-aircraft guns entering the site (2 August 2022), Maxar imagery of equipment under the cooling pipes (29 August), and Russia's own Izvestia footage of IAEA inspectors walking past "Z" trucks; above all, it geolocated video filmed from the north bank on the night of 2–3 September 2022 showing **an MLRS firing from the Enerhodar industrial area between the nuclear and thermal plants**, rating it "highly likely" from one clip and an approximate trajectory ([CIR, "Danger Zone"](https://www.info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)). The New York Times reported from Nikopol on 1 August 2022 that Russian forces had been firing from the plant's cover since mid-July, roughly when HIMARS made other Russian positions vulnerable, quoting Nikopol's mayor that the Russians were "hiding there so they cannot be hit" ([Irish Times / NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2022/08/01/ukraine-faces-new-obstacle-as-russia-uses-nuclear-power-plant-for-cover/)). In January 2026 Defense News geolocated Ukrainian drone video to the plant and saw several military-painted vehicles, but could not verify the accompanying claim of MLRS strikes from there ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/14/ukraine-says-russian-military-trucks-are-at-zaporizhzhia-power-plant/)).

The bottom rung is the Ukrainian claims, and they track the war's phases. In 2022 it was artillery and Grads: Zelensky said troops stationed at the plant fire on Nikopol and Marhanets and that anyone shooting "using the plant as cover" becomes "a special target" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/8/13/russians-damaging-or-occupying-nuclear-plant-are-targets-kyiv)); Energoatom said in December 2022 that several Grad launchers stood by the spent-fuel store next to a reactor, with no imagery published ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/energoatom-russia-places-grad-rocket-launchers-next-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-reactor/)). On 30 May 2023 Ukraine told the Security Council of about **50 heavy weapons** in three units' turbine halls, the same meeting at which Grossi said his team had seen no heavy equipment ([Wikipedia, citing WNN and UN records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Nuclear_Power_Plant_crisis)). From 2024 it was drones: HUR released intercepted drone-camera footage over the reactors and said launch sites stood "right next to the sixth reactor" and that the site had trained drone pilots since summer 2023 ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/military-intelligence-russia-flies-attack-drones-over-occupied-zaporizhzhia-plant/)); Grossi said it was impossible to determine definitively who launched drones over the plant ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2024/05/2/7453907/)). In July 2026 HUR described a "military base": Geran-type drone launch and command points, ammunition in basements, vehicles in four turbine halls, rooftop gun positions and about 1,500 Rosgvardia personnel, backed publicly by one photograph ([Defense Express](https://en.defence-ua.com/news/ukraines_defense_intelligence_russia_has_turned_zaporizhzhia_nuclear_plant_into_military_base-19124.html); [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/07/13/8043850/)). The best-supported Ukrainian claims are those about **presence**, which the IAEA confirmed for 2022 and 2023; the weakest are the specific counts and placements, and one of them (the 50 heavy weapons) was contradicted by the IAEA on the day it was made.

The scale of what lands on the north bank is documented; its origin is not. Nikopol City Council counted **0 drone attacks on Nikopol in June 2023, 290 in June 2024 and 362 in June 2025**, and Truth Hounds counted an average of **459 drone attacks a month** on Nikopol district from September 2024 to March 2025, in a report built on 39 survivor and witness interviews ([Truth Hounds, "Killzone"](https://dev.truth-hounds.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/killzone-how-russian-drones-are-devastating-the-river-dnipros-right-bank.pdf); [bpb documentation](https://www.bpb.de/themen/europa/ukraine-analysen/nr-321/573076/dokumentation-angriffe-russischer-drohnen-auf-die-ukrainische-zivilbevoelkerung-entlang-des-dnipro/)); these figures reached the notes through a search summary because the PDF returned an error, so they must be checked in the original before going on screen. Casualty totals conflict: 67 killed and 372 injured since summer 2022 by January 2024, then 61 killed and nearly 500 injured by September 2024 ([Hromadske Radio, Jan 2024](https://hromadske.radio/en/news/2024/01/14/67-civilians-have-been-killed-in-nikopol-since-the-beginning-of-the-shelling-head-of-district-military-administration); [Hromadske Radio, Sep 2024](https://hromadske.radio/en/news/2024/09/30/since-the-summer-of-2022-61-people-have-died-in-nikopol-from-russian-shelling-a-hromadske-correspondent)). The whole occupied bank opposite, from Kamianka-Dniprovska to Vodiane, is within artillery and FPV range, so the plant and Enerhodar are among several plausible firing areas. Nothing found breaks the attacks down by launch area. The defensible sentence is therefore: *Ukrainian officials say some of the fire on Nikopol comes from the plant and Enerhodar; the IAEA saw military vehicles inside the plant and heard rockets fired from close to it; open-source investigators placed one rocket launch beside it.* The indefensible one is "Nikopol is shelled from the plant".

## Both sides accuse each other, and the IAEA names no one

Each side tells a mirror-image story. Ukraine says Russia fires from the plant, shells the site and Enerhodar itself, and stages provocations; Russia says Ukraine shells the plant and Enerhodar and denies any heavy weapons there. Shoigu said in August 2022 that Russia had no heavy weapons on the plant's territory or around it ([Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2022/08/18/russia-weapons-ukraine-nuclear/amp/)), Peskov repeated it in December 2022 and invited the IAEA to confirm ([CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2022-12-13/Peskov-denies-Russian-heavy-weapons-at-Zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-1fJv98U2SwU/index.html)), and Russia's defence ministry said imagery showed "weapons, especially heavy ones, are not placed on the territory of this station" in the same week CNN geolocated trucks in a turbine hall ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/08/19/europe/ukraine-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-russian-vehicles-intl-hnk)). For strikes on the plant, the pattern repeats at every major incident. Rosatom blamed Ukraine for the 7 April 2024 drone hits on unit 6's dome, and HUR said Ukraine was not involved ([Cyprus Mail / Reuters](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/04/08/russia-says-ukraine-struck-nuclear-plant-kyiv-denies-attack)). Russian officials blamed a Ukrainian drone for the 11 August 2024 cooling-tower fire, and Kyiv blamed Russian arson or negligence ([Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2024/08/12/ukraine-russia-trade-barbs-over-fire-at-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant-a85995/pdf)). Likhachev said a fibre-optic Ukrainian drone hit unit 6's turbine hall on 30 May 2026, and the Southern Defence Forces replied that "Ukraine's Defense Forces did not strike Unit 6" and that "traditionally, the Russian side provides zero evidence" ([TSN](https://tsn.ua/en/ukrayina/why-kremlin-fabricated-ukrainian-attack-on-znpp-military-and-nsdc-expose-enemys-goal-3095327.html)). By June 2026 Likhachev was describing "dozens of strikes every day, every night" on Enerhodar's energy infrastructure, none independently verified ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/118170/)).

Against this the IAEA has held one line for four years: it describes damage and does not say who caused it. "Whoever is shelling at the Zaporizhzhya Nuclear Power Plant, is taking huge risks" (November 2022), "Whoever is behind them, they are playing with fire" (April 2024) ([Update 129](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-129-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 221](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-221-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). Grossi explained why on 19 April 2024: "We are not commentators. We are not political speculators or analysts, we are an international agency of inspectors", and attribution needs "proof, indisputable evidence", which a drone "manned from one side" and belonging to "another side" rarely gives ([WNN](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/IAEA-s-Grossi-explains-why-blame-is-not-being-attr)). Twice the IAEA's observations weighed against one side's story without naming anyone: after the cooling-tower fire it found no drone remains and judged it "unlikely that the primary source of the fire began at the base of the cooling tower" ([Update 243](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-243-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)), and it located the September 2025 line cut "about 1.5 km from the site" without saying who caused it ([IAEA Update 317 via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/09/ukraine-250930-iaea01.htm)).

Independent attribution exists for a handful of events, all from journalists and OSINT groups, none from the UN or OSCE. NPR's analysis of the plant's own security-camera footage found that Russian forces repeatedly fired heavy weapons toward the reactor area during the assault of 4 March 2022 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/03/11/1085427380/ukraine-nuclear-power-plant-zaporizhzhia)). CIR traced a shell that hit central Enerhodar in early September 2022 to the Russian-held bank ("highly likely"), which cuts against the Russian claim that Enerhodar was being shelled by Ukraine, for that one event ([CIR](https://www.info-res.org/post/danger-zone-how-russia-is-risking-nuclear-disaster-at-the-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant)). ISW assessed in August 2022 that Russia was responsible for the attacks on and around the plant, an analytic judgment rather than an investigation ([Espreso, summarising ISW](https://global.espreso.tv/russia-is-responsible-for-attacks-on-and-around-the-zaporizhia-nuclear-power-plant-isw)). Ukraine's own restraint is real but partial. It claimed a drone strike on a Grad launcher, an air-defence system and a tent camp on the site in July 2022 ([Irish Times / NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2022/08/01/ukraine-faces-new-obstacle-as-russia-uses-nuclear-power-plant-for-cover/)). The NYT's point is that it "cannot unleash volleys of shells in return", and a battalion commander, asked how Ukraine answers, said only: "This is a nuclear site" ([same](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2022/08/01/ukraine-faces-new-obstacle-as-russia-uses-nuclear-power-plant-for-cover/)). HUR's spokesman gave a self-imposed no-fire distance of about 1.5 km round the plant in 2024, one paraphrased remark rather than a published rule ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/military-intelligence-russia-flies-attack-drones-over-occupied-zaporizhzhia-plant/)).

The structure matters for the film. The two sides' claims are symmetrical, but the evidence is not. Independent sources confirm Russian troops, vehicles, mines and launches *at or beside* the site. None confirms a Ukrainian strike on the site, and none confirms a Russian one either. The "shield" itself has two layers, described by ISW, Blinken and the UK MoD in August 2022. One is tactical: launchers near the reactors are safe from counter-battery fire. The other is political: Russia is "using the NPP to play on Western fears of a nuclear disaster" ([Ukrinform summarising ISW](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3545204-isw-explains-why-russian-forces-use-power-plants-as-nuclear-shield.html); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/blinken-russia-ukraine-nuclear-plant-zaporizhzhia-equivalent-of-a-human-shield/)). The IAEA's non-attribution, principled as it is, feeds the second layer: every explosion yields two stories and no verdict.

## A zone with no size became five principles with no enforcer

The protection zone was born in Recommendation 1 of the 6 September 2022 report: shelling "should be stopped immediately", which "requires agreement by all relevant parties to the establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone around the ZNPP" ([IAEA 2nd Summary Report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)). The report gives no size. The only extent the IAEA ever named came from Grossi's Security Council briefing that day: "a nuclear safety and security protection zone **limited to the perimeter and the plant itself**" ([UN press SC/15020](https://press.un.org/en/2022/sc15020.doc.htm); [VOA via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2022/09/ukraine-220906-voa02.htm)). He also called it "a shield, a bubble around the perimeter of the facility" ([King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/why-are-the-proposals-for-a-demilitarised-zone-around-europes-biggest-nuclear-power-plant-unprecedented)). Russia's ambassador called it "not serious" ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2022/09/07/un-agency-calls-for-safety-zone-around-ukraine-nuclear-plant)). It is worth separating from Guterres's August 2022 two-step plan, which did speak of a "demilitarised perimeter", also without a distance, and which Russia's foreign ministry rejected ([Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/news/2022/08/18/russia-rejects-proposal-for-demilitarized-zone-around-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant)); a search summary attributing "a demilitarized perimeter as a second step" to the IAEA report is wrong, as the report's text contains neither phrase. The zone was pressed again after heavy shelling in November 2022 ("must happen now") ([Update 129](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-129-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)).

On **30 March 2023**, after Grossi's second visit, the IAEA recast it: the plan "had evolved from an initial proposal to establish a zone around the plant to now focus more on **what should be avoided** to ensure its protection, rather than on territorial aspects". Grossi named the principle that would carry the shield question: "a nuclear plant should not be attacked under any circumstances, and **it also shouldn't be used to attack others**" ([IAEA Update 152](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-152-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). He had told AP the approach was now "a series of principle[s] or commitments" in which the parties "are not agreeing with each other" ([Arms Control Association](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2023-05/news/iaea-shifts-priorities-zaporizhzhia)). The zone was never withdrawn by name; it dissolved into a conduct standard.

On **30 May 2023** Grossi presented the five principles to the Security Council and declared them "hereby established": (1) "There should be no attack of any kind from or against the plant"; (2) the plant "should not be used as storage or a base for heavy weapons (i.e. multiple rocket launchers, artillery systems and munitions, and tanks) or military personnel that could be used for an attack from the plant"; (3) off-site power "should not be put at risk"; (4) essential structures, systems and components "should be protected from attacks or acts of sabotage"; (5) "No action should be taken that undermines these principles". The IAEA team on site would report on their observance and the Director General "will report publicly on any violations" ([IAEA DG to the UNSC, verbatim via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2023/05/ukraine-230530-iaea01.htm); principles 1, 2, 4 and 5 checked word for word against the [IAEA Board statement, 11 April 2024](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/statements/iaea-director-generals-introductory-statement-to-the-board-of-governors-11-april-2024)). Russia said the principles matched what it was already doing without committing to them; Ukraine asked that the withdrawal of Russian personnel be added ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/05/1137172); [WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Grossi-calls-Zaporizhzhia-principles-step-in-right)). Principle 2 is the zone's nearest descendant, but narrower: it bans *heavy* weapons and personnel "that could be used for an attack from the plant", not military presence as such, so the trucks, guards and mines the IAEA actually saw fall outside its explicit words. "Never agreed" fits the zone; "established", unilaterally by the Director General, fits the principles.

The IAEA's record on compliance runs in three phases. From June 2023 to March 2024 it said there were "no indications that the five concrete principles are not being observed", while warning that it needed access to all areas to be sure ([Update 208](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-208-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). The drone strikes of 7 April 2024 were "a clear violation", then "the first clear violation", with no violator named ([Update 220](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-220-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 221](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-221-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). Since then the language has been appeal: "strict observance" (August 2024), "would violate" in the conditional (July 2025), "abide by" (February 2026), "full respect" (August 2026) ([Update 245](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-245-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 303](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-303-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 342](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-342-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 362](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-362-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). No IAEA statement found after July 2023 assesses principle 2, the one about bases and heavy weapons, either as broken or as kept. Principle 3, off-site power, is the one now most invoked, which ties the shield question directly to the film's two lines. Politically, the demands for withdrawal have stacked up without effect: the IAEA Board voted 26–2–7 in September 2022 ([WNN](https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/IAEA-board-calls-for-Russia-to-hand-over-control-o)), the UN General Assembly 99–9–60 on 11 July 2024, demanding Russia "urgently withdraw its military and other unauthorized personnel" ([UN press GA/12614](https://press.un.org/en/2024/ga12614.doc.htm)), and the IAEA General Conference every year from 2023 with a slowly shrinking majority, from 69 in favour to 63 in 2026 (counts from Ukrainian government and media) ([Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/en/news/henkonferentsiia-mahate-ukhvalyla-rezoliutsiiu-z-vymohoiu-nehainoi-deokupatsii-zaes); [UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/63-countries-supported-the-resolution-on-ukraines-nuclear-safety-sybiha)). The only zones round the plant actually agreed by both sides have been the IAEA-brokered **local repair truces**, seven from late 2025 to September 2026, temporary, for line repairs, and without any published distance ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/6/5/iaea-brokers-ukraine-ceasefire-to-allow-nuclear-plant-repairs); [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-09/article-8377/zaporizhzhia-restores-offsite-power-following-iaeabrokered-local-ceasefire/)).

## Article 56 protects the plant; "shield" is a word the law keeps for people

Additional Protocol I gives nuclear electrical generating stations **special protection**: they "shall not be made the object of attack, even where these objects are military objectives, if such attack **may** cause the release of dangerous forces", and the same goes for "other military objectives located at or in the vicinity" (Art. 56(1)) ([ICRC, AP I text](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)). Protection of the station lapses only if it supplies power "in regular, significant and direct support of military operations" and attack is "the only feasible way to terminate such support" (56(2)(b)); military objectives beside it lose protection on the same double test (56(2)(c)). The duty on the occupier is 56(5): parties "shall **endeavour to avoid** locating any military objectives in the vicinity", with an exception only for installations "erected for the sole purpose of defending the protected works", armed only to repel attacks on them. Art. 58 adds the general passive precautions: avoid placing military objectives near populated areas and protect civilians under one's control ([same](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)). The treaty never defines "vicinity", so no distance in the law could be drawn. The customary rule is weaker than the treaty: "particular care must be taken" (Rule 42) ([ICRC Customary IHL Study](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/customary-international-humanitarian-law-i-icrc-eng.pdf)). Both states remain parties to AP I. Russia withdrew in 2019 only its declaration accepting the Fact-Finding Commission under Art. 90, not the Protocol, despite a CIDOB paper's claim otherwise ([Swiss depositary notification](https://www.dfae.admin.ch/dam/eda/fr/documents/aussenpolitik/voelkerrecht/genevenotifications/191030-GENEVE_e.pdf); [Russian MFA](https://www.mid.ru/en/foreign_policy/news/1473198)).

"Shielding" in treaty law is about people. Art. 51(7) forbids using "the presence or movements of the civilian population" to "shield military objectives from attacks", customary Rule 97 bans human shields, and the Rome Statute's war crime is "utilizing the presence of a civilian or other protected person to render certain points, areas or military forces immune from military operations" (Art. 8(2)(b)(xxiii)) ([ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf); [ICC](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)). "Nuclear shield" is therefore a characterisation, by ISW, Ukraine's foreign ministry and the US Secretary of State ("the equivalent of a human shield"), not the law's term ([MFA of Ukraine](https://mfa.gov.ua/en/news/komentar-mzs-ukrayini-stosovno-zafiksovanih-komandoyu-magate-artilerijskih-obstriliv-poblizu-zaporizkoyi-aes); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/blinken-russia-ukraine-nuclear-plant-zaporizhzhia-equivalent-of-a-human-shield/)). The scholars bridge the gap carefully. Tom Dannenbaum argues Russia appears to be violating its passive precautions under Articles 56 and 58, since deliberate co-location to deter attack is "an endeavor to co-locate, rather than an endeavor to avoid it", and that its deployments fail the cumulative defensive exception. He also argues the Rome Statute crime can be read to reach co-location with a nuclear plant, because the deterrent is the risk to distant civilians, while conceding that individual intent must be proven. He judges that "anything other than a very tightly targeted Ukrainian response would violate Article 56", and notes that a demilitarised zone needs both parties' agreement under AP I Art. 60 ([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/escalating-military-use-zaporizhzhia-nuclear-plant/)). Morgandi and Betin add that "may cause" means risk at the time of attack suffices, and that the rules bind defenders as well as attackers ([EJIL:Talk!](https://www.ejiltalk.org/legal-implications-of-the-military-operations-at-the-chernobyl-and-zaporizhzhya-nuclear-power-plants/)).

Two points follow, and they matter on screen. The legal case does not need the intent the word "shield" asserts: the presence of military objectives at the plant, observed by the IAEA, is itself what 56(5) and 58 address. The intent to deter, which is what "shield" means, is inferred from the pattern, not observed, so it has to be attributed whenever it is said. And the law's asymmetry is the shield's mechanism. The same Article that tells the occupier to keep weapons away tells the other side it may not strike them if a release *may* follow, so the restraint the NYT and HUR describe is partly the law working as written. One further inference, not argued in the sources: 56(2)(b), the loss of protection for a station powering military operations, is close to inapplicable here, because the plant has generated nothing since September 2022 and draws its power in through the lines the film follows ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/08/8052353/); [Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1149222.html)).

## Only the zone meant to stop the fighting never had a size

The zones around the plant split cleanly into two kinds. The ones with published sizes are all for planning, monitoring or evacuation in case of a release. The ones meant to stop or limit the fighting have no size at all. The IAEA's planning guidance gives a precautionary action zone (PAZ) of 3–5 km, an urgent protective action planning zone (UPZ) of 15–30 km, an extended planning distance of 100 km and an ingestion and commodities planning distance of 300 km for reactors above 1,000 MWth, and insists these radii are "a first approximation" whose real edges "need to be defined by local landmarks" ([IAEA EPR-NPP-PPA 2013](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/EPR-NPP_PPA_web.pdf)). Ukraine's hydrometeorological centre draws dashed rings at 30 and 100 km round every nuclear plant in its daily radiological bulletin ([Ukrhydromet](https://www.meteo.gov.ua/f/radiolog/radiolog%20dovidka/%D0%86%D0%BD%D1%84%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%96%D0%B9%D0%BD%D0%B0%20%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%D0%BA%D0%B0%20%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B4%2031%2001%202026%20%D0%B7%D0%B0%2010-00.pdf)). On the project's own GeoNames distances (Enerhodar 6.2 km, Marhanets 13.6 km, Nikopol 15.9 km from the plant), Enerhodar's centre lies just outside the PAZ's 5 km maximum, and both north-bank towns lie inside any UPZ, so the shelled towns are inside the plant's own emergency planning area.

Drawn at true scale, the protection zone, the plant's own perimeter, sits inside the PAZ, inside the 30 km observation ring, inside the 100 km planning distance. **The one zone meant to stop the fighting is the smallest, the only one without a size, and the only one never agreed.** The zones that were agreed, the repair truces, had no published size either. The circles also carry a known distortion: Fukushima's concentric zones showed distance, not where fallout fell, which followed wind and water far past the rings ([Jacobs, "The Broken Maps of Fukushima"](https://www.dianuke.org/broken-maps-fukushima/)). Any planning ring in the film is a planning convention, and has to be said to be.

## Showing the shield: rings for what has a size, words for what does not

### Domes give an unsized, unagreed proposal the most solid form there is

An animated dome over the plant is tempting, and it has a real source: Grossi's own "a shield, a bubble around the perimeter" ([King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/why-are-the-proposals-for-a-demilitarised-zone-around-europes-biggest-nuclear-power-plant-unprecedented)). But a dome has three problems under the film's rules. It turns a proposal that was never agreed into the most solid-looking shape available. It implies a physical barrier to projectiles or fallout, which nothing in the proposal or the law is. And domes of several sizes side by side read as degrees of protection, when the sized zones are planning distances, not shelters. The defensible version uses one dome only, for Grossi's bubble. It should be a wireframe or dashed hemisphere whose footprint is exactly the plant's OSM site, the extent the IAEA named. It should never close (the arc stops short of its apex, because it was never agreed). It should carry his words and the date, and on 30 March 2023 it should come apart into text, the IAEA's "evolved ... rather than on territorial aspects", rather than vanish. A height equal to the radius would still be the film's invention, so the label should say "drawn". In the zoom-out this belongs in the zone's existing 9 s hold, which already closes in on a dashed circle round the plant's site labelled "proposed, never agreed" with no size published. In the 3D south flight, which ends near the units, the same footprint can be laid on the ground as a dashed outline rather than raised as a volume.

For the zones with published sizes, flat rings on the ground at true scale work better than domes. Drawn as **bands** where the source gives a range (PAZ 3–5 km, UPZ 15–30 km), they make the "first approximation" visible rather than inventing one edge. They can grow outward one at a time from the plant, each labelled with its size and who set it, "suggested maximum radius" for the IAEA's, dashed for Ukrhydromet's as it draws them. They pass Enerhodar, then the river, then Marhanets and Nikopol, which is how the shelled towns fall inside the plant's emergency planning. The unsized zones then arrive as **names without shapes**: type set over the plant, "UN demilitarised perimeter, Aug 2022, no size named", "AP I Art. 56(5): 'the vicinity', undefined". The absence of a ring is the information.

### Claimed firing positions can only be general regions, and only as claims

No launcher, drone point or ammunition store may be drawn as a point. That holds both by the safety rule (no position finer than a region about 3 km across) and because none is independently located inside the fence. The one geometry the sources support at that scale is a region spanning the plant and Enerhodar's industrial area, labelled by who says what: "Ukraine says Russian forces fire on the north bank from the plant and Enerhodar". Inside the same region, in a distinct style, goes "one rocket launch here was geolocated by open-source investigators, 2–3 Sep 2022 (CIR)". The north bank's side should be counts by town or district, never strike points, as Texty and Forensic Architecture do with counts over areas ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/projects/109061/nuclear-terror-how-russia-took-nuclear-plant-and-people-enerhodar-hostage/); [Forensic Architecture](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/humanitarian-violence-in-gaza)). No arc of fire should be drawn from the plant to Nikopol: it would show as a trajectory what no source has traced, and would claim a share of the fire that nobody has measured. The visual grammar should follow the film's existing conventions. Solid means observed: the IAEA's trucks, its mines, as text at the plant, not as marks. Dotted or dashed means not confirmed, as for unreported line cuts and the zone. Type carries attribution. The two sides should not be given team colours; the label carries who says it.

The same material makes a timeline in three lanes: Ukrainian claims above, Russian claims below, and the IAEA and independent observations in the middle. The mid-lane sparseness is the story. The 30 May 2023 Security Council meeting is a single held moment where all three lanes meet: Ukraine's 50 heavy weapons, Russia's "never", Grossi's "not seen" and his five principles on the same day.

The documents carry the argument in their own words. Article 56(1) and 56(5) can be set as type, with "may cause" and "endeavour to avoid" held. Article 56(7) gives the treaty's own sign for such works, "three bright orange circles placed on the same axis", which can head that passage as the law's symbol, said to be ([ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0321.pdf)). Then comes the 6 September 2022 recommendation, and Update 152's sentence of 30 March 2023, in which "a zone around the plant" gives way to "what should be avoided". The five principles follow, principle 1's "from or against" held a beat longer: the IAEA's own text names the possibility of attack *from* the plant without ever saying it happened. Grossi's "whoever" can recur as a typographic refrain each time a strike appears in the record. Each quotation must come from the source text, not from syndication. The NYT, ISW and Truth Hounds originals were reached only through reprints or summaries and need checking before they are set on screen.

### Counts and sound: the record is mostly heard, and the film must not play the fighting

Counts suit the shield sequence because they are what the sources actually give. Nikopol's drone attacks by June (0, 290, 362) can be shown as a stepped tally once the Truth Hounds figures are checked. The conflicting casualty totals should be shown as conflicting (67 killed by January 2024, 61 by September), never reconciled by the film. The votes demanding withdrawal (Board 26–2–7, Assembly 99–9–60, General Conference 69 falling to 63) are a sequence of numbers on the dates they were cast. The film's existing count of complete losses of off-site power already sits top left, and principle 3 links the two counts.

Sound is where the subject most tempts the film to break its own rule. Much of what the IAEA knows is aural: it heard "sixteen rounds of outgoing artillery fire in less than half an hour", "hundreds of rounds of small arms fire" ([Update 223](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-223-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 303](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-303-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)). The film's rule is no sounds of fighting, and a synthesized salvo would also be a generated record of an event. What fits is the opposite: the Hum drops out, and the IAEA's sentence appears as type in silence, the count of rounds set as a number rather than played. The zone's day, 6 September 2022, already falls inside island mode, when the Hum holds a single low note because every line was cut. That makes the zone's hold the natural place for the shield beat without adding a sound. A narration line, drafted in `config/narration.yaml` like the others, could carry the one sentence the evidence supports: *"The IAEA saw military trucks inside the plant, and heard rockets fired from close to it. It has never said who fired."*

## Conclusion

The question "is the plant a nuclear shield?" fuses two claims of very different weight. One is that military objectives were placed at the plant. The IAEA observed this, and it is exactly what Article 56(5) addresses. The other is that they were placed there *in order to* fire with impunity, which is what "shield" means. That is an inference from pattern, made by Ukraine, ISW and Washington, consistent with the NYT's reporting and with one geolocated launch beside the fence, and it cannot be shown as observed. The film gains by keeping the two apart rather than resolving them. The shield's force comes from exactly this gap: the law forbids striking what might release radiation, the inspectors on site decline to attribute, access is controlled by the occupier, and each explosion yields two stories. A sequence that shows trucks as seen, rockets as heard, launch points as claimed and the blame as unassigned does not weaken the story. It shows how the shield works.

The zones offer the film its most original image, and it runs against the dome. Laid at true scale, the only zone ever proposed to stop the fighting is the smallest ring, has no size of its own, was never agreed, and was talked out of territory into a list of verbs within seven months. Meanwhile the planning rings that have sizes reach across the river to the towns being shelled. A film that draws the sized zones as bands and the unsized ones as words, and lets the never-closed bubble come apart into the text that replaced it, says something no other treatment of the plant has said, without breaking the evidence rules. The work left before any of it goes on screen: check the NYT, ISW and Truth Hounds originals, read Cabinet resolution No. 841, and sweep the IAEA's 2024–2026 updates for any assessment of principle 2.
