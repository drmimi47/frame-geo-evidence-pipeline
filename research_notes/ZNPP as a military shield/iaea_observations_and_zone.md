# What the IAEA itself observed about military presence at the ZNPP, and the fate of its protection zone (March 2022 – November 2026)

Method note (9 November 2026). iaea.org blocks plain fetches (HTTP 402/403, Cloudflare), so IAEA pages were read in a headless browser (Playwright) and quoted from the rendered text. The ISAMZ report PDF came from the Wayback Machine copy of the IAEA's own file and was read with pdftotext. GlobalSecurity.org reprints (verbatim copies of IAEA texts) were used only where the iaea.org page could not be loaded, and are marked. WNN, VOA and ACA are used only to locate or date IAEA words, and are marked. Every quotation below was read in the source text. Search-engine summaries were not quoted.

Tags used below: **[OBSERVED]** = the IAEA team saw it itself. **[HEARD]** = the team heard it. **[TOLD]** = the IAEA was informed by Ukraine (SNRIU, Energoatom), by the Russian side (Rosatom, the Russian-installed ZNPP management) or by plant staff. **[DG]** = an assessment or appeal by Director General Grossi.

## Q1. What did the ISAMZ (1 September 2022 visit) report on 6 September 2022 about military equipment and personnel?

### Takeaway
The IAEA's *Nuclear Safety, Security and Safeguards in Ukraine: 2nd Summary Report by the Director General, 28 April – 5 September 2022* (released 6 September 2022) says the team itself "observed the presence of Russian military personnel, vehicles and equipment at various places at the ZNPP, including several military trucks on the ground floor of the Unit 1 and Unit 2 turbine halls and military vehicles stationed under the overpass connecting the reactor units". It recommended removing the vehicles and establishing a "nuclear safety and security protection zone". Earlier claims about the turbine halls (July–August 2022) are attributed to Ukraine.

### Cited Findings
- **[OBSERVED] Para. 39 (printed pp. 13–14):** "The team observed the presence of Russian military personnel, vehicles and equipment at various places at the ZNPP, including several military trucks on the ground floor of the Unit 1 and Unit 2 turbine halls and military vehicles stationed under the overpass connecting the reactor units. The team also observed the presence of an expert group from Rosenergoatom." Photo caption: "Military vehicles on the ground floor of the turbine hall of Unit 2 of ZNPP (Photo: IAEA)" — [IAEA 2nd Summary Report, 6 Sep 2022 (Wayback copy of IAEA PDF)](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf); original URL [iaea.org PDF](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)
- **Boxed finding after para. 39:** "these efforts are being taken under very challenging circumstances with military personnel and equipment as well as representatives of Rosatom being present on the site." — [same report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)

- **[OBSERVED/established] Para. 47 (printed p. 15), access controlled by the military:** "the team established that the operating staff did not have unrestricted access to some areas, such as the spray cooling ponds, roofs of the buildings, and structures in the area of the water intake, and that access to the cooling ponds area was required to be granted by the military personnel at the site." — [same report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)
- **[OBSERVED/HEARD] Para. 30, shelling during the mission:** "the team closely witnessed shelling in the vicinity of the ZNPP, in particular on 3 September when the team was instructed to evacuate to the ground level of the Administrative Building." - **Satellite imagery, para. 158:** the IAEA "has been acquiring and analysing satellite imagery … including to detect damage caused by shelling at the site. … Evaluations resulting from the satellite imagery analyses were corroborated on the ground by the ISAMZ team." — [same report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)
- **[DG] UNSC, 6 Sep 2022 (IAEA news story, read in GlobalSecurity's verbatim reprint):** Grossi "recommended that the military vehicles and equipment on the site be removed so as not to interfere with normal operation of the nuclear safety and security systems." — [IAEA story "UN Security Council: IAEA Grossi Calls for Establishment of Nuclear Safety and Security Protection Zone at Zaporizhzhya NPP", 7 Sep 2022, via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2022/09/ukraine-220907-iaea02.htm)

### Inferences
- The well-known "military vehicles in turbine halls" line is a first-hand IAEA observation (para. 39), not only a repeated Ukrainian claim. The report keeps the two apart: Ukraine's July–August claims are in para. 23, and the team's own sighting is in para. 39. It does not say what the vehicles were (no weapons named), only "military trucks" and "military vehicles".
- The report names no heavy weapons and attributes no shelling to either side.

### Gaps
- I could not open the IAEA's own page for the 6 Sep 2022 UNSC statement (Cloudflare challenge on iaea.org/node/108579). Its wording here comes from the IAEA news story reprinted on GlobalSecurity.
- A search-engine summary claimed the report spoke of "a demilitarized perimeter" as "a second step". **This is not in the report text** (grep for "demilitar" and "second step" finds nothing). Treat it as unsupported.

## Q2. Later IAEA observations: mines, troops, vehicles, rooftop access, what the team could not see, heavy weapons, firing, strikes and attribution

### Takeaway
Through 2023–2026 the IAEA reported first-hand: directional anti-personnel mines in the buffer zone between the inner and outer perimeter (seen 23 July 2023, still there on 1 August); military trucks in the Unit 2 turbine hall (8 August 2023); Russian troops on the Unit 6 roof engaging a drone (7 April 2024). It never reported seeing heavy weapons at the site. On 24 July 2023 it said it had seen no "heavy military equipment". It reported *hearing* "outgoing artillery fire from near the plant" (9 and 13 April 2024) without saying who fired. It consistently declined to attribute strikes ("Whoever is shelling…", Nov 2022; "Whoever is behind them…", Apr 2024). Access was repeatedly restricted: roofs (granted for Units 3–4 only on 3 August 2023), parts of turbine halls, impact sites "due to security reasons", and cooling-tower levels in August 2024.

### Inferences
- In the IAEA's own record, military presence on site is documented at the level of trucks, vehicles, personnel, mines and troops on a reactor roof. Heavy weapons (MLRS, artillery, tanks) are never reported as seen. The one explicit statement is negative (24 July 2023). "Outgoing artillery fire from near the plant" is heard, and the IAEA does not say from where exactly or by whom.
- Attribution: across every update read, the IAEA does not name who struck the plant. It uses "whoever" (Nov 2022, Apr 2024) and passive or "military activity" wording.
- Access restrictions are a recurring theme: roofs (2022 staff, 2023 IAEA), parts of turbine halls (2023), impact sites "due to security reasons" (Apr 2024), and the cooling-tower interior (Aug 2024). The Jan 2024 UNSC line "sometimes for many months" is the DG's own summary.

## Q3. The protection zone: proposal, fate, the five principles, and 30 March 2023

### Takeaway
The IAEA proposed the "nuclear safety and security protection zone" in its 6 September 2022 report. That day Grossi told the UNSC it would be "limited to the perimeter and the plant itself". It was pushed through consultations into 2023 (repeated in November 2022 after heavy shelling) but never agreed and never given a published size. On 30 March 2023, after Grossi's 29 March visit, the IAEA said the plan "had evolved from an initial proposal to establish a zone around the plant to now focus more on what should be avoided … rather than on territorial aspects." On 30 May 2023 Grossi presented and "established" five concrete principles at the UN Security Council. The IAEA's mission on site (ISAMZ) was to report on them, and the DG to report violations publicly.

### Cited Findings
- **The proposal, 6 Sep 2022 (report):** "Recommendation 1: The IAEA recommends that shelling on site and in its vicinity should be stopped immediately … This requires agreement by all relevant parties to the establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone around the ZNPP." Para. 175: "Pending the end of the conflict … there is an urgent need for interim measures to prevent a nuclear accident arising from physical damage caused by military means. This can be achieved by the immediate establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone. The IAEA is ready to start immediately the consultations leading to the urgent establishment of such a nuclear safety and security protection zone at the ZNPP." — [IAEA 2nd Summary Report](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/09/ukraine-2ndsummaryreport_sept2022.pdf)
- **"Perimeter and the plant itself", UNSC video briefing, 6 Sep 2022:** "This is why in our report we are proposing the establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone limited to the perimeter and the plant itself." — [VOA via GlobalSecurity, 6 Sep 2022](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2022/09/ukraine-220906-voa02.htm) (mirror); the same words, "to the perimeter and the plant itself", appear in [WNN, 7 Sep 2022](https://world-nuclear.org/ukraine-information/wnn-ukraine/wnn-iaea-calls-for-nuclear-safety-exclusion-zone-a) (mirror). The report itself gives no size.
- **Update 99, 7 Sep 2022:** "Yesterday, the Director General recommended the urgent establishment of a Nuclear Safety and Security Protection Zone at the ZNPP, and he is having consultations aimed at implementing this plan." — [IAEA Update 99](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-99-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- **Still pressed, Nov 2022:** Update 129 (20 Nov): the zone "must happen now". Update 130 (21 Nov): "the Director General intensified his consultations aimed at establishing a protection zone at the plant." — [Update 129](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-129-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 130](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-130-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- **30 March 2023, Update 152 (after his visit "this week"; ACA dates it 29 March):** "He said the plan to achieve this vital objective had evolved from an initial proposal to establish a zone around the plant to now focus more on what should be avoided to ensure its protection, rather than on territorial aspects." Grossi: "It is work in progress. It is very, very important that we agree on the fundamental principle that a nuclear plant should not be attacked under any circumstances, and it also shouldn't be used to attack others." On the region: "It is obvious that military activity is increasing in this whole region. There is open talk of offensives and counter offensives." — [IAEA Update 152, 30 Mar 2023](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-152-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- **Context (mirror):** ACA dates the visit to 29 March and quotes Grossi to AP on 28 March: negotiations were "affected by the ongoing military options", and his approach was "a series of principle[s] or commitments [that] everybody would be able to support", an agreement with the IAEA in which the parties "are not agreeing with each other." — [Arms Control Association, May 2023](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2023-05/news/iaea-shifts-priorities-zaporizhzhia) (secondary)
- **The five principles, UNSC, 30 May 2023 (date confirmed by Update 160: "presented to the United Nations Security Council on 30 May"):** "I have identified the following concrete principles to help ensure nuclear safety and security at ZNPP in order to prevent a nuclear accident and ensure the integrity of the plant": — [IAEA DG statement to the UNSC, 30 May 2023, verbatim via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2023/05/ukraine-230530-iaea01.htm); principles 1, 2, 4 and 5 are the same word for word in the [IAEA DG's Board statement, 11 Apr 2024](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/statements/iaea-director-generals-introductory-statement-to-the-board-of-governors-11-april-2024)
  1. "There should be no attack of any kind from or against the plant, in particular targeting the reactors, spent fuel storage, other critical infrastructure, or personnel;"
  2. "ZNPP should not be used as storage or a base for heavy weapons (i.e. multiple rocket launchers, artillery systems and munitions, and tanks) or military personnel that could be used for an attack from the plant;"
  3. "Off-site power to the plant should not be put at risk. To that effect, all efforts should be made to ensure that off-site power remains available and secure at all times;"
  4. "All structures, systems and components essential to the safe and secure operation of ZNPP should be protected from attacks or acts of sabotage;"
  5. "No action should be taken that undermines these principles."
  Then: "The IAEA experts onsite, namely the IAEA Support and Assistance Mission to Zaporizhzhya (ISAMZ), will report to the IAEA Director General on the observance of these principles. The Director General will report publicly on any violations of these principles." "The IAEA's five principles to avoid a nuclear accident are hereby established." — [same](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2023/05/ukraine-230530-iaea01.htm)
- **Update 160, 2 Jun 2023:** "While the principles won broad support at the United Nations Security Council …". "Based on their statements, I expect that these five basic principles will be adhered to … If there are any violations … I will not hesitate to report about them publicly." — [IAEA Update 160](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-160-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)

### Inferences
- The zone was never formally withdrawn by name. Update 152 recasts it as "evolved" away from "territorial aspects", and the principles replaced it in practice two months later. Principle 2 is the nearest descendant of the zone's demilitarising aim. It is narrower: it bans *heavy* weapons and military personnel "that could be used for an attack from the plant", not all military presence. Military trucks, guards and mines are not explicitly covered.
- The principles are commitments to the IAEA, not a bilateral agreement (Grossi to AP, via ACA). This is why "never agreed" fits the zone and "established" (unilaterally, by the DG) fits the principles.
- The local repair ceasefires that the IAEA negotiated from late 2025 (the "sixth" by June 2026, Update 353) are the only zones that were actually agreed. They are temporary and serve repairs, not protection.

### Gaps
- I could not open the iaea.org page of the 30 May 2023 UNSC statement (404 at the guessed URL, Cloudflare challenge at node/151189). The text above is GlobalSecurity's verbatim reprint, and four of the five principles were checked word for word against the IAEA's own 11 Apr 2024 Board statement. Principle 3's wording was checked against Update 160's partial quote ("should not be put at risk", "all efforts should be made to ensure that off-site power remains available and secure at all times"), which matches.
- No published radius or map of the zone was found in any IAEA text. RFE/RL (via search only, not read) reported that radius was discussed in September 2022.
- The project config's date of "30 March 2023" for the zone being "set aside for actions to avoid" is supported by Update 152's wording.

## Q4. Did the IAEA say the principles were being respected (2023–2026)?

### Takeaway
Until April 2024 the IAEA said there were "no indications that the five concrete principles are not being observed" (UNSC, Jan 2024; Board, Mar 2024), while noting that access gaps limited its monitoring. The drone strikes of 7 April 2024 were declared "a clear violation" and "the first clear violation". From then to late 2026, the IAEA repeatedly called for "strict observance" and "full respect" after strikes near or on the site (August 2024, July 2025, May–June 2026, August 2026) and said drone strikes "would violate" the principles. It never named a violator.

### Cited Findings
- **26 Jan 2024, UNSC (Update 208):** There "have been no indications that the five concrete principles are not being observed," … "Nevertheless … the Agency needs to have timely access to all areas of the ZNPP of significance for nuclear safety and security, to monitor that all five concrete principles are being observed at all times." — [IAEA Update 208](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-208-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)
- **11 Apr 2024, Board:** "At the Board of Governors meeting just one month ago … I was able to report to you that ISAMZ had not found any indications the five concrete principles were not being observed. The most recent attacks, however, are a clear violation of the principles". — [IAEA Board statement, 11 Apr 2024](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/statements/iaea-director-generals-introductory-statement-to-the-board-of-governors-11-april-2024)
- **7 Apr 2024 (Update 220):** "a clear violation of the five basic principles"; **9 Apr 2024 (Update 221):** "the first clear violation of the five concrete principles". — [Update 220](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-220-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine); [Update 221](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-221-iaea-director-general-statement-on-situation-in-ukraine)

### Inferences
- The IAEA's record runs in three phases: "no indications" of violation (June 2023 – March 2024); declared violation, unattributed (April 2024); then two and a half years of appeals to "respect", "observe" and "abide by" the principles, with "would violate" in the conditional. Principle 3 (off-site power) is now the one most often invoked, as the plant has run on one line since March 2026.
- Grossi's 30 May 2023 promise to "report publicly on any violations" has been kept only in the sense of *declaring* violations, never of naming who committed them.
