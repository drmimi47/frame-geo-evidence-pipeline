**The paper** (reference only, never on screen). Vyshnevskyi, V. and Shevchuk, S. (2024), "The
destruction of the Kakhovka dam and the future of the Kakhovske reservoir", International Journal
of Environmental Studies 81(1), 275-288, doi 10.1080/00207233.2024.2320033 (checked against
Crossref; no open licence listed). All 14 pages, as the user's screenshots, are in
`research_notes/Understanding the ZNPP site/Vyshnevskyi and Shevchuk 2024 pages/` (page-NN.png is
printed page 274+NN). (c) Taylor & Francis, kept out of a public commit (the folder is
gitignored). Its Figure 11 is in the storyboard as published, credited on the frame, between the
block opening and the sections (seepage-paper; the user, 5 November 2026); the dike photos stay
references only.
- [ ] before any public screening: Taylor & Francis's permission for Figure 11, or the same
      Sentinel-2A pass of 5 July 2023 (open data) in its place, the paper's claim quoted
- p. 284: the pond (8.2 km2, cut from the reservoir) "slowly subsided through evaporation and
  seepage of water through the dyke; there now being a 10 m difference between the pond level and
  the adjacent branch of the river. The satellite images (Figure 11) reveal many places where
  seepage is taking place." Figure 11 (p. 285) is one Sentinel-2 image of 5 July 2023 (via
  EarthCache/SkyWatch), with circles on wet patches at the foot of the dike's west and north sides.
- So the seepage is inferred from one image, not measured: no flow, no level series, no date for
  the "10 m". On 5 July 2023 the whole bed had been drained four weeks and was still wet, so wet
  patches at the dike's foot don't yet tell seepage from drying mud. Against it: Energoatom (June
  2023, the pond "separate", "not falling") and the IAEA (the fall put down to use and
  evaporation). An open question, not a finding.
- The seepage test (not done): the same pass and every clear pass after; wet patches at the
  dike's foot that stay wet through dry months while the bed dries and the pond falls would
  support seepage; patches that dry with the bed would not. Our numbers, not the paper's figure.
- The "10 m", checked against our levels (SWOT reaches, monthly medians, flagged "bad" as in the
  3D config, so rough): the river beside the plant (about 34.57 E) about 8.0-8.7 m in Aug-Sep
  2023, at Nikopol (about 34.33 E) about 6.0-6.8 m (EGM2008; Baltic about 0.4 m lower). The
  paper's p. 282 has the river "about 6 m asl" near Nikopol at the end of summer 2023. So its
  10 m is pond against Nikopol; at the dike itself about 8-9 m in 2023. Then the gap shrinks
  because the pond falls (16.67 m June 2023; 13.54 m August 2025; 12.27 m October 2026, HUR,
  unverified) while the river holds about 7 m beside the plant: about 6.5 m in 2025, about 5 m
  in 2026. The section's new knowledge: not a leak, the pond running down toward the IAEA's 12 m
  (below which the cooling systems stop working, Babel citing the IAEA).

**What the section tells** (agreed after the paper was read): a retelling with one sourced
future, not an imagined one.
- The retelling, dated: 2021 the pond and reservoir at about the same level; June 2023 the river
  drops away, a wall of water about 8-9 m held by earth; 2023-2026 the pond falls, the gap closes
  from the wrong side, the 12 m threshold drawn as a line.
- The seepage only as the paper's claim, with its date and that it rests on one image, unless our
  own test backs it. Never drawn as water flowing through the dike.
- No dike failing: no source predicts it (IRSN via CESBIO: the dikes withstand a river at 10 m,
  and it went lower without a breach reported); it would show the inferred as observed and read
  as a map of a weak point. With the units in cold shutdown the pond matters for the 12 m
  threshold and a restart, not a sudden flood.
- The sourced future: the paper's proposal (p. 286), the reservoir rebuilt to the dead-storage
  level 12.7 m, then about 15.5 m; a ghost line labelled a proposal. Pairs with the peace plan's
  point 12 and the Great Meadow going under again (I9).
- Safety: the dike shown as a whole, from public data, never as a weak point; any wet patches
  said as a region ("along the dike's north side").
- On screen: the bed's heights inferred (waterline method); the towers' and the dike's shapes
  representative where not measured; the river heights SWOT's, flagged unreliable here.

**What the passes found** (3-4 November 2026; the drawings themselves in `reports/S1-section/`):
- The dike reads in FABDEM (crest about 19 m Baltic, the bed outside about 10 m, the river branch
  about 7 m, SWOT Oct 2026): beside the dike the pond stands about 5 m above the river, not 10.
  The pond's depth isn't published (FABDEM shows its surface flat at 15.0 m): drawn fading out.
- True to size the valley's 30 m over 18 km is a hairline, so heights are exaggerated (said on
  screen), but one exaggeration can't serve both: x25 makes each caisson's 11 m 260 m, so the
  crossing needs x5 or less, or one scale for all (x10); at x10 the dike is a low bump (4-7 m above the pond). A
  west-facing cut puts north on the left, so cuts face east.
- Pond levels are of mixed weight: Energoatom (2023), Russia's circulars to the IAEA (2024), the
  IAEA (2025), HUR (Oct 2026, unverified). Sentinel-2's water area of the pond's western part on

## The film: title, story, brief and wording rules

Kept here for reference while N1 and S3 are open.

**Title:** Powerlines, one word, no subtitle (the user). "Power" as electricity and as control;
"lines" as the cables and the front line on the Dnipro they cross. The Conductive Seam stays the
name of the evidence folder and the research.

**Story:** A, the margin; the ceasefire for one span its central chapter. The sentence, a draft
to argue over (N1): "The lines carry almost nothing; what they carry is time. When the last line
is cut a countdown starts, and only a repair, made in a truce of hours or days over a single span,
stops it."

**The brief** (the user's own, agreed 3 November 2026: a wall text, one beat a stanza; in the
cut as brief-2 to brief-5 and one-fifth; brief-1, the dam's stanza, brief-7 and brief-8 parked or
hidden; end-card-b says the dam's beat its own way):

    Ten lines leave the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant, the largest in Europe.
    Before the war they carried more than a fifth of Ukraine's electricity.

    Occupied since March 2022, the plant has made no power since that September.
    Its six reactors are shut down, but their fuel must still be cooled,
    and the cooling runs on power drawn back from the grid it once fed.

    The lines that carried its power out now carry power in.
    What they bring is time.

    When the last line is cut, diesel generators start, and a countdown begins.
    Twenty-six times, every line was lost. The longest loss lasted a month.

    Since October 2025 the war has paused seven times, for hours or days,
    so that a crew could mend a single span of wire.

    Downstream, the dam was destroyed in June 2023.
    The towers that stood in the reservoir now stand on its dry bed.

    By September 2026, one line of ten remained.

    Powerlines follows the ten through four years of occupation,
    in satellite imagery by day and by night, maps, public reports and footage.
    It marks what is observed, what is drawn and what is inferred.

Sources, line by line: more than a fifth (22-28% by the published figures, S3), cold shutdown and the 11-50 MW draw (`reports/Understanding the
ZNPP site.md`); 26 complete losses by August 2026 (the IAEA), a month on diesel 23 Sep-23 Oct
2025, only Ferosplavna-1 on 11 Sep 2026 (the record); seven truces since October 2025, hours or
days (`reports/Ceasefire for one span.md`). Two checks before it is printed are in N1. Left out,
as in the user's version: island mode, the towns and families (I8), plants and animals (I9), the
years ahead (N3).

**Wording rules** (agreed with the user; for every text in the film):
1. Not "all of Europe": more than a fifth of Ukraine's electricity and nearly half its nuclear
   output (2020), in the past tense; it provides none now.
2. Not meltdown: in cold shutdown since 13 April 2024 (the last unit, unit 4), each core well under 1 MW; a total loss
   starts a countdown of days to weeks, worst in the spent fuel pools. The draw from the grid is
   11-50 MW, about 0.1-0.3% of Ukraine's peak.
3. Cold shutdown is the safe state; the lines keep it cooled while it is in it. The fuel is kept
   cooled, not the plant "operational".
4. Not "sabotage": "occupied" and "cut", unless a source says who. "Inferred", not "speculation",
   for what is worked out from evidence.
Also: only the last line's cut starts the diesel countdown; truces only since October 2025, not
for every repair.
